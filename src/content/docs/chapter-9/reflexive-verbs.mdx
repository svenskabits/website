A reflexive verb is used with a pronoun that refers back to the subject—the person or thing doing the action. For example, **Jag tvättar mig** means “I wash myself.” Many common Swedish verbs are used this way, and the reflexive pronoun changes with the subject.

| Subject | Reflexive pronoun | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jag (I) | mig | Jag tvättar mig. — I wash myself. |
| du (you, singular) | dig | Du tvättar dig. — You wash yourself. |
| han, hon, den, det (he, she, it) | sig | Hon tvättar sig. — She washes herself. |
| vi (we) | oss | Vi tvättar oss. — We wash ourselves. |
| ni (you, plural) | er | Ni tvättar er. — You wash yourselves. |
| de (they) | sig | De tvättar sig. — They wash themselves. |

The verb itself does not change according to the subject in the present or past. It is the pronoun that changes:

| Infinitive | Present | Past | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| tvätta sig | Jag tvättar mig. | Jag tvättade mig. | I wash / washed myself. |
| lägga sig | Du lägger dig. | Du lade dig. | You lie down / lay down. |
| känna sig | De känner sig trötta. | De kände sig trötta. | They feel / felt tired. |

## Common reflexive verbs

Learn the infinitive together with its pronoun, usually written with **sig**. In a sentence, replace **sig** with the pronoun that matches the subject.

| Infinitive | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tvätta sig | wash oneself | Han tvättar sig på morgonen. — He washes in the morning. |
| raka sig | shave | Jag rakar mig före jobbet. — I shave before work. |
| klä på sig | get dressed | Barnen klär på sig. — The children get dressed. |
| ta av sig | take off (one's clothes) | Hon tar av sig skorna. — She takes off her shoes. |
| sätta sig | sit down | Sätt dig här! — Sit down here! |
| lägga sig | lie down; go to bed | Vi lägger oss tidigt i kväll. — We are going to bed early tonight. |
| känna sig | feel | Jag känner mig bättre i dag. — I feel better today. |
| skynda sig | hurry | Ni måste skynda er. — You have to hurry. |
| göra sig illa | hurt oneself | Han gjorde sig illa på träningen. — He hurt himself at practice. |
| ta hand om sig | take care of oneself | Du behöver ta hand om dig. — You need to take care of yourself. |

Some reflexive verbs describe an action people do to themselves. Others have a meaning that is not simply “do something to oneself.” For example, **känna sig** means “feel,” and **lägga sig** commonly means “lie down” or “go to bed.”

## Reflexive and non-reflexive meanings

Some verbs can be used with or without a reflexive pronoun. With the pronoun, the subject is also the person or thing affected. Without it, there may be a different object:

- **Hon tvättar sig.** — She washes herself.
- **Hon tvättar hunden.** — She washes the dog.
- **Jag lägger mig på soffan.** — I lie down on the sofa.
- **Jag lägger boken på bordet.** — I put the book on the table.

The pronoun is also needed with a reflexive verb when the action is understood from context:

- **Han rakar sig varje morgon.** — He shaves every morning.
- **Hon klär på sig snabbt.** — She gets dressed quickly.

Without the reflexive pronoun, these sentences would not mean that the subject is shaving or getting dressed.

## Word order

In a simple statement, put the reflexive pronoun after the conjugated verb (the verb that shows tense):

- **Jag tvättar mig efter träningen.** — I wash after training.
- **De satte sig vid fönstret.** — They sat down by the window.

With a modal verb such as **kan**, **vill** or **måste**, the main verb stays in the infinitive. Put the reflexive pronoun after that infinitive, including any particle that belongs to the verb:

- **Jag måste lägga mig nu.** — I have to go to bed now.
- **Hon vill klä på sig själv.** — She wants to get dressed by herself.
- **Du kan ta av dig jackan här.** — You can take off your jacket here.

In a main clause, **inte** usually comes after the reflexive pronoun when the reflexive verb is conjugated:

- **Han känner sig inte sjuk.** — He does not feel ill.
- **Jag tvättar mig inte på kvällen.** — I do not wash in the evening.

With a modal verb, **inte** comes after the conjugated modal and before the infinitive:

- **Jag vill inte lägga mig än.** — I do not want to go to bed yet.
- **Hon kan inte sätta sig här.** — She cannot sit down here.

In a subordinate clause introduced by a word such as **att** (that), **om** (if/whether) or **när** (when), **inte** comes before the verb, while the reflexive pronoun follows the verb:

- **Jag vet att hon inte känner sig bra.** — I know that she does not feel well.
- **Han frågade om jag kunde sätta mig där.** — He asked if I could sit down there.

## Commands and other verb forms

In a command, use the pronoun that matches the person or people being addressed:

- **Klä på dig!** — Get dressed!
- **Sätt er här!** — Sit down here! (to more than one person)
- **Skynda dig inte!** — Do not hurry!

The pronoun stays with the reflexive verb in other tenses and verb forms too:

- **Hon har lagt sig.** — She has gone to bed.
- **Vi ska ta hand om oss.** — We are going to take care of ourselves.
- **Han tycker om att röra på sig.** — He likes to exercise (literally, to move around).

## When the meaning is “each other”

With a plural subject, a reflexive pronoun refers to the members of the subject individually. To clearly say that they do something to one another, use **varandra**:

- **Barnen tvättar sig.** — The children wash themselves.
- **Barnen tvättar varandra.** — The children wash each other.

The reflexive pronoun still refers to the subject within its own clause:

- **Anna säger att Erik rakar sig.** — Anna says that Erik is shaving.

Here, **sig** refers to Erik, the subject of **rakar sig**, not to Anna.
