Use **någon**, **något**, and **några** when you mean an unspecified person or thing, or an unspecified amount. Depending on the sentence, they can mean **someone**, **something**, **some**, or **any**.

## Choose the form

When the word comes before a noun, it agrees with the noun:

| Noun | Form | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singular en-word | **någon** | **Har du någon fråga?** | Do you have a question? / Do you have any questions? |
| Singular ett-word | **något** | **Har du något äpple?** | Do you have an apple? |
| Plural noun | **några** | **Vi har några frågor.** | We have some questions. |

The form depends on the noun, not on who has or wants the thing. In the singular, **någon** goes with en-words and **något** with ett-words. Use **några** with plural nouns.

## Use them without a noun

When the noun is not said because it is clear from the situation, the forms can stand alone. **Någon** usually refers to a person, **något** to a thing or event, and **några** to people or things in the plural:

- **Någon har ringt.** — Someone has called.
- **Vill du säga något?** — Do you want to say something?
- **Några väntar utanför.** — Some people are waiting outside.
- **Jag köpte fyra äpplen, men några var dåliga.** — I bought four apples, but some were bad.

## In questions: often “any”

In questions, **någon/något/några** often refer to something whose existence is not known. English often uses **any** in these questions:

- **Har du någon penna?** — Do you have a pen? / Do you have any pens?
- **Finns det något att äta?** — Is there anything to eat?
- **Har ni några barn?** — Do you have any children?

They are also common when offering or asking for something. English may use **some** in these cases:

- **Vill du ha något att dricka?** — Would you like something to drink?
- **Vill du ha några kakor?** — Would you like some cookies?

## With negative sentences

In a negative sentence, **någon/något/några** can mean **any** or **anyone/anything**. The more direct and very common way to express “no” or “not any” is often **ingen/inget/inga**. Both patterns are possible:

| With **inte** and a form of **någon** | With a form of **ingen** | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Jag har inte någon bil.** | **Jag har ingen bil.** | I don't have a car. |
| **Hon såg inte något djur.** | **Hon såg inget djur.** | She didn't see any animal. |
| **Vi har inte några frågor.** | **Vi har inga frågor.** | We don't have any questions. |

When the word stands alone, the same distinction applies:

- **Jag känner inte någon här.** — I don't know anyone here.
- **Det finns inget att göra.** — There is nothing to do.
- **Vi hittade inte några.** — We didn't find any.

Use **ingen/inget/inga** when you want to state directly that there is none; this is often the most natural choice. The forms of **någon** with **inte** are also correct, especially when the sentence is built around **inte** or when the negative is emphasized.

## “Some” and “a few”

With a plural noun, **några** means an unspecified number, often translated as **some** or **a few**:

- **Jag behöver några dagar för att tänka.** — I need a few days to think.
- **Några gäster kommer efter klockan sju.** — Some guests are coming after seven.

With a singular noun, **någon** or **något** can mean “some” or “a certain,” without saying exactly which one:

- **Vi kan träffas någon dag nästa vecka.** — We can meet some day next week.
- **Jag hörde något ljud från köket.** — I heard some kind of sound from the kitchen.

## “One of” and “some of” a known group

To refer to one or more members of a specific group, use **någon/något/några av** followed by a definite plural noun or a pronoun:

- **Någon av mina vänner bor i Malmö.** — One of my friends lives in Malmö.
- **Något av barnen behöver hjälp.** — One of the children needs help.
- **Några av böckerna är på svenska.** — Some of the books are in Swedish.
- **Några av dem kommer i kväll.** — Some of them are coming tonight.

The form still agrees with the thing or group being referred to: **någon** with an en-word, **något** with an ett-word, and **några** with plural items.

## Before adjectives and infinitives

The forms can come before an adjective when the noun is understood. The adjective usually takes its neuter form after **något** and its plural form after **några**:

- **Har du någon annan fråga?** — Do you have another question?
- **Jag vill läsa något enkelt.** — I want to read something easy.
- **Vi behöver några varma kläder.** — We need some warm clothes.

Use **något att** plus an infinitive to say “something to” do:

- **Har du något att läsa?** — Do you have something to read?
- **Det finns något att äta i kylskåpet.** — There is something to eat in the fridge.

## A special adverb use of “något”

Before some adjectives and adverbs, **något** means **somewhat** or **a little**. Here it does not agree with a noun:

- **Det är något kallare i dag.** — It is a little colder today.
- **Kan du tala något långsammare?** — Can you speak a little more slowly?

In casual speech and informal writing, **någon** and **något** are often shortened to **nån** and **nåt**. These spellings represent informal pronunciation; use the full forms in more formal writing. **Några** is also often pronounced with a reduced final vowel, but is normally still written **några**.

Remember: before a noun, choose the form by the noun (**någon fråga, något problem, några frågor**). Without a noun, **någon** usually means someone, **något** something, and **några** some people or things; the sentence tells you whether English needs “some” or “any.”
