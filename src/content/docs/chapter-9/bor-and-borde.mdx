**Bör** and **borde** are forms of the modal verb **böra**. They express what is advisable, expected, or the right thing to do. In English, they often correspond to “should” or “ought to.”

## Forms and basic pattern

The verb does not change with the subject. Put the infinitive of the main verb directly after **bör** or **borde**, without **att**:

| Form | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bör | Du bör vila. — You should rest. | Present form; often sounds relatively formal or direct. |
| borde | Du borde vila. — You should rest. | Common for advice; often sounds less direct. |

The infinitive **böra** is mostly found in dictionaries and formal writing. In everyday sentences, **bör** and **borde** are the forms learners are most likely to need. **Borde** is historically the past form of **böra**, but it often refers to advice now or in the future, not to something in the past.

## Giving advice and making recommendations

Use **bör** or **borde** to say that an action is sensible or recommended. **Bör** is common in written advice, instructions, and more formal statements. **Borde** is very common in conversation and often makes advice sound gentler. The difference is not absolute, and tone and context matter.

- **Du bör dricka mycket vatten när det är varmt.** — You should drink plenty of water when it is hot.
- **Du borde prata med läkaren om hostan.** — You should talk to the doctor about the cough.
- **Vi borde ta bussen; det regnar.** — We should take the bus; it is raining.
- **Man bör tvätta händerna före maten.** — One should wash one's hands before eating.

The subject **man** means “one” or “people in general” and is often used for general recommendations.

## Expectations and what is appropriate

The same forms can describe what is expected or considered proper, not only give personal advice:

- **Mötet bör vara slut klockan tre.** — The meeting should be over at three.
- **Bussen borde komma snart.** — The bus should arrive soon.
- **Du bör säga till om du blir sjuk.** — You should let someone know if you become ill.

In statements about expectations, **borde** can show that the speaker expects something, while allowing for uncertainty. **Bör** is also used for expectations, especially in formal or factual language.

## Talking about an action that should have happened

To say that someone should have done something, use **borde ha** followed by the main verb's supine form. The supine is the form used after **har** in a perfect tense. This construction often suggests that the action did not happen, or that the speaker is looking back critically or regretfully.

- **Du borde ha vilat i går.** — You should have rested yesterday.
- **Jag borde ha ringt min syster.** — I should have called my sister.
- **Vi borde ha tagit med en karta.** — We should have brought a map.

The construction can also express an expectation about a past event, without necessarily blaming anyone:

- **Paketet borde ha kommit i dag.** — The package should have arrived today.

The main verb's supine changes according to the verb: **vilat**, **ringt**, and **tagit** above. Do not use **att** between **borde** and **ha** or between **ha** and the supine.

## Negatives and questions

In a main-clause statement, put **inte** after **bör** or **borde** and before the infinitive:

- **Du bör inte köra när du är så trött.** — You should not drive when you are so tired.
- **Jag borde inte äta mer godis.** — I should not eat more sweets.
- **Du borde inte ha sagt så.** — You should not have said that.

For a yes/no question, put the modal verb before the subject:

- **Bör jag ringa vårdcentralen?** — Should I call the health centre?
- **Borde vi vänta här?** — Should we wait here?
- **Borde du ha bokat en tid?** — Should you have booked an appointment?

Questions with **borde** are common in everyday speech. Questions with **bör** can sound more formal.

In a subordinate clause, such as one introduced by **att** (“that”) or **om** (“if/whether”), **inte** normally comes before the verb:

- **Läkaren säger att du inte bör träna i dag.** — The doctor says that you should not exercise today.
- **Jag undrar om vi borde åka hem.** — I wonder if we should go home.

## Quick comparison

- Use **bör** for advice, recommendations, or expectations, especially in more formal language: **Du bör läsa instruktionerna.** — You should read the instructions.
- Use **borde** for common, often softer advice about now or the future: **Du borde läsa instruktionerna.** — You should read the instructions.
- Use **borde ha + supine** to look back at an action that should have happened: **Du borde ha läst instruktionerna.** — You should have read the instructions.

Neither **bör** nor **borde** tells us for certain whether the action will happen. They express a recommendation, expectation, or judgment; the context shows which meaning is intended.
