Use **ingen**, **inget**, and **inga** to say that there is no person or thing, or that someone does not have or do something. They can mean **no**, **not any**, **nobody**, or **none**, depending on how they are used.

## Choose the form

Before a noun, the form agrees with the noun:

| Noun | Form | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singular en-word | **ingen** | **Jag har ingen cykel.** | I don't have a bicycle. |
| Singular ett-word | **inget** | **Vi har inget kaffe.** | We don't have any coffee. |
| Plural noun | **inga** | **De har inga barn.** | They don't have any children. |

The form depends on the noun's grammatical gender and number, not on who has or wants it. For example, **tid** is an en-word, **vatten** is an ett-word, and **frågor** is plural:

- **Jag har ingen tid i dag.** — I don't have any time today.
- **Det finns inget vatten i flaskan.** — There is no water in the bottle.
- **Vi har inga frågor.** — We don't have any questions.

Swedish uses **ingen/inget** with a singular noun where English may use **a/an** or **any**. Use **inga** with a plural noun.

## Use them without a noun

When the noun is clear from the context, these words can stand alone. The form still matches the person or thing being referred to:

- **Ingen har ringt.** — Nobody has called.
- **Inget hände.** — Nothing happened.
- **Har du kakor? Nej, jag har inga.** — Do you have any cookies? No, I don't have any.
- **Har du en penna? Nej, jag har ingen.** — Do you have a pen? No, I don't have one.

Here, **ingen** can mean “nobody” or “no one,” while **inget** can mean “nothing.” **Inga** refers to plural people or things that are understood from the conversation.

## Put an adjective after the word

An adjective between **ingen/inget/inga** and a noun agrees with the noun:

- **Det är ingen bra idé.** — It is not a good idea.
- **Vi har inget ledigt rum.** — We don't have any available room.
- **De har inga lediga rum.** — They don't have any available rooms.

When the noun is left out, the adjective usually takes the neuter form after **inget** and the plural form after **inga**:

- **Har du något varmt? Nej, jag har inget varmt.** — Do you have anything warm? No, I don't have anything warm.
- **Finns det några rena? Nej, det finns inga rena.** — Are there any clean ones? No, there aren't any clean ones.

## Use **ingen av** for “none of”

To talk about a known group, use **ingen av** or **inget av** with a plural noun or a pronoun. The form is chosen according to the person or thing that is absent:

- **Ingen av mina vänner bor här.** — None of my friends live here.
- **Inget av äpplena är moget.** — None of the apples is ripe.
- **Inga av dem är hemma.** — None of them are at home.

You can also use **inga** before a plural noun to say there are none of that kind:

- **Inga gäster kom i går.** — No guests came yesterday.
- **Inga av mina gäster kom i går.** — None of my guests came yesterday.

## **Inget** and **ingenting** for “nothing”

When it stands alone, **inget** can mean “nothing.” **Ingenting** also means “nothing” and does not change form. Use **ingenting** on its own, not before a noun:

- **Jag hörde inget.** — I heard nothing.
- **Jag hörde ingenting.** — I heard nothing.
- **Det finns inget att äta.** — There is nothing to eat.
- **Det finns ingenting att äta.** — There is nothing to eat.

Before a noun, use **inget** when the noun is an ett-word: **inget ljud** (no sound), **inget kaffe** (no coffee). Do not use **ingenting** before a noun.

## Do not normally add **inte**

**Ingen/inget/inga** already make the sentence negative, so a simple negative sentence normally has no **inte** as well:

- **Jag har ingen bil.** — I don't have a car.
- **Hon såg inget djur.** — She didn't see any animal.
- **Vi hittade inga nycklar.** — We didn't find any keys.

You can also use **inte** with **någon/något/några** to express a similar meaning: **Jag har inte någon bil** (I don't have a car). Do not normally use **inte** together with **ingen/inget/inga** to mean “not any.”

Remember: choose **ingen** for an en-word, **inget** for an ett-word, and **inga** for plural nouns. Without a noun, the same forms refer to no one, nothing, or none of a plural group.
