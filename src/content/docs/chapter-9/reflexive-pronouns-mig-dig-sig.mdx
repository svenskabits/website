A reflexive pronoun refers back to the subject of the same clause. In English, this is often expressed with *myself*, *yourself* or *himself/herself*. In Swedish, the choice depends on the subject.

| Subject | Reflexive pronoun | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jag (I) | mig | Jag tvättar mig. — I wash myself. |
| du (you, singular) | dig | Du tvättar dig. — You wash yourself. |
| han, hon, den, det (he, she, it) | sig | Hon tvättar sig. — She washes herself. |
| vi (we) | oss | Vi tvättar oss. — We wash ourselves. |
| ni (you, plural) | er | Ni tvättar er. — You wash yourselves. |
| de (they) | sig | De tvättar sig. — They wash themselves. |

The forms **mig**, **dig** and **sig** are the object forms used when the object is the same person or thing as the subject. The third-person form **sig** is the same for he, she, it and they. The subject pronoun is normally included: the reflexive pronoun does not show who is doing the action by itself.

## Reflexive or not?

Compare a reflexive pronoun with an ordinary object pronoun. Use a reflexive pronoun when the subject does something to themselves. Use **honom**, **henne** or **dem** when the action affects someone else.

| Reflexive: the subject acts on themselves | Non-reflexive: the subject acts on someone else |
| --- | --- |
| Erik rakar sig. — Erik shaves (himself). | Erik rakar honom. — Erik shaves him. |
| Lisa tvättar sig. — Lisa washes (herself). | Lisa tvättar henne. — Lisa washes her. |
| Barnen klär på sig. — The children get dressed. | Barnen klär på dem. — The children put clothes on them. |

In the right-hand examples, **honom**, **henne** and **dem** refer to a person or people other than the subject. Context tells us who they are.

The pronoun normally refers to the subject of its own clause, even when another person is mentioned in a different clause:

- **Anna säger att Erik tvättar sig.** — Anna says that Erik is washing himself.
- **Erik säger att Anna tvättar sig.** — Erik says that Anna is washing herself.

In each example, **sig** refers to the subject of the clause it is in: **Erik** in the first, **Anna** in the second.

## Reflexive verbs

Many Swedish verbs are commonly used with a reflexive pronoun. Learn the pronoun together with the verb, because the pronoun changes with the subject.

| Verb pattern | Example | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tvätta sig (wash oneself) | Jag tvättar mig på morgonen. | I wash in the morning. |
| klä på sig (get dressed) | Han klär på sig snabbt. | He gets dressed quickly. |
| sätta sig (sit down) | Sätt dig här! | Sit down here! |
| lägga sig (lie down; go to bed) | Barnet lägger sig klockan åtta. | The child goes to bed at eight. |
| känna sig (feel) | Vi känner oss trötta. | We feel tired. |
| ta hand om sig (take care of oneself) | Du måste ta hand om dig. | You have to take care of yourself. |

The pronoun can be part of a verb's usual meaning. For example, **lägga sig** means to lie down or go to bed, while **lägga** on its own means to put or lay something somewhere:

- **Jag lägger mig på soffan.** — I lie down on the sofa.
- **Jag lägger boken på bordet.** — I put the book on the table.

Some verbs can be used with or without a reflexive pronoun, with a difference in who receives the action:

- **Hon tvättar sig.** — She washes herself.
- **Hon tvättar hunden.** — She washes the dog.

## Word order

In a simple statement, the reflexive pronoun usually comes after the finite verb (the verb that is marked for tense):

- **Jag tvättar mig varje kväll.** — I wash every evening.
- **Han satte sig bredvid mig.** — He sat down next to me.

With a modal verb such as **måste** or **vill**, the reflexive pronoun comes after the infinitive:

- **Du måste ta hand om dig.** — You have to take care of yourself.
- **De vill sätta sig här.** — They want to sit down here.

With **inte** in a main clause, put **inte** after the finite verb and before the reflexive pronoun or infinitive:

- **Hon känner sig inte sjuk.** — She does not feel ill.
- **Jag kan inte lägga mig än.** — I cannot go to bed yet.

## When it means “each other”

With a plural subject, a reflexive pronoun can sometimes have a reciprocal meaning: people do the action to themselves individually, or the context may suggest that they do it to one another. To clearly mean **each other**, use **varandra**:

- **De tittar på sig själva i spegeln.** — They look at themselves in the mirror.
- **De tittar på varandra.** — They look at each other.

Use **sig själv** (or **sig själva** for plural) when you want to emphasize “oneself/themselves” or make the self-directed meaning especially clear:

- **Han gjorde det själv.** — He did it himself.
- **Han såg sig själv på bilden.** — He saw himself in the picture.

In the first sentence, **själv** emphasizes that he did it without help; it is not a reflexive object. In the second, **sig själv** is the reflexive object: he saw himself.
