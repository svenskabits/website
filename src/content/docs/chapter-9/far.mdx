**Får** is the present form of **få**. As a modal verb, it usually says that someone is allowed to do something. It can also mean “get” or “receive.” The meaning depends on what follows and on the context.

| Form | Swedish | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive | få | to get; to be allowed to |
| Present | får | get(s); may, am/is/are allowed to |
| Past | fick | got; was/were allowed to |
| Supine | fått | got, received; been allowed to (used with **har**) |

The verb has the same form with every subject: **jag får**, **du får**, **hon får**, **vi får**.

## Permission: “may” or “be allowed to”

Use **får + infinitive** to say that someone has permission. Do not put **att** between **får** and the next verb:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| **Du får låna min cykel.** | You may borrow my bike. |
| **Vi får gå hem nu.** | We are allowed to go home now. |
| **Barnen får leka ute efter maten.** | The children may play outside after the meal. |

Use **får jag...?** or **får vi...?** to ask permission. This is a direct, common way to ask “May I...?” or “Are we allowed to...?”

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| **Får jag öppna fönstret?** | May I open the window? |
| **Får vi komma in?** | May we come in? |
| **Får hunden vara här?** | Is the dog allowed to be here? |

**Kan jag...?** is also often used to ask permission in everyday conversation. **Får jag...?** makes the question of permission especially clear.

## Prohibition: **får inte**

Use **får inte + infinitive** to say that something is forbidden or that someone is not allowed to do it. In a main-clause statement, **inte** comes after **får**:

- **Du får inte parkera här.** — You must not park here. / You are not allowed to park here.
- **Man får inte röka på bussen.** — You must not smoke on the bus. / Smoking is not allowed on the bus.
- **Ni får inte gå in utan biljett.** — You are not allowed to enter without a ticket.

This is different from **behöver inte**, which means “do not need to.”

- **Du får inte komma i morgon.** — You are not allowed to come tomorrow.
- **Du behöver inte komma i morgon.** — You do not need to come tomorrow.

## **Får** and **kan** are different

**Kan** often describes ability or possibility. **Får** describes permission. A person can be able to do something without having permission to do it:

- **Jag kan köra bil.** — I can drive a car. (I know how.)
- **Jag får köra bilen.** — I am allowed to drive the car.
- **Jag kan inte använda telefonen här.** — I cannot use the phone here. (It may be impossible or against the rules; context tells us.)
- **Jag får inte använda telefonen här.** — I am not allowed to use the phone here.

## Talking about the past

Use **fick + infinitive** for permission in the past. The infinitive still comes without **att**:

- **När jag var liten fick jag gå till skolan själv.** — When I was little, I was allowed to walk to school by myself.
- **Vi fick inte använda våra mobiler under lektionen.** — We were not allowed to use our phones during the lesson.
- **Fick du stanna hemma i går?** — Were you allowed to stay home yesterday?

**Fick** can also be the past form of **få** meaning “get” or “receive”:

- **Jag fick ett mejl från läkaren.** — I got an email from the doctor.
- **Hon fick en ny tid.** — She got a new appointment.

## **Har fått** and other forms

Use **har fått** to talk about something received or permission that has been granted. When it means permission, the next verb is an infinitive without **att**:

- **Jag har fått ett svar.** — I have received an answer.
- **Hon har fått tillstånd att resa.** — She has been given permission to travel.
- **Vi har fått komma in tidigare.** — We have been allowed to come in earlier.

Other verbs can come before **få**. For example, **ska få** often means that someone will receive something, while **skulle få** can mean that someone would receive something or would be allowed to do something:

- **Du ska få en ny nyckel i morgon.** — You will get a new key tomorrow.
- **Han trodde att han skulle få följa med.** — He thought he would be allowed to come along.

## Word order in questions and subordinate clauses

In a yes-or-no question, put **får** before the subject. In a question with a question word, put the question word first:

- **Får jag sitta här?** — May I sit here?
- **Var får vi parkera?** — Where are we allowed to park?

In a main-clause statement, **inte** comes after **får**: **Du får inte gå in.** — You are not allowed to go in.

In a subordinate clause, such as one introduced by **att** (“that”) or **om** (“if/whether”), **inte** normally comes before **får**:

- **Läraren säger att vi inte får använda ordboken.** — The teacher says that we are not allowed to use the dictionary.
- **Jag undrar om jag får ta med hunden.** — I wonder if I may bring the dog.

## **Få** meaning “get” or “receive”

The verb **få** is not always a modal verb. With a noun, it commonly means “get” or “receive”:

- **Får du många brev?** — Do you get many letters?
- **Barnet fick en present.** — The child got a present.
- **De har fått hjälp av en granne.** — They have received help from a neighbour.

It also appears in common expressions with another verb, such as **få veta** (“find out”) and **få träffa** (“get to meet”):

- **Jag fick veta resultatet i morse.** — I found out the result this morning.
- **Hon får träffa läkaren i eftermiddag.** — She gets to see the doctor this afternoon.

Remember the pattern: **får + infinitive without att** can express permission, while **får + noun** often means that someone gets or receives something.
