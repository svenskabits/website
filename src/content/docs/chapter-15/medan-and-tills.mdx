Use **medan** and **tills** to connect a main clause with a time-related subordinate clause. **Medan** usually means *while*; it can also contrast two facts, like *whereas*. **Tills** means *until* and marks the point when an action or situation ends or changes.

## Medan: actions at the same time

Use **medan** when one action happens during the time that another action is in progress. The actions may overlap for all or part of their duration:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Medan jag lagade mat**, dukade Amir bordet. | While I was cooking, Amir set the table. |
| Jag lyssnar på radio **medan jag städar**. | I listen to the radio while I clean. |
| Hon läste en bok **medan barnen sov**. | She read a book while the children were sleeping. |

The tense depends on the time and meaning. In a story about the past, the simple past is common for both actions. For something happening now or regularly, use the present tense.

## Medan: contrast

**Medan** can also introduce a contrast between two people, things, or situations. In this use, it often means *whereas* or *while* in the sense of *in contrast*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag tycker om kaffe, **medan min syster föredrar te**. | I like coffee, whereas my sister prefers tea. |
| **Medan vissa vill bo i stan**, föredrar andra att bo på landet. | While some people want to live in the city, others prefer to live in the countryside. |

The context makes it clear whether **medan** describes simultaneous actions or a contrast.

## Tills: an endpoint

Use **tills** for an action or situation that continues up to a time or event. The clause after **tills** has its own subject and verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Vänta här **tills jag kommer tillbaka**. | Wait here until I come back. |
| De bodde hos sina vänner **tills lägenheten blev klar**. | They lived with their friends until the apartment was ready. |
| Jag arbetade **tills klockan blev fem**. | I worked until it was five o’clock. |

The endpoint can also be a time or noun phrase rather than a full clause:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Butiken är öppen **tills klockan åtta**. | The shop is open until eight o’clock. |
| Vi väntade **tills på måndag**. | We waited until Monday. |

When you name an event in a full clause, use **tills + subject + verb**. **Tills dess att** is another way to say *until* before a clause, often with extra emphasis on the endpoint: *Vi väntar tills dess att alla har kommit* (We will wait until everyone has arrived). The shorter **tills** is usually enough.

## Word order and tense

The clause introduced by **medan** or **tills** is a subordinate clause. Its subject comes before sentence adverbs such as **inte** (not):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Hon lagade mat **medan barnen inte var hemma**. | She cooked while the children were not at home. |
| Vi kan stanna här **tills det inte regnar längre**. | We can stay here until it is no longer raining. |

When the subordinate clause comes first, the main clause has inversion: its verb comes before its subject. A comma is often used after the first clause for clarity:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Medan jag väntade**, läste jag tidningen. | While I was waiting, I read the newspaper. |
| **Tills läkaren kommer**, får du vila här. | Until the doctor comes, you can rest here. |

For future events, Swedish normally uses the present tense in the **tills** clause, even when English uses *will*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag stannar här **tills du kommer**. | I will stay here until you come. |
| Vi väntar **tills tåget går**. | We will wait until the train leaves. |

## Tills and “not until”

For the meaning *not until*, Swedish commonly uses **inte … förrän**, rather than translating the English words directly with **tills**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag går **inte förrän du är klar**. | I am not leaving until you are ready. |
| Hon märkte det **inte förrän hon kom hem**. | She did not notice it until she got home. |

Use **tills** for the endpoint itself (*wait until you arrive*), and **inte … förrän** when saying that something does not happen before that endpoint.
