Use **brukar + infinitive** to describe what someone usually does or what normally happens. Use **brukade + infinitive** for a habit or repeated situation in the past. In both patterns, the second verb is an infinitive without **att**:

| Time | Pattern | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present or general | **brukar + infinitive** | Jag brukar promenera efter jobbet. | I usually go for a walk after work. |
| Past | **brukade + infinitive** | Jag brukade promenera efter jobbet. | I used to go for a walk after work. |

## Forms and the infinitive

In this use, **brukar** and **brukade** have the same form with every subject. The following verb stays in the infinitive:

| Subject | Present habit | Past habit |
|---|---|---|
| jag (I) | brukar läsa (usually read) | brukade läsa (used to read) |
| du (you, singular) | brukar läsa (usually read) | brukade läsa (used to read) |
| hon/han (she/he) | brukar läsa (usually read) | brukade läsa (used to read) |
| vi (we) | brukar läsa (usually read) | brukade läsa (used to read) |
| ni (you, plural) | brukar läsa (usually read) | brukade läsa (used to read) |
| de (they) | brukar läsa (usually read) | brukade läsa (used to read) |

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Hon brukar ta bussen till jobbet. | She usually takes the bus to work. |
| Vi brukade äta middag hos mormor på söndagar. | We used to have dinner at Grandma's on Sundays. |

Do not add **att** between **brukar/brukade** and the infinitive:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag brukar laga mat hemma. | I usually cook at home. |
| När han var liten brukade han leka ute efter skolan. | When he was little, he used to play outside after school. |

## Choosing between brukar and brukade

Use **brukar** for a current routine, a general tendency, or something that normally happens. It does not have to happen every single time:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Bussen brukar komma i tid. | The bus usually arrives on time. |
| Det brukar vara lugnt här på morgonen. | It is usually quiet here in the morning. |
| Jag brukar ringa mina föräldrar på helgen. | I usually call my parents at the weekend. |

Use **brukade** for repeated actions or situations in the past, often with a past-time expression or a context that makes the past clear:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Förr brukade vi cykla till skolan. | We used to cycle to school. |
| På sommaren brukade det regna mycket där. | In the summer, it used to rain a lot there. |
| Min farfar brukade berätta historier för oss. | My grandfather used to tell us stories. |

**Brukade** describes a past pattern, not one single completed event. For an event that happened once, use the ordinary past tense instead:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| I går tog jag bussen till jobbet. | Yesterday I took the bus to work. |
| När jag bodde där brukade jag ta bussen till jobbet. | When I lived there, I used to take the bus to work. |

English **used to** often suggests that the habit has stopped. Swedish **brukade** marks the habit as past, but does not by itself state whether it has stopped; the context can make that clear:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag brukade spela fotboll, men nu har jag inte tid. | I used to play football, but now I don't have time. |
| Vi brukade träffas varje vecka. | We used to meet every week. |

The second sentence tells us about a past routine, but does not say whether the people still meet.

## Word order and sentence adverbs

In a main clause, **brukar** or **brukade** is the verb that carries the tense. It normally comes after the subject, and words such as **inte** (not), **ofta** (often), and **alltid** (always) usually come after it. The infinitive follows the adverb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag brukar inte dricka kaffe på kvällen. | I don't usually drink coffee in the evening. |
| Hon brukade ofta läsa för barnen. | She often used to read to the children. |
| De brukar låsa dörren. | They usually lock the door. |

**Brukar** already expresses what is usual, so it is often clearer to use it without **alltid** (always). To say that an action happens every time, use **alltid** with the simple present: **De låser alltid dörren** (“They always lock the door”).

If another part of the sentence comes first, the finite verb still comes second, before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| På vintern brukar vi åka skidor. | In winter, we usually go skiing. |
| Förr brukade hon gå hem från jobbet. | In the past, she used to walk home from work. |

In a subordinate clause, such as one introduced by **att** (that), **eftersom** (because), or **när** (when), put the subject and a sentence adverb before **brukar/brukade**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag vet att han brukar komma tidigt. | I know that he usually arrives early. |
| Hon stannar hemma eftersom hon inte brukar må bra efter resan. | She stays home because she doesn't usually feel well after the trip. |
| När vi bodde i Malmö brukade vi promenera längs havet. | When we lived in Malmö, we used to walk along the sea. |

## Questions and negative habits

For a yes-or-no question, put **brukar** or **brukade** before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Brukar du laga mat på helgerna? | Do you usually cook at weekends? |
| Brukade ni besöka era vänner ofta? | Did you use to visit your friends often? |

In a question with a question word, the question word comes first, followed by **brukar/brukade** and the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Vad brukar du göra efter jobbet? | What do you usually do after work? |
| Var brukade de bada på sommaren? | Where did they use to swim in the summer? |

Put **inte** after **brukar/brukade** in a main clause to say that something is not a usual or past habit:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag brukar inte äta frukost så tidigt. | I don't usually eat breakfast so early. |
| Han brukade inte tycka om kaffe. | He didn't use to like coffee. |

In the second example, **inte** negates the past state of liking coffee. To say that an action was not usually done, the same word order applies:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Vi brukade inte gå ut på vardagarna. | We didn't use to go out on weekdays. |

In a subordinate clause, **inte** comes before **brukar/brukade**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Hon säger att hon inte brukar ta bilen. | She says that she doesn't usually take the car. |
| Jag visste inte att ni inte brukade äta kött. | I didn't know that you didn't use to eat meat. |

## Brukar compared with the simple present

The simple present can also describe a habit. **Brukar** puts extra focus on what is usual, typical, or done regularly; the simple present can state the action more directly. The difference is often small, and context or an adverb such as **ofta** (often) can make the frequency clear:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag tränar på tisdagar. | I exercise on Tuesdays. |
| Jag brukar träna på tisdagar. | I usually exercise on Tuesdays. |
| Hon jobbar ofta hemifrån. | She often works from home. |
| Hon brukar jobba hemifrån. | She usually works from home. |

Use **brukar** when you want to present something as a routine or what normally happens. Use the simple present when you simply report or describe the action; neither form requires the action to happen without exception.
