Use **innan** (before) and **efter att** (after) to show the time relationship between two events or situations. When followed by a full clause, each introduces a subordinate clause with its own subject and verb:

| Connector | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| **innan** | Jag borstade tänderna **innan jag gick och lade mig**. | I brushed my teeth before I went to bed. |
| **efter att** | Jag borstade tänderna **efter att jag hade ätit**. | I brushed my teeth after I had eaten. |

The event in the subordinate clause happens before the event in the main clause with **innan**, and after it with **efter att**.

## Word order in the clauses

In the subordinate clause, use the usual subordinate-clause word order: the subject comes before sentence adverbs such as **inte** (not), and the finite verb follows them:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Vänta här **innan bussen kommer**. | Wait here before the bus comes. |
| Vi gick hem **efter att filmen hade slutat**. | We went home after the film had ended. |
| Hon ringde **innan jag hade hunnit svara**. | She called before I had had time to answer. |
| Jag gick ut **efter att det hade slutat regna**. | I went out after it had stopped raining. |

The subordinate clause can come first or last. If it comes first, the main clause has inversion: its finite verb comes before its subject. A comma is often used after an initial subordinate clause for clarity, but is not required in every case.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Innan jag flyttade hit**, hade jag aldrig sett snö. | Before I moved here, I had never seen snow. |
| **Efter att hon hade pratat med chefen**, skickade hon ett mejl. | After she had spoken to the manager, she sent an email. |
| Jag hade aldrig sett snö **innan jag flyttade hit**. | I had never seen snow before I moved here. |
| Hon skickade ett mejl **efter att hon hade pratat med chefen**. | She sent an email after she had spoken to the manager. |

In the first two examples, **hade** comes before the subject in the main clause: **hade jag** and **skickade hon**. The subordinate clause itself keeps subordinate word order.

## Choosing a tense

The connector already makes the order of events clear, so the tense depends on the context and what you want to emphasize. In past-time stories, the simple past is common in both clauses. The pluperfect (**hade** plus the supine) can emphasize that an event was already completed before another past event:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag låste dörren **innan jag gick**. | I locked the door before I left. |
| Vi började mötet **innan alla hade kommit**. | We started the meeting before everyone had arrived. |
| **Efter att vi hade ätit**, tog vi en promenad. | After we had eaten, we went for a walk. |

The first example gives a straightforward sequence. The pluperfect examples make the earlier completion more explicit. The pluperfect is not compulsory just because one event happened first.

For future events, Swedish commonly uses the present tense after **innan** and **efter att**, even when English uses *will*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag ringer dig **innan jag åker**. | I will call you before I leave. |
| Vi äter **efter att barnen kommer hem**. | We will eat after the children come home. |
| Säg till **innan du bokar biljetterna**. | Let me know before you book the tickets. |

The future meaning comes from the context or the main clause; do not normally add **ska** just to translate English *will* in the subordinate clause.

## After att + infinitive

When the subject of both actions is the same, **efter att ha + supine** is a shorter alternative to **efter att** followed by a full clause. The understood subject is the subject of the main clause:

| Full clause | Shorter form | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Efter att jag hade ätit** gick jag en promenad. | **Efter att ha ätit** gick jag en promenad. | After I had eaten, I went for a walk. |
| **Efter att hon hade läst mejlet** ringde hon sin chef. | **Efter att ha läst mejlet** ringde hon sin chef. | After she had read the email, she called her manager. |

The short form does not state its own subject. It works when the person who did the earlier action is also the person who does the main action. If the subjects are different, use a full clause:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Efter att **barnen hade somnat**, såg vi en film. | After the children had fallen asleep, we watched a film. |

Here, **barnen** did not watch the film, so the full clause is needed. The **att** in **efter att ha** belongs to the expression; it is followed by **ha** and the supine, not by an infinitive ending in **-a**.

## Innan: with a clause or a noun

When **innan** introduces a full clause, do not add **att** in careful standard Swedish. Put the subject directly after **innan**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Vi måste gå **innan tåget kommer**. | We have to leave before the train comes. |
| Stäng fönstret **innan du går**. | Close the window before you leave. |

In everyday speech, **innan att** is also heard in some varieties. For standard written Swedish, use **innan + clause** without **att**. Do not confuse this with **efter att**, where **att** is part of the standard connector.

**Innan** can also come before a noun or noun phrase, with the meaning “before”:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Vi måste gå **innan lunch**. | We have to leave before lunch. |
| Hon tog en kaffe **innan mötet**. | She had a coffee before the meeting. |

Compare this with **efter att**, which introduces a clause. Before a noun, use **efter** without **att**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Vi gick hem **efter mötet**. | We went home after the meeting. |
| Vi gick hem **efter att mötet hade slutat**. | We went home after the meeting had ended. |

## Referring back to a time

Use **innan dess** (before then / before that) and **efter det** (after that) when the earlier or later time is already clear. These expressions do not introduce a subordinate clause:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Mötet börjar klockan tre. Vi hinner äta lunch **innan dess**. | The meeting starts at three. We have time to eat lunch before then. |
| Hon flyttade till Sverige 2020. **Innan dess** bodde hon i Spanien. | She moved to Sweden in 2020. Before that, she lived in Spain. |
| Först ska vi handla. **Efter det** lagar vi middag. | First we will shop. After that, we will make dinner. |

Use **innan/efter att** when you want to state the event that provides the time reference; use **innan dess/efter det** when that reference is already known.
