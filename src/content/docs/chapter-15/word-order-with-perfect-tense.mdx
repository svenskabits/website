The Swedish perfect is formed with **har** and a main verb in the supine form: **har läst** (have read), **har kommit** (have come). The form of **har** stays the same for every subject. Word order depends on whether the clause is a main clause, a question, or a subordinate clause.

## Main clauses: the finite verb comes second

In an ordinary main clause, put the subject first, followed by **har** and then the supine:

> **subject + har + supine**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag **har köpt** en ny cykel. | I have bought a new bike. |
| De **har bott** här länge. | They have lived here for a long time. |

The verb that carries tense is called the **finite verb**. In the perfect, that verb is **har**. In a Swedish main clause, the finite verb normally comes in the second position. “Second” means the second clause element, not necessarily the second word.

If another element comes first, **har** still comes second and the subject follows it. This is often called **inversion**:

| First element | Main clause | English |
|---|---|---|
| time | I dag **har vi träffat** läraren. | Today we have met the teacher. |
| place | På jobbet **har hon lärt sig** mycket. | At work she has learned a lot. |
| subordinate clause | Eftersom det **har regnat** hela dagen, stannar vi hemma. | Because it has rained all day, we are staying at home. |

In the last example, the subordinate clause comes first. The main clause still follows the same pattern: **stannar vi**, with the finite verb before the subject.

## Sentence adverbs and negation

Words that comment on the whole sentence, such as **inte** (not), **aldrig** (never), **redan** (already), **ofta** (often), and **kanske** (perhaps), usually come after the finite verb in a main clause. In the perfect, they go between **har** and the supine:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag **har inte sett** den filmen. | I have not seen that film. |
| Vi **har redan bokat** bord. | We have already booked a table. |
| Hon **har ofta besökt** sina vänner där. | She has often visited her friends there. |

The same placement applies when another element begins the main clause. The finite verb remains second, and the sentence adverb follows it:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| I år **har jag inte rest** utomlands. | This year I have not travelled abroad. |
| Där **har de aldrig varit** förut. | They have never been there before. |

Other adverbials, such as expressions of time, place, and manner, can appear in different positions depending on what the speaker wants to emphasize. For example:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag **har läst boken noggrant**. | I have read the book carefully. |
| Jag **har noggrant läst boken**. | I have carefully read the book. |

The position of sentence adverbs like **inte** is more fixed than the position of these other adverbials.

## Questions

In a yes-or-no question, put **har** before the subject. A sentence adverb follows the subject and comes before the supine:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Har du läst** mejlet? | Have you read the email? |
| **Har hon inte ringt** ännu? | Hasn't she called yet? |

In a question that begins with a question word, the question word comes first, followed by **har**, then the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Var **har ni parkerat** bilen? | Where have you parked the car? |
| Vad **har han sagt**? | What has he said? |
| Varför **har du aldrig frågat** henne? | Why have you never asked her? |

## Subordinate clauses: the subject and sentence adverb come before the verb

A subordinate clause often begins with a word such as **att** (that), **eftersom** (because), **när** (when), or **om** (if/whether). In this kind of clause, put the subject before the sentence adverb, and put the sentence adverb before **har**:

> **subordinating word + subject + sentence adverb + har + supine**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag vet **att hon har flyttat**. | I know that she has moved. |
| Vi stannar hemma **eftersom det inte har slutat** regna. | We are staying home because it has not stopped raining. |
| Hon undrade **om vi redan har fått svar**. | She wondered whether we have already received an answer. |

The word order is the same in each subordinate clause: the sentence adverb **redan** comes before the finite verb.

Compare the position of **inte** in a main clause and a subordinate clause:

| Clause | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Main clause | Jag **har inte träffat** henne. | I have not met her. |
| Subordinate clause | … eftersom jag **inte har träffat** henne. | … because I have not met her. |

This word-order pattern also applies when the subordinate clause comes first. The following main clause then has inversion:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| När jag **har pratat** med henne, **ringer jag** dig. | When I have spoken to her, I will call you. |
| Eftersom vi **inte har fått** svar, **väntar vi** lite till. | Since we have not received an answer, we are waiting a little longer. |

## Objects and verb particles

An object usually follows the verb phrase. With a particle verb, keep the particle with the verb, before the object:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Hon **har stängt av** datorn. | She has turned off the computer. |
| Hon **har stängt av** den. | She has turned it off. |
| Jag **har lämnat tillbaka** böckerna. | I have returned the books. |

Sentence adverbs still come between **har** and the supine, even when the verb has a particle:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Hon **har inte stängt av** datorn. | She has not turned off the computer. |
| Jag **har redan lämnat tillbaka** böckerna. | I have already returned the books. |

## A quick comparison

The key difference is where the finite verb and sentence adverb appear:

| Clause type | Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main clause, subject first | subject + **har** + adverb + supine | Jag **har aldrig varit** där. | I have never been there. |
| Main clause, another element first | first element + **har** + subject + adverb + supine | I sommar **har vi inte rest**. | This summer we have not travelled. |
| Yes-or-no question | **har** + subject + adverb + supine | **Har du redan ätit**? | Have you already eaten? |
| Subordinate clause | subordinating word + subject + adverb + **har** + supine | … att du **inte har svarat**. | … that you have not replied. |

When deciding on the word order, first identify the clause type. In a main clause, keep the finite verb in second position; in a subordinate clause, put the subject and sentence adverb before it.
