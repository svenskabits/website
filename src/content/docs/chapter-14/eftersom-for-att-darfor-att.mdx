Swedish has several ways to give a reason. **Eftersom**, **därför att**, and sometimes **för att** can all mean “because.” They introduce a subordinate clause: a clause that gives information connected to another clause.

## Eftersom and därför att: giving a reason

**Eftersom** and **därför att** both mean “because” or “since” when they introduce a reason. In most sentences, you can use either one. There is no strict rule that assigns them different kinds of reasons, although **eftersom** often presents the reason as background information.

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jag tar bussen eftersom det regnar. | I’m taking the bus because it’s raining. |
| Jag tar bussen därför att det regnar. | I’m taking the bus because it’s raining. |
| Eftersom det regnar tar jag bussen. | Since it’s raining, I’m taking the bus. |

The reason clause can come before or after the main clause. If it comes first, the main clause has inversion: the verb comes before the subject. In the last example, **tar** comes before **jag**.

## Word order inside the reason clause

Because **eftersom**, **därför att**, and causal **för att** introduce subordinate clauses, the usual subordinate-clause word order applies. In particular, **inte** comes before the finite verb (the verb that carries the tense):

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Vi stannar hemma eftersom vi inte mår bra. | We’re staying home because we don’t feel well. |
| Vi stannar hemma därför att vi inte mår bra. | We’re staying home because we don’t feel well. |
| Eftersom jag inte har tid tar jag en taxi. | Since I don’t have time, I’m taking a taxi. |

Compare the position of **inte** in the main clause and the subordinate clause:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jag kommer inte. | I’m not coming. |
| Jag säger att jag inte kommer. | I say that I’m not coming. |

In the first sentence, **inte** follows the finite verb **kommer**. In the subordinate clause in the second sentence, it comes before **kommer**. The same subordinate-clause order is used after the words on this page.

## För att: reason or purpose

**För att** has two common uses. It can introduce a reason, like **eftersom** and **därför att**, or it can introduce a purpose and mean “in order to” or “so that.” The form of the clause helps show which meaning is intended.

### För att + a clause: a reason

When **för att** is followed by a subject and a finite verb, it can give a reason. The subject may be the same as or different from the subject in the main clause.

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Hon tog en taxi för att hon hade bråttom. | She took a taxi because she was in a hurry. |
| Vi gick in för att det började regna. | We went inside because it started to rain. |

The subordinate clause has the same word order as one introduced by **eftersom** or **därför att**. For example, **inte** comes before the finite verb:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jag gick hem för att jag inte mådde bra. | I went home because I didn’t feel well. |

### För att + infinitive: a purpose

When **för att** is followed by an infinitive (the basic verb form, such as **vila** or **lära**), it usually explains the purpose of an action. There is normally no separate subject before the infinitive; the person doing the main action is also understood to do the infinitive action.

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jag går och lägger mig tidigt för att vila. | I’m going to bed early in order to rest. |
| Hon studerar svenska för att hitta ett jobb. | She is studying Swedish in order to find a job. |
| De åkte till stationen för att hämta sin vän. | They went to the station to pick up their friend. |

In these examples, **vila**, **hitta**, and **hämta** are infinitives. The phrases say what the person intends to do, not why something has already happened.

## A useful contrast

Compare a finite clause with an infinitive phrase:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jag tränar för att jag vill må bättre. | I exercise because I want to feel better. |
| Jag tränar för att må bättre. | I exercise in order to feel better. |

In the first sentence, **jag vill** is a clause with its own subject and finite verb, so **för att** gives a reason. In the second, **må** is an infinitive, so **för att** gives a purpose.

## Because versus therefore

Do not confuse **därför att** (“because”) with **därför** (“therefore,” “for that reason”). **Därför att** introduces a subordinate clause; **därför** is an adverb and can connect two main clauses. When **därför** begins a main clause, the verb comes before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Det regnar, så jag tar bussen. | It’s raining, so I’m taking the bus. |
| Det regnar. Därför tar jag bussen. | It’s raining. Therefore, I’m taking the bus. |
| Jag tar bussen därför att det regnar. | I’m taking the bus because it’s raining. |

In **Därför tar jag bussen**, **därför** is first, so the main-clause verb **tar** comes before the subject **jag**. In **därför att det regnar**, the words together mean “because,” and the clause is subordinate.
