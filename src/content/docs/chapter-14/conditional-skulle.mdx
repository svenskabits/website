Swedish often uses **skulle** (“would”) to talk about an imagined result, a possible choice, or a polite request. A common conditional sentence has an **om** (“if”) clause and a main clause:

> **om** + subject + verb in the past tense, **skulle** + subject + infinitive

The past tense in the **om** clause does not necessarily refer to past time. It often marks the situation as imagined or unlikely.

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Om jag hade mer tid, skulle jag läsa fler böcker. | If I had more time, I would read more books. |
| Om hon bodde närmare, skulle vi träffas oftare. | If she lived closer, we would meet more often. |
| Om vi vann en miljon kronor, skulle vi köpa ett hus. | If we won a million kronor, we would buy a house. |

## Forming a present or future hypothetical

In the **om** clause, use a past-tense verb to present a situation as hypothetical. In the main clause, use **skulle** followed by the infinitive of the main verb. **Skulle** has the same form for every subject:

| Subject | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jag | skulle | Jag skulle stanna. — I would stay. |
| du | skulle | Du skulle stanna. — You would stay. |
| hon/han/den/det | skulle | Hon skulle stanna. — She would stay. |
| vi | skulle | Vi skulle stanna. — We would stay. |
| ni | skulle | Ni skulle stanna. — You (plural) would stay. |
| de | skulle | De skulle stanna. — They would stay. |

The verb after **skulle** is an infinitive, without **att**:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jag skulle resa mer om jag hade råd. | I would travel more if I could afford it. |
| Vi skulle laga middag om vi hade tid. | We would cook dinner if we had time. |
| Han skulle hjälpa till om han kunde. | He would help if he could. |

Some common verbs used in hypothetical **om** clauses are **hade** (“had”), **var** or **vore** (“were”), **kunde** (“could”), and **ville** (“wanted to”). **Vore** is a subjunctive form of **vara**. It is especially common in the fixed expression **om jag vore du** (“if I were you”); **om jag var du** is also used in everyday Swedish.

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Om jag vore du, skulle jag prata med henne. | If I were you, I would talk to her. |
| Om han kunde, skulle han komma tidigare. | If he could, he would come earlier. |
| Om de ville, skulle de kunna stanna längre. | If they wanted to, they could stay longer. |

## Clause order and negation

The **om** clause can come before or after the main clause. If it comes first, the main clause has inversion: the verb **skulle** comes before its subject. If the main clause comes first, it keeps ordinary main-clause order.

| Om clause first | Main clause first |
| --- | --- |
| Om jag hade semester, skulle jag åka till Norge. — If I had time off, I would go to Norway. | Jag skulle åka till Norge om jag hade semester. — I would go to Norway if I had time off. |
| Om hon bodde här, skulle hon cykla till jobbet. — If she lived here, she would cycle to work. | Hon skulle cykla till jobbet om hon bodde här. — She would cycle to work if she lived here. |

In a main clause, **inte** normally comes after the finite verb **skulle**. In an **om** clause, **inte** comes before the finite verb, as it usually does in a subordinate clause:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jag skulle inte köpa den bilen. | I wouldn’t buy that car. |
| Om jag inte hade bil, skulle jag ta bussen. | If I didn’t have a car, I would take the bus. |
| Vi skulle gå ut om det inte regnade. | We would go out if it weren’t raining. |

## Real possibilities and imagined situations

Use a present-tense **om** clause for a real possibility, often with a present or future result. The result does not need **skulle**:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Om jag har tid, ringer jag dig. | If I have time, I’ll call you. |
| Om det regnar i morgon, stannar vi hemma. | If it rains tomorrow, we’ll stay at home. |

Compare these with past tense plus **skulle**, which presents the condition as more imagined or less likely:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Om jag hade tid, skulle jag ringa dig. | If I had time, I would call you. |
| Om det regnade i morgon, skulle vi stanna hemma. | If it rained tomorrow, we would stay at home. |

The distinction depends on what the speaker wants to express, not just on whether the event is in the future. A present-tense condition sounds open or realistic; the past-tense form makes it more hypothetical.

In a condition about an uncertain future event, **om + skulle + infinitive** is also possible. It can present the event as a possibility that might arise:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Om du skulle ändra dig, kan du ringa mig. | If you should change your mind, you can call me. |

## Imagined situations in the past

To talk about an imagined past condition—something that did not happen—use **hade + supine** in the **om** clause. The supine is the verb form used after **har** or **hade**, such as **sett** in **har sett** (“have seen”). In the result clause, use **skulle ha + supine**:

> **om** + subject + **hade** + supine, **skulle** + subject + **ha** + supine

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Om jag hade vetat det, skulle jag ha ringt dig. | If I had known that, I would have called you. |
| Om vi hade gått hem tidigare, skulle vi ha hunnit med tåget. | If we had gone home earlier, we would have caught the train. |
| Hon skulle ha följt med om hon hade varit ledig. | She would have come along if she had been free. |

Again, the **om** clause can come first or last. When it comes first, the main clause has inversion: **skulle** comes before the subject. The **ha** in **skulle ha gjort** is an infinitive, and the following verb is a supine.

## **Skulle** with other verbs

Because **skulle** is a modal verb, it can be followed by another infinitive without **att**. This is useful for talking about imagined ability, wishes, or needs:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Jag skulle kunna hjälpa dig i morgon. | I could help you tomorrow. |
| Vi skulle vilja boka ett bord. | We would like to book a table. |
| Hon skulle behöva mer tid. | She would need more time. |

These forms also work in conditional sentences: **Om jag hade bil, skulle jag kunna hälsa på oftare** (“If I had a car, I could visit more often”). For a past hypothetical with another modal, use **skulle ha** followed by that modal's supine: **skulle ha kunnat** (“could have”).

## Polite questions and requests

**Skulle** can make a question or request sound more tentative and polite. In a question, **skulle** comes before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Skulle du kunna hjälpa mig? | Could you help me? |
| Skulle ni vilja komma in? | Would you like to come in? |
| Skulle jag kunna få ett glas vatten? | Could I have a glass of water? |

These questions do not need an **om** clause. The speaker uses **skulle** to soften the request rather than to state a condition.

## Other meanings of **skulle**

**Skulle** can also be the past form of **ska** in the sense of a plan or expectation viewed from the past. This is not the hypothetical conditional pattern:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Hon sa att hon skulle ringa på kvällen. | She said that she would call in the evening. |
| Jag skulle träffa läkaren, men jag blev sjuk. | I was supposed to see the doctor, but I got sick. |

In these examples, the context gives **skulle** its meaning (“was going to” or “was supposed to”). In a hypothetical condition such as **Om jag hade tid, skulle jag ringa** (“If I had time, I would call”), the **om** clause sets up an imagined situation and **skulle** describes its result.
