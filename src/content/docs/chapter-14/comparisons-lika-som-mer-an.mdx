Swedish uses **lika … som** to say that two people or things are equal in some way. To say that one is greater in degree or amount, use a comparative form with **än** (“than”).

## Equality: lika … som

Put **lika** before the word that describes the quality being compared, and use **som** before the person or thing it is compared with:

- *Lea är lika lång som sin syster.* — Lea is as tall as her sister.
- *Den här vägen är lika lång som den andra.* — This road is as long as the other one.
- *Huset är lika stort som vårt.* — The house is as big as ours.

Adjectives agree with the noun they describe. This applies whether the adjective comes after a verb such as *är* or before a noun:

- *Bilen är lika snabb som tåget.* — The car is as fast as the train.
- *Bilarna är lika snabba som tågen.* — The cars are as fast as the trains.
- *De bor i en lika stor lägenhet som vi gör.* — They live in an apartment as large as the one we live in.

The comparative word **lika** itself does not change.

You can also compare how actions are done. Use **lika** before an adverb, often an adverb ending in **-t**:

- *Maja springer lika snabbt som Erik.* — Maja runs as fast as Erik.
- *Hon talar lika tydligt som läraren.* — She speaks as clearly as the teacher.
- *Vi arbetar lika hårt som de gör.* — We work as hard as they do.

## Equal amounts: lika många and lika mycket

Use **lika många** with things you can count and **lika mycket** with an amount or something treated as a mass:

- *De har lika många barn som vi.* — They have as many children as we do.
- *Jag har läst lika många böcker som min bror.* — I have read as many books as my brother.
- *Vi behöver lika mycket tid som ni.* — We need as much time as you do.
- *Hon dricker lika mycket kaffe som jag.* — She drinks as much coffee as I do.

When comparing how much someone does something, **lika mycket** can come after the verb:

- *Jag tränar lika mycket som min vän.* — I exercise as much as my friend.
- *Han tycker lika mycket om hundar som om katter.* — He likes dogs as much as cats.

## Greater degree: comparative forms + än

To say that one person or thing has more of a quality than another, use a comparative adjective or adverb followed by **än**:

- *Min lägenhet är mindre än din.* — My apartment is smaller than yours.
- *Det här hotellet är dyrare än det förra.* — This hotel is more expensive than the previous one.
- *Hon skriver snabbare än jag gör.* — She writes faster than I do.
- *Det var lättare än vi trodde.* — It was easier than we thought.

Many short adjectives form the comparative with **-are**. The comparative form does not change for gender or plural:

| Basic form | Comparative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| snabb (fast) | snabbare (faster) | *Tåget är snabbare än bussen.* — The train is faster than the bus. |
| billig (cheap) | billigare (cheaper) | *De här skorna är billigare än de andra.* — These shoes are cheaper than the others. |
| rolig (fun) | roligare (more fun) | *Filmen var roligare än boken.* — The film was more fun than the book. |

Many longer adjectives, especially those ending in **-isk**, use **mer** before the adjective instead:

- *Det här alternativet är mer praktiskt än det andra.* — This option is more practical than the other one.
- *Hon är mer intresserad av musik än av sport.* — She is more interested in music than in sports.

There is no single ending that works for every adjective, so learn the common comparative forms with the adjective. Do not use **mer** together with an adjective that already has a comparative form: say *snabbare*, not *mer snabbare*.

Some common comparatives have an irregular form:

| Basic form | Comparative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| bra (good, well) | bättre (better) | *Det här förslaget är bättre än det förra.* — This proposal is better than the previous one. |
| dålig (bad, badly) | sämre (worse) | *Vädret är sämre än i går.* — The weather is worse than yesterday. |
| gammal (old) | äldre (older) | *Min syster är äldre än jag.* — My sister is older than I am. |
| ung (young) | yngre (younger) | *Han är yngre än sin chef.* — He is younger than his manager. |
| lång (long, tall) | längre (longer, taller) | *Kön är längre än den var förut.* — The queue is longer than it was before. |
| stor (big) | större (bigger) | *Deras hus är större än vårt.* — Their house is bigger than ours. |
| liten (small) | mindre (smaller) | *Rummet är mindre än köket.* — The room is smaller than the kitchen. |

## More, fewer, and larger amounts

Use **mer än** with uncountable amounts, time, money, and quantities viewed as a whole. It can also mean “more than” before a number:

- *Vi behöver mer mjölk än vatten.* — We need more milk than water.
- *Hon tjänar mer än sin man.* — She earns more than her husband.
- *Det tog mer än två timmar.* — It took more than two hours.
- *Mer än hundra personer kom till konserten.* — More than a hundred people came to the concert.

Use **fler än** with things that can be counted individually. **Fler** is the comparative form of **många**:

- *Fler än femtio personer sökte jobbet.* — More than fifty people applied for the job.
- *Vi har fler frågor än svar.* — We have more questions than answers.
- *I år har biblioteket fått fler besökare än förra året.* — This year the library has had more visitors than last year.

Use **lika många … som** for an equal number and **fler … än** for a greater number. Do not use **mer** directly with a plural count noun in this comparison:

- *Vi har lika många möten som förra veckan.* — We have as many meetings as last week.
- *Vi har fler möten än förra veckan.* — We have more meetings than last week.

## Comparing with a whole clause

After **än** or **som**, you can name the person or thing being compared, or use a clause to make the comparison explicit. In a clause, include its verb when needed for clarity:

- *Hon är längre än jag är.* — She is taller than I am.
- *De kom tidigare än vi hade planerat.* — They arrived earlier than we had planned.
- *Han läser lika snabbt som jag gör.* — He reads as fast as I do.
- *Vi har mer tid än vi trodde.* — We have more time than we thought.

## Lika and samma

Use **lika … som** to compare a degree or quality. Use **samma … som** when two things are identical or are the very same one:

- *Vi har lika stora väskor.* — We have bags that are the same size.
- *Vi har samma väska.* — We have the same bag.
- *Hon har samma åsikt som jag.* — She has the same opinion as I do.

The form of **samma** is the same before singular nouns, regardless of whether the noun is common or neuter gender: *samma bil*, *samma hus*. With plural nouns, use **samma** as well: *samma frågor*.
