Swedish verbs can end in **-s**. This ending has several different uses: it can form a passive, show that people do something to one another, or belong to a verb that is normally used only with **-s**. The meaning depends on the verb and the sentence.

## Passive forms

In a passive sentence, the person or thing receiving an action becomes the subject. The person doing the action can be left out or introduced with **av** (by).

| Active | Passive |
| --- | --- |
| **Personalen öppnar dörren.** — The staff open the door. | **Dörren öppnas.** — The door is opened. |
| **Någon skickade brevet.** — Someone sent the letter. | **Brevet skickades i går.** — The letter was sent yesterday. |
| **De har byggt huset.** — They have built the house. | **Huset har byggts av ett lokalt företag.** — The house has been built by a local company. |

The passive **-s** form is common when the action or result matters more than who performed it. It is especially frequent in instructions, notices, news, and descriptions of processes:

- **Ansökan skickas digitalt.** — The application is sent digitally.
- **Dörren låses klockan åtta.** — The door is locked at eight o’clock.
- **Maten ska serveras varm.** — The food is to be served hot.

If the agent is important, add **av** and the person or thing doing the action: **Beslutet fattades av kommunstyrelsen.** — The decision was made by the municipal executive board.

### Forming the passive

The passive is generally made by adding **-s** to the active verb form. In the present tense, this usually means replacing the active present-tense **-r** with **-s**. The infinitive passive adds **-s** to the infinitive.

| Active form | Passive form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **öppna**, **öppnar** | **öppnas**, **öppnas** | **Butiken öppnas klockan nio.** — The shop opens/is opened at nine. |
| **läsa**, **läser** | **läsas**, **läses** | **Texten läses av många elever.** — The text is read by many students. |
| **köpa**, **köper** | **köpas**, **köps** | **Bilen köps på nätet.** — The car is bought online. |
| **bygga**, **bygger** | **byggas**, **byggs** | **Nya hus byggs här.** — New houses are being built here. |

In the preterite and supine, **-s** is added to the corresponding active form. The ending may look like **-des** or **-tes** because of the active verb’s own ending:

| Active | Passive |
| --- | --- |
| **öppnade – öppnat** | **öppnades – öppnats** |
| **läste – läst** | **lästes – lästs** |
| **köpte – köpt** | **köptes – köpts** |
| **byggde – byggt** | **byggdes – byggts** |

For example: **Fönstret stängdes i går.** — The window was closed yesterday. **Alla biljetter har sålts.** — All the tickets have been sold.

The passive infinitive is often used after a modal verb: **Bilen måste repareras.** — The car must be repaired. Passive forms do not fit every sentence in the same way as English passive forms.

## Actions done to one another

Some **-s** verbs have a reciprocal meaning: two or more people do the action to one another. The subject is plural, or names a group of people.

- **Vi träffas efter jobbet.** — We meet each other after work.
- **De kramades när de sågs.** — They hugged when they saw each other.
- **Vi mejlas ibland.** — We email each other sometimes.

Context helps distinguish this use from the passive. In **Bilarna tvättas varje vecka** (The cars are washed every week), the cars receive an action, so the meaning is passive. In **De träffas varje vecka** (They meet each other every week), the people do the action to one another.

Some reciprocal expressions use **varandra** (each other) or **åt varandra** to make the relationship clear:

- **De hjälper varandra.** — They help each other.
- **Vi måste hjälpas åt.** — We have to help one another / work together.

## Verbs that normally use -s

Some Swedish verbs are used with **-s** in their ordinary, active meaning. These are often called *deponent verbs*. They do not have a corresponding everyday active form with the same meaning, so the **-s** is not a passive marker.

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **hoppas** | hope | **Jag hoppas att du mår bra.** — I hope you are well. |
| **lyckas** | succeed, manage | **Hon lyckades lösa problemet.** — She managed to solve the problem. |
| **trivas** | feel at home, enjoy being somewhere | **Vi trivs i vår nya lägenhet.** — We feel at home in our new apartment. |
| **finnas** | exist, be present | **Det finns ett apotek nära stationen.** — There is a pharmacy near the station. |
| **minnas** | remember | **Minns du hennes namn?** — Do you remember her name? |
| **andas** | breathe | **Han andas lugnt.** — He is breathing calmly. |

Because these verbs have **-s** as part of their normal form, they can be used in the usual tenses without becoming passive: **Hon hoppas.** (She hopes.) **Hon hoppades.** (She hoped.) **Hon har hoppats.** (She has hoped.)

## S-forms with a special or changed meaning

Some verbs have both a non-s form and an **-s** form, but the meanings are different or the **-s** form is used in a fixed expression. Learn these as vocabulary:

- **Vi ses i morgon!** — See you tomorrow! (Literally, “We’ll see each other tomorrow.”)
- **De skiljer sig.** — They are getting divorced / separating. **Skilja sig** is the usual expression for getting divorced.
- **Det hörs musik från grannens lägenhet.** — Music can be heard from the neighbour’s apartment.
- **Vad tycks?** — What do you think? (A common, somewhat informal way to ask for an opinion.)

An **-s** ending does not always mean passive. Check whether the subject receives an action, whether people act on one another, or whether the verb simply has its own established meaning.
