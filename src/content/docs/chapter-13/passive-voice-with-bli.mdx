Swedish can use a passive sentence when the action matters more than the person doing it, or when that person is unknown or unimportant. One way to form the passive is with a form of **bli** (“become”) followed by a past participle. This form often presents an action or change as an event.

## Forming the passive with *bli*

Use the appropriate form of **bli** and a past participle. The form of **bli** carries the tense; the participle agrees with the subject in gender and number.

| Subject | Past participle | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An *en*-word | often ends in **-d** or **-ad** | Dörren blir **stängd**. — The door is being closed. |
| An *ett*-word | often ends in **-t** or **-at** | Fönstret blir **stängt**. — The window is being closed. |
| A plural noun | often ends in **-da** or **-ade** | Dörrarna blir **stängda**. — The doors are being closed. |

These are common patterns, not rules that predict every participle. Some verbs have irregular forms, so learn the participle with the verb: for example, *skriva – skriven – skrivet – skrivna* (“write – written”) and *göra – gjord – gjort – gjorda* (“do/make – done/made”).

The forms of **bli** used most often are:

| Tense or form | Form of *bli* | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive | bli | Dokumentet ska **bli undertecknat** i dag. — The document is to be signed today. |
| Present | blir | Ansökan **blir granskad** av en handläggare. — The application is being reviewed by a case officer. |
| Past (preterite) | blev | Ansökan **blev godkänd** förra veckan. — The application was approved last week. |
| Perfect | har blivit | Ansökan **har blivit godkänd**. — The application has been approved. |
| Pluperfect | hade blivit | Ansökan **hade blivit godkänd** före mötet. — The application had been approved before the meeting. |
| Future | kommer att bli | Huset **kommer att bli sålt** i sommar. — The house will be sold this summer. |

In the perfect and pluperfect, **blivit** stays the same; the participle still agrees with the subject. With a modal verb, use the infinitive **bli**:

> Huset **kan bli sålt** snabbt.  
> The house can be sold quickly.

> Handlingarna **måste bli underskrivna** före fredag.  
> The documents must be signed before Friday.

## The subject and the person doing the action

In an active sentence, the subject does the action. In a passive sentence, the affected person or thing becomes the subject. The person doing the action can be left out:

> En tjänsteman **granskade ansökan**.  
> An official reviewed the application.
>
> Ansökan **blev granskad**.  
> The application was reviewed.

If it is useful to say who did the action, add **av** (“by”):

> Ansökan **blev granskad av en tjänsteman**.  
> The application was reviewed by an official.

The agent phrase with **av** is optional. It is often omitted when the agent is unknown, already clear, or not important.

## Event or resulting state?

The **bli** passive usually focuses on an event or a change: something happens to the subject. Compare it with **vara** (“be”), which commonly describes a state or result:

> Dörren **blev stängd** av vaktmästaren.  
> The door was closed by the caretaker. *(The action of closing it is in focus.)*
>
> Dörren **är stängd**.  
> The door is closed. *(This describes its state.)*

The context can affect the exact English translation, but **bli** presents the closing as an event, while **vara** describes the door's condition.

## Word order, negation, and questions

The finite form of **bli** (such as **blir** or **blev**) follows normal Swedish word order. In a main clause it usually comes second. **Inte** follows it in a main clause, but comes before it in a subordinate clause:

> Paketet **blir inte skickat** i dag.  
> The parcel is not being sent today.
>
> De sa att paketet **inte blev skickat** i går.  
> They said that the parcel was not sent yesterday.

In a yes-or-no question, the finite verb comes before the subject:

> **Blev** brevet **öppnat** av någon?  
> Was the letter opened by anyone?

## Choosing between the *bli* passive and the *-s* passive

Both passive forms can describe an action, but their emphasis and typical contexts differ. The **bli** passive often presents a particular event or change. The **-s** passive is especially common for general facts, routines, and concise official statements:

> Ansökan **blev godkänd** i går.  
> The application was approved yesterday.
>
> Ansökningar **godkänns** av myndigheten.  
> Applications are approved by the authority.

The two forms are not always interchangeable in tone or context. Choose the form that best fits whether you are describing a particular event, a general process, or a resulting state.
