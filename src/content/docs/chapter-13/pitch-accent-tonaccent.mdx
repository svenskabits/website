Swedish uses pitch movement as part of the pronunciation of many words. This word-level melody is called **pitch accent** or **tonaccent**. Swedish has two word accents, called **accent 1** and **accent 2**. In a small number of cases, the accent alone distinguishes two words with the same spelling.

Pitch accent is different from **stress**. Stress makes one syllable in a word more prominent; pitch accent describes how the voice moves in pitch around that stressed syllable. Both accents normally keep the word's ordinary stress in the same place. The difference is the melody, not a different vowel or consonant.

## Hearing the two accents

In much of central Swedish, accent 1 is often heard as one main pitch peak, while accent 2 often has an additional later pitch peak. This is a useful starting point, not a fixed tune to copy: the precise shape and timing depend on region, sentence melody, and emphasis. In some Swedish varieties, the two accents are distinguished more by when the pitch movement happens than by the number of peaks.

The names *acute* (accent 1) and *grave* (accent 2) are traditional labels. They do not refer to accent marks in Swedish spelling. The two accents are not written with special marks, so the spelling alone does not tell you the melody.

## Words that differ only in pitch accent

These pairs have the same written form, but different meanings and word accents:

| Spelling | Accent 1 | Accent 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **anden** | the duck (*and* + definite ending) | the spirit (*ande* + definite ending) |
| **tomten** | the plot of land (*tomt* + definite ending) | Santa Claus / the Christmas gnome (*tomte* + definite ending) |
| **buren** | the cage (*bur* + definite ending) | carried (the past participle of *bära*, “to carry”) |
| **stegen** | the steps (*steg* + definite ending) | the ladder (*stege* + definite ending) |

For example, the accent distinguishes *anden* in these sentences:

- **Anden** simmar i dammen. — The duck is swimming in the pond. (Accent 1.)
- **Anden** steg upp ur flaskan. — The spirit rose out of the bottle. (Accent 2.)

The meaning is usually clear from context, even if a learner does not yet produce the contrast consistently. Use these pairs to practise hearing and changing the melody, rather than worrying that every accent mistake will cause confusion.

## Where the accents tend to occur

Word accent is partly related to a word's form and history. A common tendency is for accent 1 to occur in a one-syllable word and forms built from it, such as *and* → *anden* (“duck” → “the duck”). Accent 2 is common in words with more than one syllable in the stem, such as *ande* → *anden* (“spirit” → “the spirit”). The ending may make two different stems look identical, as in these examples.

This tendency does not predict every word reliably. Some words have to be learned with their accent, and the accent assigned to a word or form can differ between regional varieties. Treat a pattern as a clue, not a spelling rule. A dictionary or pronunciation recording can help when you are unsure.

## Pitch accent in a sentence

Word accent is part of a word's pronunciation, but it is heard together with the melody of the whole sentence. A speaker can raise or lower pitch to show focus, ask a question, or express an attitude. As a result, a word's pitch contour may sound different in a sentence than when said alone. Do not try to add a separate dramatic rise to every accent-2 word; listen for the word pattern within the phrase.

The distinction is not realized identically throughout Sweden. The timing and shape of the pitch movement vary by region, and some regional varieties have a weaker or absent contrast. Finland Swedish generally does not use the same accent 1–accent 2 distinction. A regional pronunciation is not wrong simply because its melody differs from a central Swedish model.

## Practice

Listen to a speaker from the variety of Swedish you are learning, then repeat each pair. Keep the stressed syllable clear and compare the pitch movement after it:

1. **anden** — the duck (accent 1) / the spirit (accent 2)
2. **tomten** — the plot of land (accent 1) / Santa Claus (accent 2)
3. **buren** — the cage (accent 1) / carried (accent 2)

Try saying each meaning in a short sentence as well as on its own. This helps you hear how word accent interacts with natural sentence melody.
