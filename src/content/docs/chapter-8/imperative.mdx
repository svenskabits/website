The imperative is the verb form used to tell someone what to do, give instructions, or make a request. Swedish imperatives do not change according to who you are speaking to: the same form can address one person or several people. The subject (*du*, *ni*, and so on) is normally left out.

## How to form the imperative

For many verbs, use the verb stem. A common pattern is to keep the infinitive ending in **-a** for verbs such as *tala* and *lyssna*. For many other verbs, remove the final **-a**, as in *köpa → köp* and *läsa → läs*. Short verbs such as *bo* keep the same form, while strong and irregular verbs may have a special imperative. It is useful to learn the imperative with the infinitive.

| Infinitive | Imperative | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tala (to speak) | tala | Tala långsamt. — Speak slowly. |
| lyssna (to listen) | lyssna | Lyssna på läraren. — Listen to the teacher. |
| köpa (to buy) | köp | Köp mjölk. — Buy milk. |
| läsa (to read) | läs | Läs frågan. — Read the question. |
| ringa (to call) | ring | Ring mig i kväll. — Call me tonight. |
| bo (to live) | bo | Bo nära stationen. — Live near the station. |
| skriva (to write) | skriv | Skriv ditt namn här. — Write your name here. |
| öppna (to open) | öppna | Öppna fönstret. — Open the window. |

Many common verbs have a short imperative that is not predictable from the infinitive. Learn these as separate forms:

| Infinitive | Imperative | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| vara (to be) | var | Var försiktig! — Be careful! |
| ha (to have) | ha | Ha en trevlig dag! — Have a nice day! |
| gå (to go, walk) | gå | Gå rakt fram. — Go straight ahead. |
| komma (to come) | kom | Kom hit! — Come here! |
| göra (to do, make) | gör | Gör så här. — Do it like this. |
| ta (to take) | ta | Ta den här bussen. — Take this bus. |
| se (to see) | se | Se upp! — Watch out! |
| säga (to say) | säg | Säg det igen. — Say it again. |
| äta (to eat) | ät | Ät lite frukt. — Eat some fruit. |
| dricka (to drink) | drick | Drick vatten. — Drink water. |

The imperative has no ending for *du* or *ni*. For example, **Kom!** can mean “Come!” to one person or a group. The context makes clear who is being addressed.

## Word order and objects

Put the imperative verb first. Any object or other information comes after it.

- **Stäng dörren.** — Close the door.
- **Sätt boken på bordet.** — Put the book on the table.
- **Vänta här en stund.** — Wait here for a while.

An imperative can also be followed by a place or direction:

- **Gå till höger vid bron.** — Go right at the bridge.
- **Fortsätt längs gatan.** — Continue along the street.

## Negative commands

To tell someone not to do something, put **inte** after the imperative.

- **Gå inte över gatan här.** — Don’t cross the street here.
- **Glöm inte nycklarna.** — Don’t forget the keys.
- **Öppna inte dörren.** — Don’t open the door.

The verb stays in the imperative form; it does not change in a negative command.

## Reflexive verbs and verb particles

With a reflexive verb, the reflexive pronoun comes after the imperative. Reflexive pronouns include *dig* (yourself, singular) and *er* (yourselves, plural or formal).

- **Sätt dig här.** — Sit here. (Literally: “Set yourself here.”)
- **Skynda dig!** — Hurry up!
- **Klä på dig.** — Get dressed.
- **Sätt er vid bordet.** — Sit down at the table. (To more than one person.)

If a verb has a particle, the particle stays after the imperative. A particle is a short word such as *på*, *av*, or *ut* that combines with the verb to make a particular meaning.

- **Sätt på lampan.** — Turn on the lamp.
- **Ta av dig skorna.** — Take off your shoes.
- **Gå ut nu.** — Go outside now.

## Making a command more polite

A direct imperative is normal for clear instructions, signs, and familiar situations. It can sound abrupt in some situations, so Swedish speakers often soften a request with **är du snäll**, **tack**, or a question.

- **Stäng fönstret, är du snäll.** — Close the window, please.
- **Vänta lite, tack.** — Wait a moment, please.
- **Kan du öppna fönstret?** — Can you open the window?
- **Skulle du kunna hjälpa mig?** — Could you help me?

The last two examples are questions, not imperatives, but they are common polite alternatives when asking someone to do something.

## Suggestions with *låt*

Use **låt oss** followed by an infinitive to make a suggestion meaning “let’s”. This form can sound a little formal; in everyday speech, **vi kan** (“we can”) is another natural way to make a suggestion. The form **låt** is the imperative of *låta* (to let).

- **Låt oss börja.** — Let’s begin.
- **Låt oss ta bussen.** — Let’s take the bus.
- **Vi kan gå nu.** — We can go now.

## Imperatives on signs and in instructions

Imperatives are common in directions, recipes, and instructions. A short imperative can be used without naming the person who should follow it.

- **Sväng vänster vid nästa gata.** — Turn left at the next street.
- **Blanda mjöl och vatten.** — Mix flour and water.
- **Tryck på den gröna knappen.** — Press the green button.

Some signs use a passive-looking form ending in **-s**, such as **Öppnas åt andra hållet** (“Opens the other way”) or **Dörren stängs automatiskt** (“The door closes automatically”). These describe what happens rather than giving a direct command; use the ordinary imperative when telling someone what to do, for example **Stäng dörren** (“Close the door”).
