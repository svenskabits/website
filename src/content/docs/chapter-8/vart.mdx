The question word **vart** means **where to?** or **to where?** Use it to ask about a destination or the direction of movement. The destination can be a place, a person, or somewhere understood from the conversation.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vart går du?** | Where are you going? |
| **Vart ska vi i kväll?** | Where are we going tonight? |
| **Vart flyttade de?** | Where did they move to? |
| **Vart leder den här vägen?** | Where does this road lead? |

## Use vart for a destination

Use **vart** with verbs of movement such as **gå** (go, walk), **åka** (go, travel), **komma** (come), and **flytta** (move). The person does not need to be moving at the exact moment: **vart** can ask about a planned trip or a past move as well as movement happening now.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vart åker ni på semestern?** | Where are you going for your holiday? |
| **Vart kommer bussen att köra?** | Where will the bus go? |
| **Vart ska Sara flytta nästa år?** | Where is Sara moving next year? |
| **Vart sprang katten?** | Where did the cat run to? |

The destination can also be a direction or result, not only a named place:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vart ska jag svänga?** | Which way should I turn? |
| **Vart går den här stigen?** | Where does this path go? |

## Word order in direct questions

In a direct question, **vart** is followed by the conjugated verb and then the subject:

**vart + verb + subject + the rest**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vart går bussen?** | Where does the bus go? |
| **Vart åker din bror?** | Where is your brother going? |
| **Vart kan vi gå efter maten?** | Where can we go after the meal? |
| **Vart ska du inte gå ensam?** | Where should you not go alone? |

With a modal verb such as **kan** (can) or **ska** (will, be going to), that verb comes before the subject. A following main verb stays in the infinitive: **Vart kan vi gå?** (Where can we go?)

## Vart in an indirect question

An indirect question is part of a larger sentence. After **vart**, put the subject before the verb:

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Vart går bussen?** | **Jag vet vart bussen går.** | Where does the bus go? / I know where the bus goes. |
| **Vart ska vi gå?** | **Hon frågar vart vi ska gå.** | Where should we go? / She asks where we should go. |
| **Vart åkte de?** | **Vet du vart de åkte?** | Where did they go? / Do you know where they went? |

In **Vet du vart de åkte?** (Do you know where they went?), **vet du** is a direct question, so its verb comes before its subject. Inside it, **vart de åkte** is an indirect question, so the subject comes before the verb.

## Vart or var?

In standard Swedish, **var** asks about a location, while **vart** asks about a destination. Compare:

| Location: where someone or something is | Destination: where someone or something is going |
|---|---|
| **Var är du?** — Where are you? | **Vart går du?** — Where are you going? |
| **Var bor de?** — Where do they live? | **Vart flyttar de?** — Where are they moving to? |
| **Var stannar bussen?** — Where does the bus stop? | **Vart går bussen?** — Where does the bus go? |

In everyday speech, some speakers use **var** for destinations too. The distinction **var** for location and **vart** for destination is standard in careful Swedish and makes the meaning clear.

To ask about a starting point, use **varifrån** (where from), not **vart**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Varifrån kommer tåget?** | Where is the train coming from? |
| **Vart går tåget?** | Where is the train going? |

## Answering a vart question

An answer can name the destination, often with **till** (to), or use a direction word such as **hit** (to here), **dit** (to there), or **hem** (home, as a destination):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vart ska du? – Till biblioteket.** | Where are you going? – To the library. |
| **Vart åker ni? – Vi åker till Uppsala.** | Where are you going? – We are going to Uppsala. |
| **Vart ska jag lägga väskan? – Lägg den där.** | Where should I put the bag? – Put it there. |
| **Vart går du nu? – Jag går hem.** | Where are you going now? – I am going home. |

Use **vart** for the destination or direction, **var** for the place where someone or something is, and **varifrån** for the starting point.
