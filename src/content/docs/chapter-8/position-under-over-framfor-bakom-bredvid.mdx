Swedish uses prepositions to show where one person or thing is in relation to another. The preposition comes before the noun phrase. The forms below do not change to agree with the noun.

| Preposition | Basic meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **under** | below; lower than something | Katten ligger **under bordet**. — The cat is **under the table**. |
| **över** | higher than something; above | Lampan hänger **över bordet**. — The lamp is hanging **above the table**. |
| **framför** | in front of something | Cykeln står **framför huset**. — The bicycle is **in front of the house**. |
| **bakom** | behind something | Bilen står **bakom huset**. — The car is **behind the house**. |
| **bredvid** | beside; next to something | Apoteket ligger **bredvid banken**. — The pharmacy is **next to the bank**. |

The noun phrase after the preposition usually has the form you need for its meaning. For a specific thing, use the definite form: **under bordet** (under the table), **bakom huset** (behind the house). For one unspecified thing, use an indefinite form: **under ett bord** (under a table), **bredvid en bank** (next to a bank).

## Choosing the right word

### Under and över

Use **under** when something is lower than another thing. It often suggests that the thing is directly beneath it:

- Skorna står **under sängen**. — The shoes are **under the bed**.
- Parkeringen ligger **under hotellet**. — The car park is **under the hotel**.

Use **över** when something is higher than another thing:

- Klockan hänger **över dörren**. — The clock is hanging **above the door**.
- Fågeln flyger **över sjön**. — The bird is flying **over the lake**.

With movement, **över** can also mean that something passes across or over an area or obstacle:

- Vi går **över bron**. — We are walking **across the bridge**.
- Bussen kör **under bron**. — The bus is driving **under the bridge**.

The situation tells you whether **över** means a higher position or movement across/over something.

### Framför and bakom

Use **framför** for the side that is at the front of a person or thing, and **bakom** for the side at its back:

- Jag väntar **framför stationen**. — I am waiting **in front of the station**.
- Barnen leker **bakom huset**. — The children are playing **behind the house**.

What counts as the front depends on the thing. A person or vehicle has a clear front, while the front of a building is usually its entrance-facing side. The two words describe relative position, not necessarily distance: someone can stand just in front of a building or farther away in front of it.

### Bredvid

Use **bredvid** when two things are next to each other, usually side by side:

- Hon sitter **bredvid mig**. — She is sitting **next to me**.
- Det finns en liten park **bredvid skolan**. — There is a small park **next to the school**.

**Bredvid varandra** means “next to each other”:

- De två husen ligger **bredvid varandra**. — The two houses are **next to each other**.

## Describing where things are

Swedish often uses a verb that tells how something is positioned. The preposition then gives the exact relation:

| Verb | Common use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ligger** (lies/is situated) | places, objects that lie flat, or things whose position is being described | Boken ligger **under stolen**. — The book is **under the chair**. |
| **står** (stands/is) | upright objects, people, and many buildings | Väskan står **bredvid dörren**. — The bag is **next to the door**. |
| **sitter** (sits/is attached) | people sitting or things attached to a surface | Tavlan sitter **över soffan**. — The picture is **above the sofa**. |
| **hänger** (hangs) | things hanging from something | Jackan hänger **bakom dörren**. — The jacket is hanging **behind the door**. |

These are common choices, not rigid rules. The verb describes the object's position or how it is supported; the preposition describes where it is. For example, a picture can **hänga** (hang) on a wall, while a picture that is attached to the wall can also **sitta** there.

## Word order

In a simple statement, put the place phrase after the verb:

- Katten **ligger under bordet**. — The cat **is under the table**.
- Banken **ligger bredvid torget**. — The bank **is next to the square**.

You can also put the place first to emphasize it. The verb then comes before the subject:

- **Under bordet ligger katten.** — **Under the table is the cat.**
- **Framför huset står en taxi.** — **In front of the house there is a taxi.**

In everyday speech, the subject-first order is more common when simply saying where something is. Use the place-first order when the location is the focus or contrast.
