The Swedish question word **varifrån** means **where from?** Use it to ask about the starting place, origin, or source of a person or thing. It is formed from **var** (where) and **ifrån** (from).

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Varifrån kommer du?** | Where are you from? |
| **Varifrån går tåget?** | Where does the train leave from? |
| **Varifrån kommer den här lukten?** | Where is this smell coming from? |
| **Varifrån fick du den idén?** | Where did you get that idea from? |

## Ask about a starting point or source

Use **varifrån** when you want to know where a journey begins, where someone or something originates, or what source something came from. The source can be a place, a person, or something less physical, such as information or an idea.

When you ask a person **Varifrån kommer du?**, you are asking about their origin, not necessarily where they live now:

- **Jag kommer från Iran, men bor i Sverige nu.** — I am from Iran, but I live in Sweden now.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Varifrån åker bussen?** | Where does the bus leave from? |
| **Varifrån kommer dina grannar?** | Where are your neighbours from? |
| **Varifrån har du fått informationen?** | Where did you get the information from? |
| **Varifrån kommer pengarna?** | Where is the money coming from? |

## Two ways to form the question

In a direct question, the conjugated verb comes before the subject. You can keep **varifrån** together at the beginning, or put **ifrån** after the verb phrase:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Varifrån kommer din vän?** | Where is your friend from? |
| **Var kommer din vän ifrån?** | Where is your friend from? |
| **Varifrån börjar promenaden?** | Where does the walk start? |
| **Var börjar promenaden ifrån?** | Where does the walk start? |

Both patterns are natural Swedish. In the second pattern, **var** is at the beginning and **ifrån** stays with the place or source that is being asked about. The preposition **från** also means “from”; for example, **Var kommer din vän från?** has the same meaning as **Var kommer din vän ifrån?**

## Word order in direct and indirect questions

In a direct question, put the verb before the subject:

**varifrån + verb + subject**

- **Varifrån kommer tåget?** — Where is the train coming from?
- **Varifrån kan vi ta bussen?** — Where can we catch the bus from?

An indirect question is part of a larger sentence. Inside it, put the subject before the verb:

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Varifrån kommer hon?** | **Jag vet inte varifrån hon kommer.** | Where is she from? / I don't know where she is from. |
| **Var kommer tåget ifrån?** | **Vet du var tåget kommer ifrån?** | Where does the train come from? / Do you know where the train comes from? |

In **Vet du var tåget kommer ifrån?** (Do you know where the train comes from?), **vet du** is a direct question, so its verb comes before its subject. The embedded question **var tåget kommer ifrån** has the subject before the verb.

## Answering a varifrån question

Answers commonly use **från** or **ifrån** before the source. Both prepositions can mean “from.” The answer can also use **härifrån** (“from here”) or **därifrån** (“from there”) when the source is clear.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Varifrån kommer du? – Jag kommer från Malmö.** | Where are you from? – I am from Malmö. |
| **Var kommer tåget ifrån? – Det kommer från Göteborg.** | Where does the train come from? – It comes from Gothenburg. |
| **Varifrån kommer röklukten? – Härifrån.** | Where is the smell of smoke coming from? – From here. |
| **Varifrån fick hon brevet? – Därifrån.** | Where did she get the letter from? – From there. |

## Var, vart, and varifrån

These question words ask about different parts of movement or location:

| Question word | Ask about | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **var** | where someone or something is | **Var är stationen?** — Where is the station? |
| **vart** | the destination: where someone or something is going | **Vart går tåget?** — Where is the train going? |
| **varifrån** | the starting point or source: where someone or something comes from | **Varifrån går tåget?** — Where does the train leave from? |

The contrast is especially clear when asking about a journey:

- **Var går tåget?** — Where is the train (route) going? / Where does the train go?
- **Vart går tåget?** — Where is the train going to?
- **Varifrån går tåget?** — Where does the train leave from?

In everyday speech, some speakers use **var** for a destination too. **Vart** clearly asks about the destination, while **varifrån** asks about the starting point or source.
