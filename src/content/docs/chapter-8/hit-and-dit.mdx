Swedish uses **hit** and **dit** to say where someone or something is moving to. They are direction words: **hit** means movement to the place where the speaker is, while **dit** means movement to another place.

| Word | Direction | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **hit** | to here, towards the speaker or current location | **Kom hit!** — Come here! |
| **dit** | to there, towards another place | **Gå dit.** — Go there. |

## Hit: movement to where I am

Use **hit** when the destination is the place where the speaker is, or the place they are treating as “here.” The movement can be on foot, by vehicle, or in some other way.

- **Kan du komma hit efter jobbet?** — Can you come here after work?
- **Vi flyttade hit för två år sedan.** — We moved here two years ago.
- **Ta med dig väskan hit.** — Bring the bag here.
- **Bussen går hit varje halvtimme.** — The bus comes here every half hour.

The destination can be understood from the situation; you do not have to name it.

## Dit: movement to another place

Use **dit** when the destination is somewhere else, away from the speaker's current location, or a place already mentioned or pointed out.

- **Jag ska dit i morgon.** — I am going there tomorrow.
- **De åkte dit med tåg.** — They went there by train.
- **Lägg boken dit, tack.** — Put the book there, please.
- **Hur kommer vi dit?** — How do we get there?

The destination may be named in the same sentence or understood from the conversation:

- **Vi gick till parken och sedan gick vi dit igen på söndagen.** — We went to the park, and then we went there again on Sunday.
- **Ser du biblioteket? Vi ska dit.** — Do you see the library? We are going there.

## Hit and dit compared with här and där

**Här** and **där** describe a place where someone or something is. **Hit** and **dit** describe movement to a place.

| Place: no movement | Direction: movement to a place |
| --- | --- |
| **Jag är här.** — I am here. | **Jag kommer hit.** — I am coming here. |
| **Hon bor där.** — She lives there. | **Hon åker dit.** — She is going there. |
| **Nycklarna ligger där.** — The keys are there. | **Lägg nycklarna dit.** — Put the keys there. |

Compare **var** (“where?”) with **vart** (“where to?”):

- **Var är du?** — Where are you?
- **Vart går du?** — Where are you going?
- **Var bor de?** — Where do they live?
- **Vart flyttar de?** — Where are they moving to?

In everyday spoken Swedish, **var** is also often used to ask about a destination. Learning **vart** for “where to?” makes the difference clear and is standard in careful Swedish.

## Choosing between hit and dit

The choice depends on the point of view in the conversation, not on how far away the destination is. A nearby place can be **dit** if it is away from the speaker; a distant place can be **hit** if the speaker is there and someone is travelling to them.

- **Jag är på kontoret. Kom hit när du är klar.** — I am at the office. Come here when you are finished.
- **Du är hemma och jag är på kontoret. Jag kommer dit efter mötet.** — You are at home and I am at the office. I will come there after the meeting.

The same place can be **hit** or **dit** depending on who is speaking and where they are:

- From the office, someone says: **Kom hit till kontoret.** — Come here to the office.
- From home, the same person says: **Jag åker dit efter jobbet.** — I am going there after work.

## Word order

In a simple statement, **hit** or **dit** usually comes after the verb:

- **Vi går dit tillsammans.** — We are going there together.
- **Min syster kommer hit på fredag.** — My sister is coming here on Friday.

With **inte**, put **inte** after the conjugated verb and before **hit** or **dit**:

- **Jag går inte dit i kväll.** — I am not going there tonight.
- **Han kommer inte hit i dag.** — He is not coming here today.

In a question, the verb comes before the subject:

- **Kommer du hit senare?** — Are you coming here later?
- **Åker ni dit med bil?** — Are you going there by car?

## Sources and destinations

Use **hit** and **dit** for the destination. To say where movement starts, Swedish commonly uses **härifrån** (“from here”) and **därifrån** (“from there”).

- **Vi åker härifrån klockan åtta och kommer dit vid lunch.** — We are leaving from here at eight and will arrive there at lunchtime.
- **Hon gick därifrån och kom hit en stund senare.** — She left there and came here a little later.

You can also name a starting point with **från**:

- **De åkte från hotellet hit.** — They travelled here from the hotel.
- **Jag går från skolan dit.** — I am walking there from the school.
