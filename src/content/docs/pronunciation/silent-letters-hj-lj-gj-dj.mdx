At the beginning of many Swedish words, the combinations **hj**, **lj**, **gj**, and **dj** are all pronounced with the Swedish **j** sound. The first letter is silent: you do not pronounce the **h**, **l**, **g**, or **d**. The sound [j] is close to the **y** in English *yes*; it is not the English **j** sound in *jam*. The brackets show an IPA symbol for the sound.

## Four spellings, one consonant sound

| Spelling | Examples | English meanings |
|---|---|---|
| **hj** | **hjälp**, **hjärta**, **hjul** | help; heart; wheel |
| **lj** | **ljus**, **ljud**, **ljuga** | light/candle; sound; to lie |
| **gj** | **gjorde**, **gjord**, **gjuta** | did/made; made; to pour or cast |
| **dj** | **djur**, **djup**, **djungel** | animal; deep; jungle |

For example, **hjälp** begins with the same consonant sound as **jag** (I), and **ljus** begins with the same consonant sound as **jul** (Christmas). Keep the first written letter in your spelling, but leave it out when you speak.

## Hear the spelling, not an extra consonant

Try these phrases at a comfortable speed. Start each bold word with the sound in English *yes*, without adding an h, l, g, or d before it:

- **Hjälp**, jag hittar inte min telefon. — Help, I can't find my phone.
- **Ljuset** är tänt. — The light is on.
- Jag **gjorde** en smörgås. — I made a sandwich.
- Det finns många **djur** i **djungeln**. — There are many animals in the jungle.

Some words that look different are pronounced alike because of this pattern. **Hjul** (wheel) and **jul** (Christmas) sound the same, as do **gjord** (made) and **jord** (earth/soil). The spelling tells you which word is meant.

## Where the pattern applies

Learn these as common word-initial spellings, rather than as a rule to erase those letters wherever they appear. For example, in **adjektiv** (adjective), the **d** is pronounced. A word's history and parts can affect how a sequence is read, so use the familiar **hj**, **lj**, **gj**, and **dj** pattern for words that begin with those combinations, and check unfamiliar words individually.

Also notice the difference between **gj** and a **g** before a soft vowel. In **gjorde**, the initial **g** is silent because it is part of **gj**. In **göra** (to do/make) and **ge** (to give), there is no written **j**: the **g** alone has the Swedish j-sound before **ö** or **e**. The spoken consonant is similar, but the spelling pattern is different.
