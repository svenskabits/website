In everyday Swedish, some very common words are pronounced with fewer sounds than their spelling suggests. This is especially noticeable when the words are unstressed and spoken as part of a sentence. The spelling normally stays the same: write **jag** and **det**, even when they sound like **ja** and **de**.

## **Jag** often sounds like **ja**

In ordinary, connected speech, the final **g** in **jag** is often not heard. The word then sounds like **ja** (“yes”):

| Written Swedish | How it may sound in casual speech | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jag vet. | Ja vet. | I know. |
| Jag kommer snart. | Ja kommer snart. | I’ll be there soon. |
| Jag tycker om kaffe. | Ja tycker om kaffe. | I like coffee. |

This is a common pronunciation, not a change in the written word. In careful or emphatic speech, speakers may pronounce **jag** more fully, and pronunciation varies by speaker and region. Do not add a **g** just because it appears in the spelling, and do not assume that every speaker will pronounce the word in exactly the same way.

When reduced, **jag** can sound just like **ja**, the Swedish word for “yes.” The sentence makes the meaning clear:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Ja, jag vet. | Yes, I know. |
| Jag vet. | I know. |

In the first sentence, the first **ja** means “yes”; the second word is **jag**, which may also sound like “ja.”

## **Det** often sounds like **de**

In everyday speech, the final **t** in **det** is commonly left unheard, so **det** sounds like **de**:

| Written Swedish | How it may sound in casual speech | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Det är sant. | De är sant. | That’s true. |
| Jag tror det. | Ja tror de. | I think so. |
| Det ser bra ut. | De ser bra ut. | It looks good. |

The pronunciation **de** here represents the spoken form of **det**; it does not change the spelling. A clearly pronounced **t** may be heard when a speaker is speaking carefully or stressing the word, but leaving it out is very normal in conversation.

Be careful with the separate written word **de**, which means “they” in formal writing. It is often pronounced **dom** in everyday Swedish. For example, **De kommer** (“They are coming”) is commonly said like **dom kommer**, while **Det kommer** (“It is coming”) may sound like **de kommer**. The surrounding sentence helps distinguish them.

## Listening and speaking naturally

These reductions are most likely when **jag** and **det** are not stressed. They can make conversation flow more smoothly, but they are not obligatory: a speaker can keep a fuller pronunciation for clarity, emphasis, or a more careful speaking style. As a learner, it is useful to recognize the reduced forms when listening. When speaking, use the pronunciation that feels clear and comfortable, and keep the standard spellings **jag** and **det** in your writing.
