In relaxed Swedish, frequent words are often shorter and less distinct than they look in writing. Sounds can also run together across word boundaries. These are normal features of spoken language, not changes to the grammar. The amount of reduction depends on pace, emphasis, region, and speaker; clear, careful speech is natural too.

Here, **contractions** means shortened spoken forms. Swedish does not generally use apostrophes to write these pronunciations. In ordinary writing, keep the standard spelling unless you are deliberately representing informal speech.

## Frequent words with shorter pronunciations

The spellings in the middle column are informal ways of showing what a reduced form may sound like. They are listening aids, not exact pronunciation instructions or a single standard spelling system.

| Standard spelling | Possible casual form | Example and meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *jag* | *ja* | *Jag vet inte.* — I don't know. |
| *är* | *e* | *Det är bra.* — It's good. |
| *det* | *de* | *Det blir bra.* — It'll be fine. |
| *vad* | *va* | *Vad gör du?* — What are you doing? |
| *och* | *å* | *Kaffe och te.* — Coffee and tea. |
| *med* | *me* | *Vill du följa med?* — Do you want to come along? |

For example, *Jag vet inte* may sound closer to *ja vet inte* in quick conversation, while *Det är bra* may sound like *de e bra*. A speaker may reduce one word but pronounce another fully, and may use fuller forms to be clear or to add emphasis. Do not try to shorten every word.

## Common shortened word forms

Some shortened forms are especially common in speech and informal writing. The full forms remain appropriate in careful writing. These pairs do not all work in exactly the same way: for example, *sen* is a common shortened form of *sedan*, while *dom* is an informal spelling for the spoken pronouns *de* and *dem*.

| Full form | Common short form | Example and meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *någon* | *nån* | *Har du någon fråga?* — Do you have a question? |
| *något* | *nåt* | *Vill du ha något att äta?* — Would you like something to eat? |
| *några* | *nåra* | *Jag har några minuter.* — I have a few minutes. |
| *sedan* | *sen* | *Vi ses senare, eller sen.* — We'll see each other later, or afterward. |
| *sådan, sådant, sådana* | *sån, sånt, såna* | *En sån dag!* — What a day! |
| *de, dem* | *dom* | *De kommer snart. Jag såg dem igår.* — They'll be here soon. I saw them yesterday. |

In standard writing, *de* is the subject form and *dem* is the object form. In speech, both are normally pronounced like *dom*. The spelling *dom* is widespread in informal writing, but formal texts usually use *de* and *dem*.

## Short phrases can run together

Reduction is often easiest to hear in a whole phrase rather than in an isolated word. Small words lose prominence, and neighboring sounds flow together. The informal spellings below suggest one possible impression; they are not fixed pronunciations:

| Written phrase | It may sound roughly like | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Vad är det?* | *Va e de?* | What is it? |
| *Jag är här.* | *Ja e här.* | I'm here. |
| *Det är inte så farligt.* | *De e inte så farligt.* | It's not that bad. |
| *Vad ska du göra?* | The words may blend, especially around *vad* and *du*. | What are you going to do? |
| *Har du tid?* | The words may be said with little separation. | Do you have time? |

The last two examples do not have one reduced spelling or pronunciation that everyone uses. Listen for the familiar words and the sentence meaning rather than expecting to hear every written sound separately. Keep the words separate in your own writing.

## What does not change

Fast speech can make a word shorter, but it does not give you permission to change its meaning or grammar. In particular, hear the sentence before deciding whether *de* means “it” (*det* in standard spelling) or is the spoken form of “they/them” (*de/dem*). Context makes the difference:

- *De är här.* — They are here. In speech, *de* is commonly heard as *dom*.
- *Det är här.* — It is here. In casual speech, *det* may be shortened toward *de*.

Not every speaker shortens *inte*, and reductions such as a barely audible final vowel are difficult to represent consistently in writing. Spell it *inte* in normal text. Regional and personal speech habits vary, so a form that you hear often is not necessarily the only natural form.

## Listening and speaking practice

First read each sentence slowly, noticing all its words. Then say it again at a comfortable conversational pace. Let the small words become lighter, but keep the important words clear:

- *Jag vet inte vad det är.* — I don't know what it is.
- *Har du något att göra i kväll?* — Do you have anything to do tonight?
- *Vi ses sen, efter jobbet.* — We'll see each other later, after work.
- *De kommer med några vänner.* — They're coming with a few friends.

When listening, use the whole phrase to recognize shortened words. When speaking, aim for a smooth, relaxed rhythm rather than imitating every reduction. You can always use fuller forms when you want to be especially clear.
