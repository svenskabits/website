A **minimal pair** is a pair of words that differ in just one sound and have different meanings. Listening for these small differences helps you hear and produce Swedish vowels more clearly. In the examples below, the spelling also helps show what is changing.

## Vowel length and vowel quality

Swedish has nine vowel letters: **a, e, i, o, u, y, å, ä, ö**. In a stressed syllable, a vowel is usually long before one consonant and short before a doubled consonant or a consonant cluster. The vowel often changes its quality as well as its length: a short vowel is not simply a long vowel said quickly.

| Long vowel | Short vowel | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **tak** (long *a*) | **tack** (short *a*) | roof / thanks |
| **glas** (long *a*) | **glass** (short *a*) | glass / ice cream |
| **vit** (long *i*) | **vitt** (short *i*) | white (common gender) / white (neuter) |
| **ny** (long *y*) | **nytt** (short *y*) | new (common gender) / new (neuter) |
| **bok** (long *o*) | **bock** (short *o*) | book / billy goat |
| **ful** (long *u*) | **full** (short *u*) | ugly / full |
| **söt** (long *ö*) | **sött** (short *ö*) | sweet (common gender) / sweet (neuter) |

The extra consonant in spellings such as **tt** or **ck** usually signals a short vowel; it does not mean that you should hold the consonant for a long time. A consonant cluster can also follow a short vowel, as in **höst** (autumn) and **fisk** (fish). The pattern is a useful guide, not a rule that predicts every word: unstressed syllables, compounds, and borrowed words can behave differently.

## Hear the difference between vowel qualities

These pairs keep the surrounding consonants the same, so the vowel is the main difference. Say each pair slowly, then at a natural pace.

| Pair | Vowel contrast | Meanings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **vit – vet** | *i – e* | white – know (present tense of *veta*) |
| **hel – häl** | *e – ä* | whole – heel |
| **bär – bar** | *ä – a* | berry / carries – bare / carried |
| **får – far** | *å – a* | gets / sheep – father |
| **hör – hur** | *ö – u* | hears – how |
| **bok – buk** | *o – u* | book – belly |
| **bi – by** | *i – y* | bee – village |

The letters may look familiar, but several Swedish vowels have no exact English equivalent. For **y**, round your lips as for **u** while keeping the tongue position close to **i**. For Swedish **u**, round your lips and make a close vowel, but keep the tongue farther back than for **y**. For **ö**, round the lips with the tongue toward the front of the mouth. Compare **by – bi**, **hur – hör**, and **bok – buk** without relying on an English spelling to tell you the sound.

The sound written **o** in **bok** is close to the vowel in English *food*, while **å** in **får** is a different, more open-sounding rounded vowel. English is only a rough reference: listen to Swedish examples rather than substituting an English vowel.

## A compact sound guide

The symbols below are broad IPA descriptions of common Central Swedish pronunciations. The mark **ː** means that the vowel is long. Short-vowel quality varies somewhat between speakers and regions; in particular, short **e** and **ä** are often pronounced alike, as are short **o** and **å**.

| Spelling | Common long sound | Common short sound | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **i** | [iː] | [ɪ] | **vit – vitt** |
| **y** | [yː] | [ʏ] | **ny – nytt** |
| **e** | [eː] | [ɛ] | **vet – sett** |
| **ä** | [ɛː] | often [ɛ] | **bär** |
| **ö** | [øː] | [œ] | **söt – sött** |
| **u** | [ʉː] | [ɵ] | **ful – full** |
| **o** | [uː] | [ɔ] | **bok – bock** |
| **å** | [oː] | often [ɔ] | **får** |
| **a** | [ɑː] | [a] | **tak – tack** |

This is a guide to common stressed vowels, not a spelling formula for every word. For example, **sett** (“seen”) and **sätt** (“way”; also the command “put”) are often pronounced alike even though they are spelled differently. Swedish spelling preserves distinctions that do not always produce a sound contrast.

## Practise listening

1. Read each pair aloud and notice whether the vowel is long or short: **tak – tack**, **vit – vitt**, **bok – bock**, **söt – sött**.
2. Repeat **vit – vet**, **hel – häl**, **får – far**, **hör – hur**. Keep the consonants steady and change only the vowel.
3. Listen to a Swedish speaker say the pairs. First identify the vowel; then repeat the whole word. Do not add a pause between the vowel and the final consonant.

Regional accents and individual speakers differ in the exact quality of some vowels. A valid regional pronunciation may sound less rounded, more open, or slightly different in length than the examples here; the contrasts and spellings are still useful guides, not a demand to imitate one single accent.
