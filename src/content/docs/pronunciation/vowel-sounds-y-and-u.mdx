Swedish **y** and **u** are different vowel sounds. Both use rounded lips, but the tongue is farther forward for **y** and more central for **u**. Neither vowel has an exact match in every variety of English, so it helps to listen to Swedish examples rather than substitute an English vowel.

## Shape the two vowels

- For **y**, raise the front of your tongue toward the roof of your mouth and round your lips. Keep the tongue forward while the lips are rounded.
- For **u**, keep the tongue high but more central in your mouth, and round your lips. Do not pull it as far forward as for **y**.

The tongue and lip positions are useful starting points, not rigid instructions. Speakers' exact vowel qualities vary somewhat by region and by individual.

## Long and short sounds

Both letters can have a long or a short sound in a stressed syllable. A long vowel is often written before one consonant or at the end of a word. A short vowel is often written before two consonant letters or a consonant cluster. This is a useful clue, not a rule for every word.

| Letter | Long-vowel examples | Short-vowel examples |
|---|---|---|
| **y** | *ny* (new), *fyra* (four) | *nytt* (new, neuter form), *fylla* (to fill) |
| **u** | *nu* (now), *hus* (house), *ful* (ugly) | *hund* (dog), *full* (full), *dusch* (shower) |

In *nytt*, the **y** is short before **tt**; in *ny*, it is long at the end of the word. In *hus*, **u** is long before one final consonant; in *hund*, it is short before the consonant cluster **nd**. In *ful* and *full*, the spelling marks a long and short **u** respectively.

## Hear the differences

The contrast between **y** and **u** can change a word's meaning:

- *ny* — new; *nu* — now
- *ful* — ugly; *full* — full

The short forms are not simply the long vowels said quickly. Their quality also changes: short **y** is usually a little more open than long **y**, and short **u** is usually more open than long **u**. Keep the lip rounding, and listen for the different tongue position as well as the duration.

Try these natural phrases:

- *En ny bil.* — A new car. (*Ny* has long **y**.)
- *Nu är du här.* — Now you are here. (*Nu* and *du* have long **u**.)
- *Huset är nytt.* — The house is new. (*Hus* has long **u**; *nytt* has short **y**.)
- *Hon fyller fyra år.* — She turns four. (*Fyller* has short **y**; *fyra* has long **y**.)

## Variation and practice

The exact quality of **y** and **u** differs somewhat among speakers and regions of Sweden. The contrast between the two vowels, and between long and short forms, remains an important part of standard Swedish pronunciation. Do not worry if your vowel does not sound identical to every speaker's.

Practice slowly, then say each phrase at a natural pace. For **y**, feel the tongue stay forward behind the rounded lips. For **u**, keep the lips rounded but move the tongue toward the middle of the mouth. Then notice whether the vowel is long or short from the word's spelling and pronunciation.
