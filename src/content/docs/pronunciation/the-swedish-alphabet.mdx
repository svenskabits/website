Swedish uses 29 letters. The first 26 are A–Z; Å, Ä and Ö come after Z and are separate letters, not decorated versions of A and O. The alphabet is used in this order in dictionaries and lists.

Say the names naturally in Swedish. For example, *B* is *be*, *J* is *ji*, *Q* is *ku*, and *W* is *dubbel-ve*. Some letter names may sound a little different from speaker to speaker, but these are the usual forms.

The pronunciations are given in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), using broad, standard Central Swedish forms. Swedish accents differ, so a speaker's exact sounds may vary slightly. Slashes /like this/ show an IPA transcription; they are not spelling.

## A letter's name is not always its sound in a word

The name helps you identify a letter while spelling, but it does not tell you exactly how that letter sounds in every word. A letter can represent different sounds depending on the word, and the same sound can be spelled in different ways.

| Letter | In a word | Pronunciation (IPA) | What to notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | *café* (café), *cirkel* (circle) | /kafeː/, /ˈsɪrkɛl/ | The *c* is pronounced like *k* in *café* and like *s* in *cirkel*. |
| G | *glas* (glass), *ge* (give) | /ɡlɑːs/, /jeː/ | The *g* has a hard sound in *glas* and a softer sound in *ge*. |
| K | *katt* (cat), *kilo* (kilo) | /katː/, /ˈɕiːlʊ/ | The *k* sounds different in these words; before a soft vowel such as *i*, it is often soft. |
| X | *taxi* (taxi) | /ˈtaksɪ/ | The letter name *ex* is not the sound of *x* in this word. |

These are examples, not a complete pronunciation rule for each letter. Names and loanwords can have spellings that do not follow a simple pattern. When you are spelling aloud, use the letter name; when you are reading a word, pronounce the word.

## Swedish vowels: soft and hard vowels

Swedish has nine vowel letters: **A, E, I, O, U, Y, Å, Ä and Ö**. The letter *Y* is a vowel in Swedish. Vowels can be long or short in a word, and their exact sound depends on the word and the speaker.

The labels **soft vowels** and **hard vowels** are useful mainly because they help predict how *G*, *K* and *SK* are pronounced before a vowel. They do not mean that the vowels themselves are pronounced “softly” or “hardly.”

| Vowel group | Letters | Examples | Pronunciation (IPA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard vowels | A, O, U, Å | *gata* (street), *ko* (cow), *skola* (school) | /ˈɡɑːta/, /kuː/, /ˈskuːla/ |
| Soft vowels | E, I, Y, Ä, Ö | *ge* (give), *kilo* (kilo), *sked* (spoon) | /jeː/, /ˈɕiːlʊ/, /ɧeːd/ |

Before a **hard vowel**, *G* and *K* usually keep their hard sounds, as in *gata* and *ko*. Before a **soft vowel**, *G* is often pronounced like Swedish *j* (/j/), *K* often has the /ɕ/ sound, and *SK* often has the /ɧ/ sound, as in *ge*, *kilo* and *sked*. These are helpful patterns rather than exception-free rules; names, loanwords and regional accents can differ.

## Letters you may meet less often

C, Q, W and Z are part of the Swedish alphabet, even though they are less common in many everyday Swedish words than other letters. They appear in names and borrowed words, among other contexts. Their Swedish names are *se*, *ku*, *dubbel-ve* and *zäta*.

| Word | Pronunciation (IPA) | Meaning | Letter to notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| *café* | /kafeː/ | café | C |
| *quiz* | /kʋɪs/ | quiz | Q |
| *webb* | /vɛbː/ | web | W |
| *zon* | /suːn/ | zone | Z |

W is its own letter in the alphabet, even though W and V can sound alike in Swedish words. Keep W in its alphabetical position, between V and X.

## Try spelling aloud

Say each letter name, then the word. For example: *be – bil*.

| Letter name | Pronunciation (IPA) | Word | Word pronunciation (IPA) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *be* (B) | /beː/ | *bil* | /biːl/ | car |
| *ji* (J) | /jiː/ | *ja* | /jɑː/ | yes |
| *ku* (Q) | /kʉː/ | *quiz* | /kʋɪs/ | quiz |
| *dubbel-ve* (W) | /ˈdɵbːɛlveː/ | *webb* | /vɛbː/ | web |
| *zäta* (Z) | /ˈsɛːta/ | *zon* | /suːn/ | zone |

If you need to spell a word with Å, Ä or Ö, say the name of that letter too. Keep these three in the order Å, Ä, Ö, after Z.
