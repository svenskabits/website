In many Swedish words, **ng** represents one sound: a nasal sound made at the back of the mouth. The spelling **nk** usually represents that same nasal sound followed by **k**. The letters may look similar, but listen for whether a k-sound comes after the nasal.

## NG: one nasal sound

For the **ng** sound, the back of your tongue touches the soft part at the back of the roof of your mouth, as it does for **k** or **g**, while air flows through your nose. It is close to the final sound in English *sing*. In ordinary Swedish words, you do not pronounce a separate **g** after it.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en sång** | a song |
| **lång** | long |
| **pengar** | money |
| **många** | many |
| **ringa** | to call; to ring |
| **ingen** | no one; none |

The nasal can come at the end of a word, as in **sång**, or before another sound, as in **många** and **ringa**. In these words, the **g** is not a separate hard g-sound.

## NK: nasal plus k

In **nk**, the back-of-the-mouth nasal is followed by a distinct **k**. Keep the nasal and the k close together, without adding a vowel between them. This is close to the middle sounds in English *think* or *bank*.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en bank** | a bank |
| **en länk** | a link |
| **tänka** | to think |
| **vinka** | to wave |
| **en anka** | a duck |
| **sjunka** | to sink |

Compare **ringa** (to call) with **vinka** (to wave): **ringa** has the nasal sound and then a vowel; **vinka** has the nasal followed by a clear k-sound.

## When the letters meet across a word boundary

The spelling **ng** does not always stand for one nasal sound. If **n** and **g** belong to different parts of a compound or phrase, the **g** beginning the second part is pronounced:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en gång** | one time; once |
| **en ingång** | an entrance |

In **en gång**, **gång** begins with a hard g-sound. In **ingång**, the word is built from **in** + **gång**, so you can hear the g at the start of **gång**. This is different from the single ng sound in **sång** or **lång**.

## Listen for the difference

Say each word slowly, then at a natural speed. Keep the nasal sound in both spellings, and add a k only for **nk**:

- **sång — bank**
- **ringa — tänka**
- **många — vinka**

The vowel sounds in these examples are not all the same; focus on whether you hear a k after the nasal. The exact quality of Swedish vowels can vary by word and speaker, but the difference between the nasal **ng** sound and nasal-plus-**k** in **nk** remains useful to notice.
