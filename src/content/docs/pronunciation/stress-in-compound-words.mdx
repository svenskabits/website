Swedish compounds are written as one word, but they contain parts that each have their own meaning. In many compounds, the first part has the **main stress**. A later part often keeps a weaker **secondary stress**. The main stress is the strongest beat; the other syllables flow around it.

In the examples, **bold** marks the main stressed syllable and *italics* mark a weaker secondary stress. The middle dot (·) shows the boundary between parts of the compound; it is not pronounced.

## The first part usually has the main stress

Listen for a clear first beat, followed by a lighter beat on a later part:

| Compound with stress | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **barn**·*vagn* | a pram, stroller |
| **köks**·*bord* | a kitchen table |
| **cy**kel·*väg* | a cycle path |
| **som**mar·*stu*ga | a summer cottage |
| **sjuk**·*skö*terska | a nurse |

The parts do not have equal strength. For example, in **barn**·*vagn*, give **barn** the clearest prominence and make *vagn* lighter. Do not pronounce every part as a separate, equally strong word.

## Longer compounds

A compound can have more than two parts. Keep the strongest stress near the beginning, and let later parts have weaker stress where it sounds natural:

- **järn**vägs·*sta*tion — a railway station
- **buss**·*håll*plats — a bus stop
- **bo**stads·*om*råde — a residential area

The boundary dot helps show the meaning-bearing parts, but it does not tell you exactly how many beats to make. Keep the whole compound flowing as one word. In a long word, some later syllables may be very weak.

## Use the stress of each part as a clue

The main stressed syllable is often the stressed syllable of the first part when that part is used by itself. A later part may also keep the stress it has in the separate word. For example, *sommar* is stressed on *som*, and *stuga* on *stu*: **som**mar·*stu*ga. This is a useful guide, not a formula that predicts every word. The pattern can be less obvious in borrowed words, unusual compounds, or compounds with several parts, so learn the stress of the complete word by listening.

The spelling gives you a clue to the compound boundary: Swedish normally writes the parts together. But spelling alone does not show the stress marks above, so do not assume that every compound has an identical rhythm.

## Practise in phrases

Say the examples smoothly. Make the bold syllable strongest, and keep any italic syllable lighter:

- **Barn**vagnen står vid **bus**shållplatsen. — The pram is by the bus stop.
- Vi bor i ett **bo**stadsområde. — We live in a residential area.
- **Som**marstugan ligger nära **järn**vägsstationen. — The summer cottage is near the railway station.

Stress and pitch can vary somewhat between speakers and regions. Focus first on the common pattern: a stronger beat on the first part, with later stress, when present, kept weaker.
