Swedish speech does not give every word equal weight. Speakers make some syllables and words more prominent, while other parts of a sentence are lighter and often shorter. This pattern helps listeners hear what a sentence is about and what the speaker wants to emphasize.

In the examples below, **bold** marks the word with the main sentence stress. It is a guide to emphasis, not a special spelling or an exact pitch pattern.

## Word stress and sentence stress

Most Swedish words with more than one syllable have a stressed syllable. In a phrase, however, one word may carry the strongest emphasis. These are related but different kinds of stress:

- **Word stress** belongs to a word. For example, *ARbeta* (work) has stress on its first syllable.
- **Sentence stress** makes one word or idea stand out in the sentence. In *Jag arbetar hemma i dag* (I am working at home today), the strongest stress can fall on *hemma* or *i dag*, depending on what the speaker wants to highlight.

Stress is not simply the same as speaking loudly. A prominent syllable may be clearer, longer, or higher in pitch, and it can have a noticeable pitch movement. Speakers do not use all these cues equally in every sentence.

## Which word gets the main stress?

The main stress often falls on the most important new information, frequently toward the end of a neutral sentence. But the context can move it earlier.

| Sentence | What the stress highlights |
|---|---|
| Jag börjar **PÅ MÅNDAG**. — I start on Monday. | The start date is new or important. |
| **JAG** börjar på måndag. — *I* start on Monday. | I do, perhaps not someone else. |
| Jag **BÖRJAR** på måndag. — I *start* on Monday. | I start then; I do not finish or visit then. |
| Jag börjar på **MÅNDAG**. — I start on *Monday*. | Monday, not another day. |

Capital letters here show the prominent word or phrase; ordinary Swedish writing does not mark sentence stress this way. In normal conversation, the context usually makes the intended emphasis clear.

With no special contrast, the last important word often gets the strongest stress:

- Jag har sökt ett nytt **JOBB**. — I have applied for a new job.
- Vi ska prata med **CHEFEN**. — We are going to talk to the manager.
- Hon jobbar på ett **KONTOR**. — She works in an office.

This is a tendency, not a rule that the last word must always be stressed. A short final word may be light, and a speaker can stress an earlier word to correct, compare, or draw attention to it.

## Light words and a steady flow

In a sentence, important content words—such as nouns, main verbs, adjectives, and many adverbs—often carry stress. Short grammatical words are commonly less prominent. These include articles, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, and auxiliary verbs when they are not the point of the message.

Listen to the contrast in weight in these examples:

- Jag **JOBBAR** på ett **KONTOR**. — I work in an office.
- Hon har **HITTAT** ett nytt **JOBB**. — She has found a new job.
- Vi ska **TRÄFFAS** efter **MÖTET**. — We will meet after the meeting.

Words such as *jag*, *på*, *ett*, *har*, *ett*, and *efter* usually fit lightly around the stressed words. In natural speech, some unstressed syllables and words may also be shorter or less distinct than when spoken carefully. They do not simply disappear, and their exact pronunciation varies with the speaker and pace.

A word that is usually light can receive stress when it contrasts with something else:

- Jag sa **TILL** chefen, inte **FRÅN** chefen. — I said *to* the manager, not *from* the manager.
- **VI** ska skicka mejlet, inte **DE**. — *We* will send the email, not *they*.

Do not try to stress every word equally. Keep the important words clear and let the smaller words connect them.

## Stress in longer words and compounds

Many Swedish compounds have their main stress on the first part, with a weaker stress on a later part. The exact pattern depends on the word, so learn the stress with the word itself:

- **AR**betsplats — workplace
- **JOBB**intervju — job interview
- **LÖNE**samtal — salary discussion
- **AR**betsmiljö — work environment

In a sentence, the compound's main word stress remains, while sentence stress can make the whole compound especially prominent:

- Vi har ett viktigt **LÖNESAMTAL** i morgon. — We have an important salary discussion tomorrow.

The first part of the compound has the main stress; the second part may have a weaker beat. When the compound itself is the key new information, it also carries the main sentence stress. Other words in the sentence are usually lighter.

## Rhythm is not a fixed beat

Swedish rhythm comes from the contrast between prominent syllables and less prominent material between them. Stressed syllables tend to be clearer and may be longer; unstressed syllables are often shorter. This creates a flowing pattern, but Swedish does not require stressed syllables to occur at perfectly even intervals. Do not stretch or compress words mechanically to fit a beat.

The same sentence can have different rhythms because the speaker can change the focus:

- Jag **SKICKAR** mejlet i dag. — I am sending the email today. (The action is the point.)
- Jag skickar **MEJLET** i dag. — I am sending the email today. (The email, not something else, is the point.)
- Jag skickar mejlet **I DAG**. — I am sending the email today. (The timing is the point.)

Each version has a different strongest point, while the unstressed words remain part of the sentence.

## Stress and pitch

Sentence stress can be heard in changes of pitch as well as in length and clarity. Swedish also uses pitch patterns associated with individual words. These word-level pitch patterns are not the same thing as sentence stress: a word can keep its usual word pronunciation while sentence stress changes according to the message. The exact pitch contour varies with sentence type, focus, region, and speaker, so there is no single melody to copy for every stressed word.

## Practice

Say each line first with the bold part as the main focus. Then repeat it with a different focus and notice how the prominence moves. Keep small grammatical words light, but pronounce them so the sentence stays understandable.

1. Jag **ARBETAR** hemifrån på fredagar. — I work from home on Fridays.
2. Jag arbetar **HEMIFRÅN** på fredagar. — I work from home on Fridays.
3. Jag arbetar hemifrån på **FREDAGAR**. — I work from home on Fridays.
4. Vi **BOKADE** ett möte med **KUNDEN**. — We booked a meeting with the client.

For the last sentence, try stressing *vi*, *bokade*, *möte*, or *kunden* in turn. Each choice suggests a different correction or contrast. The words stay the same; the emphasis changes what the listener notices.
