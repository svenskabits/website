Swedish pitch accent is a small melody within a word. Accent 1 and accent 2 do not change the word's spelling or ordinary stress. In much of central Sweden, accent 1 often has one main pitch peak, while accent 2 often has a later additional peak. Treat this as a listening guide, not a tune that every speaker uses: the shape and timing vary by region, and sentence intonation can reshape the melody.

## Start by listening

Choose a recording of a Swedish speaker, ideally from the variety you are learning. Listen to each word in a short phrase before trying to copy it.

1. Listen once for the stressed syllable. Keep it clear; pitch accent does not move the stress to a different syllable.
2. Listen again for the movement of the voice around and after that syllable. Compare the whole word, not just its first sound.
3. Repeat the word at a natural speaking speed. Avoid adding a dramatic rise or making the word longer just to mark accent 2.
4. Listen to the recording again and compare the pitch movement, while allowing for differences between your voice and the speaker's.

## Hear the contrast in context

In each row, the spelling stays the same but the meaning and word accent change. The accent labels describe a common central Swedish pattern; your speaker's exact melody may differ.

| Word | Accent 1 | Accent 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **anden** | **Anden** simmar i dammen. — The duck is swimming in the pond. | **Anden** steg upp ur flaskan. — The spirit rose out of the bottle. |
| **tomten** | **Tomten** är ganska liten. — The plot of land is quite small. | **Tomten** delar ut julklappar. — Santa Claus is handing out Christmas presents. |
| **buren** | **Buren** står i hörnet. — The cage is in the corner. | Väskan blev **buren** uppför trappan. — The bag was carried up the stairs. |
| **stegen** | **Stegen** i trappan är branta. — The steps in the staircase are steep. | **Stegen** står mot huset. — The ladder is leaning against the house. |

First say each pair silently to yourself and decide whether the context means the Accent 1 or Accent 2 word. Then listen to a speaker and repeat the complete sentence. The surrounding words help you keep the sentence natural instead of saying each target word in isolation.

## Practise changing only the meaning

Work through one pair at a time. Say the first sentence, pause, and then say the second. Keep your general speaking pace and stress similar; change the word melody that belongs to the meaning.

- **Anden** simmar i dammen. / **Anden** steg upp ur flaskan.
- **Tomten** är ganska liten. / **Tomten** delar ut julklappar.
- **Buren** står i hörnet. / Väskan blev **buren** uppför trappan.
- **Stegen** i trappan är branta. / **Stegen** står mot huset.

If producing the contrast feels difficult, begin by listening and identifying which meaning you hear. Understanding the difference is useful even if your own accent contrast is still developing.

## Keep word accent inside the sentence

Pitch accent belongs to a word, while sentence intonation also marks things such as focus and questions. A focused word may have a stronger pitch movement, and the pitch may rise or fall across a whole phrase. Practise the examples both as statements and as answers in a conversation, without giving every Accent 2 word an exaggerated extra rise.

For example, say **Tomten delar ut julklappar** as a neutral statement, then as an answer to *Vem delar ut julklappar?* (“Who is handing out Christmas presents?”). The sentence melody changes with the focus, but *tomten* still has the word accent associated with Santa Claus in the variety being spoken.

## Check your progress

- Can you hear that **anden** means a duck in one sentence and a spirit in the other?
- Can you recognize the intended meaning of **tomten**, **buren**, and **stegen** from context and the speaker's word melody?
- Can you repeat the words without shifting their stress or forcing a theatrical pitch pattern?

Accents 1 and 2 are not distinguished equally in every Swedish variety; some have a weaker contrast, and Finland Swedish generally does not use the same distinction. Follow the speech variety you are learning. A different regional melody is a valid pronunciation, not a mistake.
