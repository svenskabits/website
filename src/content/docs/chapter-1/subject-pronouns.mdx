# Subject pronouns
*Subjektspronomen*

## Goal

After this page you can choose the right subject pronoun for a person or thing in a simple Swedish sentence.

---

## Overview

| Swedish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **jag** | I | **Jag** kokar kaffe. *(I am making coffee.)* |
| **du** | you (one person) | **Du** har en hund. *(You have a dog.)* |
| **han** | he | **Han** bor här. *(He lives here.)* |
| **hon** | she | **Hon** läser en bok. *(She reads a book.)* |
| **hen** | they (one person; gender-neutral) | **Hen** jobbar hemma. *(They work from home.)* |
| **den** | it (singular en-words) | Bilen är ny. **Den** är blå. *(The car is new. It is blue.)* |
| **det** | it (singular ett-words) | Huset är stort. **Det** är vitt. *(The house is big. It is white.)* |
| **vi** | we | **Vi** äter nu. *(We are eating now.)* |
| **ni** | you (more than one person) | **Ni** har kaffe. *(You have coffee.)* |
| **de** | they | **De** bor här. *(They live here.)* |

---

## How it works

1. **A subject pronoun tells who or what a sentence is about.** In a simple statement, it usually comes before the verb.
   *Här lagar **jag** mat.* (I am cooking here.) · *Nu äter **vi**.* (We are eating now.)
2. **Du is one person; ni is more than one.** Use *du* for the person you are speaking to, and *ni* for a group.
   *Sara, **du** bor här.* (Sara, you live here.) · *Sara och Ali, **ni** bor här.* (Sara and Ali, you live here.)
3. **Use han, hon or hen for people.** *Hen* is a gender-neutral pronoun for a person when gender is unknown, not relevant, or the person uses *hen*.
   *Ali är hemma. **Han** lagar mat.* (Ali is at home. He is cooking.) · *Kim är hemma. **Hen** lagar mat.* (Kim is at home. They are cooking.)
4. **Use den or det for one thing.** Swedish nouns have an *en* or *ett* form: *en bil* takes **den**, and *ett hus* takes **det**.
   *Bilen är ny. **Den** är bra.* (The car is new. It is good.) · *Huset är nytt. **Det** är bra.* (The house is new. It is good.)

---

## Good to know

- **You hear *dom* for *de*.** In standard writing, use *de* as the subject pronoun.
- **Du is used with everyone.** It is normal with people you know and people you have just met.
- **Jag is usually lowercase.** Unlike English *I*, it has a capital letter only at the start of a sentence.

---

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Mig** lagar mat. | **Jag** lagar mat. | *Jag* is the subject form |
| Sara och Ali, **du** bor här. | Sara och Ali, **ni** bor här. | Use *ni* for more than one person |
| Bilen är ny. **Det** är bra. | Bilen är ny. **Den** är bra. | *En bil* takes *den* |
| Huset är nytt. **Den** är bra. | Huset är nytt. **Det** är bra. | *Ett hus* takes *det* |
| **Dem** bor här. | **De** bor här. | Use *de* as the written subject form |

---

## Recap

- The subject pronouns are **jag, du, han, hon, hen, den, det, vi, ni, de**.
- **Du** is for one person; **ni** is for a group.
- Use **han, hon, hen** for people, **den** for singular en-words and **det** for singular ett-words.
- In a simple statement, the subject pronoun usually comes **before the verb**.
