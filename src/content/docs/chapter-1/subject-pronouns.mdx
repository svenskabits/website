A **subject pronoun** stands for the person or thing that the sentence is about. In a simple statement, it often comes before the verb.

| Swedish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **jag** | I | **Jag** bor i Uppsala. — I live in Uppsala. |
| **du** | you (one person) | **Du** talar svenska. — You speak Swedish. |
| **han** | he | **Han** arbetar här. — He works here. |
| **hon** | she | **Hon** läser en bok. — She is reading a book. |
| **hen** | they (one person) | **Hen** studerar svenska. — They are studying Swedish. |
| **den** | it (an *en*-word) | Bilen är ny. **Den** är röd. — The car is new. It is red. |
| **det** | it (an *ett*-word) | Huset är stort. **Det** är gammalt. — The house is big. It is old. |
| **vi** | we | **Vi** äter lunch nu. — We are having lunch now. |
| **ni** | you (more than one person) | **Ni** kommer tidigt. — You are arriving early. |
| **de** | they | **De** bor nära stationen. — They live near the station. |
| **man** | people / you / one (in general) | **Man** dricker ofta kaffe här. — People often drink coffee here. |

## People: singular and plural

Use **jag** for yourself and **du** when speaking to one person. **Vi** includes the speaker and at least one other person. **Ni** is the usual plural form of “you,” and **de** means “they.”

Swedish uses **du** with people you know and people you have just met. **Ni** is normally plural; it can also be used for one person in some formal or service situations, but **du** is the everyday choice for one person.

Use **han**, **hon**, or **hen** for a person, following the person's pronouns when you know them. **Hen** is a gender-neutral pronoun. It can be used when a person's gender is unknown or not relevant, or when that person uses **hen**.

> **Kim** är hemma. **Hen** lagar mat. — Kim is at home. They are cooking.

Use **man** when you mean people generally, rather than one particular person. English often translates it as “people,” “you,” or “one.” It takes the place of the subject; don't add another subject pronoun for the same meaning.

> **Man** lär sig genom att öva. — You learn by practising.

## Referring to things and animals

Swedish nouns have grammatical gender: they are **en**-words or **ett**-words. When **den** or **det** refers back to a singular noun, its form follows the noun's grammatical gender, not the object's size or natural gender.

| Noun | Pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **en** bil (a car) | **den** | Jag har en bil. **Den** är blå. — I have a car. It is blue. |
| **ett** hus (a house) | **det** | Jag har ett hus. **Det** är blått. — I have a house. It is blue. |

The same pattern can be used for animals. People may also use **han** or **hon** for an animal, especially a pet, when its sex is known or they treat it as an individual.

> Katten sover. **Den** är trött. — The cat is sleeping. It is tired.

## **Det** without a specific thing

Sometimes **det** is a placeholder subject: it fills the subject position but does not refer to a particular noun. Swedish uses it in common expressions about weather, conditions, and existence.

> **Det** regnar. — It is raining.
>
> **Det** är kallt i dag. — It is cold today.
>
> **Det** finns en bank här. — There is a bank here.

**Det** can also refer to a whole event or situation, rather than to one noun.

> Vi vann matchen. **Det** var roligt. — We won the match. That was fun.

## Where the subject pronoun goes

In a simple statement, the subject usually comes before the verb:

> **Jag** arbetar hemma. — I work from home.

But it does not always come first. In a Swedish main statement, the verb normally comes in the second position. If another part of the sentence comes first, the subject follows the verb:

> I dag arbetar **jag** hemma. — Today I am working from home.

In a yes-or-no question, the verb comes before the subject:

> Arbetar **du** hemma? — Do you work from home?

## **De** and **dem**

**De** is the subject form meaning “they.” **Dem** is an object form, used for the people or things receiving an action. In careful standard writing, use **de** for the subject. Both are usually pronounced *dom*; the spelling **dom** is common in informal writing.

**De** can refer to people or to plural things:

> Böckerna är gamla. **De** ligger på bordet. — The books are old. They are on the table.

> **De** känner oss. — They know us.
>
> Jag känner **dem**. — I know them.

## Capitalization

Unlike English “I,” Swedish **jag** is lowercase except at the beginning of a sentence.
