## The basic pattern

In a simple Swedish statement that begins with its subject, the usual order is:

> **Subject + verb + object**

The **subject** tells who or what the sentence is about. The **verb** says what the subject does or is. The **object** tells who or what is affected by the action.

| Subject | Verb | Object | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sara** | **läser** | **en bok** | Sara reads a book. |
| **Ali** | **äter** | **ett äpple** | Ali eats an apple. |
| **Vi** | **ser** | **en film** | We watch a film. |

The subject can be a name, a pronoun, or a longer noun phrase:

- **Hon läser en bok.** — She reads a book.
- **Min bror lagar middag.** — My brother is making dinner.

The verb in these examples is in the present tense. The same basic order works in the past:

- **Sara läste en bok.** — Sara read a book.
- **Ali åt ett äpple.** — Ali ate an apple.

## Not every sentence has an object

Some verbs do not need an object. The sentence can simply have a subject and a verb:

- **Barnet sover.** — The child is sleeping.
- **Vi skrattar.** — We are laughing.

After a form of **vara** (“to be”), a word may describe or identify the subject rather than act as an object:

- **Sara är trött.** — Sara is tired.
- **Ali är läkare.** — Ali is a doctor.

So, subject–verb–object is a common pattern, not a requirement that every sentence must contain all three parts.

## Objects and other information

An object can be a whole phrase, not just one word. Other information, such as place or time, can follow it:

- **Sara läser en bok hemma.** — Sara reads a book at home.
- **Vi äter lunch klockan tolv.** — We eat lunch at twelve o’clock.

Some verbs can have a person who receives something as well as the thing itself. The receiver often comes between the verb and the thing:

- **Lina ger sin bror en bok.** — Lina gives her brother a book.

Here, **sin bror** is the receiver and **en bok** is the thing given. The basic subject–verb order remains the same.

## The verb usually stays in second position in a statement

Swedish statements follow a useful rule: the **finite verb**—the verb that carries the tense, such as *läser* or *läste*—usually comes in the second position in the main clause. In the basic pattern, the subject fills the first position, so the verb follows it:

- **Sara läser en bok.** — Sara reads a book.

The first position can also contain a time or place phrase. Then the verb still comes second, and the subject moves after it:

- **I dag läser Sara en bok.** — Today Sara is reading a book.
- **Hemma äter Ali lunch.** — At home, Ali eats lunch.
- **Nästa vecka arbetar min syster i Malmö.** — Next week my sister is working in Malmö.

“Second position” means the second sentence part, not necessarily the second word. In **Nästa vecka arbetar min syster i Malmö**, the two-word phrase **Nästa vecka** takes the first position, and **arbetar** is the verb in the second position. This is why Swedish statements do not always begin with the subject.

## Where *inte* goes

In a basic statement, **inte** (“not”) comes after the finite verb. If there is an object, it comes before the object:

- **Jag läser inte tidningen.** — I am not reading the newspaper.
- **Sara äter inte kött.** — Sara does not eat meat.

If a time phrase comes first, the verb still follows it, with the subject and **inte** after the verb:

- **I dag arbetar Sara inte.** — Today Sara is not working.

## Statements and questions have different patterns

The subject–verb–object pattern above is for statements. A yes/no question commonly starts with the verb instead:

- **Läser du en bok?** — Are you reading a book?
- **Äter Ali lunch?** — Is Ali eating lunch?

## Quick check

- A basic statement often follows **subject + verb + object**: **Vi ser en film.** — We watch a film.
- Some verbs have no object: **Barnet sover.** — The child is sleeping.
- If a time or place phrase begins the statement, the verb still comes second: **I dag läser Sara.** — Today Sara is reading.
- In a simple statement, **inte** comes after the finite verb: **Jag läser inte.** — I am not reading.
