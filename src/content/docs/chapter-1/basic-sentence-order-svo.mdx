# Basic sentence order (SVO)
*Grundläggande ordföljd (SVO)*

## Goal

After this page you can put the subject, verb and object in the right order in a simple Swedish statement.

---

## Overview

```text
subject + verb + object
```

- **Sara läser en bok.** *(Sara reads a book.)*
- **Ali äter ett äpple.** *(Ali eats an apple.)*
- **Vi ser en film.** *(We watch a film.)*

---

## How it works

1. **Start with the subject.** It tells who or what does the action in a basic statement.
   *Min vän **Sara** läser en bok.* (My friend Sara reads a book.)
2. **Put the verb after the subject.** The verb tells what the subject does.
   *Ali **äter** ett äpple.* (Ali eats an apple.) · *Vi **ser** en film.* (We watch a film.)
3. **Put the object after the verb.** The object tells what or whom the action affects.
   *Sara läser **en bok**.* (Sara reads a book.) · *Ali äter **ett äpple**.* (Ali eats an apple.)

---

## Good to know

- **Some verbs have no object.** *Sara sover.* (Sara sleeps.) The sentence still has a subject and a verb.
- **This pattern is for simple statements that start with the subject.** Keep the subject first when you use this basic order.

---

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Jag **en bok läser**. | Jag **läser en bok**. | Put the verb before the object |
| Sara **kaffe dricker**. | Sara **dricker kaffe**. | Put the verb before the object |
| Vi **en film ser**. | Vi **ser en film**. | Put the verb before the object |

---

## Recap

- In a basic statement, use **subject + verb + object**.
- Put the **subject first**, the **verb second**, and the **object after the verb**.
- Some verbs have no object, as in **Sara sover**.
