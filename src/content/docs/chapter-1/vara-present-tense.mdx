## The present form: är

The infinitive **vara** means “to be.” Its present-tense form is **är**. Unlike English *am*, *is* and *are*, Swedish uses the same form with every subject: **är**.

| Subject | Present form | English |
|---|---|---|
| jag | **är** | I am |
| du | **är** | you are (one person) |
| han / hon / hen | **är** | he / she is; singular they are |
| den / det | **är** | it is |
| vi | **är** | we are |
| ni | **är** | you are (usually more than one person) |
| de | **är** | they are |

For example, **Jag är hemma.** means “I am at home,” and **De är hemma.** means “They are at home.” The subject changes, but the verb stays **är**. **Ni** is normally plural; some speakers also use it as a polite singular “you.”

Use the spelling **är**, with **ä**, for every subject. The infinitive **vara** is used in other constructions, not as the present-tense form: **Jag vill vara hemma.** *(I want to be at home.)* In the present tense, say **Jag är hemma.** *(I am at home.)*

## What är can tell you

### Identity, role or category

Use **är** to say who someone is or what something is. With a simple job or role, Swedish commonly uses the noun without **en** or **ett**:

- **Jag är lärare.** *(I am a teacher.)*
- **Hon är läkare.** *(She is a doctor.)*
- **Han är min bror.** *(He is my brother.)*

An adjective describing the role can be followed by the appropriate article:

- **Hon är en erfaren läkare.** *(She is an experienced doctor.)*

You can also identify or classify a thing with **är**:

- **Det är en bok.** *(It is a book.)*
- **Stockholm är en stad.** *(Stockholm is a city.)*
- **Det är min väska.** *(That is my bag.)*

### A quality, condition or feeling

Use **är** before an adjective to describe a person or thing. The verb does not change, but the adjective may change to match the subject: adjectives usually take an **-t** form with a singular *ett*-word and an **-a** form with a plural subject.

| Subject | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| en-word or person | **Sara är glad.** | Sara is happy. |
| ett-word | **Barnet är glatt.** | The child is happy. |
| plural | **Barnen är glada.** | The children are happy. |

The change is in **glad**, not in **är**. More examples:

- **Jag är trött.** *(I am tired.)*
- **Kaffet är varmt.** *(The coffee is hot.)*
- **Dörrarna är öppna.** *(The doors are open.)*

### Location

Use **är** to say where a person or thing is. The place often follows the verb:

- **Vi är i Stockholm.** *(We are in Stockholm.)*
- **Nyckeln är på bordet.** *(The key is on the table.)*
- **Barnen är hemma.** *(The children are at home.)*

**Vara hemma** means “to be at home.” It describes location, not movement toward home.

### Age and time

Swedish uses **är** to give someone’s age. The age is followed by **år** (“years”):

- **Jag är trettio år.** *(I am thirty years old.)*
- **Hur gammal är du?** *(How old are you?)*

Use **är** for the day, date or time as well:

- **Det är måndag i dag.** *(It is Monday today.)*
- **Klockan är tre.** *(It is three o’clock.)*

### General and impersonal statements

Swedish often uses **det är** (“it is”) when English uses “it is” for a general condition or situation:

- **Det är kallt här.** *(It is cold here.)*
- **Det är viktigt att vila.** *(It is important to rest.)*

**Det är** can also introduce people or things that are present:

- **Det är två personer i rummet.** *(There are two people in the room.)*

## Present-time meaning

The present tense can describe what is true now, a general fact or a repeated situation. Context tells you which meaning is intended:

- **Jag är på jobbet nu.** *(I am at work now.)*
- **Vattnet är vått.** *(Water is wet.)*
- **Vi är hemma varje kväll.** *(We are at home every evening.)*

With a future time expression, the present form can refer to an arranged or expected future situation:

- **Jag är hemma i morgon.** *(I will be at home tomorrow.)*
- **Mötet är på fredag.** *(The meeting is on Friday.)*

The verb remains **är**; the time expression shows that the situation is in the future.

## Negatives and questions

To make a statement negative, put **inte** after **är**:

- **Jag är inte sjuk.** *(I am not ill.)*
- **De är inte här.** *(They are not here.)*

For a yes-or-no question, put **är** before the subject:

- **Är du trött?** *(Are you tired?)*
- **Är ni hemma?** *(Are you at home?)*

With a question word, put the question word first, followed by **är** and then the subject:

- **Var är Anna?** *(Where is Anna?)*
- **Hur är läget?** *(How are things?)*

## Useful reminders

- Use **är**, not the infinitive **vara**, in a present-tense statement: **Jag är hemma.** *(I am at home.)*
- Give your age with **är**: **Jag är tjugo år.** *(I am twenty years old.)*
- Keep the verb as **är** with a plural subject, while making the adjective plural: **Barnen är glada.** *(The children are happy.)*

Remember: **vara** is the infinitive, and **är** is its present form for every subject. The English translation may be *am*, *is* or *are*, depending on who or what the subject is.
