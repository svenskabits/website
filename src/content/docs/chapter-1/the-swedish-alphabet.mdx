Swedish uses 29 letters. The first 26 are A–Z; Å, Ä and Ö come after Z and are separate letters, not decorated versions of A and O. The alphabet is used in this order in dictionaries and lists.

## Swedish letter names

When you spell a name or a word aloud, say the Swedish name of each letter. Here are the usual names, in alphabetical order. The italic forms show how the names are commonly written; they are not English-style sound instructions.

| B | *be* |
| C | *se* |
| D | *de* |
| E | *e* |
| F | *eff* |
| G | *ge* |
| H | *hå* |
| I | *i* |
| J | *ji* |
| K | *kå* |
| L | *ell* |

Say the names naturally in Swedish. For example, *B* is *be*, *J* is *ji*, *Q* is *ku*, and *W* is *dubbel-ve*. Some letter names may sound a little different from speaker to speaker, but these are the usual forms.

## A letter's name is not always its sound in a word

The name helps you identify a letter while spelling, but it does not tell you exactly how that letter sounds in every word. A letter can represent different sounds depending on the word, and the same sound can be spelled in different ways.

| Letter | In a word | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| C | *café* (café), *cirkel* (circle) | The *c* is pronounced like *k* in *café* and like *s* in *cirkel*. |
| G | *glas* (glass), *ge* (give) | The *g* has a hard sound in *glas* and a softer sound in *ge*. |
| K | *katt* (cat), *kilo* (kilo) | The *k* sounds different in these words; before *i*, it is often soft. |
| X | *taxi* (taxi) | The letter name *ex* is not the sound of *x* in this word. |

These are examples, not a complete pronunciation rule for each letter. Names and loanwords can have spellings that do not follow a simple pattern. When you are spelling aloud, use the letter name; when you are reading a word, pronounce the word.

## Letters you may meet less often

C, Q, W and Z are part of the Swedish alphabet, even though they are less common in many everyday Swedish words than other letters. They appear in names and borrowed words, among other contexts. Their Swedish names are *se*, *ku*, *dubbel-ve* and *zäta*.

| Word | Meaning | Letter to notice |
|---|---|---|
| *café* | café | C |
| *quiz* | quiz | Q |
| *webb* | web | W |
| *zon* | zone | Z |

W is its own letter in the alphabet, even though W and V can sound alike in Swedish words. Keep W in its alphabetical position, between V and X.

## Try spelling aloud

Say each letter name, then the word. For example: *be – bil*.

| Letter name | Word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *be* (B) | *bil* | car |
| *ji* (J) | *ja* | yes |
| *ku* (Q) | *quiz* | quiz |
| *dubbel-ve* (W) | *webb* | web |
| *zäta* (Z) | *zon* | zone |

If you need to spell a word with Å, Ä or Ö, say the name of that letter too. Keep these three in the order Å, Ä, Ö, after Z.
