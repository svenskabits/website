Use **den här** and **den där** to point to or identify one specific thing. They usually mean **this** and **that**. The form changes according to the noun: use **den** with a singular en-word, **det** with a singular ett-word, and **de** with a plural noun.

| Meaning | Singular en-word | Singular ett-word | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| this / these | **den här bilen** (this car) | **det här huset** (this house) | **de här böckerna** (these books) |
| that / those | **den där bilen** (that car) | **det där huset** (that house) | **de där böckerna** (those books) |

## The noun is definite

When **den här**, **det här**, **de här**, **den där**, **det där**, or **de där** comes before a noun, the noun has its definite form. This is different from English, where *this* or *that* comes before the ordinary noun form:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **den här stolen** | this chair |
| **det där äpplet** | that apple |
| **de här skorna** | these shoes |
| **Jag vill köpa den där tröjan.** | I want to buy that sweater. |

Choose **den/det/de** by the noun, not by the speaker or the distance:

| Noun | This | That |
|---|---|---|
| **en bok** | **den här boken** (this book) | **den där boken** (that book) |
| **ett bord** | **det här bordet** (this table) | **det där bordet** (that table) |
| **böcker** (books) | **de här böckerna** (these books) | **de där böckerna** (those books) |

## Add an adjective

An adjective comes between the demonstrative and the noun. The noun stays in its definite form, and the adjective normally takes its definite form, often ending in **-a**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **den här röda bilen** | this red car |
| **det där stora huset** | that big house |
| **de här nya skorna** | these new shoes |
| **Jag gillar de där gröna äpplena.** | I like those green apples. |

## Use the phrases without a noun

You can leave out the noun when it is clear from the conversation. In this use, the phrase means **this one**, **that one**, **these**, or **those**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vilken bok vill du ha? – Den här.** | Which book would you like? – This one. |
| **Vill du ha det här eller det där?** | Would you like this one or that one? |
| **Jag tar de där.** | I'll take those. |

The form still matches the thing you mean: **den här/den där** for an en-word, **det här/det där** for an ett-word, and **de här/de där** for plural things.

## Here and there

**Här** means **here**, and **där** means **there**. So **den här** normally points to something near the speaker, while **den där** often points to something farther away. The choice can also depend on the situation or on which thing the speaker wants to distinguish; it is not always about exact physical distance:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Den här stolen är ledig.** | This chair is free. |
| **Kan du ge mig den där boken?** | Can you give me that book? |
| **Jag bor i det här huset.** | I live in this house. |

## A related, more compact form

Swedish also has **denna**, **detta**, and **dessa**, which mean **this/these** and are often more formal or common in written language. With these forms, the following noun is normally indefinite, without the definite ending:

| Common phrase | More compact form | English |
|---|---|---|
| **den här bilen** | **denna bil** | this car |
| **det här huset** | **detta hus** | this house |
| **de här böckerna** | **dessa böcker** | these books |

In everyday conversation, **den här bilen**, **det här huset**, and **de här böckerna** are very common. Do not combine the two patterns: say **den här bilen** or **denna bil**, not *den här bil* or *denna bilen*.
