Swedish uses different words for a large or small amount depending on what is being counted. **Många** goes with countable things, **mycket** with an amount or mass, and **lite** with a small amount. The choice is about the noun's meaning, not whether it is an **en-word** or an **ett-word**.

## Många: countable things

Use **många** before a plural noun when you can count the things one by one. The noun is normally in its indefinite plural form:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **många äpplen** | many apples |
| **många böcker** | many books |
| **Det finns många restauranger här.** | There are many restaurants here. |
| **Hon har många vänner.** | She has many friends. |

Use **hur många** to ask for a number:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hur många barn har de?** | How many children do they have? |
| **Hur många äpplen vill du ha?** | How many apples would you like? |

When you mean many of a particular, known group, use **många av** followed by a definite plural noun or a pronoun:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Många av barnen spelar fotboll.** | Many of the children play football. |
| **Många av dem bor här.** | Many of them live here. |

## Mycket: amounts and mass nouns

Use **mycket** with a noun that is treated as an amount rather than as separate countable items. These nouns are usually singular and do not have an article after **mycket**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **mycket vatten** | much water / a lot of water |
| **mycket kaffe** | much coffee / a lot of coffee |
| **Vi har mycket tid.** | We have a lot of time. |
| **Det ligger mycket snö på marken.** | There is a lot of snow on the ground. |

Use **hur mycket** to ask about an amount:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hur mycket mjölk behöver vi?** | How much milk do we need? |
| **Hur mycket kostar brödet?** | How much does the bread cost? |

Some words that look plural are normally used with **mycket** when talking about an amount. For example, **pengar** (money) is plural, but Swedish commonly says **mycket pengar**. If you count the units, use **många** instead:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Han tjänar mycket pengar.** | He earns a lot of money. |
| **Det kostar många kronor.** | It costs many kronor. |

With a particular amount of a known substance, use **mycket av** plus the definite noun or a pronoun:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Mycket av maten är redan klar.** | Much of the food is already ready. |
| **Jag drack mycket av det.** | I drank a lot of it. |

## Lite: a small amount

Use **lite** before a mass noun to mean **a little** or **some**. The noun is usually singular:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **lite vatten** | a little water |
| **lite socker** | a little sugar |
| **Kan jag få lite kaffe?** | Can I have some coffee? |
| **Vi har lite tid före tåget.** | We have a little time before the train. |

Use **lite av** when you mean a small amount of a particular, known thing:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag vill ha lite av kakan.** | I would like a little of the cake. |
| **Hon tog lite av maten.** | She took a little of the food. |

**Lite** can also stand on its own, without a noun, when the amount is clear from the conversation:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vill du ha kaffe? – Ja, lite, tack.** | Would you like coffee? – Yes, a little, please. |

## Lite as an adverb

**Lite** can describe a small degree or amount with an adjective, another adverb, or a verb. In these uses it means **a little** or **slightly**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag är lite trött.** | I am a little tired. |
| **Kan du prata lite långsammare?** | Can you speak a little more slowly? |
| **Jag sov lite i bilen.** | I slept a little in the car. |

**Mycket** can also be an adverb meaning **a lot** or **very**, and it does not change to match the adjective:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag tycker mycket om kaffe.** | I like coffee very much. |
| **Det är mycket kallt i dag.** | It is very cold today. |

## Comparing amounts

You can add words such as **så** (so), **för** (too), or **inte** (not) before the quantifier. The choice between **många** and **mycket** still depends on whether you mean countable items or an amount:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det finns så många böcker här!** | There are so many books here! |
| **Du har för många väskor.** | You have too many bags. |
| **Det är för mycket socker i kaffet.** | There is too much sugar in the coffee. |
| **Vi har inte mycket mjölk kvar.** | We do not have much milk left. |
| **Det finns inte många platser kvar.** | There are not many seats left. |

For a small number of countable things, Swedish often uses **få** (few), not **lite**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det finns få bussar på söndagar.** | There are few buses on Sundays. |

Do not confuse the quantifier **lite** (a little) with **liten/litet/lilla**, forms of the adjective meaning **small**. The adjective describes the size of a noun and changes form; the quantifier expresses an amount:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **ett litet hus** | a small house |
| **lite kaffe** | a little coffee |
