Use **skulle vilja** to express what you would like, or to make a request sound more polite and less direct than **vill** (want). The phrase is made from **skulle** (would) + **vilja** (to want). After it, use an infinitive without **att**, or a noun phrase.

| Pattern | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| **skulle vilja** + infinitive | **Jag skulle vilja boka ett bord.** | I would like to book a table. |
| **skulle vilja ha** + noun | **Jag skulle vilja ha ett glas vatten.** | I would like a glass of water. |
| **skulle vilja** + infinitive | **Vi skulle vilja betala, tack.** | We would like to pay, please. |

## Making a request

To ask someone politely to do something, put **skulle** before the subject in a yes-or-no question, then use **vilja** and the action:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Skulle du vilja hjälpa mig?** | Would you be willing to help me? |
| **Skulle du vilja stänga dörren, tack?** | Would you be willing to close the door, please? |
| **Skulle ni vilja vänta här en stund?** | Would you be willing to wait here for a moment? |

**Du** means one person you are speaking to. **Ni** can address several people, or one person politely in some contexts. Add **tack** (please/thank you) to make a request warmer; Swedish also commonly uses **snälla** (please), especially in familiar situations.

The same question can be an offer or a question about what someone wants, depending on the situation. For example, **Skulle du vilja ha kaffe?** is usually an offer: “Would you like some coffee?” Context makes the intended meaning clear.

## Word order and forms

**Skulle** does not change with the subject. In a statement, put the subject first; in a yes-or-no question, put **skulle** first:

| Form | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | **Jag skulle vilja prata med dig.** | I would like to talk to you. |
| Question/request | **Skulle du vilja prata med mig?** | Would you be willing to talk to me? |
| Plural subject | **De skulle vilja komma tidigare.** | They would like to come earlier. |

The verb after **vilja** is in the infinitive and has no **att**: **skulle vilja komma**, not *skulle vilja att komma*. Use **att** only when it introduces a new clause with its own subject, as in **Jag skulle vilja att du kommer i tid.** (I would like you to arrive on time.)

## Making the request softer

You can add **gärna** (gladly / would like to) after **skulle** to emphasize that you would be happy to do or receive something:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag skulle gärna vilja boka en tid.** | I would very much like to book an appointment. |
| **Skulle du gärna vilja följa med?** | Would you like to come along? |

The second question asks about the other person's wish; for a request that they do something, **Skulle du vilja följa med?** is the more usual phrasing.

For a direct but still polite request, Swedish often uses **Skulle du kunna ...?** (Could you ...?): **Skulle du kunna öppna fönstret?** (Could you open the window?) This asks whether the person can do it, while **Skulle du vilja ...?** asks whether they would be willing to.

## Negative forms

Put **inte** after **skulle** and before **vilja** to say what you would not like to do or have:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag skulle inte vilja störa dig.** | I would not like to disturb you. |
| **Vi skulle inte vilja ha mer kaffe.** | We would not like any more coffee. |

For a polite refusal, **Nej tack, jag skulle inte vilja ha mer.** means “No, thank you, I would not like any more.”
