Swedish participles are verb forms that can describe a person or thing, much like ordinary adjectives. A **present participle** often describes something doing an action or causing an effect. A **past participle** often describes something affected by an action or the state that results from it. In both cases, the participle can come before a noun or after a linking verb such as **vara** (be).

## Present participles

Present participles usually end in **-ande** or **-ende**. Many are formed by replacing the final **-a** of an infinitive with **-ande**; many other verbs take **-ende**. Some forms or meanings are less predictable:

| Infinitive | Participle | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **växa** (grow) | **växande** | **en växande stad** | a growing city |
| **tala** (speak) | **talande** | **ett talande exempel** | a telling example |
| **le** (smile) | **leende** | **ett leende barn** | a smiling child |
| **vänta** (wait) | **väntande** | **en väntande kund** | a customer who is waiting |

As adjectives, present participles do not change for an **en** noun, an **ett** noun, plural, or definiteness:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en spännande bok** | an exciting book |
| **ett spännande möte** | an exciting meeting |
| **spännande böcker** | exciting books |
| **Mötet var spännande.** | The meeting was exciting. |

They can describe an action in progress, as in **ett skrattande barn** (a laughing child), or a quality or effect, as in **en krävande uppgift** (a demanding task). Many have established meanings that are not simply a description of an action happening at that moment: **en givande kurs** means *a rewarding course*.

## Past participles

Past participles often describe a result or condition. Unlike present participles, they usually change form to agree with the noun. Common patterns include **-d, -t, -da**, **-ad, -at, -ade**, and **-en, -et, -na**, but the correct form depends on the verb. Learn unfamiliar forms with all their agreement forms:

| Infinitive | En form | Ett form | Plural/definite form | Example and English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **stänga** (close) | **stängd** | **stängt** | **stängda** | **ett stängt fönster** — a closed window |
| **öppna** (open) | **öppnad** | **öppnat** | **öppnade** | **ett öppnat paket** — an opened package |
| **skriva** (write) | **skriven** | **skrivet** | **skrivna** | **ett skrivet mejl** — a written email |
| **göra** (do, make) | **gjord** | **gjort** | **gjorda** | **en gjord ändring** — a change that has been made |
| **läsa** (read) | **läst** | **läst** | **lästa** | **en läst artikel** — a read article |

The participle agrees before a noun and after **vara** or **bli**:

| Noun | Before the noun | After **vara** | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **en dörr** (a door) | **en stängd dörr** | **Dörren är stängd.** | a closed door; The door is closed. |
| **ett fönster** (a window) | **ett stängt fönster** | **Fönstret är stängt.** | a closed window; The window is closed. |
| **dörrar** (doors) | **stängda dörrar** | **Dörrarna är stängda.** | closed doors; The doors are closed. |

## Definite noun phrases

Before a definite noun, a past participle normally has its plural-like **-a** form, even when the noun is singular. Swedish also uses **den**, **det**, or **de** before the participle, and the noun has its definite ending:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **den stängda dörren** | the closed door |
| **det stängda fönstret** | the closed window |
| **de stängda dörrarna** | the closed doors |
| **det skrivna meddelandet** | the written message |

With a possessive word, the participle still normally takes **-a**, but **den/det/de** is not used: **min stängda dörr** means *my closed door*; **mitt skrivna meddelande** means *my written message*.

Present participles do not take this **-a** ending: **den spännande boken** means *the exciting book*, and **de spännande böckerna** means *the exciting books*.

## Meaning and use after linking verbs

A past participle often describes the condition left by an action. **Vara** commonly presents that condition, while **bli** often presents a change or event. The distinction depends on context:

- **Datorn är reparerad nu. — The computer is repaired now.**
- **Datorn blev reparerad i morse. — The computer was repaired this morning.**

Some participles describe a person's feeling or experience. The person who has the feeling is the subject, while **av** can introduce what caused it:

- **Jag är intresserad av historia. — I am interested in history.**
- **Barnen blev överraskade av beskedet. — The children were surprised by the news.**

Present participles can describe the thing that causes a feeling or reaction:

- **Filmen är spännande. — The film is exciting.**
- **Nyheten var chockerande. — The news was shocking.**

The participle remains an adjective in these examples. A form with **vara** or **bli** can also be part of a passive construction, especially when the sentence focuses on the event or names the person who did it:

- **Artikeln blev skriven av en journalist. — The article was written by a journalist.**
- **Artikeln är skriven på enkel svenska. — The article is written in simple Swedish.**

## Participles are not the same as finite verbs

A participle does not carry the sentence's tense. The finite verb does. Compare an adjective describing the door with a sentence about the action of closing it:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Dörren är stängd.** | The door is closed. |
| **Dörren stängs klockan tio.** | The door is closed at ten. |
| **Dörren blev stängd i går.** | The door was closed yesterday. |

In **Dörren är stängd**, **stängd** agrees with **dörr** and describes its state. In **Dörren stängs**, **stängs** is a finite passive verb, not an adjective.

Do not confuse a past participle with the **supine** used after **har** or **hade**. The supine does not agree with a noun:

- **Jag har skrivit ett mejl. — I have written an email.** Here **skrivit** is the supine.
- **Mejlet är skrivet på svenska. — The email is written in Swedish.** Here **skrivet** is a past participle agreeing with **mejl**.

## Modifiers

Adverbs can come before a participle and explain how, when, or how often an action happened. The participle still agrees with the noun when it is a past participle:

- **ett nyligen publicerat inlägg — a recently published post**
- **en noggrant skriven text — a carefully written text**
- **ett ofta omtalat ämne — a frequently discussed topic**

Some participial adjectives can be compared with **mer** (more) and **mest** (most):

- **Den här lösningen är mer övertygande. — This solution is more convincing.**
- **Det var den mest uppskattade delen av kursen. — It was the most appreciated part of the course.**
