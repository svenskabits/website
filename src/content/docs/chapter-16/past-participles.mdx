A Swedish past participle is a verb form that often describes someone or something affected by an action. It can work like an adjective, or help form a passive sentence. It does not change the tense of a sentence by itself.

## Forms and agreement

Past participles usually agree with the noun they describe. Many have three forms: one for **en** nouns, one for **ett** nouns, and one for plural nouns. The plural form is also used with a definite noun.

| Form | Example with **stänga** (close) |
|---|---|
| **en** noun | **en stängd dörr** — a closed door |
| **ett** noun | **ett stängt fönster** — a closed window |
| plural | **stängda dörrar** — closed doors |
| definite | **den stängda dörren** — the closed door |

The same agreement appears after verbs such as **vara** (be) and **bli** (become/get):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Dörren är stängd.** | The door is closed. |
| **Fönstret är stängt.** | The window is closed. |
| **Dörrarna är stängda.** | The doors are closed. |

Many participles follow patterns like **-d, -t, -da** or **-ad, -at, -ade**. Strong and irregular verbs have other patterns, so it is useful to learn a participle together with its agreement forms.

| Infinitive | Past participle forms | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **öppna** (open) | **öppnad, öppnat, öppnade** | **ett öppnat paket** — an opened package |
| **köpa** (buy) | **köpt, köpt, köpta** | **en köpt biljett** — a purchased ticket |
| **läsa** (read) | **läst, läst, lästa** | **en läst artikel** — a read article |
| **skriva** (write) | **skriven, skrivet, skrivna** | **ett skrivet meddelande** — a written message |
| **göra** (do, make) | **gjord, gjort, gjorda** | **en gjord ändring** — a change that has been made |
| **ta** (take) | **tagen, taget, tagna** | **ett taget foto** — a photograph that has been taken |
| **se** (see) | **sedd, sett, sedda** | **en sedd film** — a film that has been seen |

The form can sometimes be difficult to predict from the infinitive. A dictionary may list only the common-gender form; check the neuter and plural forms too when you learn an unfamiliar participle.

## Describing a result or condition

As an adjective, a past participle often describes a state resulting from an action. It can come before a noun or after **vara**. The participle agrees with the noun in both positions:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **ett skrivet mejl** | a written email |
| **Mejlet är skrivet på svenska.** | The email is written in Swedish. |
| **en reparerad dator** | a repaired computer |
| **Datorerna är reparerade.** | The computers are repaired. |

Some participles describe a person's experience or feeling. These often end in **-ad/-at/-ade** or have another established form:

- **Jag är intresserad av nyheter. — I am interested in news.**
- **Barnen blev överraskade av beskedet. — The children were surprised by the news.**

Many participles also have established adjective meanings. Their meaning may be less literal than “affected by the action”: **en känd författare** means *a well-known author*, and **ett välkänt problem** means *a well-known problem*. In these expressions, **känd/känt/kända** agrees like an adjective.

## Forming passive sentences

With **bli** or **vara**, a past participle can describe what happens to a person or thing rather than who does it. The person doing the action can be added with **av** (by):

- **Artikeln blev skriven av en journalist. — The article was written by a journalist.**
- **Artikeln är skriven på enkel svenska. — The article is written in simple Swedish.**
- **Meddelandena blev skickade i går. — The messages were sent yesterday.**

**Bli** often presents an event or change; **vara** often describes a resulting state. Context matters, and the distinction is not always sharp:

- **Datorn blev reparerad i morse. — The computer was repaired this morning.**
- **Datorn är reparerad nu. — The computer is repaired now.**

The participle agrees with the thing affected: **artikeln är skriven**, **mejlet är skrivet**, **mejlen är skrivna**. Swedish also commonly uses a passive verb ending in **-s**, as in **Artikeln skrevs i går. — The article was written yesterday.** This is a different passive form and does not use a past participle.

## Past participle or supine?

Do not confuse the past participle with the **supine**, the verb form used after **har** or **hade** to make perfect tenses. The forms may look alike, but they have different jobs. The supine does not agree with a noun.

| Construction | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect tense: **har + supine** | **Jag har stängt dörren.** | I have closed the door. |
| Pluperfect: **hade + supine** | **Jag hade stängt dörren.** | I had closed the door. |
| Participle describing the door | **Dörren är stängd.** | The door is closed. |

Compare the forms with **skriva**:

- **Hon har skrivit ett mejl. — She has written an email.** (**skrivit** is the supine.)
- **Mejlet är skrivet på svenska. — The email is written in Swedish.** (**skrivet** is the past participle, agreeing with **mejl**.)

In the perfect passive, Swedish can use **har blivit** followed by an agreeing past participle, or the more compact **-s** passive:

- **Brevet har blivit skickat. — The letter has been sent.**
- **Breven har blivit skickade. — The letters have been sent.**
- **Brevet har skickats. — The letter has been sent.**

## Word order and adverbs

When a participle comes before a noun, adverbs can describe how or when the action happened:

- **ett nyligen publicerat inlägg — a recently published post**
- **en noggrant skriven text — a carefully written text**
- **ett ofta omtalat ämne — a frequently discussed topic**

The participle still agrees with the noun: **ett noggrant skrivet mejl**, **noggrant skrivna mejl**. It is the main verb—not the participle—that carries tense in a sentence such as **Mejlet publicerades i går. — The email was published yesterday.**
