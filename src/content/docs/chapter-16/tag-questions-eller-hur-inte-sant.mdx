A **tag question** is a short question added to the end of a statement. It checks information, invites the listener to agree, or shows that the speaker expects agreement. In Swedish, common tags include **eller hur?** (right?), **inte sant?** (isn't that right?), and **visst?** (right?/surely?).

## The basic pattern

Keep the statement in its normal word order, then add a comma and a tag. Unlike English, Swedish usually does not form a tag by repeating an auxiliary verb and changing the pronoun. The same tag can follow either a positive or a negative statement.

| Statement + tag | English |
|---|---|
| **Du bor i Malmö, eller hur?** | You live in Malmö, right? |
| **Du bor inte i Malmö, eller hur?** | You don't live in Malmö, right? |
| **Hon har redan ringt, inte sant?** | She's already called, hasn't she? |
| **Mötet börjar klockan nio, visst?** | The meeting starts at nine, right? |

The tag does not change to match the subject or verb. For example, **eller hur?** (right?) works after **du** (you), **hon** (she), and **de** (they), and after present, past, or perfect-tense statements.

## Choosing a tag

### *Eller hur?*

**Eller hur?** is a common, flexible way to check or invite confirmation. It can follow both positive and negative statements:

- **Du såg nyheten i morse, eller hur? — You saw the news this morning, right?**
- **Du har inte träffat honom förut, eller hur? — You haven't met him before, right?**

In casual conversation, speakers sometimes shorten it to **eller?** (right?/or?): **Vi ses efter jobbet, eller?** means *We'll see each other after work, right?* This shorter form is informal.

### *Inte sant?*

**Inte sant?** literally means *not true?* and asks whether the statement is true. It is often more formal or deliberate than **eller hur?**, though it is also used in speech:

- **Det var en ovanlig upplevelse, inte sant? — That was an unusual experience, wasn't it?**
- **Du har inte ändrat lösenordet, inte sant? — You haven't changed the password, have you?**

### *Visst?* and *väl*

**Visst?** is a short way to ask for confirmation. Depending on the context and intonation, it can suggest that the speaker expects the answer to be yes:

- **Du kommer på fredag, visst? — You're coming on Friday, right?**

**Väl** is usually placed inside the statement rather than added as a separate tag. It often means that the speaker assumes something is true but wants confirmation:

- **Du kommer väl på fredag? — You're coming on Friday, aren't you? / I assume you're coming on Friday?**
- **Det går väl att boka biljetter på nätet? — You can book tickets online, can't you?**

The exact force of **visst?** and **väl** depends on the situation and tone. They are not always interchangeable with **eller hur?**.

## Meaning and intonation

A tag can be a genuine request for confirmation, or it can invite agreement with something the speaker already believes. Intonation and context help show which meaning is intended. A more questioning rise can make the speaker sound less certain; a falling or less questioning tone can make the tag sound more like an invitation to agree. This is a tendency, not a fixed rule.

Compare:

- **Du känner henne, eller hur? — You know her, right?** (The speaker may be checking.)
- **Det var en fantastisk konsert, eller hur? — That was a fantastic concert, wasn't it?** (The speaker may be inviting agreement.)

In writing, use a question mark after the tag. In speech, the tag can make a statement sound friendlier or more conversational, but it can also sound as if the speaker is pressing for agreement. Use it when it fits the relationship and situation.

## Answering a tag question

Answer according to what is true. With a negative statement, **nej** confirms the negative, while **jo** contradicts it. Swedish uses **jo** to answer positively when the question or statement is negative.

| Question | Answer | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Du har sett filmen, eller hur?** | **Ja, det har jag.** | You've seen the film, right? — Yes, I have. |
| **Du har sett filmen, eller hur?** | **Nej, det har jag inte.** | You've seen the film, right? — No, I haven't. |
| **Du har inte sett filmen, eller hur?** | **Nej, det har jag inte.** | You haven't seen the film, right? — No, I haven't. |
| **Du har inte sett filmen, eller hur?** | **Jo, det har jag.** | You haven't seen the film, right? — Yes, I have. |

The short answers **ja**, **nej**, and **jo** can be followed by a fuller reply to make the meaning clear, especially after a negative statement.

## Do not translate English tags word for word

English changes the tag to match the statement: *She is here, isn't she?* Swedish normally uses an invariant tag instead:

- **Hon är här, eller hur? — She's here, right?**
- **Hon är här, inte sant? — She's here, isn't she?**

Do not mechanically copy the English auxiliary-and-pronoun pattern. Use a natural Swedish tag and let the context and intonation carry the meaning.
