The Swedish question word **vems** means **whose**. Use it to ask who owns or is connected to a person or thing. **Vems** has the same form with singular en-words, singular ett-words, and plural nouns:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **vems bok** | whose book |
| **vems hus** | whose house |
| **vems böcker** | whose books |

The noun after **vems** does not change the question word.

## Question forms

When **vems + noun** asks about the object of an action, put the finite verb before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vems bok läser du?** | Whose book are you reading? |
| **Vems jacka har hon på sig?** | Whose jacket is she wearing? |

When **vems + noun** is the subject—the person or thing doing the action—the phrase is followed by the verb, with no other subject after it:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vems hund skäller?** | Whose dog is barking? |
| **Vems barn går i den här skolan?** | Whose child goes to this school? |

You can also use **vems** without a noun, often with **är**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vems är den här boken?** | Whose book is this? |
| **Vems är den?** | Whose is it? |

## Short answers

Answer with the owner's name or with a possessive word:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vems cykel är det? – Det är Leas cykel.** | Whose bicycle is it? – It is Lea's bicycle. |
| **Vems bok är det? – Den är min.** | Whose book is it? – It is mine. |
| **Vems hus är det? – Det är vårt.** | Whose house is it? – It is ours. |

## Vems or vem?

**Vem** means **who** and asks which person: **Vem läser boken?** (Who is reading the book?) **Vems** means **whose** and asks who owns something: **Vems bok läser du?** (Whose book are you reading?)

Remember: **vems** does not change for the noun's en/ett group or number. In a direct question, the verb follows the question phrase; the subject comes after the verb unless **vems + noun** is itself the subject.
