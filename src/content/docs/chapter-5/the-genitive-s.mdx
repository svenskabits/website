Swedish often shows possession or a close relationship by adding **-s** to the possessor. The possessor comes first, followed by the thing connected to it:

**owner + -s + thing**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Annas bok** | Anna's book |
| **barnets rum** | the child's room |
| **Sveriges huvudstad** | Sweden's capital |
| **dagens nyheter** | today's news |

The genitive can show ownership, family or other relationships, and association. It does not always translate into English with an apostrophe-s.

## Add -s to names and nouns

For most names and nouns, attach **-s** directly to the end of the possessor. Keep any ending the noun already has:

| Possessor | Genitive phrase | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Anna** | **Annas hund** | Anna's dog |
| **pojken** | **pojkens cykel** | the boy's bicycle |
| **barnet** | **barnets rum** | the child's room |
| **eleverna** | **elevernas frågor** | the students' questions |

If the possessor is a plural noun, add the genitive **-s** after its plural ending: **lärare** (teachers) becomes **lärares** (teachers'), and **lärarna** (the teachers) becomes **lärarnas** (the teachers').

Names that end in a vowel normally take **-s** too: **Marias katt** (Maria's cat), **Oskars rum** (Oskar's room). Swedish does not normally use an apostrophe before the genitive ending: write **Annas bok**, not *Anna's bok*.

## Names and words ending in s, x, or z

If a name already ends in **s**, **x**, or **z**, it normally stays unchanged in the genitive. Do not add another **-s**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Lars bok** | Lars's book |
| **Max cykel** | Max's bicycle |
| **Beatriz hund** | Beatriz's dog |

Swedish may use an apostrophe after such a name as a rare aid to clarity, but it is not followed by another **s**. In ordinary writing, use the unchanged name, as in **Lars bok**. Do not copy the English apostrophe-s spelling.

## Compounds and longer names or phrases

Add **-s** to the end of a compound written as one word:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **barnvagnens hjul** | the stroller's wheels |
| **sommarstugans dörr** | the summer cottage's door |

For a multiword name or possessor phrase, the genitive **-s** normally goes at the end of the whole phrase:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Astrid Lindgrens böcker** | Astrid Lindgren's books |
| **mannen i den blå jackans bil** | the car belonging to the man in the blue jacket |

In the second example, **mannen i den blå jackan** (the man in the blue jacket) is the possessor phrase, so **-s** attaches to its final word, **jackan**. With a long phrase, it may be clearer to rephrase: **bilen som tillhör mannen i den blå jackan** (the car that belongs to the man in the blue jacket).

## The noun after the genitive

The genitive phrase usually identifies which thing you mean, so the following noun normally has no definite ending. If an adjective comes before that noun, it usually takes its definite form:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Annas bok** | Anna's book |
| **Annas nya bok** | Anna's new book |
| **husets röda dörr** | the house's red door |

Say **Annas bok**, not *Annas boken*. In **Annas nya bok** (Anna's new book), the adjective **nya** has its definite form, but **bok** has no definite ending.

Remember: put the possessor first and add **-s** directly to it. Most names, nouns, and compounds take the ending; names ending in **s**, **x**, or **z** usually take no extra **-s**.
