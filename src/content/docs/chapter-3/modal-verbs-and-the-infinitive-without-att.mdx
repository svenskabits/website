Swedish modal verbs such as **kan** (can), **vill** (want to), and **måste** (must/have to) are followed directly by another verb in the infinitive. Do not put **att** (to) between the modal and that infinitive:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag kan simma.** | I can swim. |
| **Hon vill läsa.** | She wants to read. |
| **Vi måste gå nu.** | We have to go now. |

The modal is the finite verb: it carries the sentence's tense and comes in the usual verb position. The following infinitive does not change for the subject. These modal forms stay the same with different subjects: **jag kan** (I can), **du kan** (you can), **de kan** (they can).

## More than one infinitive

You can use a modal verb before another modal or infinitive verb. Only the first modal is finite; the verbs after it remain infinitives, with no **att**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag vill kunna simma.** | I want to be able to swim. |
| **Du måste kunna visa biljetten.** | You must be able to show the ticket. |
| **Vi kan börja äta.** | We can start eating. |

In **Jag vill kunna simma** (I want to be able to swim), **vill** is the finite verb, while **kunna** and **simma** are infinitives. The same pattern can continue through a verb chain.

## Negatives and questions

Put **inte** (not) after the first, finite modal and before the infinitive or infinitives:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag kan inte komma.** | I cannot come. |
| **Hon vill inte äta.** | She does not want to eat. |
| **Vi måste inte gå än.** | We do not have to leave yet. |
| **Du kan inte börja arbeta nu.** | You cannot start working now. |

With **måste**, **måste inte** usually means that something is not necessary. To say that something is forbidden, use **får inte** (must not/not be allowed to): **Du får inte röka här.** (You must not smoke here.)

In a yes-or-no question, put the first modal before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Kan du hjälpa mig?** | Can you help me? |
| **Vill ni äta nu?** | Do you want to eat now? |
| **Måste jag vänta?** | Do I have to wait? |

With a question word, put the question word first, then the modal, then the subject: **Vad kan vi göra?** (What can we do?)

## When att is used

The bare-infinitive pattern belongs to modal verbs; it is not a rule that removes **att** everywhere. Some other verbs and expressions are followed by **att + infinitive**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag tycker om att läsa.** | I like reading. |
| **Hon hoppas att få ett jobb.** | She hopes to get a job. |
| **Det är bra att vila.** | It is good to rest. |

The phrase **för att + infinitive** can express a purpose:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag går till affären för att köpa mjölk.** | I am going to the shop to buy milk. |

Sometimes **att** means **that** and introduces a whole clause with its own subject. It is not an infinitive marker in this use: **Jag vill att du ska komma.** (I want you to come.) The clause **du ska komma** (you are going to come) has its own subject and finite verb.

There is also a different construction, **vara tvungen att** (to be obliged to), which requires **att**: **Jag är tvungen att gå.** (I have to leave.) Here the infinitive follows **tvungen**, not a modal verb.

Remember the contrast: **Jag vill gå.** (I want to go.) uses a modal followed by a bare infinitive, while **Jag tycker om att gå.** (I like walking.) uses a verb expression followed by **att + infinitive**. Learn which pattern belongs to each expression.
