**Kan** is the present form of **kunna**. It is a modal verb, used to talk about ability, possibility, and—in questions—permission or requests. Its form is the same with every subject.

| Form | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **kunna** | to be able to |
| Present | **kan** | can, am/is/are able to |
| Past | **kunde** | could, was/were able to |
| Supine | **kunnat** | been able to (with **har**) |

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag kan simma.** | I can swim. |
| **Du kan simma.** | You can swim. |
| **Hon kan simma.** | She can swim. |

## Ability

Use **kan** to say that someone knows how to do something or is able to do it. Follow **kan** with another verb in the infinitive, without **att**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Han kan laga mat.** | He can cook. |
| **Vi kan läsa svenska.** | We can read Swedish. |
| **Kan du hjälpa mig?** | Can you help me? |

The last question may ask about ability, but it is also a common polite request.

## Possibility and permission

**Kan** can describe something that is possible:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det kan regna i kväll.** | It may rain this evening. |
| **Du kan ta bussen till centrum.** | You can take the bus to the city centre. |

In a question, **kan jag/vi...?** often asks permission or makes a polite request:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Kan jag sitta här?** | Can I sit here? / May I sit here? |
| **Kan vi öppna fönstret?** | Can we open the window? / May we open the window? |

**Får jag...?** (May I...? / Am I allowed to...?) asks more explicitly whether something is permitted. For example, **Får jag använda din telefon?** (May I use your phone?)

## Negatives and questions

Put **inte** (not) after **kan** and before the infinitive:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag kan inte komma i dag.** | I cannot come today. |
| **Hon kan inte köra bil.** | She cannot drive a car. |

For a yes-or-no question, put **kan** before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Kan du vänta?** | Can you wait? |
| **Kan de komma i morgon?** | Can they come tomorrow? |

With a question word, put the question word first, then **kan**, then the subject: **Vad kan jag göra?** (What can I do?)

## Talking about the past

Use **kunde** for past ability or possibility. It is followed by an infinitive without **att**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **När jag var liten kunde jag cykla.** | When I was little, I could ride a bike. |
| **Vi kunde inte hitta hotellet.** | We could not find the hotel. |

Use **har kunnat** for “have/has been able to.” The main verb after it is still an infinitive: **Hon har kunnat läsa sedan hon var fem år.** (She has been able to read since she was five.)

Remember: use **kan + infinitive** without **att**. The context tells you whether **kan** means ability, possibility, or a polite request for permission.
