**Vill** is the present form of **vilja** (to want). It expresses a desire or intention. It is a modal verb, and its present form stays the same with every subject.

| Subject | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| **jag** (I) | **Jag vill vila.** | I want to rest. |
| **du** (you) | **Du vill vila.** | You want to rest. |
| **hon** (she) | **Hon vill vila.** | She wants to rest. |
| **de** (they) | **De vill vila.** | They want to rest. |

## Vill + infinitive

Use **vill** followed directly by another verb in the infinitive, without **att** (to):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag vill lära mig svenska.** | I want to learn Swedish. |
| **Han vill köpa en cykel.** | He wants to buy a bicycle. |
| **Vi vill äta nu.** | We want to eat now. |

The same pattern works with **ha** (to have): **Vill du ha kaffe?** (Would you like some coffee?) It also works when the next verb is another modal verb: **Jag vill kunna tala svenska.** (I want to be able to speak Swedish.)

**Vill** expresses what someone wants or intends; it does not guarantee that the action will happen.

## Questions and negatives

In a yes-or-no question, put **vill** before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vill du följa med?** | Would you like to come along? |
| **Vill ni se filmen?** | Do you want to see the film? |

With a question word, put the question word first, then **vill**, then the subject: **Vad vill du göra?** (What do you want to do?)

Put **inte** (not) after **vill** and before the infinitive:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag vill inte gå hem.** | I do not want to go home. |
| **De vill inte vänta.** | They do not want to wait. |
| **Vill du inte följa med?** | Don't you want to come along? |

## Present, past, and supine forms

The main forms of **vilja** are irregular and do not change to match the subject:

| Form | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **vilja** | to want |
| Present | **vill** | want/wants |
| Past (preterite) | **ville** | wanted |
| Supine | **velat** | wanted (used with **har**) |

Use **ville** to talk about wanting something in the past. It is followed by an infinitive without **att**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **När jag var barn ville jag bli lärare.** | When I was a child, I wanted to become a teacher. |
| **Ville du följa med?** | Did you want to come along? |

Use **har velat** for “have/has wanted”: **Jag har alltid velat resa till Japan.** (I have always wanted to travel to Japan.)

## When att follows vill

Normally, do not put **att** between **vill** and an infinitive: say **Jag vill läsa.** (I want to read.), not *Jag vill att läsa.*

However, **att** can introduce a new clause with its own subject. In **Jag vill att du ska läsa.** (I want you to read.), **att** means *that*, and **du ska läsa** (you will read) is a clause—not an infinitive directly following **vill**.

Remember: use **vill + infinitive** without **att** when the person who wants something is also the person doing the action. Use **ville** for past wanting, and put **inte** after the modal verb.
