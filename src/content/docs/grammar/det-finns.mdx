Use **det finns** to say that something exists or is present somewhere. It usually introduces a new or unspecified person or thing. English translates it as **there is** or **there are**.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det finns en soffa i vardagsrummet.** | There is a sofa in the living room. |
| **Det finns ett bord i köket.** | There is a table in the kitchen. |
| **Det finns två stolar vid bordet.** | There are two chairs by the table. |

The verb **finns** stays the same whether the noun is singular or plural. English changes *is* to *are*, but Swedish still uses **finns**: **Det finns en stol.** (There is a chair.) **Det finns fyra stolar.** (There are four chairs.)

## Forms of finnas

The main forms of **finnas** are:

| Form | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **finnas** | to exist, to be found |
| Present | **finns** | exists, is/are present |
| Past | **fanns** | existed, was/were present |
| Supine | **funnits** | existed (used with **har**) |

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det fanns en liten butik här.** | There was a small shop here. |
| **Det har funnits många små butiker här.** | There have been many small shops here. |

## Negatives and questions

To say that none are present, use **ingen**, **inget**, or **inga** (no/not any) with the noun:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det finns ingen hiss i huset.** | There is no lift in the building. |
| **Det finns inget kaffe kvar.** | There is no coffee left. |
| **Det finns inga lediga rum i kväll.** | There are no rooms available tonight. |

In a yes-or-no question, put **finns** before **det**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Finns det en bank i närheten?** | Is there a bank nearby? |
| **Finns det några frågor?** | Are there any questions? |
| **Fanns det en restaurang här förut?** | Was there a restaurant here before? |

With a question word, put the question word first: **Vad finns det i lådan?** (What is there in the box?)

## Indefinite and definite nouns

After existential **det finns**, the noun is usually indefinite because you are introducing something not yet identified: a singular noun has **en** or **ett**, and an indefinite plural has no article. For a specific or already known thing, Swedish normally makes that noun the subject instead:

| Introducing something | Referring to a specific thing | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Det finns en bok på bordet.** | **Boken finns på bordet.** | There is a book on the table. / The book is on the table. |
| **Det finns en toalett här.** | **Toaletten finns här.** | There is a toilet here. / The toilet is here. |

In the second sentence of each pair, **boken** (the book) or **toaletten** (the toilet) is the subject. The verb **finns** then means that the known thing is located or can be found there. This use is related to, but different from, existential **det finns**.

Remember: use **det finns + indefinite noun** to introduce what is present. The form **finns** does not change for a plural noun; **fanns** refers to the past, and **har funnits** uses the supine.
