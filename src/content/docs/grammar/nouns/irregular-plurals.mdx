## When a plural is irregular

Many Swedish nouns form their plural with a familiar ending, but some common nouns change their vowel, use a different form, or keep the same form. These plurals are best learned together with the singular. The lesson on [regular plural patterns](/grammar/nouns/plural/) covers the more predictable endings.

| Singular | Indefinite plural | Definite plural | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **en bok** | **böcker** | **böckerna** | a book; books; the books |
| **en man** | **män** | **männen** | a man; men; the men |
| **en mus** | **möss** | **mössen** | a mouse; mice; the mice |
| **en fot** | **fötter** | **fötterna** | a foot; feet; the feet |
| **en tand** | **tänder** | **tänderna** | a tooth; teeth; the teeth |
| **en hand** | **händer** | **händerna** | a hand; hands; the hands |
| **en gås** | **gäss** | **gässen** | a goose; geese; the geese |
| **ett barn** | **barn** | **barnen** | a child; children; the children |
| **ett öga** | **ögon** | **ögonen** | an eye; eyes; the eyes |
| **ett öra** | **öron** | **öronen** | an ear; ears; the ears |

Several of these forms change the vowel: **a** can change to **ä** in *man – män* and *gås – gäss*, while **u** changes to **ö** in *mus – möss*. Other nouns combine a vowel change with an ending, as in *bok – böcker* and *fot – fötter*. The pattern is not fully predictable, so learn each plural with its noun.

Some plurals, such as **barn**, have no change in the indefinite form. The context or a number makes the plural meaning clear: **ett barn** means “a child,” while **tre barn** means “three children.” The definite plural is **barnen** (“the children”).

### Using the definite plural

To talk about specific things, use the definite plural in the table: **böckerna** means “the books,” and **tänderna** means “the teeth.” The definite ending attaches to the plural form, which may itself be irregular.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag läser två böcker.** — I’m reading two books.
2. **Männen väntar på bussen.** — The men are waiting for the bus.
3. **Det finns möss i källaren.** — There are mice in the basement.
4. **Mina fötter är kalla.** — My feet are cold.
5. **Borsta tänderna varje dag.** — Brush your teeth every day.
6. **Tvätta händerna före maten.** — Wash your hands before eating.
7. **Gässen simmar i sjön.** — The geese are swimming in the lake.
8. **De har tre barn.** — They have three children.
9. **Hon har blå ögon.** — She has blue eyes.
10. **Han har ont i öronen.** — His ears hurt.
