title: The Genitive -s
description: Learn how Swedish uses the genitive -s to show possession and other relationships between nouns.
---

## Showing possession and association

Swedish often shows who something belongs to—or what it is connected with—by putting that person, place, or thing first and adding **-s** to it. This form is called the **genitive**. It can express ownership, family relationships, parts, and other associations:

- **Eriks cykel** — Erik’s bicycle
- **Sveriges huvudstad** — Sweden’s capital
- **hundens namn** — the dog’s name

The genitive noun comes before the noun it relates to. Unlike English, ordinary Swedish spelling does not put an apostrophe before the genitive **-s**: write **Eriks cykel**, not *Erik’s cykel*.

## Adding the genitive ending

Add **-s** directly to the noun form that shows the person or thing being referred to. Keep any plural or definite ending already on that noun, then add the genitive **-s**:

| Noun form | Genitive phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Erik** | **Eriks cykel** | Erik’s bicycle |
| **pojken** (the boy) | **pojkens cykel** | the boy’s bicycle |
| **barnen** (the children) | **barnens rum** | the children’s room |

The genitive phrase normally makes the following noun definite, so do not add a definite ending to that noun as well: **hundens namn**, not *hundens namnet*.

## Names and nouns ending in s, x, or z

If a name or noun already ends in the letters **s**, **x**, or **z**, it normally takes no extra genitive **-s** in its basic form:

- **Lars bil** — Lars’s car
- **Max cykel** — Max’s bicycle

This is about spelling, not just pronunciation. For example, **Alice** ends in *e*, so its genitive is **Alices**: **Alices bok** (“Alice’s book”).

With an ordinary noun that ends in **s**, a genitive **-s** can still follow a definite ending: **huset** (“the house”) → **husets tak** (“the roof of the house”). If a phrase with a name ending in **s**, **x**, or **z** could be misunderstood, an apostrophe after the name may rarely be used for clarity, as in **Andreas’ cykel**. It is optional; do not use the English-style apostrophe plus **s**.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Det här är Annas bok.** — This is Anna’s book.
2. **Eriks cykel står i garaget.** — Erik’s bicycle is in the garage.
3. **Min systers lägenhet ligger nära stationen.** — My sister’s apartment is near the station.
4. **Hundens namn är Bamse.** — The dog’s name is Bamse.
5. **Barnens rum är ljust.** — The children’s room is bright.
6. **Husets tak behöver repareras.** — The roof of the house needs repairing.
7. **Sveriges flagga är blå och gul.** — Sweden’s flag is blue and yellow.
8. **Företagets adress står på hemsidan.** — The company’s address is on the website.
9. **Lars bil är parkerad utanför.** — Lars’s car is parked outside.
10. **Max hund älskar att springa.** — Max’s dog loves to run.
