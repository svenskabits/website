Swedish uses **i**, **på**, **om**, and **för … sedan** in several common time expressions. They do not match English prepositions one-to-one. The time phrase and its meaning determine which form to use.

## I for periods and durations

Use **i** with many names of periods and with a duration that tells how long something lasts. In the duration pattern, **i + length of time** often means *for* that long:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har bott här i tre år.** | I have lived here for three years. |
| **Hon arbetade i två timmar.** | She worked for two hours. |
| **Vi ska vara i Göteborg i en vecka.** | We will be in Gothenburg for a week. |

Use **i** with months, years, and many seasons and parts of the year:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Han är född i april.** | He was born in April. |
| **De flyttade hit år 2022.** | They moved here in 2022. |
| **Vi brukar åka skidor i januari.** | We usually go skiing in January. |
| **Jag vill resa i sommar.** | I want to travel this summer. |

With a season, the form can show whether you mean the current or coming season, or the previous one: **i sommar** usually means *this/coming summer*, while **i somras** means *last summer*. The same contrast applies to **i vår** (this/coming spring) and **i våras** (last spring), and to **i höst / i höstas** and **i vinter / i vintras**.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi ska flytta i höst.** | We are going to move this autumn. |
| **Vi flyttade i höstas.** | We moved last autumn. |

## På for days, times of day, and time taken

Use **på** with a named weekday when you mean a particular upcoming or current day. Compare this with the special past forms such as **i måndags** (on Monday, referring to a past Monday):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har en tid hos tandläkaren på tisdag.** | I have a dentist's appointment on Tuesday. |
| **Vi ses på fredag.** | See you on Friday. |
| **Jag träffade henne i tisdags.** | I met her on Tuesday. |

Use **på** in common expressions for parts of the day and recurring seasons. Some expressions use **i** instead, so learn these as phrases:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag brukar läsa på kvällen.** | I usually read in the evening. |
| **Vi åker till landet på sommaren.** | We go to the countryside in summer. |
| **Hon tränar på morgonen.** | She exercises in the morning. |
| **Det snöade i natt.** | It snowed last night. |
| **Jag ringde honom i morse.** | I called him this morning. |

With **på + a duration**, the phrase can say how long it takes to complete something, often with a focus on reaching a result. It often translates as *in* or *within*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hon läste boken på två dagar.** | She read the book in two days. |
| **Vi lagade middag på en halvtimme.** | We made dinner in half an hour. |

Compare **i två dagar**, which describes how long an activity or state lasts, with **på två dagar**, which describes the time taken to complete something:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi bodde på hotell i två dagar.** | We stayed at a hotel for two days. |
| **Vi målade hela rummet på två dagar.** | We painted the whole room in two days. |

## Om for time from now

Use **om + a period of time** for a point that is that far in the future. It usually translates as *in* or *... from now*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Bussen kommer om tio minuter.** | The bus is coming in ten minutes. |
| **Vi åker om en vecka.** | We are leaving in a week. |
| **Hon börjar sitt nya jobb om två månader.** | She starts her new job in two months. |

Swedish commonly uses the present tense for a future event when the time expression makes the future meaning clear: **Bussen kommer om tio minuter** means the bus *will come* in ten minutes. A future form with **ska** is also possible when it fits the meaning, as in **Vi ska åka om en vecka.** (We are going to leave in a week.)

## För … sedan for time in the past

Use **för + a period of time + sedan** to say how long ago something happened. **Sedan** comes after the time period:

**för + length of time + sedan**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag flyttade hit för tre år sedan.** | I moved here three years ago. |
| **Hon ringde för en timme sedan.** | She called an hour ago. |
| **De kom hem för fem minuter sedan.** | They came home five minutes ago. |
| **Det hände för länge sedan.** | It happened a long time ago. |

The verb normally describes a past event, so use the past tense in these examples: **för två dagar sedan** means *two days ago*, not *in two days*.

## Compare the three directions in time

The same length of time can point to a duration, a future point, or a past point. Compare:

| Swedish | English | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Jag stannar i en vecka.** | I am staying for a week. | The stay lasts one week. |
| **Jag åker om en vecka.** | I am leaving in a week. | The departure is one week from now. |
| **Jag åkte för en vecka sedan.** | I left a week ago. | The departure was one week ago. |

The word order matters: **om en vecka** points forward from now; **för en vecka sedan** points backward; **i en vecka** gives a duration.
