Swedish adjectives change form to match the noun they describe. The form depends mainly on whether the noun is singular en-word, singular ett-word, or plural. The examples below use **stor** (big) and **röd** (red):

| Noun | Adjective form | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singular en-word | Basic form | **en stor bil** | a big car |
| Singular ett-word | Usually add **-t** | **ett stort hus** | a big house |
| Plural | Usually add **-a** | **stora bilar** | big cars |

For another adjective, **röd** (red), the forms are **en röd bil** (a red car), **ett rött hus** (a red house), and **röda bilar** (red cars). The spelling may change when an ending is added, so learn common forms with the adjective.

## Before an indefinite noun

An adjective directly before an indefinite singular noun uses its basic form with an en-word and usually an **-t** form with an ett-word. Plural adjectives usually end in **-a**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en ny cykel** | a new bicycle |
| **ett nytt bord** | a new table |
| **nya cyklar** | new bicycles |
| **nya bord** | new tables |

The adjective agrees with the noun, not with the person describing it. For example, **ett nytt bord** (a new table) uses **nytt** because **bord** is an ett-word.

## Before a definite noun

When an adjective comes before a definite noun, Swedish normally uses **den** with a singular en-word, **det** with a singular ett-word, and **de** with a plural noun. The adjective usually takes its definite form, often ending in **-a**, and the noun also has its definite ending:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **den stora bilen** | the big car |
| **det stora huset** | the big house |
| **de stora bilarna** | the big cars |
| **de stora husen** | the big houses |

This pattern has two markers of definiteness: **den/det/de** and the definite ending on the noun. With a possessive word, the adjective also normally has the definite **-a** form, but **den/det/de** is not used: **min nya bil** (my new car), **mitt nya hus** (my new house), **mina nya bilar** (my new cars).

In some definite phrases referring to a male person, an adjective can end in **-e** instead of **-a**: **den unge mannen** (the young man). The **-a** form is also common: **den unga mannen** (the young man).

## After verbs such as är

An adjective after a linking verb such as **är** (is/are) is called *predicative*. It agrees with the subject in the same way as the adjective before an indefinite noun: basic form with a singular en-word, usually **-t** with an ett-word, and usually **-a** in the plural.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Bilen är stor.** | The car is big. |
| **Huset är stort.** | The house is big. |
| **Bilarna är stora.** | The cars are big. |
| **Koppen är röd.** | The cup is red. |
| **Glaset är rött.** | The glass is red. |
| **Kopparna är röda.** | The cups are red. |

The adjective does not take the definite **-a** form just because the subject is definite: **Huset är stort** (The house is big.), not *Huset är stora*.

## Irregular and unchanged adjectives

Some common adjectives have less predictable forms. **Liten** (small) changes to **litet** with an ett-word and **små** in the plural. Its definite forms are **lilla** and **små**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en liten katt** | a small cat |
| **ett litet hus** | a small house |
| **små katter** | small cats |
| **den lilla katten** | the small cat |
| **det lilla huset** | the small house |
| **de små katterna** | the small cats |
| **Katten är liten.** | The cat is small. |
| **Huset är litet.** | The house is small. |
| **Katterna är små.** | The cats are small. |

Some adjectives do not change with the noun. **Bra** (good) is one common example:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en bra bok** | a good book |
| **ett bra hus** | a good house |
| **bra böcker** | good books |
| **Boken är bra.** | The book is good. |
| **Huset är bra.** | The house is good. |

## Common mistakes

| Avoid | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *ett stor hus* | **ett stort hus** (a big house) | Add **-t** for this ett-word. |
| *två stor bilar* | **två stora bilar** (two big cars) | Use the plural adjective form. |
| *Huset är stora.* | **Huset är stort.** (The house is big.) | A predicative adjective agrees with the singular ett-word subject. |

Remember: use the basic adjective form with a singular en-word, usually **-t** with a singular ett-word, and usually **-a** in the plural. Definite nouns with an adjective normally use a definite adjective form, while predicative adjectives agree with the subject's number and noun group.
