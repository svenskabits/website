Many Swedish verbs are normally used with a particular preposition. The verb and preposition form a pattern that you learn together, because the choice often does not match the preposition used in English. The preposition introduces the person, thing, or idea connected to the verb.

| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| **vänta på** (wait for) | **Vi väntar på bussen.** | We are waiting for the bus. |
| **lyssna på** (listen to) | **Hon lyssnar på musik.** | She is listening to music. |
| **leta efter** (look for) | **Jag letar efter mina nycklar.** | I am looking for my keys. |
| **prata med** (talk to/with) | **Han pratade med sin chef.** | He spoke with his manager. |
| **prata om** (talk about) | **De pratade om semestern.** | They talked about the vacation. |
| **lita på** (trust) | **Du kan lita på mig.** | You can trust me. |
| **be om** (ask for) | **Hon bad om hjälp.** | She asked for help. |

## The preposition stays with the pattern

The verb changes for tense and person, but the preposition in the pattern usually stays the same. A different preposition can change the meaning or sound unnatural, so learn the verb together with its preposition and an example.

| Present | Past | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Jag väntar på tåget.** | **Jag väntade på tåget.** | I am waiting for the train. / I waited for the train. |
| **Hon tänker på sin familj.** | **Hon tänkte på sin familj.** | She is thinking about her family. / She thought about her family. |

The preposition comes before its object: a noun, a noun phrase, or a pronoun. After a preposition, use an object pronoun such as **mig**, **dig**, **honom**, **henne**, **oss**, **er**, or **dem**—not the subject forms **jag**, **du**, **han**, **hon**, **vi**, **ni**, or **de**.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hon väntar på honom.** | She is waiting for him. |
| **De pratar om oss.** | They are talking about us. |
| **Jag tänker ofta på dem.** | I often think about them. |

## Common patterns and changes in meaning

Some verbs can be followed by different prepositions. The preposition tells you how the person or thing relates to the action, and can change the meaning of the whole expression.

| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| **prata med** someone | **Jag pratade med läkaren.** | I spoke with the doctor. |
| **prata om** a topic | **Vi pratade om vårt nya jobb.** | We talked about our new job. |
| **fråga efter** someone or something you want to find | **Han frågade efter Anna i receptionen.** | He asked for Anna at reception. |
| **fråga om** information or a subject | **Hon frågade om arbetstiderna.** | She asked about the working hours. |
| **tänka på** someone or something | **Jag tänker på dig.** | I am thinking of you. |
| **tänka på att** do something | **Vi tänker på att flytta till Malmö.** | We are considering moving to Malmö. |

The same preposition can also be used with several verbs, but its meaning is not identical in every pattern. For example, **på** appears in **vänta på** (wait for), **lyssna på** (listen to), and **lita på** (trust). Learn each combination as a unit rather than trying to translate the preposition on its own.

## When the object is an action

When the object of a preposition is an action, Swedish often uses **att** and an infinitive. The preposition still comes before **att**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag ser fram emot att träffa dig.** | I am looking forward to meeting you. |
| **Hon tänker på att byta jobb.** | She is thinking about changing jobs. |
| **De insisterade på att få ett skriftligt svar.** | They insisted on getting a written answer. |

Do not add a preposition just because English has one. For instance, English *hope for something* corresponds to **hoppas på något**, but when the following part is a full clause, Swedish normally uses **hoppas att** without **på**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag hoppas på ett snabbt svar.** | I hope for a quick reply. |
| **Jag hoppas att du svarar snart.** | I hope that you reply soon. |

## Prepositions at the end of questions and relative clauses

In Swedish, a preposition often stays at the end when its object is asked about or has already been mentioned. This is normal in everyday Swedish; do not remove the preposition that belongs to the verb pattern.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vad väntar du på?** | What are you waiting for? |
| **Vem pratade du med?** | Who did you talk to? |
| **Det är personen som jag arbetade med förra året.** | That is the person I worked with last year. |
| **Boken som hon pratade om är intressant.** | The book she talked about is interesting. |

## Do not confuse a preposition with a verb particle

Some verbs are followed by a short word that is part of the verb itself, rather than a preposition introducing an object. This word is called a **particle**. The distinction can affect the meaning:

| Verb pattern | Meaning | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **slå på** + an object | switch on | **Kan du slå på lampan?** | Can you turn on the light? |
| **slå på** + a surface | hit or knock on | **Någon slog på dörren.** | Someone knocked on the door. |
| **tänka på** + an object | think about | **Tänk på barnen.** | Think about the children. |

The meaning and role of the following word help you identify the pattern. With a preposition, it introduces something related to the verb; with a particle, it combines with the verb to create a meaning of its own. When in doubt, check the meaning and learn the full expression with an example.

## Word order with negation

In a main clause, **inte** usually comes after the finite verb and before the preposition phrase:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag väntar inte på ett svar längre.** | I am no longer waiting for an answer. |
| **Hon tänker inte på jobbet ikväll.** | She is not thinking about work tonight. |

The preposition phrase stays together: the preposition is still followed by its object, even when the sentence is negative or the object is a question word.
