When you explain why something happens and what follows from it, you can connect ideas with clauses, linking words, and prepositional phrases. The choice of form affects both the meaning and the word order.

## Giving a reason with a subordinate clause

**Eftersom**, **därför att**, and **då** can introduce the cause of something. They are followed by a subordinate clause, which has its own subject and verb. In a subordinate clause, **inte** normally comes before the finite verb:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Flera arter hotas eftersom deras livsmiljöer försvinner. | Several species are threatened because their habitats are disappearing. |
| Vägen stängdes därför att ett träd hade fallit över den. | The road was closed because a tree had fallen across it. |
| Då marken är så torr finns det en stor brandrisk. | Since the ground is so dry, there is a high risk of fire. |
| Vi använder mindre el eftersom vi inte behöver ha lamporna tända hela dagen. | We use less electricity because we don’t need to keep the lights on all day. |

**Eftersom** and **därför att** are common ways to state a reason. **Då** can also mean “since” or “as” when the reason is presented as background information; it can also mean “then” in other contexts, so the sentence helps determine its meaning.

The reason clause can come before or after the main clause. When it comes first, the verb in the main clause comes before its subject. This is called inversion:

| Reason first | Main clause first |
| --- | --- |
| Eftersom utsläppen har ökat behöver kommunen agera. — Because emissions have increased, the municipality needs to act. | Kommunen behöver agera eftersom utsläppen har ökat. — The municipality needs to act because emissions have increased. |
| Då sjön har frusit kan vi inte åka båt. — Since the lake has frozen, we can’t go by boat. | Vi kan inte åka båt då sjön har frusit. — We can’t go by boat since the lake has frozen. |

In the first column, **behöver** and **kan** come before the subjects **kommunen** and **vi** because the main clause follows an opening subordinate clause.

## Giving a reason with a noun phrase

To name a cause rather than state it as a full clause, use a phrase such as **på grund av** (“because of”) or **till följd av** (“as a result of; owing to”). These are followed by a noun phrase:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Många växter dog på grund av den långa torkan. | Many plants died because of the long drought. |
| Till följd av stormen blev flera vägar avstängda. | As a result of the storm, several roads were closed. |

**På grund av att** can introduce a whole clause instead:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Matchen ställdes in på grund av att planen var översvämmad. | The match was cancelled because the field was flooded. |

**Tack vare** means “thanks to” and presents a cause as helpful or beneficial:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Tack vare de nya våtmarkerna har fler fåglar hittat en livsmiljö här. | Thanks to the new wetlands, more birds have found a habitat here. |

It is also possible to use **tack vare att** before a clause: **Tack vare att kommunen planterade fler träd blev luften svalare.** — “Thanks to the fact that the municipality planted more trees, the air became cooler.”

## Showing a consequence

Use **så** to connect a cause to a consequence in a single sentence. The clause after **så** normally has ordinary subject–verb order:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Det har regnat mycket, så älven har stigit. | It has rained a lot, so the river has risen. |
| Priserna på el är höga, så många hushåll försöker spara energi. | Electricity prices are high, so many households are trying to save energy. |

**Därför** means “therefore” or “for that reason.” It is an adverb, not the same as **därför att** (“because”). If **därför** begins a main clause, the verb comes before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Älven har stigit. Därför har kommunen utfärdat en varning. | The river has risen. Therefore, the municipality has issued a warning. |
| Många arter saknar en trygg livsmiljö. Därför måste områdena skyddas. | Many species lack a safe habitat. For that reason, the areas must be protected. |

Compare the two forms:

| Cause | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Vi stannade hemma därför att vägarna var hala. — We stayed home because the roads were slippery. | Vägarna var hala, så vi stannade hemma. — The roads were slippery, so we stayed home. |
| Eftersom marken var frusen gick det inte att plantera. — Because the ground was frozen, it wasn’t possible to plant. | Marken var frusen. Därför gick det inte att plantera. — The ground was frozen. Therefore, it wasn’t possible to plant. |

**Alltså** (“therefore; so”) can also draw a conclusion or state a result. It often sounds like the speaker is making an inference:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Temperaturen har sjunkit under noll, alltså är vattnet fruset. | The temperature has fallen below zero, so the water is frozen. |

When a consequence adverb such as **därför** or **alltså** comes first in a main clause, use inversion: **Därför behöver vi agera**, not **Därför vi behöver agera**.

## Describing cause and effect with verbs

Verbs can make the relationship between a cause and a result especially clear:

- **bero på** means “be due to” or “depend on.” It names the cause.
- **leda till** means “lead to” or “result in.” It names the consequence.
- **göra att** means “cause something to happen” or “make it so that.”

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Förseningen berodde på ett tekniskt problem. | The delay was due to a technical problem. |
| Långvarig torka kan leda till vattenbrist. | Prolonged drought can lead to water shortages. |
| De högre temperaturerna gör att snön smälter tidigare. | The higher temperatures cause the snow to melt earlier. |
| Bristen på pollinerande insekter har lett till mindre skördar. | The lack of pollinating insects has led to smaller harvests. |

In **bero på** and **leda till**, the prepositions are part of the expressions: **beror på** introduces the cause, while **leder till** introduces the outcome.

## Naming a result with a clause or noun

**Vilket** can refer to the whole situation in the preceding clause and add its consequence. The verb in this relative clause follows subordinate-clause word order:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Isen smälte tidigare än vanligt, vilket påverkade djurens möjligheter att hitta mat. | The ice melted earlier than usual, which affected the animals’ ability to find food. |
| Flera träd har avverkats, vilket har förändrat landskapet. | Several trees have been cut down, which has changed the landscape. |

You can also make the result into a noun phrase, often with **resultatet** (“the result”) or **följden** (“the consequence”):

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Resultatet blev att fler familjer valde att åka kollektivt. | The result was that more families chose to use public transport. |
| Följden av beslutet blev högre kostnader för kommunen. | The consequence of the decision was higher costs for the municipality. |

## Cause, result, and purpose are different

A cause explains why something happened. A result states what happened because of it. A purpose explains what someone intends to achieve. Compare:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Vi stängde av vattnet eftersom ett rör hade gått sönder. | We turned off the water because a pipe had broken. |
| Ett rör hade gått sönder, så vi stängde av vattnet. | A pipe had broken, so we turned off the water. |
| Vi stängde av vattnet för att reparera röret. | We turned off the water in order to repair the pipe. |

**För att** followed by an infinitive commonly expresses purpose (“in order to”). A full clause introduced by **så att** can express an intended purpose or an actual result, depending on context. The surrounding meaning tells you whether it answers “What was the aim?” or “What happened as a result?”
