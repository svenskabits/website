## Two groups of Swedish nouns

Every Swedish noun belongs to one of two grammatical-gender groups: **en-words** and **ett-words**. The labels come from the word used with a singular, indefinite noun:

- **en** stol — a chair
- **ett** bord — a table

The article belongs with the noun; it is not chosen according to whether the thing is male or female. For people and animals, grammatical gender still depends on the Swedish noun, not necessarily on the real-world sex of the person or animal.

### Learn the article with each noun

There is no dependable rule that tells you whether a noun takes **en** or **ett**. Learn the noun and its article together, for example **en bil** (“a car”) and **ett hus** (“a house”). Most Swedish nouns are en-words, but that is only a useful tendency—not a way to predict every noun.

The choice also appears in other forms. In the definite singular, an en-word commonly takes **-en** and an ett-word commonly takes **-et**:

- **en katt** → **katten** — a cat → the cat
- **ett äpple** → **äpplet** — an apple → the apple

Adjectives also change to agree: **en röd bil** (“a red car”) but **ett rött hus** (“a red house”). The noun’s group determines these forms, so keep the article attached when you learn new vocabulary.

### Common confusion

English **a** and **an** are chosen by sound; they do not tell you which Swedish article to use. For example, **ett äpple** starts with a vowel sound, but **en** is used with **bil** even though *car* also starts with a consonant. Choose **en** or **ett** based on the Swedish noun, not its English translation.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag har en hund.** — I have a dog.
2. **Hon läser en bok.** — She is reading a book.
3. **Vi bor i ett hus.** — We live in a house.
4. **Han köper ett äpple.** — He is buying an apple.
5. **Det står en stol vid fönstret.** — There is a chair by the window.
6. **Jag behöver ett nytt pass.** — I need a new passport.
7. **De har en liten trädgård.** — They have a small garden.
8. **Barnet äter ett päron.** — The child is eating a pear.
9. **Kan du öppna en dörr?** — Can you open a door?
10. **Vi väntar på ett tåg.** — We are waiting for a train.
