**Ha** means **have**. Use it to talk about possession, relationships, and many common states or conditions. The verb is irregular, but its forms are the same with every subject.

| Form | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **ha** | to have |
| Present | **har** | have/has |
| Past | **hade** | had |
| Supine | **haft** | had (used with **har**) |

## Possession and common expressions

Use **har** to say that someone owns, has, or keeps something, or has a family relationship:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har en cykel.** | I have a bicycle. |
| **Hon har två bröder.** | She has two brothers. |
| **Vi har ett hus vid havet.** | We have a house by the sea. |
| **Jag hade en hund som barn.** | I had a dog as a child. |
| **Jag har haft den här cykeln i fem år.** | I have had this bicycle for five years. |

**Ha** also appears in common expressions for needs, time, and physical conditions:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Har du tid?** | Do you have time? |
| **Jag har en fråga.** | I have a question. |
| **Han har ont i huvudet.** | He has a headache. |
| **Vi har mycket att göra.** | We have a lot to do. |

English *have* does not always translate as **ha**. For age, Swedish uses **vara** (to be): **Jag är tjugo år.** (I am twenty years old.)

## Negatives and questions

In a statement, put **inte** (not) after the finite verb **har** or **hade**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har inte tid i dag.** | I do not have time today. |
| **Vi hade inte tid då.** | We did not have time then. |

For ordinary possession of a countable thing, Swedish commonly uses **ingen/inget/inga** (no/not any) instead of **inte + en/ett**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har ingen bil.** | I do not have a car. |
| **Hon har inget husdjur.** | She does not have a pet. |
| **De har inga barn.** | They do not have any children. |

To ask a yes-or-no question, put the finite verb before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Har du en syster?** | Do you have a sister? |
| **Hade ni tid?** | Did you have time? |
| **Har hon ett husdjur?** | Does she have a pet? |

With a question word, put the question word first, then the verb and subject: **Vad har du i väskan?** (What do you have in your bag?)

## Har as an auxiliary

**Har** is also used as an auxiliary verb to form the perfect. In this pattern, **har** comes before the supine form of another verb:

**har + supine**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har ätit.** | I have eaten. |
| **Hon har köpt en bok.** | She has bought a book. |
| **De har kommit hem.** | They have come home. |

In a negative sentence, **inte** comes after the auxiliary **har**, before the supine: **Jag har inte läst boken.** (I have not read the book.) In a yes-or-no question, put **har** before the subject: **Har du sett filmen?** (Have you seen the film?)

Compare **Jag har en bok.** (I have a book.), where **har** means possession, with **Jag har läst en bok.** (I have read a book.), where **har** is an auxiliary followed by the supine **läst**.

Remember: **ha, har, hade, haft** are the main forms. Use **har** for possession and common expressions, and **har + supine** for a past action or experience connected to the present.
