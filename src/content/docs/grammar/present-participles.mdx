A Swedish present participle is a verb form that often describes someone or something doing an action, or a quality associated with that action. It usually ends in **-ande** or **-ende**. Present participles are commonly used like adjectives, and they can also describe how someone moves or acts.

## Forming the present participle

For most verbs whose infinitive ends in **-a**, replace the final **-a** with **-ande**. For many other verbs, add **-ende** to the infinitive:

| Infinitive | Present participle | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **tala** (speak) | **talande** | **Ett talande exempel** — A telling example |
| **läsa** (read) | **läsande** | **en läsande student** — a student who is reading |
| **skriva** (write) | **skrivande** | **Hon är en skrivande person.** — She is a person who writes. |
| **växa** (grow) | **växande** | **en växande stad** — a growing city |
| **bo** (live) | **boende** | **de boende i huset** — the people living in the building |
| **le** (smile) | **leende** | **ett leende barn** — a smiling child |
| **gå** (walk, go) | **gående** | **gående patienter** — patients who are walking |

Some forms have a changed stem or a meaning that is not fully predictable from the verb. For example, **ge** (give) has the participle **givande**, which often means *rewarding* or *enriching*: **Det var en givande kurs. — It was a rewarding course.** Learn less predictable forms with their common meanings.

## Used like an adjective

Present participles can come before a noun or after a verb such as **vara** (be). They do not change for the noun's gender, number, or definiteness:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en spännande bok** | an exciting book |
| **ett spännande möte** | an exciting meeting |
| **spännande böcker** | exciting books |
| **Mötet var spännande.** | The meeting was exciting. |

The participle often describes an action in progress or something that causes an effect. Compare the person or thing doing the action with the effect it causes:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Ett barn skrattar.** | A child is laughing. |
| **ett skrattande barn** | a laughing child |
| **Filmen är spännande.** | The film is exciting. |
| **Jag blev överraskad av det överraskande beskedet.** | I was surprised by the surprising news. |

Some participles become established adjectives with meanings that are more specific than a literal description of an action. For example, **en krävande uppgift** means *a demanding task*, and **en fungerande lösning** means *a working solution*. Context determines the exact meaning.

When comparing these adjectives, **mer** (more) and **mest** (most) are common:

- **Den här förklaringen är mer övertygande. — This explanation is more convincing.**
- **Det var den mest spännande delen av filmen. — It was the most exciting part of the film.**

## Describing an action or movement

A present participle can describe what someone is doing while moving or acting. It stays in the same form, and it does not carry a tense of its own:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Barnen kom springande ut.** | The children came running out. |
| **Han gick visslande hem.** | He went home whistling. |
| **Hon stod väntande vid dörren.** | She stood waiting by the door. |

This use often appears with verbs of movement or position. It gives extra information about the subject's action; the main verb tells you the tense.

## Not the usual way to say “is doing”

Although the English name contains *present*, a Swedish present participle is not a present-tense verb form. Swedish normally uses the ordinary present tense to say that an action is happening now:

- **Jag läser just nu. — I am reading right now.**
- **Jag håller på att läsa en bok. — I am in the middle of reading a book.**

Use a participle to describe a noun or add information about an action, not as a direct equivalent of English **be + -ing**. For example, **en läsande person** means *a person who is reading* or *a person who reads*; it does not by itself mean *a person who is reading right now*.

## Participles used as nouns

Some present participles are also established as nouns. Their meanings may be conventional, so they do not always mean simply “someone doing the verb right now”:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **En sökande ringde i morse.** | An applicant called this morning. |
| **De sökande väntar utanför.** | The applicants are waiting outside. |
| **De boende har fått ett brev.** | The residents have received a letter. |

In **de sökande** and **de boende**, the participle names a group of people. **Ett boende** is also a common noun meaning *accommodation* or *a place to live*.
