Swedish **i** and **på** can both describe where someone or something is. They often correspond to English *in*, *on*, or *at*, but the choice depends on the place and the expression. The patterns below are useful starting points, not rules without exceptions; learn common place expressions as whole phrases.

## I: inside, and many place names

Use **i** for being inside a room, building, or container. It is also commonly used with city, country, and region names:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Barnen leker i rummet.** | The children are playing in the room. |
| **Nycklarna ligger i väskan.** | The keys are in the bag. |
| **Vi bor i Sverige.** | We live in Sweden. |
| **Hon arbetar i Stockholm.** | She works in Stockholm. |
| **De vandrar i norra Sverige.** | They are hiking in northern Sweden. |

The idea of being inside often fits **i**, but it is not the only factor in the choice. For example, people usually say **i bilen** (in the car), but **på bussen** (on/aboard the bus).

## På: surfaces and common places

Use **på** for something resting on a surface:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Boken ligger på bordet.** | The book is on the table. |
| **Tavlan hänger på väggen.** | The picture is hanging on the wall. |

Use **på** in many established expressions for places, activities, institutions, and public transport. It does not always mean that something is physically on top of a surface:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Han är på jobbet.** | He is at work. |
| **Vi ses på biblioteket.** | We will meet at the library. |
| **De äter på restaurang.** | They are eating at a restaurant. |
| **Jag väntar på stationen.** | I am waiting at the station. |
| **Hon sitter på bussen.** | She is on the bus. |

With some place words, different prepositions highlight different meanings or situations. For example, **Barnen är i skolan.** (The children are at school.) commonly describes being at school as a student, while **Hon arbetar på skolan.** (She works at the school.) describes the school as a workplace. Actual usage can vary with the intended meaning and the place.

## Common contrasts

Some useful contrasts show why it helps to learn the preposition with the place word:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag är i affären.** | I am in the shop. |
| **Vi träffas på caféet.** | We are meeting at the café. |
| **Katten ligger i sängen.** | The cat is in the bed. |
| **Katten ligger på sängen.** | The cat is on the bed. |
| **De är i havet.** | They are in the sea. |
| **Vi är på stranden.** | We are at the beach. |

The difference between **i** and **på** can change the picture: **i sängen** means in the bed, while **på sängen** means on the bed.

Many country and city names take **i**, but some island names and other place names traditionally take **på**. For example, **på Island** means *in Iceland*, and **på Kuba** means *in Cuba*. These are conventional expressions; **på** here does not mean “on top of.”

## Place is not the same as direction

This page focuses on location—where someone or something is. For movement toward a place, Swedish often uses **till** (to), rather than **i** or **på**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag är i skolan.** | I am at school. |
| **Jag går till skolan.** | I am going to school. |
| **Vi är på biblioteket.** | We are at the library. |
| **Vi går till biblioteket.** | We are going to the library. |

There are other patterns for movement, so do not change **i** or **på** to **till** mechanically. Choose the expression that fits the place and meaning.

## A few useful details

- Some place expressions are short and conventional: **hemma** means *at home*, as in **Jag är hemma.** (I am at home.)
- Some transport expressions use **i**, especially a small enclosed vehicle: **Hon sitter i en taxi.** (She is sitting in a taxi.)
- For transport, **på** is common with buses and trains: **Han sitter på tåget.** (He is sitting on the train.) These are usage patterns, not rules based only on the physical shape of the vehicle.

Because prepositions do not map neatly from English, pay attention to the full Swedish phrase—for example, **i Stockholm** (in Stockholm), **på jobbet** (at work), **i bilen** (in the car), and **på bussen** (on the bus).
