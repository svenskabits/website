Use the **comparative** to compare two people or things: *warmer* or *more interesting*. Use the **superlative** to say that someone or something has the most of a quality in a group: *warmest* or *most interesting*.

| Form | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Basic form | **Våren är varm.** | Spring is warm. |
| Comparative | **Våren är varmare än vintern.** | Spring is warmer than winter. |
| Superlative | **Juli är varmast.** | July is the warmest. |

## Forming the comparative and superlative

Many short adjectives form the comparative with **-are** and the superlative with **-ast**. The superlative form used in a sentence does not always match the English word-for-word: Swedish often has no separate word for *the* in a predicate such as **Juli är varmast**.

| Basic form | Comparative | Superlative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **varm** | **varmare** | **varmast** | warm, warmer, warmest |
| **kall** | **kallare** | **kallast** | cold, colder, coldest |
| **snabb** | **snabbare** | **snabbast** | fast, faster, fastest |
| **billig** | **billigare** | **billigast** | cheap, cheaper, cheapest |
| **rolig** | **roligare** | **roligast** | fun, more fun, most fun |

Some adjectives have shorter forms ending in **-re** and **-st**. Learn these common forms as a set:

| Basic form | Comparative | Superlative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hög** | **högre** | **högst** | high, higher, highest |
| **låg** | **lägre** | **lägst** | low, lower, lowest |
| **lång** | **längre** | **längst** | long, longer, longest |
| **ung** | **yngre** | **yngst** | young, younger, youngest |
| **gammal** | **äldre** | **äldst** | old, older, oldest |

Longer adjectives often use **mer** (more) and **mest** (most) before the basic form, especially many words ending in **-isk** and participles. Some adjectives allow more than one pattern, so learn the common form with the word. Do not combine the two patterns: use either an **-are/-ast** ending or **mer/mest**, not both.

| Basic form | Comparative | Superlative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **intressant** | **mer intressant** | **mest intressant** | interesting, more interesting, most interesting |
| **praktisk** | **mer praktisk** | **mest praktisk** | practical, more practical, most practical |
| **avslappnad** | **mer avslappnad** | **mest avslappnad** | relaxed, more relaxed, most relaxed |

Some common adjectives are irregular:

| Basic form | Comparative | Superlative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bra** | **bättre** | **bäst** | good, better, best |
| **dålig** | **sämre** | **sämst** | bad, worse, worst |
| **liten** | **mindre** | **minst** | small, smaller, smallest |

For amounts, use **många – fler – flest** with countable things, and **mycket – mer – mest** with amounts or uncountable things. **Lite – mindre – minst** means *little – less – least*.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi har fler soliga dagar i år.** | We have more sunny days this year. |
| **Det finns flest hotell i centrum.** | There are the most hotels in the city centre. |
| **Jag vill ha mer kaffe.** | I would like more coffee. |
| **Det regnade mindre i juni än i maj.** | It rained less in June than in May. |

For a smaller number of countable things, use **färre** (fewer): **Vi hade färre gäster i år.** (We had fewer guests this year.) The superlative **färst** exists but is uncommon; speakers usually say **minst** or **minst antal** instead: **Vi hade minst antal gäster i år.** (We had the fewest guests this year.)

## Comparing two things

Put **än** (than) after a comparative:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **I dag är det varmare än i går.** | Today it is warmer than yesterday. |
| **Tåget är snabbare än bussen.** | The train is faster than the bus. |
| **Det här hotellet är mer centralt än det andra.** | This hotel is more central than the other one. |
| **Hon springer snabbare än sin bror gör.** | She runs faster than her brother does. |

The compared words or ideas should match. If the second part is a full clause, its verb can make the comparison clear, as in **än sin bror gör** (than her brother does). The subject pronoun **jag** is used when comparing subjects: **Hon är längre än jag.** (She is taller than I am.) In everyday speech, **än mig** is also common.

## Equal and unequal comparisons

Use **lika + adjective/adverb + som** to say that two things are equal in some way. The adjective agrees with its noun when needed:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Sommaren är lika varm som våren.** | Summer is as warm as spring. |
| **Rummet är lika stort som köket.** | The room is as big as the kitchen. |
| **De här skorna är lika bekväma som de andra.** | These shoes are as comfortable as the others. |
| **Han kör lika försiktigt som sin pappa.** | He drives as carefully as his father. |

To say that something is not as … as another thing, use **inte lika … som** or **inte så … som**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vädret är inte lika varmt som i går.** | The weather is not as warm as yesterday. |
| **Hotellet är inte så dyrt som jag trodde.** | The hotel is not as expensive as I thought. |

## Using the superlative

When the quality describes the subject, use the superlative form, often followed by a phrase with **i** (in) or **av** (of):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Augusti är varmast.** | August is the warmest. |
| **Det här rummet är billigast av alla.** | This room is the cheapest of all. |
| **Hon springer snabbast i laget.** | She runs the fastest on the team. |
| **Det var den bästa resan.** | It was the best trip. |

When the superlative comes before a definite noun, use a definite form, usually ending in **-aste**. The noun is also definite, and Swedish normally uses **den**, **det**, or **de** before the adjective and noun. The same adjective form is used with en-words, ett-words, and plurals:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **den varmaste dagen** | the warmest day |
| **det varmaste vädret** | the warmest weather |
| **de varmaste sommardagarna** | the warmest summer days |
| **det mest intressanta museet** | the most interesting museum |
| **de bästa hotellen** | the best hotels |

The same definite form can stand without a noun when the noun is understood: **Det här är den billigaste.** (This is the cheapest one.)

## Comparatives with adverbs and change over time

Adverbs tell how an action happens. Their comparative and superlative forms are often the same as the corresponding adjective forms:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Bussen går långsammare än tåget.** | The bus goes more slowly than the train. |
| **Sara sjunger bäst i familjen.** | Sara sings the best in the family. |
| **Han arbetar mer effektivt nu.** | He works more efficiently now. |

Use **allt** (ever/increasingly) before a comparative to describe a change that continues. You can also repeat **mer och mer** (more and more) or **-are och -are** (more and more):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det blir allt kallare.** | It is getting colder and colder. |
| **Dagarna blir längre och längre.** | The days are getting longer and longer. |
| **Resan blev mer och mer intressant.** | The trip became more and more interesting. |

Another useful pattern is **ju … desto …**, meaning *the …, the …*:

> **Ju tidigare vi åker, desto snabbare kommer vi fram.**  
> The earlier we leave, the sooner we will arrive.

The comparative and superlative forms do not change for en-words, ett-words, or plural nouns: **en varmare dag** (a warmer day), **ett varmare klimat** (a warmer climate), **varmare dagar** (warmer days). But when a superlative comes before a definite noun, use the definite **-aste** form: **den varmaste dagen** (the warmest day).
