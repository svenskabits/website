Swedish uses four related words to distinguish being in a place from moving towards or away from it:

| Word | Basic meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **hemma** | at home; in the home place | **Jag är hemma.** — I am at home. |
| **hem** | home, as a destination | **Jag går hem.** — I am going home. |
| **borta** | away; not here or not at home | **Hon är borta.** — She is away. |
| **bort** | away, as a direction; at a distance | **Gå bort!** — Go away! |

The distinction is a useful starting point: **hemma** and **borta** commonly describe a state or location, while **hem** and **bort** commonly describe a destination, direction, or distance.

## Hemma: being at home

Use **hemma** to say that someone is at home or in their home place. It often follows **vara** (to be):

- **Är du hemma i kväll?** — Are you at home this evening?
- **Barnen är hemma i dag.** — The children are at home today.
- **Vi stannar hemma när det regnar.** — We stay at home when it rains.
- **Jag jobbar hemma på fredagar.** — I work at home on Fridays.

To say that someone is at another person's home, use **hemma hos** followed by the person:

- **Vi är hemma hos Sara.** — We are at Sara's home.
- **Han äter middag hemma hos sina föräldrar.** — He is having dinner at his parents' home.

## Hem: going home

Use **hem** when home is the destination. It usually comes after the verb of movement, without **till**:

- **Jag går hem nu.** — I am going home now.
- **De åker hem efter jobbet.** — They are going home after work.
- **Kom hem före klockan tio.** — Come home before ten o'clock.
- **När ska ni flytta hem?** — When are you moving back home?

You can name whose home is the destination with **hem till**:

- **Vi går hem till min syster.** — We are going to my sister's home.
- **Hon åker hem till sina föräldrar över helgen.** — She is going to her parents' home for the weekend.

For the starting point “from home,” use **hemifrån**:

- **Jag tar bussen hemifrån.** — I take the bus from home.
- **De åkte hemifrån tidigt.** — They left home early.

## Borta: being away

Use **borta** to describe someone or something as away, absent, or not here. It often follows **vara**:

- **Är din chef borta i dag?** — Is your manager away today?
- **Vi är borta hela helgen.** — We are away all weekend.
- **Nycklarna är borta.** — The keys are missing.
- **Jag var borta när du ringde.** — I was away when you called.

**Borta** can also describe being at someone else's place. **Borta hos** means “at” or “over at” that person's home:

- **De är borta hos grannarna.** — They are over at the neighbours' home.

## Bort: away, or at a distance

Use **bort** for movement away from a place or person. It can also describe how far away something is:

- **Gå inte bort från gruppen.** — Don't go away from the group.
- **Han sprang bort till bilen.** — He ran over to the car.
- **Lägg väskan där borta.** — Put the bag over there.
- **Stationen ligger långt bort.** — The station is far away.

With **bort till**, the meaning is often “over to” a place or person:

- **Jag går bort till affären.** — I am going over to the shop.
- **Kan du komma bort till oss efter jobbet?** — Can you come over to our place after work?

## Compare the pairs

| Being somewhere or away | Moving somewhere or away |
| --- | --- |
| **Hon är hemma.** — She is at home. | **Hon går hem.** — She is going home. |
| **Han är borta.** — He is away. | **Han går bort.** — He is going away. |
| **De är hemma hos Ali.** — They are at Ali's home. | **De går hem till Ali.** — They are going to Ali's home. |

The distinction is about the meaning in the sentence, not simply whether a person is physically moving. For example, **bort** can describe a distance with no movement:

- **Vårt hotell ligger långt bort från centrum.** — Our hotel is far away from the city centre.

## Word order and other meanings

These words usually come after the conjugated verb in a simple statement. With **inte**, **inte** normally comes just after the verb:

- **Jag är inte hemma ännu.** — I am not at home yet.
- **Hon åker inte hem i dag.** — She is not going home today.
- **De är inte borta länge.** — They are not away for long.

**Hem** is also a noun meaning “home” or “a home.” As a noun, it can have an article or a possessive; as a destination adverb, it does not:

- **De söker ett nytt hem.** — They are looking for a new home.
- **Nu går de hem.** — Now they are going home.

**Bort** also appears in common verb combinations with meanings such as “put away” or “remove”:

- **Lägg undan maten och ställ bort tallrikarna.** — Put away the food and put the plates away.
- **Ta bort den gamla bilden.** — Remove the old picture.

In these combinations, **bort** is part of the verb's meaning, rather than simply saying where someone is. The key contrast remains: **hemma / borta** often tell where someone is, while **hem / bort** often tell where someone or something is going, or how far away it is.
