Many Swedish nouns form plurals with common endings, but some change their stem, change a vowel, or keep the same form in the indefinite plural. Learn these forms with each noun; the patterns below are useful clues, not rules that predict every word.

| Pattern | Singular | Indefinite plural | Definite plural | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vowel change | **en bok** | **böcker** | **böckerna** | a book; books; the books |
| Vowel and consonant change | **en fot** | **fötter** | **fötterna** | a foot; feet; the feet |
| Vowel change | **en hand** | **händer** | **händerna** | a hand; hands; the hands |
| Vowel change | **en man** | **män** | **männen** | a man; men; the men |
| Vowel change | **en gås** | **gäss** | **gässen** | a goose; geese; the geese |
| Vowel change | **ett land** | **länder** | **länderna** | a country; countries; the countries |
| Stem change | **en dotter** | **döttrar** | **döttrarna** | a daughter; daughters; the daughters |
| Stem change | **en son** | **söner** | **sönerna** | a son; sons; the sons |
| Different plural ending | **ett öga** | **ögon** | **ögonen** | an eye; eyes; the eyes |
| Different plural ending | **ett öra** | **öron** | **öronen** | an ear; ears; the ears |
| No visible indefinite-plural ending | **ett barn** | **barn** | **barnen** | a child; children; the children |
| No visible indefinite-plural ending | **ett får** | **får** | **fåren** | a sheep; sheep; the sheep |

## Vowel changes and stem changes

Some plurals change the vowel in the noun, sometimes while also adding an ending. For example, **bok** changes to **böck-** in **böcker**, and **land** changes to **länd-** in **länder**. With **fot**, the vowel changes and the **t** is doubled in the spelling: **fot → fötter** (foot → feet).

Other nouns have a changed stem or a plural ending that is not easy to predict from the singular. For example, **dotter → döttrar** (daughter → daughters), **öga → ögon** (eye → eyes), and **öra → öron** (ear → ears). These forms are best learned as complete pairs or sets.

## Indefinite and definite plurals

An indefinite plural has no **en** or **ett** before it: **Jag har två böcker.** (I have two books.) The definite plural has its own ending, which can differ across nouns: **böckerna** (the books), **männen** (the men), **ögonen** (the eyes), and **barnen** (the children).

Some nouns keep the same form in the indefinite plural, including **ett barn → barn** (a child → children) and **ett får → får** (a sheep → sheep). The definite plural still takes an ending: **barnen** (the children), **fåren** (the sheep). An unchanged plural is not always an irregular exception; for instance, many common **-are** nouns have the same indefinite plural as singular: **en lärare → lärare → lärarna** (a teacher → teachers → the teachers).

Use a number or another word before the plural when needed: **tre fötter** (three feet), **många länder** (many countries). Do not add a singular article to an indefinite plural.

Remember the singular, indefinite plural, and—when you need to refer to a specific group—definite plural together. Similar-looking nouns can form their plurals differently, so treat these patterns as learning aids rather than automatic rules.
