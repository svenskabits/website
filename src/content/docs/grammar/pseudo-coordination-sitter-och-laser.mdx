Swedish often uses two verbs joined by **och** (and) to describe a person's position or movement together with an activity. In **Hon sitter och läser**, the same person is sitting and reading. This pattern is called pseudo-coordination. It is especially common with **sitta** (sit), **stå** (stand), and **ligga** (lie), and it can make an activity sound as if it is in progress or taking time.

## The basic pattern

The subject is shared by both verbs. The first verb describes position or movement, and the second gives the activity:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag sitter och läser.** | I am sitting and reading. |
| **Barnen står och väntar.** | The children are standing and waiting. |
| **Katten ligger och sover.** | The cat is lying down and sleeping. |

The first verb changes to match the subject and the time. The form of the second verb changes as part of the verb construction too:

| Time or construction | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Present | **Hon sitter och skriver.** | She is sitting and writing. |
| Past | **Hon satt och skrev.** | She was sitting and writing. |
| Perfect | **Hon har suttit och skrivit.** | She has been sitting and writing. |
| With a future or modal verb | **Hon ska sitta och skriva.** | She will sit and write. |

There is no **att** between the verbs. The second verb is not always an infinitive: compare present **sitter och skriver** with past **satt och skrev** and perfect **har suttit och skrivit**.

## What the pattern adds

The construction often emphasizes that an activity is happening over a period of time, or that someone is occupied with it. With **sitta**, **stå**, or **ligga**, it can also tell you the person's physical position. The position may be important or simply part of the way the speaker describes the activity.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi satt och pratade hela kvällen.** | We sat talking all evening. |
| **Han står och lagar mat.** | He is standing and cooking. |
| **De ligger och tittar på film.** | They are lying down and watching a film. |

The construction is not required whenever an activity is in progress. A simple verb can describe the same activity without highlighting the position or the ongoing activity in the same way:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hon läser en bok.** | She is reading a book. / She reads a book. |
| **Hon sitter och läser en bok.** | She is sitting and reading a book. |

Swedish does not need a special progressive verb form here. The context and the choice between the simple verb and pseudo-coordination help show how the speaker views the activity.

## Movement verbs and common expressions

The pattern also occurs with movement verbs, especially **gå** (go, walk). In these cases, it can describe doing something while moving, or going somewhere in order to do an activity:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Han går och pratar i telefon.** | He is walking around and talking on the phone. |
| **Jag går och hämtar en kopp kaffe.** | I am going to get a cup of coffee. |

Some combinations have a familiar, conventional meaning. For example, **gå och lägga sig** means *go to bed*; it does not normally mean that someone walks while lying down:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Barnen går och lägger sig klockan åtta.** | The children go to bed at eight. |

With movement verbs, use the context to understand whether the sentence describes two activities happening together, going off to do something, or a conventional expression.

## Questions and negative sentences

In a question, the finite verb comes before the subject, as in other Swedish yes/no questions:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Sitter du och jobbar?** | Are you sitting and working? |
| **Stod de och väntade länge?** | Were they standing and waiting for a long time? |

Put **inte** (not) after the finite verb and before **och**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag sitter inte och tittar på tv.** | I am not sitting and watching TV. |
| **Hon stod inte och väntade på mig.** | She was not standing and waiting for me. |

With a modal or auxiliary verb, **inte** usually follows that verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi ska inte sitta och vänta här.** | We are not going to sit and wait here. |

## Distinguishing the pattern from ordinary coordination

In pseudo-coordination, the two verbs normally have one shared subject and describe one connected situation. Ordinary coordination can instead join two complete clauses, each with its own subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Maja sitter och läser.** | Maja is sitting and reading. |
| **Maja sitter och hennes bror läser.** | Maja is sitting, and her brother is reading. |

In the first sentence, Maja does both things. In the second, each person has a separate activity.
