In English, **the** comes before a plural noun: *the cars*. In Swedish, a definite plural usually has an ending attached to the noun: **bilar → bilarna** (*cars → the cars*). The ending depends mainly on the noun's indefinite plural form, not on whether it is an **en-word** or an **ett-word**.

## Common endings

| Indefinite plural ends in | Common definite ending | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-or**, **-ar**, or **-er** | **-na** | **flickor → flickorna** | girls → the girls |
| **-n** | **-a** | **äpplen → äpplena** | apples → the apples |
| no visible ending | usually **-en** | **hus → husen** | houses → the houses |

These are useful patterns, not rules that predict every noun. Some nouns have a different definite plural ending or a changed form, so learn the indefinite and definite plural together.

### Plurals in -or, -ar, and -er

Add **-na** to many plurals that end in **-or**, **-ar**, or **-er**:

| Indefinite plural | Definite plural | English |
|---|---|---|
| **flickor** | **flickorna** | girls; the girls |
| **bilar** | **bilarna** | cars; the cars |
| **filmer** | **filmerna** | films; the films |
| **lärare** | **lärarna** | teachers; the teachers |

The form **lärare** is the same in the singular and indefinite plural, but the definite plural is **lärarna**.

### Plurals in -n

Many plurals ending in **-n** take **-a**:

| Indefinite plural | Definite plural | English |
|---|---|---|
| **äpplen** | **äpplena** | apples; the apples |
| **pianon** | **pianona** | pianos; the pianos |
| **meddelanden** | **meddelandena** | messages; the messages |

Some common nouns ending in **-on** instead take **-en**: **ögon → ögonen** (eyes → the eyes) and **öron → öronen** (ears → the ears). Learn these forms as whole words.

### Plurals with no visible ending

Many nouns whose indefinite plural has no ending add **-en**:

| Indefinite plural | Definite plural | English |
|---|---|---|
| **hus** | **husen** | houses; the houses |
| **bord** | **borden** | tables; the tables |
| **barn** | **barnen** | children; the children |
| **får** | **fåren** | sheep; the sheep |

The ending makes the definite form visible even when the indefinite plural looks like the singular.

## Irregular forms

Some nouns change their vowel or stem in the plural. Their definite endings follow familiar patterns in many cases, but some forms are irregular:

| Singular | Indefinite plural | Definite plural | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **en bok** | **böcker** | **böckerna** | a book; books; the books |
| **en fot** | **fötter** | **fötterna** | a foot; feet; the feet |
| **en hand** | **händer** | **händerna** | a hand; hands; the hands |
| **en man** | **män** | **männen** | a man; men; the men |
| **en gås** | **gäss** | **gässen** | a goose; geese; the geese |
| **ett land** | **länder** | **länderna** | a country; countries; the countries |
| **ett öga** | **ögon** | **ögonen** | an eye; eyes; the eyes |
| **ett öra** | **öron** | **öronen** | an ear; ears; the ears |

Notice that **männen** and **gässen** do not use the most common ending for their plural forms. It is safer to learn these complete forms than to try to build every definite plural from one rule.

## Using the definite plural

Use the definite plural when you mean a specific group that the listener can identify. For example, you can first mention things generally and then refer to those same things as a definite group:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag köper äpplen.** | I'm buying apples. |
| **Äpplena är röda.** | The apples are red. |
| **Barnen är hemma.** | The children are at home. |

With a possessive word such as **mina** (my), use the ordinary plural form, not the definite ending: **mina böcker** (my books), not *mina böckerna*. The possessive already identifies the group.

With an adjective before a definite plural noun, Swedish normally uses **de**, an adjective ending in **-a**, and the noun's definite plural form:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **de röda äpplena** | the red apples |
| **de små barnen** | the small children |
| **Jag ser de gula blommorna.** | I see the yellow flowers. |

## A quick way to choose

First find the noun's indefinite plural form. If it ends in **-or**, **-ar**, or **-er**, **-na** is common; if it ends in **-n**, **-a** is common; and if it has no visible ending, **-en** is common. Check for exceptions and learn the definite plural with the noun: for example, **äpplen → äpplena**, **hus → husen**, but **ögon → ögonen** and **män → männen**.
