Swedish does not have a separate formal pronoun like French *vous* or German *Sie*. In most situations, people use **du** for one person, including colleagues, managers, officials, teachers, and people they have just met. Formality is mainly shown through your choice of words, how direct you are, and the tone of the conversation.

The right level depends on the relationship and situation. A message to a close friend can be relaxed; a message to an authority or an unfamiliar person may need a more careful tone. Swedish communication is often fairly direct, so polite language does not need to be long or elaborate.

## Addressing people

Use **du** when speaking to one person and **ni** when speaking to more than one person. In modern Sweden, **ni** is not a general formal equivalent of *du*. Using it for one person can sound unusually formal, old-fashioned, or like a service interaction, depending on the person and context. When unsure, **du** is usually the natural choice. In a very formal letter, you can also avoid a pronoun and address the person's role or use their name.

| Swedish | English | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Hej Anna! Kan du hjälpa mig? | Hi Anna! Can you help me? | Normal, friendly, and also suitable for many work situations |
| Hej! Kan ni hjälpa oss? | Hello! Can you help us? | Addressing several people |
| Hej! Skulle du kunna hjälpa mig? | Hello! Could you help me? | A more careful request to one person, still using *du* |

## Making requests less direct

An imperative is natural for instructions, invitations, and quick requests. Add *tack* when it fits; Swedish does not require a special word corresponding to English *please* in every request.

| More direct | More careful | English |
|---|---|---|
| Skicka blanketten i dag. | Kan du skicka blanketten i dag? | Send the form today. / Can you send the form today? |
| Vänta här. | Skulle du kunna vänta här en stund? | Wait here. / Could you wait here for a moment? |
| Ge mig kvittot, tack. | Skulle jag kunna få kvittot? | Give me the receipt, please. / Could I have the receipt? |

Use **kan du…?** for an ordinary request. **Skulle du kunna…?** is more tentative and considerate. **Skulle jag kunna få…?** frames the request as asking whether something is possible. These are not strict levels: your relationship, voice, and context also matter.

The modal verb **skulle** can make a suggestion or invitation sound less forceful too:

- **Vi kan prata efter mötet.** — We can talk after the meeting.
- **Vi skulle kunna prata efter mötet.** — We could talk after the meeting.
- **Vill du ta en kaffe?** — Do you want to have a coffee?
- **Skulle du vilja ta en kaffe?** — Would you like to have a coffee?

## Choosing words and tone

Everyday spoken Swedish often uses short, familiar expressions. In more formal situations, choose neutral wording, give enough context, and avoid slang. This does not mean that every formal message must sound distant.

| More informal | More neutral or formal | English |
|---|---|---|
| Hej! | Hej! / God dag! | Hi! / Good day! |
| Kan du kolla det här? | Kan du kontrollera detta? | Can you check this? |
| Jag vill ha ett möte. | Jag skulle vilja boka ett möte. | I want a meeting. / I would like to book a meeting. |
| Det funkar inte. | Det fungerar inte. | It doesn't work. |
| Hör av dig snart! | Återkom gärna när du har möjlighet. | Get in touch soon! / Please get back to me when you have a chance. |

The alternatives are not interchangeable in every situation. For instance, **det funkar inte** is perfectly natural in a casual conversation, while **det fungerar inte** is often a better fit in a work email or a report. **Gärna** can soften a request or express that something is welcome; it is not a word that must be added to every polite sentence.

## Emails and messages

Start with a greeting that suits the relationship. **Hej** is widely used in both personal and professional messages. Add the recipient's first name when you know it. In a formal email to an organization, you can use a role or department if you do not know the recipient's name.

- **Hej Sara,** — Hi Sara,
- **Hej,** — Hello,
- **Hej kundservice,** — Hello, customer service,
- **Till handläggaren,** — To the case officer,

State why you are writing, make your request clear, and include relevant details. In a more careful message, phrases such as **Jag skriver angående…** (“I am writing about…”) and **Jag undrar om…** (“I wonder whether…”) introduce the purpose without sounding abrupt.

> **Hej Sara,**  
> Jag skriver angående min ansökan. Jag undrar om du kan säga när jag får besked.  
> Tack på förhand!  
> Vänliga hälsningar,  
> Amir

> **Hi Sara,**  
> I am writing about my application. I wonder if you can tell me when I will get a decision.  
> Thanks in advance!  
> Kind regards,  
> Amir

Common sign-offs include **Hälsningar** (“Regards”), **Vänliga hälsningar** (“Kind regards”), and **Med vänlig hälsning** (“Yours sincerely” / “Kind regards”). A short message to someone you know can end with just your name or **Hälsningar**. **Med vänlig hälsning** is a conventional, more formal sign-off; it is often written in the singular even when the sender is a group.

## Polite expressions

Politeness often comes from the whole sentence rather than one special word. A brief **tack** is common when accepting help, receiving something, or asking for a small service.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Tack för hjälpen! | Thanks for your help! |
| Tack så mycket. | Thank you very much. |
| Skicka gärna svaret via e-post. | Please feel free to send the reply by email. |
| Ursäkta, kan jag ställa en fråga? | Excuse me, can I ask a question? |
| Jag ber om ursäkt för det sena svaret. | I apologize for the late reply. |

**Vänligen** can mean “please” in a written instruction, often in notices or official messages: **Vänligen fyll i alla fält.** (“Please fill in all the fields.”) In ordinary conversation it can sound stiff, and in a personal message a simple question is often more natural. **Tack på förhand** (“Thanks in advance”) is common in requests, but use it when you genuinely expect a response or action.

## Adapting to the situation

- With friends and family, relaxed wording, shortened messages, and humor are common.
- At work, **du** is still normal. Use a friendly, clear tone and adjust how direct you are to the task and relationship.
- When contacting an authority, bank, or healthcare provider, explain the matter clearly, use complete sentences, and include identifying or case details only when relevant and safe.
- In customer service, both customers and staff usually use **du**. A courteous tone matters more than switching to **ni**.
- In a formal speech, application, or official notice, neutral vocabulary and conventional greetings or sign-offs may be appropriate.

These are tendencies rather than fixed rules. If someone introduces a preferred form of address or a workplace has its own style, follow that. When uncertain, a friendly **Hej**, **du**, and a clear, considerate request are a safe starting point.
