The question word **hur** means **how**. It asks about the way something happens, a condition, or the degree or amount of something. Common expressions with **hur** are best learned as phrases.

## Direct questions with hur

In a direct question, put the question phrase first, then the finite verb and the subject:

**hur + (description word) + verb + subject + the rest**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hur tar jag mig till stationen?** | How do I get to the station? |
| **Hur stavar du ditt namn?** | How do you spell your name? |
| **Hur mår din syster?** | How is your sister? |
| **Hur trött är du?** | How tired are you? |

Swedish does not add *do* or *does* to these questions. The verb comes after **hur** or after the phrase beginning with **hur**.

## Common expressions with hur

| Expression | Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hur mycket** | an amount or price | **Hur mycket kostar biljetten?** | How much does the ticket cost? |
| **hur många** | a number of countable things | **Hur många syskon har du?** | How many siblings do you have? |
| **hur gammal** | a person's or thing's age | **Hur gammal är din son?** | How old is your son? |
| **hur ofta** | how frequently | **Hur ofta tränar ni?** | How often do you exercise? |
| **hur länge** | duration | **Hur länge stannar du?** | How long are you staying? |
| **hur långt** | distance | **Hur långt är det till centrum?** | How far is it to the city centre? |

Use **hur mycket** with an amount, including an uncountable amount, and to ask a price. Use **hur många** with things you can count, normally named by a plural noun. **Hur mycket vatten dricker du?** (How much water do you drink?) **Hur många böcker läser du?** (How many books do you read?)

The adjective in **hur gammal** changes to match the person or thing being described:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hur gammal är du?** | How old are you? |
| **Hur gammalt är huset?** | How old is the house? |
| **Hur gamla är barnen?** | How old are the children? |

## Hur in indirect questions

In an indirect question, the subject comes before the verb after the **hur** phrase:

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Hur gammal är han?** | **Jag vet hur gammal han är.** | How old is he? / I know how old he is. |
| **Hur mycket kostar det?** | **Kan du säga hur mycket det kostar?** | How much does it cost? / Can you tell me how much it costs? |

In **Kan du säga** (Can you tell me), the whole sentence is a direct question, so its verb comes before the subject. Inside it, **hur mycket det kostar** (how much it costs) is an indirect question, so **det** comes before **kostar**.

Remember the common phrases: **hur mycket** for an amount or price, **hur många** for a number, **hur gammal** for age, **hur ofta** for frequency, **hur länge** for duration, and **hur långt** for distance.
