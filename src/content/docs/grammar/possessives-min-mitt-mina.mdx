Swedish **min**, **mitt**, and **mina** all mean **my**. Choose the form to match the thing that belongs to you—not the person who owns it:

| Form | Use with | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **min** | a singular en-word | **min bok** | my book |
| **mitt** | a singular ett-word | **mitt hus** | my house |
| **mina** | any plural noun | **mina böcker** | my books |

The owner can be anyone: **min** does not mean that the speaker is male, and **mitt** does not mean the speaker is female. The form depends on the possessed noun.

## Position with nouns and adjectives

Put the possessive word before the noun. If an adjective describes the noun, the possessive comes before the adjective:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **min bok** | my book |
| **mitt hus** | my house |
| **mina böcker** | my books |
| **min nya bok** | my new book |
| **mitt nya hus** | my new house |
| **mina nya böcker** | my new books |

After **min**, **mitt**, or **mina**, the noun normally has no definite ending: say **min bok** (my book), not *min boken* (my the-book). The adjective usually has its definite form, often ending in **-a**, as in **min nya bok** (my new book).

## Other possessive forms

The same noun-based pattern appears with **din/ditt/dina** (your):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **din stol** | your chair |
| **ditt bord** | your table |
| **dina stolar** | your chairs |

Possessives such as **hans** (his) and **hennes** (her) do not change to match the possessed noun:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **hans bok** | his book |
| **hans hus** | his house |
| **hennes böcker** | her books |

## His or her own: sin, sitt, sina

For a third-person subject's own possession, Swedish often uses **sin**, **sitt**, or **sina**. Like **min/mitt/mina**, these forms match the possessed noun: **sin** with a singular en-word, **sitt** with a singular ett-word, and **sina** with a plural noun.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Erik läser sin bok.** | Erik is reading his own book. |
| **Erik städar sitt rum.** | Erik is cleaning his own room. |
| **Maja hämtar sina barn.** | Maja is picking up her own children. |

Use **hans** or **hennes** when the thing belongs to someone other than the sentence's subject: **Erik läser hennes bok.** (Erik is reading her book.) Here, **hennes** refers to a woman other than Erik. For first- and second-person subjects, use **min/mitt/mina** or **din/ditt/dina**, not **sin/sitt/sina**: **Jag läser min bok.** (I am reading my book.)

## Common mistakes

| Avoid | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *mitt bok* | **min bok** (my book) | **Bok** is a singular en-word. |
| *min hus* | **mitt hus** (my house) | **Hus** is a singular ett-word. |
| *min böcker* | **mina böcker** (my books) | Use **mina** with a plural noun. |
| *min nya boken* | **min nya bok** (my new book) | Do not add the definite noun ending after a possessive. |

Remember: **min/mitt** match the noun's singular group, and **mina** is used with plural nouns. Put the possessive before any adjective and the noun.
