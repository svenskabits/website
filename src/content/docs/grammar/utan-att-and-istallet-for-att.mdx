Use **utan att** to say that an action happens without another action. Use **istället för att** to say that one action replaces another. Both expressions can be followed by an infinitive, and they can also introduce a full clause with its own subject and finite verb.

## **Utan att** + infinitive

When the person doing the action is the same in both parts of the sentence, **utan att** is followed by the infinitive form of a verb. The subject is not repeated:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Hon gick ut utan att ta på sig jackan. | She went out without putting on her jacket. |
| Vi kan minska utsläppen utan att försämra vår livskvalitet. | We can reduce emissions without lowering our quality of life. |
| Han lämnade mötet utan att säga hej då. | He left the meeting without saying goodbye. |

The infinitive marker **att** belongs to the expression; do not add another **att** before the verb. Say **utan att ta**, not **utan att att ta**.

The phrase can come before or after the main clause. If it comes first, the main clause has inversion: its verb comes before its subject.

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Utan att tänka efter svarade jag ja. | Without thinking it through, I said yes. |
| Utan att använda bekämpningsmedel odlar de grönsaker. | They grow vegetables without using pesticides. |

## When the actions have different subjects

If the person or thing doing the action after **utan att** is different from the subject of the main clause, state that subject and use a finite verb (a verb marked for tense):

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Hon gick ut utan att någon såg henne. | She went out without anyone seeing her. |
| Vi kan inte skydda skogen utan att kommunen samarbetar med markägarna. | We cannot protect the forest without the municipality cooperating with the landowners. |

In these examples, **såg** and **samarbetar** are finite verbs, and **någon** and **kommunen** are the subjects of their own clauses. The verb in this subordinate clause normally comes after its subject. If the clause contains **inte**, it normally comes before the finite verb.

## **Istället för att** + infinitive

Use **istället för att** when one action is chosen in place of another. When the same person would do both actions, the second verb is an infinitive and its subject is understood:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Vi tog tåget istället för att köra bil. | We took the train instead of driving a car. |
| Istället för att köpa nytt lagade hon den gamla cykeln. | Instead of buying a new one, she repaired the old bicycle. |
| De planterade träd istället för att bygga en parkeringsplats. | They planted trees instead of building a car park. |

The phrase can also come before a noun or pronoun, without **att**:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Vi tog tåget istället för bilen. | We took the train instead of the car. |
| Istället för mig fick min kollega hålla presentationen. | My colleague had to give the presentation instead of me. |

When the phrase begins the sentence, the following main clause has inversion, as in **Istället för att köpa nytt lagade hon ...** The verb **lagade** comes before the subject **hon**.

## When the replacement action has a different subject

If you need to name a different subject after **istället för att**, use a finite verb:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Istället för att vi åker bil kan vi ta bussen. | Instead of us driving, we can take the bus. |
| Istället för att kommunen bygger en ny väg borde den förbättra kollektivtrafiken. | Instead of the municipality building a new road, it should improve public transport. |

The clause after **istället för att** is subordinate: the subject comes before the finite verb, and **inte** normally comes before that verb.

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Istället för att kommunen inte gör något borde den agera. | Instead of the municipality doing nothing, it should act. |

When the subject is the same in both actions, the infinitive is usually more direct: **Istället för att bygga en ny väg borde kommunen förbättra kollektivtrafiken** — “Instead of building a new road, the municipality should improve public transport.”

## Choosing between the expressions

**Utan att** presents an action as absent: something happens, but another action does not. **Istället för att** presents one action as the alternative to another. Compare:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| De åkte vidare utan att stanna i byn. | They continued on without stopping in the village. |
| De åkte vidare istället för att stanna i byn. | They continued on instead of stopping in the village. |

The first sentence simply says they did not stop. The second makes stopping the alternative they chose not to take.

Do not confuse **istället för att** with **för att**. **För att** before an infinitive often explains a purpose (“in order to”); **istället för att** means that an action replaces another:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Vi tog bussen för att minska utsläppen. | We took the bus in order to reduce emissions. |
| Vi tog bussen istället för att köra. | We took the bus instead of driving. |

Both **istället** and **i stället** are standard spellings. The corresponding expression can therefore be written **istället för att** or **i stället för att**.
