Swedish verbs are often organized into four groups according to how their forms are made. The groups are useful patterns for learning and looking up verbs, not perfect rules for predicting every form. Learn a verb's important forms together.

The forms commonly shown are the **infinitive** (dictionary form), **present**, **preterite** (simple past), and **supine**. The supine is used with **har** (have) to make the perfect, as in **har talat** (have spoken).

| Group | Infinitive | Present | Preterite | Supine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **tala** | **talar** | **talade** | **talat** | speak |
| 2a | **ringa** | **ringer** | **ringde** | **ringt** | call |
| 2b | **köpa** | **köper** | **köpte** | **köpt** | buy |
| 3 | **bo** | **bor** | **bodde** | **bott** | live |
| 4 | **skriva** | **skriver** | **skrev** | **skrivit** | write |

## Group 1: -ar, -ade, -at

This is the largest and most regular group. The infinitive usually ends in **-a**. Remove that **-a** and add **-ar** for the present, **-ade** for the preterite, and **-at** for the supine:

| Form | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **arbeta** | to work |
| Present | **arbetar** | work/works |
| Preterite | **arbetade** | worked |
| Supine | **arbetat** | worked (with *have*) |

**Jag arbetar på ett kontor.** (I work in an office.) **I går arbetade jag hemma.** (Yesterday I worked at home.) **Jag har arbetat här länge.** (I have worked here for a long time.)

## Group 2: -er in the present

Group 2 verbs usually have an infinitive ending in **-a** and a present form ending in **-er**. Their preterite and supine forms divide into two common patterns:

- **Group 2a** usually has **-de** in the preterite and **-t** in the supine: **ringa, ringer, ringde, ringt** (to call, call/calls, called, called with *have*).
- **Group 2b** usually has **-te** in the preterite and **-t** in the supine: **köpa, köper, köpte, köpt** (to buy, buy/buys, bought, bought with *have*).

As a rough guide, 2a stems often end in a voiced sound, while 2b stems often end in a voiceless sound such as **p**, **t**, **k**, or **s**. There are exceptions, so check the preterite and supine when learning a new verb.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag ringer min vän.** | I am calling my friend. |
| **Jag ringde min vän i går.** | I called my friend yesterday. |
| **Jag har ringt min vän.** | I have called my friend. |
| **Vi köper kaffe.** | We are buying coffee. |
| **Vi köpte kaffe i går.** | We bought coffee yesterday. |
| **Vi har köpt kaffe.** | We have bought coffee. |

## Group 3: -r, -dde, -tt

Group 3 is a smaller group. The infinitive ends in a vowel other than **-a**. Add **-r** for the present, **-dde** for the preterite, and **-tt** for the supine:

| Form | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **bo** | to live |
| Present | **bor** | live/lives |
| Preterite | **bodde** | lived |
| Supine | **bott** | lived (with *have*) |

**Vi bor i Lund.** (We live in Lund.) **Vi bodde i Lund förut.** (We lived in Lund before.) **Vi har bott i Lund.** (We have lived in Lund.)

## Group 4: strong and irregular verbs

Group 4 contains many **strong verbs**. Instead of using the regular past endings above, they often change a vowel in the stem. Their forms vary, so there is no single ending rule that predicts them all:

| Infinitive | Present | Preterite | Supine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **skriva** | **skriver** | **skrev** | **skrivit** | write |
| **dricka** | **dricker** | **drack** | **druckit** | drink |
| **komma** | **kommer** | **kom** | **kommit** | come |

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hon skriver ett mejl.** | She is writing an email. |
| **Hon skrev ett mejl i går.** | She wrote an email yesterday. |
| **Hon har skrivit ett mejl.** | She has written an email. |
| **De dricker vatten.** | They are drinking water. |
| **De drack vatten efter träningen.** | They drank water after the workout. |
| **De har druckit vatten.** | They have drunk water. |

Some common verbs do not fit the usual patterns neatly. For example, **gå, går, gick, gått** means *go/walk, go/goes, went, gone*; learn its forms as a set. Other verbs, including **vara, är, var, varit** (be, am/is/are, was/were, been), are also irregular.

## Using the present form

The present form is the same with every subject. For example, **talar** is used with **jag** (I), **du** (you), and **de** (they): **Jag talar svenska.** (I speak Swedish.) **Du talar svenska.** (You speak Swedish.) **De talar svenska.** (They speak Swedish.)

The group helps you recognize a pattern, but verbs that look alike can belong to different groups. For instance, **tala, talar** (speak, speak/speaks) is group 1, while **läsa, läser** (read, read/reads) is group 2b. The infinitive alone is not enough to predict the present form reliably.

When you learn a new verb, note at least its infinitive and present form. Add the preterite and supine too when you need to talk about past events or use **har** with the supine.
