## Learn the forms as sets

Most Swedish adjectives follow the familiar pattern of a base form with an en-word, **-t** with an ett-word, and **-a** in the plural. Some common adjectives change more than that. Their form still depends on the noun’s gender, number, and sometimes definiteness, so learn the forms together:

| Adjective | Singular en-word | Singular ett-word | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **liten** (small) | liten | litet | små |
| **egen** (own) | egen | eget | egna |
| **gammal** (old) | gammal | gammalt | gamla |

The table shows forms used before a noun. Forms of adjectives such as **liten** and **gammal** also appear after a linking verb such as **är**, agreeing with the subject:

- **En liten hund bor här.** — A small dog lives here.
- **Ett litet barn sover.** — A small child is sleeping.
- **Huset är litet.** — The house is small.
- **Barnen är små.** — The children are small.
- **Hon har ett eget rum.** — She has a room of her own.
- **De bor i gamla hus.** — They live in old houses.

In the meaning “own,” **egen** normally comes before a noun; it is not usually used by itself after **är**.

### Definite forms

Before a definite noun, **egen** and **gammal** usually take **-a** in all genders and numbers. **Liten** is different in the singular: use **lilla**. Its plural remains **små**.

| Adjective | Definite singular | Definite plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **liten** | den lilla bilen / det lilla huset | de små bilarna |
| **egen** | den egna bilen / det egna huset | de egna rummen |
| **gammal** | den gamla bilen / det gamla huset | de gamla husen |

For example: **Det lilla huset är gammalt.** — The small house is old. Here **lilla** describes a definite noun, while **gammalt** agrees with the ett-word subject after **är**.

## Common confusion

The plural of **liten** is not *liten* or *lita*: it is **små**—for example, **små hundar** and **barnen är små**. In a definite singular phrase, use **lilla** (**den lilla hunden**, **det lilla huset**); in the plural, use **små** (**de små hundarna**). Remember that definiteness changes the form as well as gender and number.

## Useful Expressions

1. **En liten kopp kaffe, tack.** — A small cup of coffee, please.
2. **Det lilla barnet skrattar.** — The little child is laughing.
3. **Små fåglar sitter i trädet.** — Small birds are sitting in the tree.
4. **Jag har ett eget skrivbord.** — I have a desk of my own.
5. **Hon lagar sin egen mat.** — She cooks her own food.
6. **Vi har våra egna nycklar.** — We have our own keys.
7. **Min gamla cykel fungerar fortfarande.** — My old bicycle still works.
8. **Det gamla huset står nära sjön.** — The old house is near the lake.
9. **De gamla böckerna ligger på bordet.** — The old books are on the table.
10. **Barnen är små, men hunden är gammal.** — The children are small, but the dog is old.
