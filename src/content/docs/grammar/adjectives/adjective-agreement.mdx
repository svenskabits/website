## Adjectives agree with nouns

In Swedish, an ordinary adjective usually changes to match the noun it describes. This agreement appears both when the adjective comes **before a noun** and when it comes **after a linking verb** such as **är** (“is/are”):

| Noun type | Before a noun | After **är** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular en-word | **en röd bil** — a red car | **Bilen är röd.** — The car is red. |
| Singular ett-word | **ett rött hus** — a red house | **Huset är rött.** — The house is red. |
| Plural noun | **röda bilar** — red cars | **Bilarna är röda.** — The cars are red. |

The common pattern is:

- With a singular **en-word**, use the adjective’s base form: **röd**.
- With a singular **ett-word**, the adjective often takes **-t**: **rött**.
- With a plural noun, the adjective often takes **-a**: **röda**.

The noun determines the adjective form, not whether the adjective is before the noun or after **är**. These are common patterns, not exceptionless endings: some adjectives have other forms, so learn their forms with examples.

## Common confusion

English adjectives do not change for singular, plural, or grammatical gender: *a red car*, *a red house*, and *red cars*. Swedish does change the adjective, even after **är**: **Bilen är röd**, **Huset är rött**, **Bilarna är röda**. Do not leave the adjective in its base form in every sentence.

## Useful Expressions

1. **En liten hund sover.** — A small dog is sleeping.
2. **Ett litet barn skrattar.** — A small child is laughing.
3. **Små barn leker ute.** — Small children are playing outside.
4. **Kaffet är varmt.** — The coffee is hot.
5. **Soppan är god.** — The soup is tasty.
6. **Äpplena är gröna.** — The apples are green.
7. **Vi har en ny bil.** — We have a new car.
8. **De har ett nytt kök.** — They have a new kitchen.
9. **Rummen är stora.** — The rooms are large.
10. **Min jacka är svart.** — My jacket is black.
