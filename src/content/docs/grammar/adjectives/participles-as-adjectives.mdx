title: Participles as Adjectives
description: Learn how Swedish verb-derived participles describe nouns and how present and past participles behave differently.
---

## Participles can describe nouns

A participle is a verb form that can also work like an adjective. It describes a person or thing by telling you what it is doing or what has happened to it.

## Present participles: **-ande** and **-ende**

Present participles often describe an active or ongoing characteristic. Before a noun, forms ending in **-ande** or **-ende** commonly stay the same with en-words, ett-words, and plural nouns:

- **en leende person** — a smiling person
- **ett leende barn** — a smiling child
- **leende människor** — smiling people
- **en sovande hund** — a sleeping dog

The participle describes the noun; it is not the main verb of the sentence. For example, in **ett leende barn**, **leende** describes **barn**.

## Past participles: agreement with the noun

Past participles often describe a result or state. When used before a noun, they usually agree with the noun in a pattern like an adjective. The exact forms depend on the verb, but a common pattern is:

| Noun | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular en-word | **en stängd dörr** | a closed door |
| Singular ett-word | **ett stängt fönster** | a closed window |
| Plural | **stängda dörrar** | closed doors |

The participle describes the noun's resulting state: the door or window is closed. Other past participles have different base forms, but they commonly change for an ett-word and a plural noun too:

- **en skriven text** — a written text
- **ett skrivet brev** — a written letter
- **skrivna brev** — written letters

## Common confusion

Present participles in **-ande/-ende** commonly remain unchanged: **ett leende barn**, not *ett leendet barn*. Past participles used before nouns commonly agree: **ett stängt fönster**, not *ett stängd fönster*. These are adjective uses; this lesson is not about using participles to make verb tenses.

## Useful Expressions

1. **En leende kvinna vinkar.** — A smiling woman is waving.
2. **Ett sovande barn behöver lugn och ro.** — A sleeping child needs peace and quiet.
3. **Vi såg lekande hundar i parken.** — We saw playing dogs in the park.
4. **Han drack en värmande kopp te.** — He drank a warming cup of tea.
5. **En stängd butik öppnar klockan nio.** — A closed shop opens at nine.
6. **Ett öppet fönster släpper in frisk luft.** — An open window lets in fresh air.
7. **Hon läste ett skrivet meddelande.** — She read a written message.
8. **Vi bor i ett målat hus.** — We live in a painted house.
9. **De köpte nymålade stolar.** — They bought newly painted chairs.
10. **Kokta ägg står i kylen.** — Boiled eggs are in the fridge.
