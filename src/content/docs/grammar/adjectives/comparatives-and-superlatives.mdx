---
title: Comparative and Superlative
description: Learn common Swedish adjective forms for comparing things and describing the highest or lowest degree.
---

## Compare with an adjective

Use a comparative adjective to say that one thing has more of a quality than another. Many short adjectives form the comparative with **-are** and the superlative (“the most” or “the -est”) with **-ast**:

| Base adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **snabb** (fast) | **snabbare** (faster) | **snabbast** (fastest) |
| **billig** (cheap) | **billigare** (cheaper) | **billigast** (cheapest) |

For many longer adjectives, put **mer** (“more”) or **mest** (“most”) before the adjective:

| Base adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **intressant** (interesting) | **mer intressant** (more interesting) | **mest intressant** (most interesting) |

These are common patterns, not rules that work for every adjective. The choice and spelling of comparative forms vary, so learn unfamiliar forms as a set.

## Use **än** for “than”

Put **än** (“than”) after the comparative when you name what you are comparing it with. The comparative form does not change to agree with the noun:

- **Min cykel är snabbare än din.** — My bike is faster than yours.
- **Den här filmen är mer intressant än den förra.** — This film is more interesting than the previous one.

Use a superlative to compare one person or thing with a whole group. Before a definite noun, the superlative commonly has **-aste**; with **mest**, the adjective also takes its definite form:

- **Hon springer snabbast i laget.** — She runs fastest on the team.
- **Det här är den mest intressanta boken i butiken.** — This is the most interesting book in the shop.

## Common confusion

Do not add **mer** or **mest** automatically to every adjective, or add **-are** and **-ast** to every one. Many short adjectives use the endings, while many longer ones use **mer** and **mest**, but forms vary. Some common adjectives have special comparison forms; **bra–bättre–bäst** (“good–better–best”) is covered separately.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Min hund är snabbare än din.** — My dog is faster than yours.
2. **Tåget är billigare än bussen.** — The train is cheaper than the bus.
3. **Den blå väskan är lättare än den svarta.** — The blue bag is lighter than the black one.
4. **Kaffe är starkare än te.** — Coffee is stronger than tea.
5. **Den här uppgiften är mer intressant än den förra.** — This assignment is more interesting than the previous one.
6. **Den nya restaurangen är mer populär än den gamla.** — The new restaurant is more popular than the old one.
7. **Hon är snabbast i klassen.** — She is fastest in the class.
8. **Det här är stadens billigaste hotell.** — This is the city’s cheapest hotel.
9. **Vilken bok är mest spännande?** — Which book is the most exciting?
10. **Det var den mest intressanta filmen.** — It was the most interesting film.
