## The definite singular

English usually puts **the** before a noun. Swedish normally marks a definite singular noun by adding an ending to the noun itself:

- **en bil** (a car) → **bilen** (the car)
- **ett hus** (a house) → **huset** (the house)

The ending depends on whether the noun is an **en-word** or an **ett-word**, and on how the noun ends.

## Choosing the ending

For many nouns ending in a consonant, add **-en** to an en-word and **-et** to an ett-word:

| Indefinite | Definite | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| en katt | katt**en** | a cat → the cat |
| ett bord | bord**et** | a table → the table |

When a noun ends in a vowel, the ending is often shorter: **-n** for an en-word and **-t** for an ett-word. Attach it to the noun:

- **en flicka** → **flickan** — a girl → the girl
- **ett piano** → **pianot** — a piano → the piano

A final unstressed **-e** is often dropped before the definite ending: **en pojke → pojken** (“a boy → the boy”) and **ett äpple → äpplet** (“an apple → the apple”). The exact form is not always predictable from the last letter alone, so learn the definite form with the noun.

These are common patterns, not a rule that predicts every noun. Some nouns have changes that need to be learned individually; this lesson focuses on the usual definite endings.

## Common confusion

Do not put a separate Swedish word for **the** before an ordinary definite noun: say **bilen**, not *the bil*. The suffix does this job. Swedish can use a separate word such as **den** or **det** in other constructions, but that is a different pattern.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag låser bilen.** — I lock the car.
2. **Katten sover på soffan.** — The cat is sleeping on the sofa.
3. **Flickan läser boken.** — The girl is reading the book.
4. **Pojken öppnar fönstret.** — The boy is opening the window.
5. **Huset ligger nära skogen.** — The house is near the forest.
6. **Äpplet är rött.** — The apple is red.
7. **Pianot står i vardagsrummet.** — The piano is in the living room.
8. **Barnet leker i rummet.** — The child is playing in the room.
9. **Jag ställer glaset på bordet.** — I put the glass on the table.
10. **Nyckeln ligger i väskan.** — The key is in the bag.
