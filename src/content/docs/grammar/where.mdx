## Asking “where” with *var*

Swedish **var** means **where** when you ask about a place or location.

In a direct question, **var** comes first, followed by the verb and then the subject:

> **Var + verb + subject?**

- **Var är du?** — Where are you?
- **Var bor du?** — Where do you live?
- **Var ligger stationen?** — Where is the station?

Swedish does not add an equivalent of English *do* in questions: **Var bor du?** is literally “Where live you?”

When the question is part of a longer sentence, keep the subject before the verb:

- **Vet du var nyckeln är?** — Do you know where the key is?
- **Jag vet inte var hon bor.** — I don’t know where she lives.

Compare the direct question **Var är nyckeln?** (verb before subject) with the embedded question **Vet du var nyckeln är?** (subject before verb). Don’t carry the direct-question order into an embedded question.

> **Note:** Lowercase **var** can also mean **was**, as the past form of *vara* (“to be”): **Han var hemma** — “He was at home.” The examples in this lesson use **var** meaning “where.”

## Useful Expressions

1. **Var är toaletten?** — Where is the restroom?
2. **Var bor du?** — Where do you live?
3. **Var ligger det närmaste apoteket?** — Where is the nearest pharmacy?
4. **Var arbetar du?** — Where do you work?
5. **Var kommer ni ifrån?** — Where are you all from?
6. **Var träffas vi?** — Where shall we meet?
7. **Var kan jag köpa en biljett?** — Where can I buy a ticket?
8. **Vet du var busshållplatsen ligger?** — Do you know where the bus stop is?
9. **Jag vet inte var min telefon är.** — I don’t know where my phone is.
10. **Kan du visa mig var stationen ligger?** — Can you show me where the station is?
