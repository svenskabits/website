The infinitive is the basic form of a verb—the form you usually find in a dictionary. It often corresponds to English *to* plus a verb, as in **att läsa** (to read). Swedish infinitives do not change to show who does the action.

| Infinitive | English |
|---|---|
| **bo** | to live |
| **läsa** | to read |
| **vara** | to be |
| **gå** | to go |

Many Swedish infinitives end in **-a**, but some common ones do not. Learn the infinitive as part of each verb. The present form is different for many verbs: **bo** (to live) becomes **bor** (live/lives) in the present.

## The infinitive with and without att

The word **att** can mark an infinitive, much like English *to*. Dictionaries often list the verb without **att**, though both **läsa** (to read) and **att läsa** (to read) name the same infinitive form.

Use the infinitive without **att** after modal verbs such as **kan** (can), **vill** (want to), **ska** (will/be going to), **måste** (must), and **får** (may/be allowed to):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag kan simma.** | I can swim. |
| **Hon vill läsa.** | She wants to read. |
| **Vi ska resa i morgon.** | We are going to travel tomorrow. |
| **Du måste vänta.** | You must wait. |

Many other verbs are followed by **att** and an infinitive. Common examples include **tycka om** (like), **försöka** (try), and **lova** (promise):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Ali tycker om att laga mat.** | Ali likes cooking. |
| **Vi försöker att förstå.** | We are trying to understand. |
| **Hon lovar att ringa.** | She promises to call. |

Some verbs can be used with or without **att** before the next infinitive. For example, **börja** can take either form: **De börjar arbeta.** (They are starting to work.) **De börjar att arbeta.** (They are starting to work.)

The verb **bruka**, meaning *usually do*, is followed by an infinitive without **att**: **Jag brukar gå hem.** (I usually walk home.)

## Other common uses of att + infinitive

An infinitive phrase can be the subject of a sentence:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Att läsa är roligt.** | Reading is fun. |
| **Att lära sig svenska tar tid.** | Learning Swedish takes time. |

It also commonly follows an adjective or expression:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det är skönt att vila.** | It is nice to rest. |
| **Jag har tid att prata.** | I have time to talk. |

Use **för att** before an infinitive to explain the purpose of an action. It often means *in order to* or simply *to*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hon går till affären för att köpa mjölk.** | She is going to the shop to buy milk. |
| **Jag ringde för att fråga en sak.** | I called to ask something. |

## Att can also mean “that”

Not every **att** introduces an infinitive. It can also join a sentence and mean *that*. In that case, a person or thing does the action in a complete clause, and the verb is not in the infinitive:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag vet att du arbetar.** | I know that you are working. |
| **Jag vill att du arbetar här.** | I want you to work here. |

Compare **att arbeta** (to work), where **arbeta** is an infinitive, with **att du arbetar** (that you are working), where **arbetar** is a present-tense verb.

## Bare infinitives after some other verbs

Modal verbs are the most common pattern for beginners, but some other constructions also use an infinitive without **att**. For example, verbs of seeing or hearing can be followed by a person or thing and then an infinitive:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag såg henne springa.** | I saw her run. |
| **Vi hörde barnen sjunga.** | We heard the children sing. |

The causative verb **låta** (let/make) can also have this pattern: **De lät oss vänta.** (They let us wait.)

## Common mistakes

| Avoid | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Jag vill att läsa.* | **Jag vill läsa.** (I want to read.) | Use a bare infinitive after **vill**. |
| *Vi kan att gå.* | **Vi kan gå.** (We can go.) | Use a bare infinitive after **kan**. |
| *Hon tycker om läsa.* | **Hon tycker om att läsa.** (She likes reading.) | This expression is followed by **att** and an infinitive. |
| *Jag vill att du läsa.* | **Jag vill att du läser.** (I want you to read.) | After **att** meaning “that,” use a finite verb in the clause. |

The infinitive itself does not show tense or the subject. In a sentence, other words show whether the action is happening, planned, or wanted: **Jag läser.** (I am reading.) **Jag vill läsa.** (I want to read.)
