Swedish present tense is called *presens*. It is used for actions happening now, regular activities, facts, and—in the right context—future plans. Unlike English, Swedish does not add a different present-tense ending for **I**, **you**, or **they**.

| Subject | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Jag** (I) | **Jag arbetar.** | I work. |
| **Du** (you) | **Du arbetar.** | You work. |
| **Hon** (she) | **Hon arbetar.** | She works. |
| **Vi** (we) | **Vi arbetar.** | We work. |

## Forming the present tense

Swedish verbs do not change for the subject. The present form stays the same with **jag** (I), **du** (you), **han/hon** (he/she), and **vi/de** (we/they).

Many verbs form the present tense with **-ar** or **-er**. These are useful patterns, but the infinitive does not always tell you which form to use, so learn the present form with the verb.

| Infinitive | Present | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **arbeta** (to work) | **arbetar** | **Jag arbetar på ett kontor.** | I work in an office. |
| **tala** (to speak) | **talar** | **De talar svenska.** | They speak Swedish. |
| **läsa** (to read) | **läser** | **Hon läser en bok.** | She is reading a book. |
| **köpa** (to buy) | **köper** | **Vi köper mat.** | We are buying food. |

Many verbs whose infinitive ends in **-a** form the present with **-ar**, while others use **-er**. Verbs whose infinitive ends in another vowel often add **-r**:

| Infinitive | Present | English |
|---|---|---|
| **bo** (to live) | **bor** | live/lives |
| **tro** (to believe) | **tror** | believe/believes |

Some frequent verbs have forms that are best learned individually:

| Infinitive | Present | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **vara** (to be) | **är** | **Jag är trött.** | I am tired. |
| **ha** (to have) | **har** | **Han har en cykel.** | He has a bicycle. |
| **gå** (to go/walk) | **går** | **Vi går hem.** | We are walking home. |
| **göra** (to do/make) | **gör** | **Vad gör du?** | What are you doing? |
| **komma** (to come) | **kommer** | **Bussen kommer snart.** | The bus is coming soon. |
| **se** (to see) | **ser** | **Jag ser en fågel.** | I see a bird. |
| **säga** (to say) | **säger** | **Hon säger hej.** | She says hello. |

Modal verbs also have present forms and are followed by an infinitive: **Jag kan simma.** (I can swim.) **De vill äta.** (They want to eat.)

## What the present tense means

The same present form can describe something happening now or something that happens regularly. Swedish does not have a separate verb form for the English *am/is/are doing* pattern:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag lagar mat nu.** | I am cooking now. |
| **Jag lagar mat varje kväll.** | I cook every evening. |

The present is also used for general facts and usual situations:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vatten fryser när det är kallt.** | Water freezes when it is cold. |
| **Hon bor i Uppsala.** | She lives in Uppsala. |

The present form can refer to a planned future event when a time or context makes the future meaning clear:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi åker till Malmö i morgon.** | We are going to Malmö tomorrow. |
| **Tåget går klockan åtta.** | The train leaves at eight. |

To emphasize that an activity is in progress right now, Swedish can use **håller på att** (is in the middle of): **Jag håller på att laga middag.** (I am in the middle of making dinner.) This is optional; the ordinary present often works too: **Jag lagar middag.** (I am making dinner.)

## Negatives and questions

In a simple statement, **inte** (not) normally comes after the present-tense verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag arbetar inte i dag.** | I am not working today. |
| **Hon bor inte här.** | She does not live here. |

If a modal verb comes before an infinitive, put **inte** after the modal and before the infinitive: **Jag kan inte komma.** (I cannot come.)

For a yes-or-no question, put the present-tense verb before the subject. Swedish does not use an extra word like English *do* or *does*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Bor du här?** | Do you live here? |
| **Arbetar hon i dag?** | Is she working today? |

In a question with a question word, put the question word first, then the verb, then the subject: **Var arbetar du?** (Where do you work?)

## Common mistakes

| Avoid | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Jag bo här.* | **Jag bor här.** (I live here.) | Use the present form, not the infinitive. |
| *Hon läsa en bok.* | **Hon läser en bok.** (She is reading a book.) | This verb's present form is **läser**. |
| *Vi är bo här.* | **Vi bor här.** (We live here.) | A simple present statement uses the present form of the main verb. |

Remember: one present form works with all subjects, and the meaning—now, regularly, generally, or a planned future—comes from context and time words.
