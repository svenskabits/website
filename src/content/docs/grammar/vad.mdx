The question word **vad** means **what**. Use it to ask about a thing, an activity, or information. It is different from **vem** (who), which asks about a person, and **var** (where), which asks about a place.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vad äter du?** | What are you eating? |
| **Vad gör Anna?** | What is Anna doing? |
| **Vem kommer i dag?** | Who is coming today? |
| **Var arbetar Ali?** | Where does Ali work? |

## Vad in direct questions

When **vad** asks about the object or information in a direct question, it usually comes first, followed by the verb and then the subject:

**vad + verb + subject + the rest**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vad läser Sara?** | What is Sara reading? |
| **Vad vill du dricka?** | What do you want to drink? |
| **Vad heter din lärare?** | What is your teacher's name? |
| **Vad är klockan?** | What time is it? |

Swedish does not add *do* or *does* in these questions. The present-tense verb comes directly after **vad**: **Vad gillar du?** (What do you like?)

Sometimes **vad** itself is the subject—the thing doing or causing the action. In that case, there is no other subject after the verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vad händer?** | What is happening? |
| **Vad luktar gott?** | What smells good? |

Compare **Vad gör du?** (What are you doing?), where **du** is the subject, with **Vad händer?** (What is happening?), where **vad** is the subject.

## Vad, vem, and var

Choose the question word based on the information you want:

| Ask about | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| A thing or action | **Vad köper du?** | What are you buying? |
| A person as the subject | **Vem köper kaffe?** | Who is buying coffee? |
| A person as the object | **Vem träffar du?** | Who are you meeting? |
| A location | **Var köper du kaffe?** | Where are you buying coffee? |

Use **vad** for a name: **Vad heter du?** (What is your name?) To ask about a person, use **vem**: **Vem är läraren?** (Who is the teacher?)

## Vad in an indirect question

An indirect question is inside a larger statement or question. In the clause after **vad**, put the subject before the verb:

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Vad läser du?** | **Jag vet vad du läser.** | What are you reading? / I know what you are reading. |
| **Vad heter läraren?** | **Kan du säga vad läraren heter?** | What is the teacher's name? / Can you tell me what the teacher's name is? |

In **Kan du säga** (Can you tell me), the whole sentence is a direct question, so the verb comes before its subject. Inside it, **vad läraren heter** (what the teacher's name is) is an indirect question, so **läraren** comes before **heter**. An indirect question in a statement usually ends with a period; the whole sentence takes a question mark if it is itself a direct question.

## Short answers

You can answer with a short phrase or a complete sentence:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vad äter du? – En smörgås.** | What are you eating? – A sandwich. |
| **Vad gör ni? – Vi lagar middag.** | What are you doing? – We are making dinner. |

Remember: use **vad** for a thing, action, or information; **vem** for a person; and **var** for a location. In a direct question, put the verb before the subject unless **vad** itself is the subject. In an indirect question, put the subject before the verb after **vad**.
