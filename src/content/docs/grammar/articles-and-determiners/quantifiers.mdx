## Countable and mass nouns

Quantifiers tell us how many things there are or how much of something there is. A **countable noun** names things you can count separately, such as books or chairs. A **mass noun** names something treated as an amount rather than separate items, such as water, milk, or time.

- Use **många** (“many” or “a lot of”) with plural countable nouns: **många böcker** (“many books”).
- Use **få** (“few” or “not many”) with plural countable nouns: **få stolar** (“few chairs”).
- Use **mycket** with mass nouns: depending on the sentence, it can mean “much” or, more naturally in many affirmative sentences, “a lot of.”
- Use **lite** with mass nouns for a small or unspecified amount: often “a little” or “some.” The best English translation depends on context; **lite** does not always mean exactly “a little.”

The quantifier normally comes directly before the noun: **många böcker**, **mycket vatten**, **lite mjölk**. A quantifier does not mean Swedish always needs an article: these phrases do not have one, though a sentence may use an article for other reasons.

## Examples

- **Jag har många böcker.** — I have a lot of books.
- **Det finns få lediga stolar.** — There are few chairs available.
- **Hon dricker mycket vatten.** — She drinks a lot of water.
- **Har du mycket tid?** — Do you have much time?
- **Kan jag få lite mjölk?** — Can I have some milk?
- **Vi har lite kaffe kvar.** — We have a little coffee left.

## Common confusion

Choose between **många/få** and **mycket/lite** by the kind of noun, not by how large the quantity feels: **många äpplen** (“many apples”), but **mycket juice** (“a lot of juice”). **Få** goes with plural countable nouns; **lite** goes with mass nouns. English often prefers “a lot of” over “much” in positive statements, while “much” is common in questions and negatives. For example, **mycket vatten** can be “a lot of water,” but **inte mycket vatten** is naturally “not much water.”

## Useful Expressions

1. **Vi har många gäster i kväll.** — We have a lot of guests tonight.
2. **Det finns många parker här.** — There are many parks here.
3. **Jag har få frågor.** — I have few questions.
4. **Få människor känner till den platsen.** — Few people know about that place.
5. **Han dricker mycket kaffe.** — He drinks a lot of coffee.
6. **Det är mycket snö ute.** — There is a lot of snow outside.
7. **Har du mycket pengar med dig?** — Do you have much money with you?
8. **Jag behöver lite hjälp.** — I need a little help.
9. **Kan vi få lite bröd?** — Can we have some bread?
10. **Det finns lite vatten kvar.** — There is a little water left.
