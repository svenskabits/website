## When Swedish uses no article

Swedish often leaves out an article when a plural noun or an uncountable noun is used generally or refers to an unspecified amount. English works similarly in many of these cases:

- **Jag köper äpplen.** — I’m buying apples.
- **Hon dricker kaffe.** — She drinks coffee.
- **Barn behöver sömn.** — Children need sleep.

But a singular countable noun usually needs **en** or **ett**, even when English does not use an article:

- **Jag köper ett äpple.** — I’m buying an apple.
- **Hon är läkare.** — She is a doctor.

The second sentence is a common exception: a profession or role can appear without an article after **vara** (“to be”) or **som** (“as”): **Han är student** (“He is a student”), **Hon arbetar som lärare** (“She works as a teacher”). If the role noun has a describing adjective, an article is normally used: **Hon är en erfaren läkare** (“She is an experienced doctor”).

Some fixed expressions also have no article, for example **gå till fots** (“go on foot”). These expressions are limited; they do not mean that singular countable nouns can generally stand alone.

## Common confusion

Do not omit **en** or **ett** just because English omits “a” in a particular expression. Bare role nouns such as **är läkare** are a specific pattern; for an ordinary singular item, say **Jag har en bil** (“I have a car”), not *Jag har bil*.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag dricker kaffe på morgonen.** — I drink coffee in the morning.
2. **Vi äter ris till middag.** — We eat rice for dinner.
3. **Barn behöver sömn.** — Children need sleep.
4. **Böcker kostar pengar.** — Books cost money.
5. **Hon läser tidningar på tåget.** — She reads newspapers on the train.
6. **De säljer möbler här.** — They sell furniture here.
7. **Min bror är läkare.** — My brother is a doctor.
8. **Sara arbetar som lärare.** — Sara works as a teacher.
9. **Vi går till fots.** — We go on foot.
10. **Jag behöver hjälp.** — I need help.
