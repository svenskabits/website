title: Indefinite Article
description: Learn when to use en or ett with a singular indefinite noun and how this differs from the definite form.

## What en and ett do

Swedish uses **en** or **ett** before a singular noun when referring to something indefinite—roughly, “a” or “an” in English:

- **en bil** — a car
- **ett hus** — a house

Use the article that belongs with the Swedish noun. Put it directly before the noun, or before an adjective that describes the noun: **en bil**, **ett hus**, **en röd bil**, **ett rött hus**. The adjective changes with the noun too, but the choice of **en** or **ett** is based on the noun, not the English translation.

## Indefinite and definite forms

The indefinite article is a separate word. In the definite singular, Swedish usually attaches an ending to the noun instead:

| Indefinite (“a/an”) | Definite (“the”) |
| --- | --- |
| **en katt** — a cat | **katten** — the cat |
| **ett äpple** — an apple | **äpplet** — the apple |

So use **en katt**, not *en katten*, when you mean “a cat.” The definite ending replaces the indefinite article; it does not follow it.

## Common confusion

English chooses **a** or **an** by the next sound. Swedish **en** or **ett** depends on the Swedish noun instead: **ett äpple** takes **ett** even though *apple* begins with a vowel sound in English. Learn the article with each noun.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag har en hund.** — I have a dog.
2. **Hon läser en bok.** — She is reading a book.
3. **Vi bor i ett hus.** — We live in a house.
4. **Han köper ett äpple.** — He is buying an apple.
5. **Det står en stol här.** — There is a chair here.
6. **Jag behöver ett pass.** — I need a passport.
7. **De har en trädgård.** — They have a garden.
8. **Barnet äter ett päron.** — The child is eating a pear.
9. **Kan du öppna en dörr?** — Can you open a door?
10. **Vi väntar på ett tåg.** — We are waiting for a train.
