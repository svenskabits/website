## What the present tense means

Swedish uses the present tense for actions happening now, repeated habits, and general truths:

- **Jag läser just nu.** — I am reading right now.
- **Hon arbetar varje dag.** — She works every day.
- **Vatten kokar vid 100 °C.** — Water boils at 100 °C.

The present tense can also describe a scheduled future event, often with a time expression:

- **Tåget går klockan åtta imorgon.** — The train leaves at eight tomorrow.

## The verb form stays the same

Unlike English, Swedish verbs do not change to agree with the subject. The present form **arbetar** is the same with *jag* (I), *du* (you), and *de* (they):

- **Jag arbetar.** — I work.
- **Du arbetar.** — You work.
- **De arbetar.** — They work.

Many Swedish present-tense forms end in **-ar**, **-er**, or **-r**. The ending depends on the verb; the different verb groups are covered in the next lesson.

### Common confusion: one form, two English translations

Swedish does not use a separate verb form to distinguish the English simple present from the present progressive. For example, **jag läser** can mean “I read” or “I am reading”; context tells you which meaning fits. A phrase such as **just nu** (“right now”) or **varje dag** (“every day”) can make the intended meaning clear.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag arbetar på ett kontor.** — I work in an office.
2. **Du läser just nu.** — You are reading right now.
3. **Hon dricker kaffe varje morgon.** — She drinks coffee every morning.
4. **Vi bor nära stationen.** — We live near the station.
5. **De tränar på tisdagar.** — They exercise on Tuesdays.
6. **Vatten kokar vid 100 °C.** — Water boils at 100 °C.
7. **Bussen går om tio minuter.** — The bus leaves in ten minutes.
8. **Filmen börjar klockan sju ikväll.** — The movie starts at seven tonight.
9. **Jag förstår frågan.** — I understand the question.
10. **Barnen spelar i parken.** — The children are playing in the park.
