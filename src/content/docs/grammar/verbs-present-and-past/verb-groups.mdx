---
title: Verb Groups
description: Learn the four main Swedish verb groups and how their present, past, and supine forms commonly differ.
---

Swedish verbs are often organized into four groups according to how their forms change. The groups are useful patterns, but they do not let you predict every verb with certainty. Learn each verb with its principal forms: the **infinitive**, **present**, **past**, and **supine**.

## The four groups

| Group | Infinitive | Present | Past | Supine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | tala (speak) | talar | talade | talat |
| 2 | stänga (close) | stänger | stängde | stängt |
| 2 | köpa (buy) | köper | köpte | köpt |
| 2 | läsa (read) | läser | läste | läst |
| 3 | bo (live) | bor | bodde | bott |
| 4 | skriva (write) | skriver | skrev | skrivit |

### Group 1: present **-ar**

Regular group 1 verbs typically form the present with **-ar**, the past with **-ade**, and the supine with **-at**:

- **Jag talar svenska.** — I speak Swedish.
- **Vi talade med läraren.** — We spoke with the teacher.
- **Vi har talat med läraren.** — We have spoken with the teacher.

### Group 2: present **-er**, past **-de** or **-te**

Group 2 verbs commonly have **-er** in the present. Some form the past with **-de**, as in **stängde**. Others use **-te**, often when the verb stem ends in an unvoiced consonant such as **p** or **s**: **köpte**, **läste**. The supine commonly ends in **-t**:

- **Hon stänger dörren.** — She closes the door.
- **Hon stängde dörren.** — She closed the door.
- **Jag köper bröd.** — I buy bread.
- **Jag köpte bröd.** — I bought bread.
- **De läser tidningen.** — They read the newspaper.
- **De har läst tidningen.** — They have read the newspaper.

### Group 3: short verbs with past **-dde**

Many short verbs whose infinitive does not end in **-a** belong to group 3. They commonly have **-r** in the present, **-dde** in the past, and **-tt** in the supine:

- **Vi bor i Malmö.** — We live in Malmö.
- **Vi bodde i Malmö förut.** — We lived in Malmö before.
- **Vi har bott i Malmö.** — We have lived in Malmö.

### Group 4: strong verbs and changing stems

Strong verbs commonly change their stem vowel in the past. For example, **skriva** becomes **skrev** in the past and **skrivit** in the supine. Group 4 includes varied forms, so do not expect one suffix rule to predict every verb:

- **Han skriver ett brev.** — He is writing a letter.
- **Han skrev ett brev igår.** — He wrote a letter yesterday.
- **Han har skrivit ett brev.** — He has written a letter.

## Common confusion: past and supine

The **past** form can stand on its own: **Jag köpte bröd** (“I bought bread”). The **supine** is used after **har** to make the perfect: **Jag har köpt bröd** (“I have bought bread”). These are different forms, even when they look similar.

Because the groups have exceptions and group 4 forms vary, it is safer to memorize a verb together with its principal forms than to guess from its infinitive. The separate lesson on strong and irregular verbs covers those forms in more detail.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag talar svenska.** — I speak Swedish.
2. **Hon talade med sin vän.** — She spoke with her friend.
3. **Vi har talat om filmen.** — We have talked about the movie.
4. **Han stänger fönstret.** — He closes the window.
5. **De stängde butiken tidigt.** — They closed the shop early.
6. **Jag köper en biljett.** — I am buying a ticket.
7. **Vi har köpt kaffe.** — We have bought coffee.
8. **Barnen bor nära skolan.** — The children live near the school.
9. **Hon bodde här förut.** — She lived here before.
10. **Jag har skrivit ett mejl.** — I have written an email.
