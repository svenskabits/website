---
title: Particle Verbs
description: Learn how Swedish verbs combine with short, stressed particles to create common meanings.
---

Swedish often combines a verb with a short word called a **particle**. The particle is stressed, and together the verb and particle often mean something different from the verb alone.

## The pattern

A particle verb is usually written as two words. In a simple sentence, the particle follows the verb:

- *gå* means “walk” or “go”; *gå **ut*** means “go out.”
- *komma* means “come”; *komma **tillbaka*** means “come back.”
- *tycka* means “think”; *tycka **om*** means “like.”

The particle often carries the main stress, as in *gå **UT*** and *tycka **OM***. The whole combination has to be learned as a unit, because its meaning is not always predictable from the verb by itself.

Some combinations depend on what follows. *Sätta på* can mean “switch on” with an appliance, or “put on” with clothing:

- Sätt på lampan. — Switch on the lamp.
- Sätt på dig jackan. — Put on your jacket.

## Examples

- Jag går **ut** nu. — I’m going out now.
- Hon kommer **tillbaka** i morgon. — She’s coming back tomorrow.
- Vi tycker **om** den här låten. — We like this song.
- Ta **av** dig skorna, tack. — Take off your shoes, please.
- Kan du sätta **på** tv:n? — Can you switch on the TV?

**Particle or preposition?** In a particle verb, the stressed particle helps form the verb’s meaning (*tycka **om***, “like”). A preposition instead introduces a phrase of its own, as in *gå till skolan* (“go to school”).

## Useful Expressions

- Jag går **ut** en stund. — I’m going out for a while.
- Kom **tillbaka** snart! — Come back soon!
- Tycker du **om** kaffe? — Do you like coffee?
- Jag tycker **om** att läsa. — I like reading.
- Ta **av** dig mössan. — Take off your hat.
- Han tar **av** sig skorna. — He takes off his shoes.
- Sätt **på** lampan. — Switch on the lamp.
- Sätt **på** dig jackan. — Put on your jacket.
- Stäng **av** datorn. — Turn off the computer.
- De går **ut** tillsammans. — They go out together.
