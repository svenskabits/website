---
title: Past Tense
description: Learn how Swedish preteritum describes actions and states in the past.
---

Swedish **preteritum** (the simple past tense) describes an action or state in the past. It is often used with a finished time expression such as **igår** (“yesterday”) or **förra veckan** (“last week”).

## Forming the past tense

The past form depends on the verb. Many **group 1** verbs form preteritum with **-ade**:

| Infinitive | Preteritum | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **prata** (talk) | **pratade** | **Vi pratade länge.** — We talked for a long time. |
| **jobba** (work) | **jobbade** | **Hon jobbade igår.** — She worked yesterday. |

Many **group 2** verbs use **-de** or **-te**. For example, verbs with a voiced final sound often take **-de**, while some with an unvoiced sound take **-te**:

- **ringa** → **ringde**: **Jag ringde min vän.** — I called my friend.
- **köpa** → **köpte**: **De köpte en ny cykel.** — They bought a new bicycle.

Some common verbs have irregular past forms, which need to be learned individually:

- **vara** → **var**: **Han var hemma.** — He was at home.
- **gå** → **gick**: **Vi gick till affären.** — We went to the shop.
- **ha** → **hade**: **Jag hade en fråga.** — I had a question.

The verb form does **not** change for the person. For example, **jag pratade**, **du pratade**, and **de pratade** all use the same past form.

## When to use preteritum

Use preteritum to present something as happening or being true in the past. A finished time expression often makes the time clear, but it is not required:

- **Vi såg en film igår.** — We watched a film yesterday.
- **Hon bodde i Malmö förra året.** — She lived in Malmö last year.
- **Barnen sov.** — The children were asleep.

## Common confusion: preteritum and perfect

Preteritum often appears with a finished past time: **Jag köpte den igår.** — “I bought it yesterday.” The perfect, **har + supinum**, connects a past event to the present or describes experience without focusing on a finished time: **Jag har köpt den.** — “I have bought it.” A separate lesson, **Preteritum vs. Perfect**, compares the two forms in more detail.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag vaknade tidigt.** — I woke up early.
2. **Vi åt frukost hemma.** — We ate breakfast at home.
3. **Hon läste en bok igår.** — She read a book yesterday.
4. **De spelade fotboll efter skolan.** — They played football after school.
5. **Du stängde fönstret.** — You closed the window.
6. **Han blev sjuk förra veckan.** — He became ill last week.
7. **Jag skrev ett mejl.** — I wrote an email.
8. **Vi bodde nära stationen.** — We lived near the station.
9. **Mötet började klockan nio.** — The meeting started at nine o’clock.
10. **De kom hem sent.** — They came home late.
