## The infinitive form

The **infinitive** is the basic form of a verb, the form you find in a dictionary. Many Swedish infinitives end in **-a**, such as **läsa** (to read) and **arbeta** (to work), but not all do: **bo** (to live), **gå** (to walk/go), and **se** (to see) are also infinitives.

Swedish often uses **att** before an infinitive when English uses “to”:

- **Jag tycker om att läsa.** — I like to read.
- **Hon gillar att bada.** — She likes swimming.
- **De hoppas att vinna.** — They hope to win.

Here, **att** comes directly before the infinitive. Some verbs and expressions, including **tycka om** and **gilla**, take **att** before another verb.

## Infinitives for purpose

Use **för att** before an infinitive to explain the purpose of an action—“to” or “in order to” in English:

- **Jag går till biblioteket för att studera.** — I’m going to the library to study.
- **Hon ringde för att fråga en sak.** — She called to ask something.

Other phrases can also introduce an infinitive. For example, **utan att** means “without” doing something, and **istället för att** means “instead of” doing something:

- **Han gick utan att säga hej.** — He left without saying hello.
- **Vi promenerade istället för att ta bussen.** — We walked instead of taking the bus.

## When *att* is omitted

Do not put **att** before every infinitive. After a modal verb, it is normally omitted:

- **Jag kan simma.** — I can swim.
- **Vi vill stanna.** — We want to stay.

The separate modal-verb topic covers this pattern in more detail.

### Common confusion

Infinitive **att** comes before a verb in its basic form: **Jag tycker om att läsa** (“I like to read”). The same word **att** can also mean “that” before a full clause: **Jag vet att hon läser** (“I know that she is reading”). In the second sentence, **att** introduces a clause with its own subject, **hon**.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag älskar att dansa.** — I love to dance.
2. **Han tycker om att laga mat.** — He likes cooking.
3. **Vi försöker att komma i tid.** — We are trying to arrive on time.
4. **Hon vill lära sig att spela piano.** — She wants to learn to play the piano.
5. **De stannade hemma för att vila.** — They stayed home to rest.
6. **Jag öppnade fönstret för att få frisk luft.** — I opened the window to get some fresh air.
7. **Hon gick utan att väcka barnet.** — She left without waking the child.
8. **Han valde att vänta.** — He chose to wait.
9. **Vi behöver prata.** — We need to talk.
10. **Kan du hjälpa mig att bära väskan?** — Can you help me carry the bag?
