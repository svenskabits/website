The passive lets you make the person or thing affected by an action the focus of a sentence. It is especially useful when the person doing the action is unknown, unimportant, or obvious from the context. In formal Swedish, it is also a common way to describe processes and present information objectively.

## Choosing a passive form

Swedish has two main ways to form a passive: add **-s** to the verb, or use **bli** with a past participle. Both can be used in many tenses, but they often create a different emphasis.

| Form | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| Present passive with **-s** | Skogen **skyddas**. | The forest is being protected / The forest is protected. |
| Past passive with **-s** | Skogen **skyddades**. | The forest was protected. |
| Perfect passive with **-s** | Skogen **har skyddats**. | The forest has been protected. |
| **bli** + past participle | Skogen **blir skyddad**. | The forest is being protected. |
| Past **bli** + past participle | Skogen **blev skyddad**. | The forest was protected. |
| Perfect **bli** + past participle | Skogen **har blivit skyddad**. | The forest has been protected. |

The **-s** form is compact and frequent in formal, written, and general statements. The **bli** passive often presents an action as an event or change, and it can make the event itself more prominent. These are tendencies, not strict rules: context and the verb matter, and the two forms can sometimes describe the same event.

Compare:

- **Nya regler införs nästa år.** — New rules will be introduced next year. (A neutral announcement.)
- **Nya regler blir införda nästa år.** — New rules will be introduced next year. (Focus on the change taking place; this wording is less common than the **-s** form here.)
- **Bron byggdes på 1950-talet.** — The bridge was built in the 1950s. (A concise account of what happened.)
- **Bron blev byggd på bara sex månader.** — The bridge was built in just six months. (The completed event is highlighted.)

In the **bli** passive, the past participle agrees with the subject in number and gender:

| Subject | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **ett** word | **Ett hus blev byggt.** | A house was built. |
| **en** word | **En bro blev byggd.** | A bridge was built. |
| Plural | **Flera hus blev byggda.** | Several houses were built. |

## Leaving out or naming the agent

The person or organization doing the action is called the **agent**. It is often left out because it is unknown or does not matter:

- **Provet rättas automatiskt.** — The test is marked automatically.
- **Beslutet fattades i går.** — The decision was made yesterday.

If the agent is important, introduce it with **av** (by):

- **Rapporten skrevs av en oberoende forskare.** — The report was written by an independent researcher.
- **Området skyddas av kommunen.** — The area is protected by the municipality.

An active sentence is often clearer when the agent is the main point. A passive sentence is useful when the affected person or thing deserves more attention:

- **Kommunen skyddar området.** — The municipality protects the area. (The municipality is the focus.)
- **Området skyddas av kommunen.** — The area is protected by the municipality. (The area is the focus.)

## General statements and instructions

Passive forms can express rules, routines, or processes without naming a particular person. This is common in notices, instructions, reports, and explanations:

- **Ansökan skickas in digitalt.** — The application is submitted digitally.
- **Dörrarna låses klockan tio.** — The doors are locked at ten.
- **Plasten sorteras separat.** — The plastic is sorted separately.
- **Fröna planteras på våren.** — The seeds are planted in spring.

English often uses “you,” “we,” or “they” in similar instructions. Swedish can use a passive instead, especially in formal or impersonal writing.

## Impersonal passive

An **impersonal passive** is possible with some verbs that do not have an object to become the subject. The verb is in the **-s** form, and **det** fills the subject position. Here **det** does not refer to a particular thing:

- **Det arbetas hårt inför konferensen.** — People are working hard ahead of the conference.
- **Det dansades hela natten.** — People danced all night.
- **Det talas ofta om klimatet i debatten.** — Climate is often talked about in the debate.

The last example keeps the preposition **om** after **tala**. A prepositional object does not automatically become the subject of a passive sentence; the impersonal pattern is often the natural choice.

## Verbs with a preposition

When a verb takes a direct object and a preposition, the direct object can become the subject of a passive sentence. The preposition stays with the rest of the phrase:

- **Flera organisationer kritiserade förslaget för dess höga kostnad.** — Several organizations criticized the proposal for its high cost.
- **Förslaget kritiserades för sin höga kostnad.** — The proposal was criticized for its high cost.

Some verbs are used with a preposition but no direct object. With these, an impersonal passive is often more natural:

- **Man diskuterar ofta klimatet.** — People often discuss the climate.
- **Klimatet diskuteras ofta.** — The climate is often discussed.
- **Man talar ofta om klimatet.** — People often talk about the climate.
- **Det talas ofta om klimatet.** — The climate is often talked about.

The different patterns depend on how the verb works; do not assume that every noun after a preposition can become the subject.

## Two objects

With some verbs that take two objects, either object can sometimes be made the subject of a passive. The choice depends on what you want to emphasize and on what sounds natural with that verb:

- **Företaget erbjöd henne en tjänst.** — The company offered her a job.
- **Hon erbjöds en tjänst.** — She was offered a job.
- **En tjänst erbjöds henne.** — A job was offered to her. (More formal.)

The active version is often the clearest if both people and things need to be mentioned and neither needs special emphasis.

## Passive with modal verbs and infinitives

After a modal verb such as **kan**, **måste**, or **bör**, use the passive infinitive. The passive marker **-s** remains on the infinitive:

- **Våtmarkerna måste skyddas.** — The wetlands must be protected.
- **Resultatet kan påverkas av vädret.** — The result can be affected by the weather.
- **Ansökan bör skickas före fredag.** — The application should be sent before Friday.

The **bli** passive can also follow a modal when the event or change is emphasized:

- **Problemet måste bli löst så snart som möjligt.** — The problem must be solved as soon as possible.

## Tense, negation, and word order

The auxiliary verb carries tense in a compound passive, while the passive verb form stays in place. In a main clause, the finite verb follows the usual second-position pattern. In a subordinate clause, the subject comes before the finite verb, and **inte** usually comes before it:

- **Arbetet har inte avslutats än.** — The work has not been finished yet.
- **Vi hoppas att arbetet inte har avslutats än.** — We hope that the work has not been finished yet.
- **I rapporten beskrivs flera möjliga lösningar.** — Several possible solutions are described in the report.

In the last sentence, **I rapporten** comes first, so the finite verb **beskrivs** comes before the subject **flera möjliga lösningar**.

## Passive meaning and result states

Do not confuse an action with the state that results from it. **Bli** commonly presents a change or event; **vara** with a participle describes a state. Some **vara** phrases are passive in meaning, while others use an adjective:

- **Dörren låstes av vaktmästaren.** — The door was locked by the caretaker. (An action.)
- **Dörren blev låst av vaktmästaren.** — The door was locked by the caretaker. (The event or change is highlighted.)
- **Dörren är låst.** — The door is locked. (Its current state.)
- **Dörren är öppen.** — The door is open. (**Öppen** is an adjective; this is not a passive.)

The surrounding context helps show whether a participle describes an event or a resulting state.
