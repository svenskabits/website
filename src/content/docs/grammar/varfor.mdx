Use **varför** to ask for a reason. It means **why** and does not change form.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Varför är du trött?** | Why are you tired? |
| **Varför går hon hem nu?** | Why is she going home now? |
| **Varför ringde du inte?** | Why didn't you call? |
| **Varför måste vi vänta?** | Why do we have to wait? |

## Word order in direct questions

In a direct question, put **varför** first, then the finite verb, then the subject:

**varför + verb + subject + the rest**

The finite verb is the verb that carries the tense. With a modal verb such as **kan** or **måste**, the modal comes before the subject and the next verb stays in the infinitive:

- **Varför arbetar du hemma i dag?** — Why are you working from home today?
- **Varför stannade bussen här?** — Why did the bus stop here?
- **Varför kan hon inte komma?** — Why can't she come?
- **Varför ska de flytta?** — Why are they going to move?

The word order is the same whether the question is in the present, past, or future. In a negative question, **inte** normally comes after the subject and before the rest of the sentence: **Varför kommer du inte?** (Why aren't you coming?)

## **Varför** in an indirect question

An indirect question is part of a larger sentence. After **varför**, use ordinary statement order: put the subject before the verb. Do not use the verb-before-subject order of a direct question inside the indirect question.

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Varför är Sara hemma?** | **Jag vet inte varför Sara är hemma.** | Why is Sara at home? / I don't know why Sara is at home. |
| **Varför gick tåget tidigt?** | **Han frågar varför tåget gick tidigt.** | Why did the train leave early? / He asks why the train left early. |
| **Varför kan du inte sova?** | **Kan du berätta varför du inte kan sova?** | Why can't you sleep? / Can you tell me why you can't sleep? |

In an indirect question, **inte** normally comes before the verb: **varför du inte kan sova**. The whole sentence takes a question mark only if it is itself a question, as in **Kan du berätta varför du inte kan sova?**

## Answering with a reason

Common ways to give a reason are **för att** and **eftersom**, both meaning **because** when they introduce a clause. **Därför att** also means **because**. These words introduce a reason clause, where the subject comes before the verb:

- **Varför tar du ledigt? – Jag tar ledigt eftersom jag är sjuk.** — Why are you taking time off? – I'm taking time off because I'm ill.
- **Varför går ni hem? – Vi går hem för att barnen är trötta.** — Why are you going home? – We're going home because the children are tired.
- **Varför stannar Erik hemma? – Därför att han har feber.** — Why is Erik staying home? – Because he has a fever.

**Eftersom** can begin a sentence. A comma may be used to mark the end of the opening reason clause:

- **Eftersom det regnar tar vi bussen.** — Since it's raining, we'll take the bus.

When the reason clause follows the main clause, a comma is usually not needed: **Vi tar bussen eftersom det regnar.** (We'll take the bus because it's raining.)

## **Därför** means “for that reason”

Do not confuse **därför** with **därför att**. **Därför** means **therefore** or **for that reason** and can stand at the beginning of a main clause. When it comes first, the verb comes before the subject:

- **Jag har feber. Därför stannar jag hemma.** — I have a fever. Therefore, I'm staying at home.
- **Bussen är sen. Därför kommer vi inte i tid.** — The bus is late. That's why we won't arrive on time.

Compare:

- **Jag stannar hemma därför att jag är sjuk.** — I'm staying at home because I'm ill. (**därför att** introduces the reason.)
- **Jag är sjuk. Därför stannar jag hemma.** — I'm ill. That's why I'm staying at home. (**därför** means “for that reason.”)

## **För att** with a reason or a purpose

When **för att** means **because**, it introduces a clause with a subject and a conjugated verb: **Jag tar en paus för att jag är trött.** (I'm taking a break because I'm tired.)

It can also introduce a purpose, meaning **in order to** or simply **to**. In that use, it is followed by an infinitive, usually with no stated subject of its own:

- **Jag tar en paus för att vila.** — I'm taking a break to rest.
- **Hon går till apoteket för att köpa medicin.** — She is going to the pharmacy to buy medicine.

Use the subject and verb after **för att** when explaining a reason; use the infinitive when explaining what someone does something in order to do.

Remember: a direct **varför** question has the verb before the subject; an indirect question has the subject before the verb. To give a reason, use **för att**, **eftersom**, or **därför att**. **Därför** on its own means “therefore” or “that's why.”
