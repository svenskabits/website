Swedish nouns form their plural in several ways. The endings **-or**, **-ar**, **-er**, and **-n** are common, and some nouns have no visible ending in the indefinite plural. The noun's en- or ett-group can offer a clue, but it does not predict every plural. Learn the plural form with each noun.

| Singular | Indefinite plural | Definite plural | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **en flicka** | **flickor** | **flickorna** | a girl; girls; the girls |
| **en bil** | **bilar** | **bilarna** | a car; cars; the cars |
| **en film** | **filmer** | **filmerna** | a film; films; the films |
| **ett äpple** | **äpplen** | **äpplena** | an apple; apples; the apples |
| **ett hus** | **hus** | **husen** | a house; houses; the houses |

The definite plural means *the* plus a plural noun. The examples show common patterns; definite plural endings also need to be learned with the noun.

## Common indefinite plural patterns

### -or

Many en-words ending in **-a** form the plural with **-or**. The final **-a** is usually replaced:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en flicka → flickor** | a girl → girls |
| **en blomma → blommor** | a flower → flowers |

### -ar

Many en-words form the plural with **-ar**. This is common with several noun patterns, including short nouns ending in a consonant:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en bil → bilar** | a car → cars |
| **en stol → stolar** | a chair → chairs |

### -er

Many other en-words form the plural with **-er**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en film → filmer** | a film → films |
| **en station → stationer** | a station → stations |

The singular ending alone is not enough to know whether a noun takes **-ar** or **-er**. For example, both **en bil → bilar** (a car → cars) and **en film → filmer** (a film → films) end in a consonant but use different plural endings.

### -n

Many ett-words that end in a vowel form the plural with **-n**. A final **-e** is often dropped before **-n**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **ett äpple → äpplen** | an apple → apples |
| **ett piano → pianon** | a piano → pianos |

### No visible ending

Many ett-words ending in a consonant have the same form in the singular and the indefinite plural. No-ending plurals are not limited to ett-words: some en-words, especially many nouns ending in **-are**, also keep the same form.

| Singular | Indefinite plural | Definite plural | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ett hus** | **hus** | **husen** | a house; houses; the houses |
| **ett bord** | **bord** | **borden** | a table; tables; the tables |
| **en lärare** | **lärare** | **lärarna** | a teacher; teachers; the teachers |

The indefinite plural has no visible ending in these examples, but the definite plural does have an ending.

## Learn each noun's forms

These endings are useful tendencies, not fixed rules. Nouns can use a different ending, change their stem, or have spelling changes when they form the plural. The definite plural ending also varies. When you learn a noun, record its singular and plural together; include the definite plural if you need to talk about specific groups.

In the indefinite plural, do not add **en** or **ett**: **flickor** (girls), **hus** (houses). Use a number or another word before the plural when needed: **två bilar** (two cars), **många filmer** (many films).

Remember the five patterns: **-or**, **-ar**, **-er**, **-n**, and no visible ending. Use them as clues, then check and learn the noun's actual plural.
