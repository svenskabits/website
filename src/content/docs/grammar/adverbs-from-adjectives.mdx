Many Swedish adverbs that describe **how** someone does something are formed from adjectives by adding **-t**. The resulting form often looks like the adjective's form for an ett-word:

| Adjective | Adverb | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| snabb (fast) | snabbt | **Hon springer snabbt.** | She runs fast. |
| lugn (calm) | lugnt | **Han talar lugnt.** | He speaks calmly. |
| tydlig (clear) | tydligt | **Säg det tydligt.** | Say it clearly. |
| vacker (beautiful) | vackert | **Hon sjunger vackert.** | She sings beautifully. |
| hård (hard) | hårt | **De arbetar hårt.** | They work hard. |

## The adverb does not agree

An adjective describes a noun and changes form to agree with that noun. An adverb describes an action or another description, and its form does not change to match the subject. In many cases, the adjective's ett-form and the adverb have the same spelling, but they do different jobs:

| Adjective before a noun | Adverb describing an action |
|---|---|
| **ett snabbt tåg** — a fast train | **Tåget går snabbt.** — The train goes fast. |
| **en tydlig röst** — a clear voice | **Hon talar tydligt.** — She speaks clearly. |
| **ett vackert hus** — a beautiful house | **Huset ligger vackert.** — The house is beautifully situated. |

The adverb stays the same even when the subject is plural or has a different noun gender:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Barnet springer snabbt.** | The child runs fast. |
| **Barnen springer snabbt.** | The children run fast. |
| **Hon talar tydligt.** | She speaks clearly. |
| **De talar tydligt.** | They speak clearly. |

## Spelling

Many adjectives form adverbs by adding **-t**. Some forms also involve a spelling change, so learn the adverb with the adjective:

| Adjective | Adverb | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| glad (happy) | glatt | **Hon vinkar glatt.** | She waves happily. |
| kall (cold) | kallt | **Det blåser kallt.** | The wind is cold. |
| fri (free) | fritt | **Barnen kan röra sig fritt.** | The children can move freely. |

Adjectives ending in **-lig** or **-isk** also commonly form adverbs with **-t**:

| Adjective | Adverb | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| vänlig (friendly) | vänligt | **Hon svarar vänligt.** | She answers kindly. |
| praktisk (practical) | praktiskt | **Hon tänker praktiskt.** | She thinks practically. |

The exact form is not always predictable from spelling alone, so learn it with the adjective when it is new.

## Adverbs that do not add -t

Some adjectives and adverbs have the same form. A common example is **bra** (good / well):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en bra film** | a good film |
| **ett bra svar** | a good answer |
| **Hon sjunger bra.** | She sings well. |

The meaning and the word being described help you recognize the function. In **en bra film**, **bra** describes the noun *film*. In **sjunger bra**, it describes how she sings.

## Where the adverb goes

An adverb of manner usually comes after the verb, and often after the object if there is one:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Han kör försiktigt.** | He drives carefully. |
| **Hon läser texten högt.** | She reads the text aloud. |
| **De gör jobbet noggrant.** | They do the job carefully. |

Adverbs can also describe adjectives. **Mycket** (very) is an adverb in the examples below; it makes the adjective stronger:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Huset är mycket stort.** | The house is very big. |
| **Hon är mycket glad.** | She is very happy. |

Here, **stort** and **glad** describe the house and the person, so they are adjectives. **Mycket** describes how big or happy, so it is an adverb.

## Common mistake

Do not add **-t** to an adverb to match a plural subject. The adverb is unchanged:

| Avoid | Use | English |
|---|---|---|
| *Barnen springer snabba.* | **Barnen springer snabbt.** | The children run fast. |

Use the adjective to describe a noun, and use the adverb to describe how an action happens. Many adverbs are made with **-t**, but some common ones, such as **bra**, do not change.
