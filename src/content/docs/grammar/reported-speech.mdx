Use reported speech to tell someone what another person said, thought, asked, or requested without repeating their exact words. Swedish often introduces a reported statement with **att** (that). A reported clause is subordinate, so it uses subordinate word order.

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| Sara säger: ”Jag är trött.” — Sara says, “I’m tired.” | Sara säger att hon är trött. — Sara says that she is tired. |
| Sara sa: ”Jag är trött.” — Sara said, “I’m tired.” | Sara sa att hon var trött. — Sara said that she was tired. |

Quotation marks show a person’s exact words. In reported speech, the words are part of the reporter’s sentence, so pronouns and sometimes verb tenses change.

## Word order in reported statements

After **att**, put the subject before the verb. Sentence adverbs such as **inte** (not), **aldrig** (never), and **ofta** (often) usually come before the finite verb—the verb that carries the tense or is first in a verb phrase.

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
|---|---|
| ”Jag kommer inte i dag.” — “I’m not coming today.” | Hon sa att hon inte kom den dagen. — She said that she wasn’t coming that day. |
| ”Jag har aldrig varit där.” — “I have never been there.” | Han sa att han aldrig hade varit där. — He said that he had never been there. |
| ”Vi kan kanske hjälpa dig.” — “Perhaps we can help you.” | De sa att de kanske kunde hjälpa mig. — They said that they might be able to help me. |

Do not use main-clause word order inside the **att**-clause. For example, say **Hon sa att hon inte kunde komma**, not *Hon sa att kunde hon inte komma*.

## Tense changes after a past reporting verb

When the reporting verb is in the past, Swedish commonly moves the tense in the reported clause back as well. This is sometimes called **backshift**. The change helps show that the words were spoken in the past; it is not a mechanical rule, and the tense may stay the same when the information is still true or relevant.

| Direct words | Report after a past-tense verb |
|---|---|
| ”Jag bor i Uppsala.” — “I live in Uppsala.” | Han sa att han bodde i Uppsala. — He said that he lived in Uppsala. |
| ”Jag arbetar hemma.” — “I work from home.” | Hon berättade att hon arbetade hemma. — She said that she worked from home. |
| ”Jag har tappat nyckeln.” — “I have lost the key.” | Han sa att han hade tappat nyckeln. — He said that he had lost the key. |
| ”Jag ska ringa i morgon.” — “I’ll call tomorrow.” | Hon sa att hon skulle ringa nästa dag. — She said that she would call the next day. |
| ”Jag kan komma senare.” — “I can come later.” | Han sa att han kunde komma senare. — He said that he could come later. |

The past perfect (**hade** + supine) usually stays in the past perfect when reported:

- **”Jag hade redan ätit.” — “I had already eaten.”**
- **Hon sa att hon redan hade ätit. — She said that she had already eaten.**

The tense can remain present when the statement is still true, especially for general facts:

- **Läraren sa att jorden kretsar runt solen. — The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the sun.**

With **sa** (said), **berättade** (told), and similar past reporting verbs, either present or past may be natural when the information is still current. Choose the tense that fits the meaning and context.

## Change the point of view when needed

Pronouns refer to the people in the original situation. Change them to match the reporter’s point of view:

| Exact words | Report |
|---|---|
| Erik sa till mig: ”Jag ringer dig.” — Erik said to me, “I’ll call you.” | Erik sa att han skulle ringa mig. — Erik said that he would call me. |
| Anna sa: ”Vi träffar er här i morgon.” — Anna said, “We’ll meet you here tomorrow.” | Anna sa att de skulle träffa oss där nästa dag. — Anna said that they would meet us there the next day. |

Words for time and place may also change if the reporter is speaking from a different time or place. For example, **i dag** (today) can become **den dagen** (that day), **i morgon** (tomorrow) can become **nästa dag** (the next day), and **här** (here) can become **där** (there). These changes depend on the situation; keep the original word if it still fits the reporter’s point of view.

## Reported questions

Reported questions use subordinate word order, just like other reported clauses. Keep a question word such as **var** (where), **när** (when), or **varför** (why). For a yes/no question, use **om** (whether/if).

| Direct question | Reported question |
|---|---|
| ”Var bor du?” — “Where do you live?” | Han frågade var jag bodde. — He asked where I lived. |
| ”Har du läst artikeln?” — “Have you read the article?” | Hon frågade om jag hade läst artikeln. — She asked whether I had read the article. |
| ”Varför fungerar inte appen?” — “Why doesn’t the app work?” | De frågade varför appen inte fungerade. — They asked why the app wasn’t working. |

When **vem** (who) or **vad** (what) is the subject of a reported question, use **som** after it:

- **”Vem ringde?” — “Who called?”**
- **Hon frågade vem som ringde. — She asked who had called.**

The whole sentence is a statement, so it normally ends with a full stop, not a question mark. Use a question mark only if the main sentence is itself a question:

- **Han frågade var jag bodde. — He asked where I lived.**
- **Vet du vad hon sa? — Do you know what she said?**

## Reported requests and commands

An imperative (a direct command) is usually reported with a verb such as **be** (ask) or **säga åt** (tell), followed by the person addressed and an infinitive with **att**. **Be** often presents something as a request; **säga åt** is more direct and can sound like an instruction or order.

- **”Stäng fönstret!” — “Close the window!”**
- **Hon sa åt mig att stänga fönstret. — She told me to close the window.**
- **”Kom i tid, tack.” — “Please arrive on time.”**
- **Läraren bad oss att komma i tid. — The teacher asked us to arrive on time.**

A question used as a polite request can also be reported as a request:

- **”Kan du hjälpa mig?” — “Can you help me?”**
- **Han bad mig att hjälpa honom. — He asked me to help him.**

## *Att* can sometimes be omitted

In everyday Swedish, **att** is sometimes left out after a reporting verb when the reported statement follows it. Keeping **att** is always a clear choice, especially in careful writing:

- **Hon sa att hon var trött. / Hon sa hon var trött. — She said that she was tired.**

Do not omit **att** when it is needed for another meaning or structure. In the examples on this page, using it consistently makes the reported clause easy to recognize.
