---
title: Impersonal Pronoun
description: Learn how Swedish man refers to people in general and why it takes a singular verb.
---

Swedish **man** is an impersonal subject pronoun for people in general. Depending on the sentence, it can mean **you**, **one**, **people**, or sometimes **we** in English. It does not refer to a particular person.

## Use *man* as the subject

Put **man** before the verb in an ordinary statement, just like other subjects:

> **Man + verb + …**

The pronoun is grammatically singular. Swedish verbs usually have the same form with singular and plural subjects, so the verb may not look different, but use the regular singular-subject construction:

- **Man bor** ofta nära jobbet. — People often live close to work.
- **Man kan** köpa biljetter på nätet. — You can buy tickets online.
- **Man måste** vara försiktig. — One has to be careful.
- **I Sverige äter man** ofta middag ganska tidigt. — In Sweden, people often eat dinner fairly early.
- **Hur gör man** det? — How do you do that?
- **När man är trött, behöver man vila.** — When you’re tired, you need to rest.

Translate **man** naturally for the context; English does not use one word for every general-subject sentence. Although **we** can be a natural translation when the speaker includes themself in a general statement, Swedish **man** does not specifically mean “we.”

## Common confusion

The pronoun **man** is not the same as the noun **en man**, meaning “a man.” The pronoun refers to people in general and is not specifically male. The noun names a male person; its plural is **män**.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Man säger att tåget är försenat.** — They say the train is delayed.
2. **Här får man inte röka.** — You’re not allowed to smoke here.
3. **Man brukar ta av sig skorna hemma.** — People usually take off their shoes at home.
4. **Var parkerar man?** — Where do you park?
5. **Man lär sig genom att öva.** — You learn by practicing.
6. **Ibland behöver man lite hjälp.** — Sometimes you need a little help.
7. **Man kan betala med kort.** — You can pay by card.
8. **Man går ofta till fots här.** — People often walk here.
9. **Man måste boka bord i förväg.** — You have to book a table in advance.
10. **När man har tid, kan man läsa en bok.** — When you have time, you can read a book.
