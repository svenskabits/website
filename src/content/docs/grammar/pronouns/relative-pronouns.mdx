---
title: Relative Pronouns
description: Learn how som, vars, and vad connect Swedish clauses to people, things, and possession.
---

Swedish uses **som**, **vars**, and **vad** to connect clauses in different ways. The right word depends on whether the clause refers back to a noun, shows possession, or means “what.”

## Som: who, that, or which

Use **som** to introduce a relative clause that gives more information about a person or thing already mentioned. It can mean **who**, **that**, or **which**; it does not change for people and things.

- *Boken **som** jag läser är spännande.* — The book **that** I am reading is exciting.
- *Mannen **som** bor här är läkare.* — The man **who** lives here is a doctor.
- *Filmen **som** vi såg var rolig.* — The film **that** we saw was funny.

After **som**, the clause continues with its own word order. In *boken som jag läser*, **jag** is the subject and **läser** is the verb.

## Vars: whose

Use **vars** to show that someone or something in the relative clause owns or is associated with a noun. Put **vars** directly before that noun. **Vars** does not change form.

- *Jag träffade en kvinna **vars hund** heter Max.* — I met a woman **whose dog** is called Max.
- *Vi känner en man **vars barn** går i samma skola.* — We know a man **whose children** go to the same school.
- *Det är ett hus **vars fönster** är blå.* — It is a house **whose windows** are blue.

## Vad: what

**Vad** can introduce a clause that means **what**. The whole clause refers to a thing, rather than describing a noun just before it: *Jag tar **vad du erbjuder*** — “I’ll take **what you offer**.” This is sometimes called a free relative clause.

This use is also common after **allt** (“everything/all”), as in *Hon berättade allt vad hon visste* — “She told everything she knew.” You may also see **allt som** in this kind of sentence.

## Common confusion

Although **vad** can mean “what” in a free relative clause, it also appears in direct questions. Compare *Vad läser du?* (“What are you reading?”) with *Jag läser vad du rekommenderar* (“I’m reading what you recommend”). In the second sentence, **vad du rekommenderar** is part of the sentence, not a direct question.

## Useful Expressions

1. *Jag gillar låten som spelas på radion.* — I like the song that is playing on the radio.
2. *Var är koppen som stod här?* — Where is the cup that was here?
3. *Vi har en granne som talar svenska.* — We have a neighbor who speaks Swedish.
4. *Hon läser boken som du gav henne.* — She is reading the book you gave her.
5. *Han har en vän vars syster bor i Malmö.* — He has a friend whose sister lives in Malmö.
6. *Jag såg en hund vars svans var vit.* — I saw a dog whose tail was white.
7. *De bor i ett hus vars tak är rött.* — They live in a house whose roof is red.
8. *Jag tar vad du erbjuder.* — I’ll take what you offer.
9. *Gör vad du vill.* — Do what you want.
10. *Hon berättade allt vad hon visste.* — She told everything she knew.
