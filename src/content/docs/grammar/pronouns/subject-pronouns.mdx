## The subject pronouns

A subject pronoun tells you who or what does something or is being described. Swedish subject pronouns are:

| Swedish | English | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **jag** | I | The speaker |
| **du** | you | One person you are speaking to |
| **han** | he | A male person |
| **hon** | she | A female person |
| **hen** | they / he or she | A gender-neutral pronoun for a person, including when their gender is unknown or not relevant |
| **den** | it | A singular noun of common gender (*en*-word) |
| **det** | it | A singular noun of neuter gender (*ett*-word) |
| **vi** | we | The speaker and at least one other person |
| **ni** | you | More than one person; it can also address one person formally, but that is less common in everyday Swedish |
| **de** | they | More than one person or thing |

## Position and verb form

In an ordinary statement, the subject usually comes before the finite verb—the verb form that shows the tense:

- **Jag bor** här. — I live here.
- **Hon arbetar** idag. — She is working today.
- **De kommer** snart. — They are coming soon.

Unlike English, Swedish verbs do not change form to match the subject. For example, the present-tense verb **bor** stays the same with **jag**, **du**, and **de**:

- **Jag bor** i Malmö. — I live in Malmö.
- **Du bor** i Malmö. — You live in Malmö.
- **De bor** i Malmö. — They live in Malmö.

Use **den** and **det** for things according to the noun's grammatical gender, not its natural characteristics: **en bil** is referred to as **den**, while **ett hus** is referred to as **det**. Use **de** for plural nouns.

## Common confusion

In speech, **de** is commonly pronounced *dom*. In standard writing, the subject pronoun is still spelled **de**. Also, **hen** is a normal gender-neutral option for a person; it does not refer to a thing.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag** heter Lina. — My name is Lina.
2. **Du** talar svenska. — You speak Swedish.
3. **Han** bor i Uppsala. — He lives in Uppsala.
4. **Hon** läser en bok. — She is reading a book.
5. **Hen** kommer klockan tre. — They are coming at three.
6. Bilen är ny. **Den** är röd. — The car is new. It is red.
7. Huset är stort. **Det** har fyra rum. — The house is big. It has four rooms.
8. **Vi** äter middag hemma. — We are eating dinner at home.
9. **Ni** arbetar tillsammans. — You are working together.
10. **De** väntar på bussen. — They are waiting for the bus.
