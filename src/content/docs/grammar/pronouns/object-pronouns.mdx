## The object forms

An object pronoun refers to the person or thing affected by an action, or follows a preposition such as **med** (with) or **till** (to). These are the common Swedish object forms:

| Meaning | Object form |
| --- | --- |
| me | **mig** |
| you (one person) | **dig** |
| him | **honom** |
| her | **henne** |
| them (one person, gender-neutral) | **hen** |
| it (an *en*-word) | **den** |
| it (an *ett*-word) | **det** |
| us | **oss** |
| you (more than one person) | **er** |
| them (plural) | **dem** |

In a simple statement, an object pronoun commonly follows the verb. Compare the subject, which does the action, with the object, which receives it:

- **Hon** ser **honom**. — She sees him.
- **Han** hjälper **henne**. — He is helping her.
- **De** känner **mig**. — They know me.

Object pronouns also follow prepositions:

- Kom **med oss**! — Come with us!
- Jag skickar ett meddelande **till dig**. — I’m sending a message to you.

Use **den** for an *en*-word and **det** for an *ett*-word, just as you do when referring to those things elsewhere in the sentence: **Jag hittar bilen. Jag ser den.** (I find the car. I see it.) For plural people or things, use **dem**.

## Common confusion

In standard writing, **de** is the subject form (“they”) and **dem** is the object form (“them”). Both are commonly pronounced *dom* in everyday speech, but *dom* is informal spelling; use **de** and **dem** in standard writing. The reflexive form **sig** is separate and is used when the object refers back to the subject; see the lesson on reflexive pronouns.

## Useful Expressions

1. Kan du hjälpa **mig**? — Can you help me?
2. Jag ringer **dig** senare. — I’ll call you later.
3. Vi väntar på **honom**. — We’re waiting for him.
4. Jag känner **henne** väl. — I know her well.
5. Jag förstår **hen**. — I understand them.
6. Boken är bra. Jag läser **den**. — The book is good. I’m reading it.
7. Jag tar **det**. — I’ll take it.
8. De hämtar **oss** klockan sex. — They’re picking us up at six.
9. Kan jag sitta bredvid **er**? — Can I sit next to you?
10. Vi träffar **dem** imorgon. — We’re meeting them tomorrow.
