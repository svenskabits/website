---
title: Possessives
description: Learn how to use min, mitt, and mina to say “my” in Swedish.
---

Swedish uses **min**, **mitt**, or **mina** to say **“my.”** The form depends on the noun that follows it.

## The three forms

| Noun | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular *en*-word | **min** | *min bok* — my book |
| Singular *ett*-word | **mitt** | *mitt hus* — my house |
| Plural noun | **mina** | *mina böcker* — my books |

The possessive agrees with the thing owned, not with the owner's gender. Use **min** with an *en*-word and **mitt** with an *ett*-word, whether the owner is a man, a woman, or anyone else. Use **mina** with plural nouns.

Possessives normally come before the noun, and the noun has its indefinite form: *min bok*, not *min boken*. The possessive already shows that the noun is specific, so do not add the definite ending.

## Examples

- *Min syster bor i Malmö.* — My sister lives in Malmö.
- *Mitt kaffe är varmt.* — My coffee is hot.
- *Mina barn går i skolan.* — My children go to school.
- *Var är min telefon?* — Where is my phone?
- *Jag har glömt mitt paraply.* — I have forgotten my umbrella.

### Common mistake

Choose the form from the noun, not from yourself. A woman says *min bil* (“my car”) just as a man does, because *bil* is an *en*-word. Also avoid adding a definite ending after the possessive: say *mitt hus*, not *mitt huset*.

## Useful Expressions

1. *Min mamma är hemma.* — My mum is at home.
2. *Min nyckel ligger här.* — My key is here.
3. *Min katt sover.* — My cat is sleeping.
4. *Mitt namn är Alex.* — My name is Alex.
5. *Mitt rum är litet.* — My room is small.
6. *Mitt tåg går snart.* — My train leaves soon.
7. *Mina vänner kommer ikväll.* — My friends are coming tonight.
8. *Mina skor är blöta.* — My shoes are wet.
9. *Mina föräldrar bor i Sverige.* — My parents live in Sweden.
10. *Var är mina glasögon?* — Where are my glasses?
