---
title: Reflexive Pronouns
description: Learn how Swedish reflexive object pronouns refer back to the subject of a sentence.
---

Swedish uses **mig**, **dig**, and **sig** when an object refers back to the subject—the person or people doing the action.

## The forms

| Subject | Reflexive object pronoun | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **jag** (I) | **mig** (myself) | *Jag tvättar mig.* — I wash myself. |
| **du** (you, one person) | **dig** (yourself) | *Du tvättar dig.* — You wash yourself. |
| **han**, **hon**, or **de** (he, she, or they) | **sig** (himself, herself, or themselves) | *De tvättar sig.* — They wash themselves. |

The reflexive pronoun is the object of the verb and refers to the sentence subject. **Sig** has the same form with third-person singular subjects (**han**, **hon**) and the plural subject **de**.

## Examples

- *Jag känner mig trött.* — I feel tired.
- *Du kan sätta dig här.* — You can sit down here.
- *Hon sminkar sig före festen.* — She puts on makeup before the party.
- *De förbereder sig för provet.* — They are preparing for the test.

## Common confusion

Use **sig** only when the object refers back to the subject. Compare **Han tvättar sig** (“He washes himself”) with **Han tvättar honom** (“He washes another man”). The possessive forms **sin**, **sitt**, and **sina** are a separate topic; they are not object pronouns.

## Useful Expressions

1. *Jag klär på mig.* — I get dressed.
2. *Du måste skynda dig.* — You have to hurry.
3. *Han rakar sig varje morgon.* — He shaves every morning.
4. *Hon sätter sig vid bordet.* — She sits down at the table.
5. *De känner sig hemma här.* — They feel at home here.
6. *Jag tvättar mig innan frukost.* — I wash myself before breakfast.
7. *Du kan se dig själv i spegeln.* — You can look at yourself in the mirror.
8. *Han förbereder sig för mötet.* — He is preparing for the meeting.
9. *Hon lär sig svenska.* — She is learning Swedish.
10. *De hjälper sig själva.* — They help themselves.
