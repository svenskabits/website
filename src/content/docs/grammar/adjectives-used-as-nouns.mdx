An adjective can sometimes stand in for a noun when the person or thing it describes is clear, or when it names a group or an abstract idea. Swedish often uses an article before the adjective. The adjective keeps an agreement ending even though there is no noun after it.

## Groups of people: de + adjective

Use **de** followed by an adjective in its definite plural form, usually ending in **-a**, to refer to a group of people with that quality or situation:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **De rika har också problem.** | The rich have problems too. |
| **De arbetslösa får stöd av kommunen.** | The unemployed receive support from the municipality. |
| **De äldre får rabatt på tåget.** | Older people get a discount on the train. |
| **De unga vill ofta bo nära centrum.** | Young people often want to live close to the city centre. |

The adjective takes its definite plural form, commonly **-a**, regardless of the gender of the people in the group: **de rika**, **de arbetslösa**. The phrase refers to more than one person, so it normally takes a plural verb: **De rika betalar skatt** (The rich pay tax).

This pattern refers to a group, not one individual. For one person, a noun such as **en rik person** (a rich person) or **en arbetslös person** (an unemployed person) is clear. Some adjectives can also be used as nouns for one person, for example **en arbetslös** (an unemployed person), but this is not equally natural with every adjective; **en rik person** is often clearer than **en rik**.

## A specific person or thing: den, det, or de

An adjective can also stand alone when the person or thing is identifiable from the situation or has already been mentioned. Use **den** for a common-gender singular noun, **det** for a neuter singular noun, and **de** for plural nouns. The adjective usually takes its definite form, commonly **-a**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Den röda är min.** | The red one is mine. |
| **Det gamla fungerar fortfarande.** | The old one still works. |
| **De billigaste var redan sålda.** | The cheapest ones had already been sold. |

The missing noun is understood from context. For example, **den röda** can mean *the red car*, *the red shirt*, or another common-gender item; **det gamla** can mean *the old house*, *the old phone*, or another neuter item. The adjective does not tell you which noun is missing.

The same pattern appears with adjectives in the superlative:

- **Vilken jacka ska jag köpa? — Ta den billigaste.** — Which jacket should I buy? — Get the cheapest one.
- **Av alla förslag valde vi det bästa.** — Of all the suggestions, we chose the best one.
- **De yngsta började skolan i augusti.** — The youngest ones started school in August.

## Abstract ideas: det + adjective

Use **det** with a definite-form adjective to refer to an abstract quality, idea, or general matter. These expressions are singular and take a singular verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det viktigaste är att alla får komma till tals.** | The most important thing is that everyone gets to speak. |
| **Det okända kan kännas skrämmande.** | The unknown can feel frightening. |
| **Hon försöker alltid se det positiva.** | She always tries to see the positive side. |
| **Det bästa återstår.** | The best is yet to come. |

These phrases do not refer to a particular omitted noun such as *thing* or *side*. They name an idea more generally. English often adds a word such as *thing*, *part*, or *side* in the translation, but Swedish does not need one.

## Adjectives after words like något and inget

An adjective can follow an indefinite pronoun such as **något** (something/anything), **inget** (nothing/not anything), or **några** (some). In this pattern, the pronoun supplies the meaning of the missing noun, and the adjective agrees with it:

- **Har du hört något nytt?** — Have you heard anything new?
- **Jag vill ha något enkelt.** — I want something simple.
- **Hon sa inget särskilt.** — She said nothing in particular.
- **Vi köpte några billiga.** — We bought some cheap ones.

Use the neuter **-t** form after **något** and **inget**; use the plural form, usually **-a**, after **några**. The exact noun is understood from context. Compare **något nytt** (anything new) with **ett nytt beslut** (a new decision), where the noun is stated and the adjective follows the normal agreement rules.

## Adjectives that have become nouns

Some adjective forms are also established nouns. They can be used with an indefinite article for one person and have ordinary noun forms for definiteness and number. Learn these as vocabulary items; not every adjective can be used this way.

| Singular | Plural | Example and English |
|---|---|---|
| **en bekant** (an acquaintance) | **bekanta** (acquaintances) | **En bekant ringde i morse.** — An acquaintance called this morning. |
| **en anställd** (an employee) | **anställda** (employees) | **Företaget har femtio anställda.** — The company has fifty employees. |
| **en närstående** (a close relative / loved one) | **närstående** (close relatives / loved ones) | **Hon kom till mötet med en närstående.** — She came to the meeting with a loved one. |

These words can still be adjectives when they describe a noun: **en bekant röst** (a familiar voice), **en anställd person** (an employed person), **en närstående vän** (a close friend). In the noun use, the adjective form itself names the person, and its plural or definite form follows the noun pattern. Some forms overlap with adjective endings, so context shows how the word is being used.

## Telling the uses apart

An adjective before a stated noun is not being used as a noun: **rika människor** means *rich people*, with **människor** (people) present. A predicate adjective after a linking verb is also still an adjective: **Människorna är rika** means *The people are rich*. In **de rika**, by contrast, there is no noun after the adjective; **de rika** itself means *the rich*.

| Construction | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Adjective + noun | **Rika människor bor i området.** | Rich people live in the area. |
| Predicate adjective | **Människorna är rika.** | The people are rich. |
| Adjective used for a group | **De rika bor också i området.** | The rich also live in the area. |

The form depends on the expression's role: **de rika** names a plural group, **det viktiga** names an abstract matter, and **något viktigt** refers to something indefinite. In each case, the adjective ending helps show how the phrase is understood.
