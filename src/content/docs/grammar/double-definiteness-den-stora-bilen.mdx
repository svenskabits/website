In Swedish, a noun with an adjective often has **two signs of definiteness**: a word such as **den**, **det**, or **de** before the adjective, and a definite ending on the noun. This is called *double definiteness*.

| Meaning | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| a big car | **en stor bil** | a big car |
| the car | **bilen** | the car |
| the big car | **den stora bilen** | the big car |

The definite phrase is built like this:

> **den / det / de + adjective in its definite form + noun in its definite form**

For many adjectives, the definite form ends in **-a**. The noun keeps its own definite ending:

| Noun type | Indefinite phrase | Definite phrase |
|---|---|---|
| En-word | **en stor bil** — a big car | **den stora bilen** — the big car |
| Ett-word | **ett stort hus** — a big house | **det stora huset** — the big house |
| Plural | **stora bilar** — big cars | **de stora bilarna** — the big cars |

The choice of **den**, **det**, or **de** follows the noun: use **den** with an en-word, **det** with an ett-word, and **de** with a plural noun. The adjective in a definite phrase usually has the same **-a** form for all three.

## With different adjectives

The adjective changes to its definite form, but that form is not always made by simply adding **-a** to the basic form. For example, **liten** has the forms **lilla** in singular definite phrases and **små** in the plural:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **den lilla hunden** | the little dog |
| **det lilla barnet** | the little child |
| **de små hundarna** | the little dogs |
| **den nya lägenheten** | the new apartment |
| **det nya köket** | the new kitchen |
| **de nya lägenheterna** | the new apartments |

If there is more than one adjective, each adjective normally has a definite form:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **den stora, ljusa lägenheten** | the big, bright apartment |
| **det gamla röda huset** | the old red house |
| **de varma, bekväma skorna** | the warm, comfortable shoes |

For male people, the adjective may end in **-e** instead of **-a**. Both forms are used, and **-e** makes the masculine reference clear:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **den gamle mannen** / **den gamla mannen** | the old man |
| **den trevlige läraren** / **den trevliga läraren** | the pleasant teacher |

## With demonstratives

Words such as **den här** (this) and **det där** (that) also come before an adjective, while the noun remains definite:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **den här stora bilen** | this big car |
| **det där gamla huset** | that old house |
| **de här nya skorna** | these new shoes |

Without an adjective, the noun still has its definite ending: **den här bilen** (this car), **det där huset** (that house), **de här skorna** (these shoes).

## When the adjective comes after the noun

An adjective that describes the subject after a verb such as **är** (is/are) does not use the definite form. The noun is definite, but the adjective agrees with its gender or number:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Bilen är stor.** | The car is big. |
| **Huset är stort.** | The house is big. |
| **Bilarna är stora.** | The cars are big. |

Compare **den stora bilen** (the big car), where the adjective comes before the noun, with **bilen är stor** (the car is big), where the adjective comes after the verb.

## Possessives and names

With a possessive word or a name in the possessive form, Swedish normally does **not** add **den**, **det**, or **de** before the adjective. The noun is still definite, and the adjective still has a definite form:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **min nya bil** | my new car |
| **mitt gamla hus** | my old house |
| **mina stora väskor** | my big bags |
| **Annas röda jacka** | Anna's red jacket |
| **Peters nya cykel** | Peter's new bicycle |

So the usual patterns are **den stora bilen** but **min stora bil** and **Annas stora bil**.

## Remember the two parts

When an adjective comes before an ordinary definite noun, include both parts: the definite word before the adjective and the definite ending on the noun.

| Correct | English | Not the standard pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **den stora bilen** | the big car | *den stor bilen* |
| **det stora huset** | the big house | *det stora hus* |
| **de stora bilarna** | the big cars | *de stora bilar* |

If there is no adjective, use the definite noun alone (**bilen**, **huset**, **bilarna**). If the adjective follows a verb, use the adjective form that agrees with the noun (**bilen är stor**).
