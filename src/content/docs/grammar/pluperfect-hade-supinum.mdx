The **pluperfect** (also called the past perfect) describes something that had happened, or had been true, before a point in the past. It is formed with **hade** plus the verb's **supine** form:

> **subject + hade + supine**

> När jag kom hem **hade de redan ätit**.
>
> *When I got home, they had already eaten.*

The eating happened before the past event of my arriving home. The earlier event uses the pluperfect; the later past event often uses the simple past (preteritum).

## How to form it

The auxiliary **hade** is the same for every subject. Add the supine form of the main verb after it:

| Subject | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| jag (I) | hade arbetat | had worked |
| du (you, singular) | hade arbetat | had worked |
| hon (she) | hade arbetat | had worked |
| vi (we) | hade arbetat | had worked |
| de (they) | hade arbetat | had worked |

The supine is the form used after **har** and **hade**. Many supines end in **-t**, but the form varies, so learn it with the verb:

| Infinitive | Supine | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| skriva (to write) | skrivit | hade skrivit | had written |
| göra (to do, make) | gjort | hade gjort | had done, had made |
| vara (to be) | varit | hade varit | had been |
| komma (to come) | kommit | hade kommit | had come |
| se (to see) | sett | hade sett | had seen |
| köpa (to buy) | köpt | hade köpt | had bought |

The main verb does not change for the subject. In a verb phrase with a modal verb, the modal is the supine and the following verb stays in the infinitive:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag hade kunnat hjälpa dig. | I could have helped you. |
| Hon hade velat stanna längre. | She would have wanted to stay longer. |

## Use it for an event before another past event

Use the pluperfect when you want to make clear that one event happened before another past event or past time. The later event often appears in preteritum, but it can also be clear from the surrounding context.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| När filmen började hade vi redan hittat våra platser. | When the film started, we had already found our seats. |
| Jag kände igen henne eftersom vi hade träffats förut. | I recognized her because we had met before. |
| Bussen hade gått när vi kom till hållplatsen. | The bus had left when we got to the stop. |

The earlier event does not need to be a single completed action. A situation can continue up to the past reference point:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| De hade bott i Lund i fem år när de flyttade till Stockholm. | They had lived in Lund for five years when they moved to Stockholm. |
| Jag hade aldrig varit utomlands före den resan. | I had never been abroad before that trip. |

Words such as **redan** (already), **aldrig** (never), **innan** (before), and **före** (before) often help show the time relationship. They do not form the tense; **hade + supine** does.

## Compare it with the perfect and the simple past

The choice depends on the time you are talking about. The perfect connects an earlier event to the present; the pluperfect connects it to a point in the past. The simple past places an event in past time without marking it as earlier than another past reference point.

| Tense | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect: **har + supine** | Jag har redan ätit. | I have already eaten. |
| Pluperfect: **hade + supine** | Jag hade redan ätit när de ringde. | I had already eaten when they called. |
| Simple past | Jag åt innan de ringde. | I ate before they called. |

Use the pluperfect when the earlier-than-past relationship matters or needs to be made clear. If the order is already obvious, Swedish can also use the simple past:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Efter att hon stängde dörren gick hon till bussen. | After she closed the door, she went to the bus. |
| Efter att hon hade stängt dörren gick hon till bussen. | After she had closed the door, she went to the bus. |

Both sentences put the closing before the walk to the bus. The pluperfect makes the earlier timing explicit; it is not required just because **efter att** (“after”) is present.

## Word order and negation

In an ordinary main clause, **hade** is the finite verb and comes after the subject. Put **inte** and similar sentence adverbs after **hade**, before the supine:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Vi hade inte bokat bord. | We had not booked a table. |
| Hon hade aldrig sett havet förut. | She had never seen the sea before. |

If another sentence element comes first, the finite verb still comes second, so it comes before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Innan dess hade jag aldrig rest ensam. | Before then, I had never travelled alone. |
| Då hade tåget redan lämnat stationen. | By then, the train had already left the station. |

In a yes-or-no question, put **hade** before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Hade ni träffats tidigare? | Had you met before? |
| Hade hon inte fått meddelandet? | Hadn't she received the message? |

In a subordinate clause introduced by a word such as **eftersom** (because), **när** (when), or **innan** (before), the subject and **inte** come before **hade**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Vi gick hem eftersom vi hade arbetat hela dagen. | We went home because we had worked all day. |
| Hon blev förvånad när hon inte hade hört nyheten tidigare. | She was surprised when she had not heard the news before. |

## Imagined past situations

The same form, **hade + supine**, is used in an **om** (“if”) clause about an imagined past situation that did not happen. The result commonly uses **skulle ha + supine** (“would have” + supine):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Om jag hade vetat det, skulle jag ha ringt dig. | If I had known that, I would have called you. |
| Om de hade tagit en taxi, skulle de ha kommit i tid. | If they had taken a taxi, they would have arrived on time. |

Here the **om** clause describes an unreal past condition. **Skulle ha + supine** gives its imagined result; it is a related construction, not another form of the pluperfect.

## Pluperfect passive

The pluperfect can also be passive, when the focus is on what happened to something rather than who did it. One common pattern is **hade + supine in the s-passive**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Huset hade byggts före kriget. | The house had been built before the war. |
| Alla biljetter hade sålts när vi kom fram. | All the tickets had been sold by the time we arrived. |

To build the tense, keep **hade** and use the passive supine of the main verb, such as **byggts** or **sålts**.
