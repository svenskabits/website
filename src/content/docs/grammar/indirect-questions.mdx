An indirect question is a question included inside a larger sentence. It is useful when you ask politely, report what someone wants to know, or say that you are unsure about something.

Compare a direct question with an indirect one:

| Direct question | Indirect question |
|---|---|
| Var ligger stationen? — Where is the station? | Vet du var stationen ligger? — Do you know where the station is? |
| När öppnar banken? — When does the bank open? | Jag undrar när banken öppnar. — I wonder when the bank opens. |

## Keep the question word, but use statement word order

In a direct Swedish question, the verb usually comes before the subject: *Var ligger stationen?* In an indirect question, the subject comes before the verb, as in an ordinary subordinate clause. The question word stays at the beginning of the clause.

| Direct question | Indirect question |
|---|---|
| Var arbetar hon? — Where does she work? | Vet du var hon arbetar? — Do you know where she works? |
| Varför är tåget försenat? — Why is the train delayed? | Kan du förklara varför tåget är försenat? — Can you explain why the train is delayed? |
| Hur mycket kostar det? — How much does it cost? | Jag vet inte hur mycket det kostar. — I don't know how much it costs. |

The same pattern applies when the main clause is itself a question:

- **Kan du säga var jag ska skriva under?** — Can you tell me where I should sign?
- **Vet du när nästa buss går?** — Do you know when the next bus leaves?

Do not use the verb–subject order of a direct question after the question word. For example, say **Vet du var stationen ligger?**, not *Vet du var ligger stationen?*

## Yes/no questions use *om*

A yes/no question has no question word such as *var* or *när*. To report or ask about it indirectly, put **om** (if/whether) at the beginning of the embedded clause. Then use subject–verb order.

| Direct question | Indirect question |
|---|---|
| Har du fått brevet? — Have you received the letter? | Jag undrar om du har fått brevet. — I wonder whether you have received the letter. |
| Är kontoret öppet i dag? — Is the office open today? | Vet du om kontoret är öppet i dag? — Do you know if the office is open today? |
| Kan jag betala med kort? — Can I pay by card? | Hon frågade om hon kunde betala med kort. — She asked whether she could pay by card. |

Here, *om* introduces the indirect question. It has the same form as *om* meaning “if” in a condition; the context makes the meaning clear:

- **Jag vet inte om apoteket är öppet.** — I don't know if the pharmacy is open.
- **Om apoteket är öppet, går jag dit nu.** — If the pharmacy is open, I'll go there now.

## Questions where *vem*, *vad* or *vilken* is the subject

When *vem*, *vad* or *vilken/vilket/vilka* is the subject of the embedded clause, use **som** after it. This is different from a question word that refers to the object or another part of the sentence.

- **Vem beställde kortet?** — Who ordered the card?
- **Vet du vem som beställde kortet?** — Do you know who ordered the card?
- **Vad hände efter mötet?** — What happened after the meeting?
- **Kan du berätta vad som hände efter mötet?** — Can you tell me what happened after the meeting?

When the person or thing asked about is not the subject, do not add *som*:

- **Vem kontaktade du?** — Who did you contact?
- **Jag vet inte vem du kontaktade.** — I don't know who you contacted.
- **Vilken blankett behöver hon?** — Which form does she need?
- **Vet du vilken blankett hon behöver?** — Do you know which form she needs?

## Negation and other sentence adverbs

In an indirect question, **inte** normally comes before the finite verb, as it does in a subordinate clause. The finite verb is the verb that changes for tense or agrees with the subject; in a verb phrase with an auxiliary, *inte* comes before the auxiliary.

- **Varför fungerar appen inte?** — Why doesn't the app work?
- **Jag förstår inte varför appen inte fungerar.** — I don't understand why the app doesn't work.
- **Har du inte fått någon kod?** — Haven't you received a code?
- **Hon frågade om jag inte hade fått någon kod.** — She asked whether I hadn't received a code.

Other sentence adverbs, such as *alltid* (always) and *kanske* (perhaps), also usually come before the finite verb in an indirect question:

- **När kommer läkaren?** — When is the doctor coming?
- **Vet du när läkaren kanske kommer?** — Do you know when the doctor might come?

## Punctuation and reported questions

The punctuation depends on the whole sentence, not just on the embedded question. Use a question mark if the main clause is a question. Use a full stop if the whole sentence is a statement.

- **Vet du var receptionen ligger?** — Do you know where reception is?
- **Jag vet inte var receptionen ligger.** — I don't know where reception is.
- **Hon frågade när tåget skulle gå.** — She asked when the train was due to leave.

In reported questions, the tense may change to fit the time of the reporting verb, as in *skulle* in the last example. The indirect-question word order remains the same.
