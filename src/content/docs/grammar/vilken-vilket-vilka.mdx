Use **vilken**, **vilket**, and **vilka** to ask which person or thing someone means, usually when choosing from a group. The form agrees with the noun you are asking about:

| Noun | Form | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singular en-word | **vilken** | **Vilken bok vill du läsa?** | Which book do you want to read? |
| Singular ett-word | **vilket** | **Vilket äpple vill du ha?** | Which apple would you like? |
| Plural noun | **vilka** | **Vilka skor ska du köpa?** | Which shoes are you going to buy? |

The noun after the question word is normally indefinite: **vilken bok**, **vilket hus**, **vilka böcker**. The question word already shows that you are asking the person to identify a choice, so do not add the definite ending to the noun.

## Choose the form by the noun

The form does not depend on who is asking or answering. Look at the noun that follows, or the noun that is understood if it is left out:

| Noun | Question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **en tröja** (a sweater) | **Vilken tröja är din?** | Which sweater is yours? |
| **ett bord** (a table) | **Vilket bord är ledigt?** | Which table is free? |
| **böcker** (books) | **Vilka böcker har du läst?** | Which books have you read? |

You can use the forms without repeating a noun when it is clear from the conversation. The form still matches the thing being discussed:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vilken vill du ha?** (talking about a en-word, such as **en kopp**) | Which one would you like? |
| **Vilket vill du ha?** (talking about an ett-word, such as **ett glas**) | Which one would you like? |
| **Vilka vill du ha?** (talking about plural things, such as **skor**) | Which ones would you like? |

## Asking a direct question

When **vilken**, **vilket**, or **vilka** asks about the object of a question, the question phrase comes first, followed by the verb and then the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vilken bok läser du?** | Which book are you reading? |
| **Vilket kaffe brukar hon köpa?** | Which coffee does she usually buy? |
| **Vilka filmer tycker ni om?** | Which films do you like? |

When the question phrase is the subject—the person or thing doing the action—it comes before the verb, with no other subject after the verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vilken buss går till centrum?** | Which bus goes to the city centre? |
| **Vilka elever kommer i dag?** | Which students are coming today? |

Compare **Vilken buss tar du?** (Which bus are you taking?), where **du** is the subject, with **Vilken buss går dit?** (Which bus goes there?), where **vilken buss** is the subject.

## Choosing from a known group

To ask which one or ones from a group, use **vilken/vilket/vilka av** followed by a definite noun or a pronoun such as **dem** (them):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vilken av böckerna vill du låna?** | Which of the books do you want to borrow? |
| **Vilket av husen är ditt?** | Which of the houses is yours? |
| **Vilka av dem bor här?** | Which of them live here? |

Use **vilken** with an en-word, **vilket** with an ett-word, and **vilka** with plural items, even after **av**.

## Indirect questions

When the question is part of a larger sentence, put the subject before the verb in that embedded clause:

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Vilken bok läser du?** | **Jag vet vilken bok du läser.** | Which book are you reading? / I know which book you are reading. |
| **Vilka skor vill hon köpa?** | **Kan du säga vilka skor hon vill köpa?** | Which shoes does she want to buy? / Can you say which shoes she wants to buy? |

In the second example, **Kan du säga** is a direct question, so its verb comes before its subject (**du**). Inside it, **vilka skor hon vill köpa** is an indirect question, so the subject (**hon**) comes before the verb (**vill**).

## Which one or what kind?

Use **vilken/vilket/vilka** when asking someone to identify or choose a particular person or thing. To ask about a type or kind, Swedish also uses **vad för en/ett** with singular nouns, **vad för** with plural nouns, or **vilken sorts**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vilken bok vill du läsa?** | Which book do you want to read? |
| **Vad för en bok läser du helst?** | What kind of book do you prefer to read? |
| **Vilken sorts musik tycker du om?** | What kind of music do you like? |

Remember: **vilken** goes with singular en-words, **vilket** with singular ett-words, and **vilka** with plural nouns. The noun is normally indefinite when it follows the question word; use **av** plus a definite noun when asking which one from a specific group.
