Swedish uses **tycka**, **tro** and **tänka** for several meanings that English often expresses with *think*. The choice depends on whether you are giving an opinion, believing something, or describing a thought or intention.

## The three verbs

| Infinitive | Present | Past | Supine | Main idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tycka | tycker | tyckte | tyckt | have an opinion; like |
| tro | tror | trodde | trott | believe; think something is probably true |
| tänka | tänker | tänkte | tänkt | think, consider, remember, or intend |

The forms do not change according to the subject: *jag tycker*, *du tycker*, *de tycker*.

## Tycka: opinions and likes

Use **tycka** to give a personal opinion or evaluation. It is often followed by **att** and a complete clause. In everyday Swedish, **att** is often left out after *tycka*.

- *Jag tycker att filmen är bra.* — I think the film is good.
- *Vi tycker att det är viktigt att alla får säga sitt.* — We think it is important that everyone gets to have their say.
- *Tycker du (att) den här färgen passar?* — Do you think this colour works?
- *De tyckte inte att maten var särskilt god.* — They did not think the food was particularly good.

Use **tycka om** to say that you like someone or something. The particle **om** is part of this expression; it is not the same as the conjunction *om* meaning “if” or “whether.”

- *Jag tycker om kaffe, men jag tycker inte om te.* — I like coffee, but I do not like tea.
- *Hon tycker om att promenera vid vattnet.* — She likes walking by the water.
- *Tycker du om honom?* — Do you like him?

When asking for a view or reaction, use **Vad tycker du om …?** (“What do you think of …?”). Context distinguishes this question from **Tycker du om …?** (“Do you like …?”).

- *Vad tycker du om den nya chefen?* — What do you think of the new manager?
- *Tycker du om den nya chefen?* — Do you like the new manager?

## Tro: belief and probability

Use **tro** when you believe something is true, but may not know for certain. It commonly introduces a clause with **att**. The conjunction is often omitted in conversation.

- *Jag tror att bussen kommer snart.* — I think the bus will come soon.
- *Tror du (att) hon är hemma?* — Do you think she is at home?
- *Han trodde att mötet började klockan nio.* — He thought the meeting started at nine.
- *Jag tror inte att det blir regn.* — I do not think it will rain.

Use **tro på** when you believe in or have confidence in a person, idea, or thing. Here **på** is a preposition and is always included.

- *Hon tror på ett liv efter döden.* — She believes in life after death.
- *Jag tror på dig.* — I believe in you.
- *De tror på den här lösningen.* — They believe in this solution.

Compare **Jag tror att du klarar det** (“I think you can manage it,” a belief about what will happen) with **Jag tror på dig** (“I believe in you,” expressing confidence in you).

## Tänka: thoughts, consideration, and intention

Use **tänka** for the activity of thinking, considering something, or having a thought. To say what is on someone’s mind, use **tänka på**.

- *Vad tänker du på?* — What are you thinking about?
- *Jag tänker ofta på min familj.* — I often think about my family.
- *Vi måste tänka på miljön.* — We must think about the environment.
- *Hon tänkte länge innan hon svarade.* — She thought for a long time before she answered.

**Tänka på** can also mean to remember or make sure to do something. The exact meaning depends on context.

- *Tänk på att låsa dörren.* — Remember to lock the door.
- *Jag ska tänka på saken.* — I will think about it.

Use **tänka sig** to imagine or picture something. The pronoun changes with the subject: *tänka mig, tänka dig, tänka sig, tänka oss, tänka er, tänka sig*.

- *Kan du tänka dig att bo på landet?* — Can you imagine living in the countryside?
- *Jag kan inte tänka mig ett liv utan musik.* — I cannot imagine a life without music.

Use **tänka + infinitive without att** to express an intention or plan. The present tense refers to what someone intends to do, not only to what they are thinking at this moment.

- *Jag tänker söka ett nytt jobb.* — I intend to apply for a new job.
- *Vad tänker ni göra i helgen?* — What are you planning to do this weekend?
- *Hon tänkte ringa, men hon glömde.* — She intended to call, but she forgot.

With **tänka att + clause**, the verb introduces a thought or view. In this use, it is close to “think that,” and often presents an idea for consideration.

- *Jag tänker att vi kan börja nu.* — I think we can start now.
- *Vad tänker du att vi ska göra?* — What do you think we should do?

## Choosing between them

The same English question, “What do you think?”, can be expressed in different ways depending on what you want to ask:

- *Vad tycker du om planen?* — What is your opinion of the plan?
- *Tror du att planen fungerar?* — Do you believe the plan will work?
- *Vad tänker du på?* — What is on your mind?
- *Vad tänker du göra?* — What do you intend to do?

For a judgment or preference, choose **tycka**. For a belief or guess about whether something is true, choose **tro**. For a thought, consideration, or intention, choose **tänka**.

## Word order and negation

In a main clause, **inte** normally comes after the finite verb:

- *Jag tycker inte om stark mat.* — I do not like spicy food.
- *Hon tror inte på rykten.* — She does not believe rumours.
- *Vi tänker inte stanna länge.* — We are not planning to stay long.

When one of these verbs introduces a subordinate clause, the clause has its own word order: the subject comes before **inte**, and **inte** comes before the verb. This applies whether the main verb itself is negative or not.

- *Jag tror inte att hon kommer.* — I do not think she is coming.
- *Jag tror att hon inte kommer.* — I think she is not coming.
- *Han tycker att filmen inte är särskilt rolig.* — He thinks the film is not particularly funny.

In the first sentence, **inte** negates the belief (*I do not think …*). In the second, it negates the subordinate clause (*I think she is not coming*).

## Talking about past and perfect

The past forms are **tyckte**, **trodde**, and **tänkte**. The supine forms **tyckt**, **trott**, and **tänkt** are used with *har* to talk about something up to now or with present relevance.

- *Jag tyckte att konserten var fantastisk.* — I thought the concert was fantastic.
- *Vi trodde att tåget var inställt.* — We thought the train was cancelled.
- *Hon tänkte på frågan hela kvällen.* — She thought about the question all evening.
- *Jag har alltid tyckt om att läsa.* — I have always liked reading.
- *De har trott på honom från början.* — They have believed in him from the beginning.
- *Har du tänkt på vad du ska säga?* — Have you thought about what you are going to say?
