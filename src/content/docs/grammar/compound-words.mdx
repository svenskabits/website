A compound is one word made by joining two or more words. Swedish often puts words together this way, especially to name a specific kind of person, place, or thing. The last part usually names the main thing; the first part tells you more about it.

- **en bildörr** — a car door (a kind of door)
- **en barnbok** — a children's book (a kind of book)
- **ett köksbord** — a kitchen table (a kind of table)

## Write compounds as one word

In Swedish, the parts of a compound are normally written together, without a space. This is a common difference from English, where the same idea may be written as two words.

- **en sjuksköterska** — a nurse
- **en sjuk sköterska** — a sick nurse

The space changes the meaning: **sjuksköterska** is one compound, while **sjuk sköterska** is an adjective and a noun. Compounds can also have a meaning that is more specific than the separate parts suggest, so learn the whole word and its meaning.

## How the parts join

The first part often appears in a shortened or changed form. Some compounds have an **-s-** at the join, and some keep or change a vowel. There is no single rule that predicts every form; the linking sound is usually part of the word you learn.

| Parts | Compound | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| arbete (work) + plats (place) | **en arbetsplats** | a workplace |
| kök (kitchen) + bord (table) | **ett köksbord** | a kitchen table |
| gata (street) + namn (name) | **ett gatunamn** | a street name |
| flicka (girl) + rum (room) | **ett flickrum** | a girl's room |
| pojke (boy) + rum (room) | **ett pojkrum** | a boy's room |

In **arbetsplats** and **köksbord**, an **-s-** joins the parts. In **gatunamn**, the first part changes from **gata** to **gatu-**. In **flickrum** and **pojkrum**, the final vowel of the first part is dropped. These are common patterns, not formulas that work for every word.

Some compounds contain more than two parts. Join all the parts into one word:

- **en järnvägsstation** — a railway station
- **en busshållplats** — a bus stop

## The last part controls the noun

For a noun compound, the last part usually determines whether the whole word is an **en**-word or an **ett**-word. It also determines how the compound changes for definiteness and plural. Add the endings to the end of the whole compound, not between its parts.

| Form | Indefinite singular | Definite singular | Indefinite plural | Definite plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **en bildörr** (a car door) | bildörr | bildörren (the car door) | bildörrar (car doors) | bildörrarna (the car doors) |
| **ett köksbord** (a kitchen table) | köksbord | köksbordet (the kitchen table) | köksbord (kitchen tables) | köksborden (the kitchen tables) |

For example, **dörr** (door) is an **en**-word, so **bildörr** is an **en**-word. **Bord** (table) is an **ett**-word, so **köksbord** is an **ett**-word. Check the complete compound if you are unsure about its forms.

## Compounds are not only nouns

Compounds can also be adjectives and other kinds of words. For example, **mörkblå** (dark blue) combines **mörk** (dark) and **blå** (blue), and **jättebra** (very good) combines **jätte-** (very) and **bra** (good).

- **Hon har en mörkblå jacka.** — She has a dark blue jacket.
- **Filmen var jättebra.** — The film was really good.

The parts are still written together when the compound is used in a sentence.

## Hyphens with letters and numbers

When a compound begins with an abbreviation or a numeral written with digits, use a hyphen to make the parts clear:

- **ett EU-land** — an EU country
- **en 3-åring** — a three-year-old

When the number is written as a word, the compound is normally joined without a hyphen: **en treåring** — a three-year-old.

## A useful check

When you see two words that seem to form one idea, check whether Swedish treats them as a compound. If it does, write them together, identify the last part to help with the noun's **en/ett** group and endings, and learn any linking letter as part of the word.
