## Building Swedish compound words

Swedish commonly joins two or more words to make one noun. Unlike English, which often writes a similar phrase as separate words, Swedish compounds are generally written as **one word**:

- **en bok** (a book) + **en hylla** (a shelf) → **en bokhylla** (a bookshelf)
- **en cykel** (a bicycle) + **en hjälm** (a helmet) → **en cykelhjälm** (a bicycle helmet)
- **ett sommarhus** (a summer house) combines **sommar** (summer) and **ett hus** (a house)

Usually, the parts help you work out the meaning: the first part describes or narrows the meaning of the last part. In **bokhylla**, for example, **bok** tells you what kind of **hylla** (“shelf”) it is. The order is the reverse of the English phrase *bookshelf*: the main thing comes last in Swedish.

Sometimes a linking letter appears between the parts, often **-s-**. For example, **ett arbete** (a job or task) + **en dag** (a day) becomes **en arbetsdag** (a workday). The linking letter is part of the compound’s spelling; the words are still written together.

### The last part controls the noun

The final part is the main noun, or **head**, of the compound. It determines whether the compound is an **en-word** or an **ett-word**, and it determines the plural pattern. For example, **en hylla** gives **en bokhylla**, with plural **bokhyllor**. If the final noun has an irregular plural, the compound keeps that pattern too: **en bok – böcker**, so **en barnbok – barnböcker**.

The same applies to the definite form: add the ending used by the final noun to the whole compound, not between its parts:

- **en bokhylla** → **bokhyllan** — a bookshelf → the bookshelf
- **ett sommarhus** → **sommarhuset** — a summer house → the summer house

### Common confusion

Do not put a space between the parts as you might in English: write **en bokhylla**, not *en bok hylla*. Also, do not choose **en** or **ett** based on the first part or on the English translation; look at the final part.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag läser en barnbok.** — I’m reading a children’s book.
2. **Barnboken ligger på bordet.** — The children’s book is on the table.
3. **Hon köpte en cykelhjälm.** — She bought a bicycle helmet.
4. **Cykelhjälmen är blå.** — The bicycle helmet is blue.
5. **Vi bor i ett sommarhus.** — We live in a summer house.
6. **Sommarhuset ligger nära sjön.** — The summer house is near the lake.
7. **Jag behöver en köksstol.** — I need a kitchen chair.
8. **Köksstolarna står vid fönstret.** — The kitchen chairs are by the window.
9. **Måndag är en arbetsdag.** — Monday is a workday.
10. **Böckerna står i bokhyllan.** — The books are in the bookcase.
