Frequency adverbs tell how often something happens. Common Swedish examples include:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **alltid** | always |
| **ofta** | often |
| **vanligtvis** | usually |
| **ibland** | sometimes |
| **sällan** | rarely, seldom |
| **aldrig** | never |

## Position in a main clause

In a simple main clause, a frequency adverb usually comes after the finite verb—the verb that carries the tense—and before the rest of the sentence:

**subject + finite verb + frequency adverb + the rest**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag dricker alltid kaffe på morgonen.** | I always drink coffee in the morning. |
| **Hon går ofta till biblioteket.** | She often goes to the library. |
| **Vi äter ibland ute.** | We sometimes eat out. |
| **Han är sällan hemma på helgerna.** | He is rarely at home on weekends. |
| **De tränar aldrig på söndagar.** | They never exercise on Sundays. |

If a time or place phrase comes first, the finite verb still comes second. The frequency adverb usually follows the subject: **På vintern åker vi ofta skidor.** (In winter, we often go skiing.)

You can also place a frequency adverb first for emphasis. Then the finite verb comes before the subject: **Ibland går jag till jobbet.** (Sometimes I walk to work.)

## With auxiliaries and modal verbs

When there is an auxiliary or modal verb, the frequency adverb usually follows that finite verb and comes before the non-finite verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har ofta läst här.** | I have often read here. |
| **Hon kan ibland hjälpa till.** | She can sometimes help. |
| **Vi har ofta ätit hemma.** | We have often eaten at home. |

In a yes-or-no question, the finite verb comes before the subject, and the frequency adverb follows the subject: **Tränar du ofta?** (Do you exercise often?) **Kan hon ibland hjälpa till?** (Can she sometimes help?)

## Frequency adverbs and inte

In a main clause, **inte** (not) normally follows the finite verb too. When both **inte** and a frequency adverb appear, their order affects the meaning:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag tränar inte alltid på morgonen.** | I do not always exercise in the morning. |
| **Jag tränar aldrig på morgonen.** | I never exercise in the morning. |
| **Han kan inte alltid komma.** | He cannot always come. |

**Inte alltid** means *not always*; it does not mean *never*. **Aldrig** means *never* on its own. With a modal verb, **inte** and the frequency adverb come after the modal and before the infinitive, as in **kan inte alltid komma** (cannot always come).

## In a subordinate clause

A subordinate clause is part of a larger sentence and often begins with a word such as **att** (that), **när** (when), or **eftersom** (because). In a neutral subordinate clause, frequency adverbs usually come before the finite verb:

| Main clause | Subordinate clause | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Hon arbetar ofta hemma.** | **Jag vet att hon ofta arbetar hemma.** | She often works at home. / I know that she often works at home. |
| **Han kan inte alltid komma.** | **Hon säger att han inte alltid kan komma.** | He cannot always come. / She says that he cannot always come. |
| **Hon har aldrig sett snö.** | **Jag vet att hon aldrig har sett snö.** | She has never seen snow. / I know that she has never seen snow. |

Compare **Hon arbetar ofta hemma.** (She often works at home.) with **att hon ofta arbetar hemma** (that she often works at home). In the main clause, the adverb follows the finite verb; in the subordinate clause, it comes before the finite verb. The same placement applies to **inte**: **att han inte alltid kan komma** (that he cannot always come).

Remember the usual pattern: after the finite verb in a main clause, but before the finite verb in a subordinate clause. Fronting an adverb can change the main-clause order, so treat these as common patterns rather than an unchanging position for every sentence.
