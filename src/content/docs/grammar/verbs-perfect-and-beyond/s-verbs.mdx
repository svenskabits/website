---
title: S-Verbs
description: "Learn the main uses of Swedish verbs ending in -s: passive forms, reciprocal actions, and verbs that normally keep the ending."
---

Swedish verbs ending in **-s** do not all have the same meaning. The ending can mark a passive action, an action people do to one another, or belong to a verb that is normally used with **-s**.

## Passive actions

A passive sentence focuses on what happens to a person or thing, rather than who does it. For example, the active sentence **Någon öppnar dörren** (“Someone opens the door”) can be expressed with a passive **-s** form:

- **Dörren öppnas.** — The door is opened / The door opens.

Here, **öppnas** tells us what happens to the door; the person opening it is not named. Passive **-s** forms can be used in different tenses, for example **öppnas** (present), **öppnades** (past), and **har öppnats** (has been opened). This is one use of **-s**, not a rule that applies to every verb ending in **-s**.

## Actions people do to one another

Some **-s** verbs describe a reciprocal action: two or more people do something to each other. The subject is usually plural or names more than one person.

- **Vi träffas på lördag.** — We’ll meet on Saturday.
- **De kramas när de ses.** — They hug each other when they see each other.

In these examples, **träffas** and **kramas** do not mean that something is done to the subject by an unnamed person. The people in the subject do the action to one another.

## Verbs normally used with -s

Some verbs normally occur with **-s** and are learned in that form. Their meaning is not passive or reciprocal:

- **Jag hoppas på fint väder.** — I hope for good weather.
- **Hon minns sin första lärare.** — She remembers her first teacher.
- **Trivs du här?** — Do you like it here?

These are sometimes called **deponent verbs**. For a learner, the useful point is that **hoppas**, **minnas**, and **trivas** are ordinary vocabulary items with **-s**; do not remove the ending to make a different verb.

## Common confusion: not every -s means “is done”

Use the sentence meaning and the verb to tell the uses apart: **Dörren öppnas** is passive, **Vi träffas** describes a mutual action, and **Jag hoppas** is simply “I hope.” Some verbs have an **-s** form in more than one use, but you cannot freely add **-s** to every verb and expect a natural passive or reciprocal meaning. Learn which **-s** forms are used with each verb.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Dörren öppnas klockan nio.** — The door is opened at nine.
2. **Butiken stängdes tidigt.** — The shop was closed early.
3. **Boken har översatts till svenska.** — The book has been translated into Swedish.
4. **Vi träffas efter jobbet.** — We’ll meet after work.
5. **Vi ses på måndag.** — We’ll see each other on Monday.
6. **Barnen kramas.** — The children hug each other.
7. **Jag hoppas att du mår bra.** — I hope you’re well.
8. **Minns du hennes namn?** — Do you remember her name?
9. **Vi trivs i den nya lägenheten.** — We like it in the new apartment.
10. **Hon lyckades hitta nycklarna.** — She managed to find the keys.
