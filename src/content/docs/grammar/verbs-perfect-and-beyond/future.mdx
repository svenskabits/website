---
title: Talking About the Future
description: Learn three common Swedish patterns for talking about future events and when each is often used.
---

Swedish does not have one obligatory future tense. Instead, speakers commonly use **ska + infinitive**, **kommer att + infinitive**, or the **present tense** with a future time expression. The choice often reflects whether something is a plan, a prediction, or an arrangement, but context matters and the patterns can overlap.

## Three ways to talk about the future

### *Ska* + infinitive: plans and intentions

Use a present form of **ska** followed by the infinitive, without **att**. This often presents a plan, intention, or decision:

> **subject + ska + infinitive**

- **Jag ska ringa imorgon.** — I’m going to call tomorrow.
- **Vi ska äta på restaurang ikväll.** — We’re going to eat at a restaurant tonight.
- **Hon ska börja ett nytt jobb nästa vecka.** — She’s going to start a new job next week.

### *Kommer att* + infinitive: predictions and expected developments

Use **kommer att** followed by the infinitive to present something as likely or expected to happen:

> **subject + kommer att + infinitive**

- **Det kommer att regna i eftermiddag.** — It’s going to rain this afternoon.
- **Priserna kommer att öka.** — Prices will go up.
- **Du kommer att trivas här.** — You’ll enjoy living here.

### Present tense: arrangements and schedules

The present tense can refer to the future when a future time is clear from context. It is common for arrangements, timetables, and scheduled events:

> **present-tense verb + future time expression**

- **Tåget går klockan åtta imorgon.** — The train leaves at eight tomorrow.
- **Jag träffar läkaren på fredag.** — I’m seeing the doctor on Friday.
- **Filmen börjar om tio minuter.** — The film starts in ten minutes.

## Choosing a pattern

These are common tendencies, not strict rules. **Ska** can also occur in predictions, and **kommer att** can describe an event someone expects or intends; the situation and the speaker’s perspective affect the choice. A future time expression can make a present-tense sentence clearly future:

- **De ska flytta snart.** — They’re planning to move soon.
- **De kommer att flytta snart.** — They’re expected to move soon.
- **De flyttar snart.** — They’re moving soon.

All three can describe a future move, with slightly different emphasis depending on context.

## Common confusion: *ska* and *skulle*

Do not treat **skulle** as another future form interchangeable with **ska**. **Skulle** commonly means “would” and is used in conditional or hypothetical statements:

- **Jag ska ringa dig imorgon.** — I’m going to call you tomorrow.
- **Jag skulle ringa dig, men jag glömde.** — I would call you, but I forgot.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag ska laga middag ikväll.** — I’m going to make dinner tonight.
2. **Vi ska hälsa på mormor imorgon.** — We’re going to visit Grandma tomorrow.
3. **Ska du jobba i helgen?** — Are you going to work this weekend?
4. **Det kommer att bli kallt i natt.** — It’s going to get cold tonight.
5. **Hon kommer att förstå snart.** — She’ll understand soon.
6. **De kommer att vara här lite senare.** — They’ll be here a little later.
7. **Bussen går om fem minuter.** — The bus leaves in five minutes.
8. **Butiken stänger klockan sex idag.** — The shop closes at six today.
9. **Vi träffas efter jobbet imorgon.** — We’re meeting after work tomorrow.
10. **Kursen börjar nästa måndag.** — The course starts next Monday.
