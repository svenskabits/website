---
title: Present and Past Participles
description: Learn how Swedish present and past participles describe actions and results, and how to distinguish a past participle from the supine.
---

Swedish participles are verb forms that can describe a person or thing. **Present participles** often suggest an ongoing action or characteristic, while **past participles** commonly describe something affected by an action or in a resulting state.

## Present participles

Present participles commonly end in **-ande** or **-ende**. The ending depends on the verb, so learn the form with the verb. These participles do not change to match the noun they describe:

- **ett växande problem** — a growing problem
- **en leende kvinna** — a smiling woman
- **ett fungerande system** — a working system

The meaning can describe an action in progress, as in “growing,” or a characteristic, as in “smiling” or “working.” It does not always mean that an action is happening at this exact moment.

## Past participles

Past participles have different endings depending on the verb. Many common forms end in **-ad, -at, -ade** or **-en, -et, -na**. When a past participle describes a noun, it commonly agrees with that noun in gender and number:

| Noun | Past participle | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common-gender singular (*en*) | **-d** or **-en** | **en stängd dörr** — a closed door |
| Neuter singular (*ett*) | **-t** or **-et** | **ett stängt fönster** — a closed window |
| Plural | **-da** or **-na** | **stängda dörrar** — closed doors |

The exact form depends on the verb: compare **stängd, stängt, stängda** with **skriven, skrivet, skrivna**. Past participles occur in descriptions such as **en skriven text** (“a written text”), and with verbs such as **vara** or **bli** to describe a result or an affected subject:

- **Fönstret är stängt.** — The window is closed.
- **Texten blev skriven på svenska.** — The text was written in Swedish.

## Common confusion: past participle or supine?

The **past participle** and **supine** can look similar, but they have different uses. The past participle describes a noun and can agree with it; the supine is the form used after **har** to make the perfect. For **skriva** (“write”):

| Use | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Past participle with a noun | **skriven** (common), **skrivet** (neuter), **skrivna** (plural) | **en skriven text** — a written text |
| Supine after **har** | **skrivit** | **Jag har skrivit.** — I have written. |

So say **en skriven text**, but **Jag har skrivit**. Do not use the past participle after **har** in the perfect.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Ett växande problem.** — A growing problem.
2. **En leende pojke.** — A smiling boy.
3. **Ett fungerande kök.** — A working kitchen.
4. **En stängd dörr.** — A closed door.
5. **Ett stängt fönster.** — A closed window.
6. **Stängda dörrar.** — Closed doors.
7. **En skriven text.** — A written text.
8. **Ett skrivet brev.** — A written letter.
9. **Brevet är skrivet på svenska.** — The letter is written in Swedish.
10. **Jag har skrivit ett brev.** — I have written a letter.
