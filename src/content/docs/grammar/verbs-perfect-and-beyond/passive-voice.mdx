---
title: "Passive Voice: -s and Bli"
description: "Learn how Swedish uses passive -s and bli with a past participle to focus on an action or its result."
---

Swedish has two common ways to make a passive sentence. A passive sentence focuses on what happens to a person or thing, rather than on who does the action.

## The -s passive

Add **-s** to the verb form. In the present, **öppnar** (“opens”) becomes **öppnas** (“is opened”); in the past, **skrev** (“wrote”) becomes **skrevs** (“was written”). This form is often concise and common for general processes, instructions, and events when the person doing the action is not important.

- **Dörren öppnas automatiskt.** — The door opens automatically.
- **Boken skrevs på svenska.** — The book was written in Swedish.
- **Maten serveras klockan tolv.** — The food is served at twelve.

The ending can also appear in other tenses: **Dörren har öppnats** means “The door has been opened.”

## Bli + past participle

Use a form of **bli** (“become”) followed by a past participle. This pattern often puts more emphasis on an event or a change—on something getting done. The participle agrees with the subject in gender and number:

- **Bilen blev tvättad igår.** — The car was washed yesterday.
- **Huset blev målat i somras.** — The house was painted this summer.
- **Fönstren blev putsade i morse.** — The windows were cleaned this morning.

The participle endings in these examples are **-ad** with the common-gender singular **bilen**, **-at** with the neuter singular **huset**, and **-ade** with the plural **fönstren**. The form of **bli** carries the tense: **blir tvättad** (“is getting washed”), **blev tvättad** (“was washed”), **har blivit tvättad** (“has been washed”).

## Comparing the two

Both patterns can describe a passive action, but they are not always interchangeable. The **-s** form often presents an action or process concisely; **bli** often highlights an event or change. These are tendencies, not fixed rules, and the best choice depends on the sentence and context.

Compare the active sentence with two passive versions:

- **Någon tvättade bilen igår.** — Someone washed the car yesterday. *(active)*
- **Bilen tvättades igår.** — The car was washed yesterday. *(-s passive)*
- **Bilen blev tvättad igår.** — The car got/was washed yesterday. *(bli passive)*

## Common confusion: not every -s is passive

Passive **-s** focuses on what is done to the subject, as in **Dörren öppnas**. As in the previous lesson on **-s verbs**, an **-s** form can instead describe a mutual action or belong to a verb normally used with **-s**. Use the meaning and the verb in context to tell these apart.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Dörren öppnas klockan nio.** — The door is opened at nine.
2. **Butiken stängs på söndagar.** — The shop is closed on Sundays.
3. **Boken skrevs för länge sedan.** — The book was written a long time ago.
4. **Maten serveras i matsalen.** — The food is served in the dining room.
5. **Här säljs färska grönsaker.** — Fresh vegetables are sold here.
6. **Bilen blev tvättad igår.** — The car was washed yesterday.
7. **Mötet blev inställt.** — The meeting was canceled.
8. **Huset blev byggt 1920.** — The house was built in 1920.
9. **Fönstren blir putsade varje vår.** — The windows are cleaned every spring.
10. **Ansökan har blivit godkänd.** — The application has been approved.
