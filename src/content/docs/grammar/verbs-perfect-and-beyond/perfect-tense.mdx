title: Perfect Tense
description: Learn how to form the Swedish perfect with har and the supine form of a verb.

The Swedish **perfect** describes a past event that is relevant now or talks about experience. It is formed with **har** (“have/has”) followed by the verb’s **supine** form.

## Forming the perfect

Use this pattern:

> **subject + har + supine**

Here, **har** is the auxiliary verb, and the supine is the main verb form that follows it. The supine does not change for different subjects:

| Infinitive | Supine | Perfect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| läsa (read) | läst | **Jag har läst.** — I have read. |
| bo (live) | bott | **Vi har bott här.** — We have lived here. |
| köpa (buy) | köpt | **Hon har köpt en bok.** — She has bought a book. |
| skriva (write) | skrivit | **De har skrivit ett mejl.** — They have written an email. |

Supine forms vary, so learn them with the verb. The perfect often presents a past event as connected to the present, or describes something someone has experienced:

- **Jag har tappat min telefon.** — I’ve lost my phone. (It matters now.)
- **Har du varit i Sverige?** — Have you been to Sweden? (Asking about experience.)

The English translation may use “have” or “has,” but Swedish **har + supine** does not always translate word for word as an English perfect. The best translation depends on context.

## Common confusion: which form follows *har*?

After **har**, use the **supine**, not the infinitive or the preterite (simple past):

- **har läsa** — incorrect; **läsa** is the infinitive.
- **har läste** — incorrect; **läste** is the preterite.
- **har läst** — correct; **läst** is the supine.

The perfect and the preterite are both used for past events. Their detailed contrast is covered in the next lesson.

## Useful Expressions

1. **Jag har redan ätit.** — I’ve already eaten.
2. **Vi har bott här i två år.** — We’ve lived here for two years.
3. **Har du sett den här filmen?** — Have you seen this film?
4. **Hon har köpt en ny jacka.** — She’s bought a new jacket.
5. **De har kommit hem.** — They’ve come home.
6. **Han har gjort klart läxan.** — He’s finished his homework.
7. **Jag har glömt nycklarna.** — I’ve forgotten the keys.
8. **Vi har läst den boken.** — We’ve read that book.
9. **Tåget har redan gått.** — The train has already left.
10. **Jag har aldrig varit i Göteborg.** — I’ve never been to Gothenburg.
