---
title: Pluperfect
description: Learn how to describe an event that happened before another past event in Swedish.
---

The Swedish **pluperfect** (also called the past perfect) describes something that had happened before another past event or past point in time. It is formed with **hade** followed by the verb’s **supine** form.

## Forming the pluperfect

Use this pattern:

> **subject + hade + supine**

**Hade** is the past form of **har** (“have/has”). The main verb stays in the supine form, which does not change for different subjects:

- **När jag kom, hade hon gått.** — When I arrived, she had left.
- **Vi hade redan ätit när gästerna kom.** — We had already eaten when the guests arrived.
- **Jag märkte att jag hade glömt plånboken.** — I noticed that I had forgotten my wallet.
- **Hade du bokat bord innan du kom?** — Had you booked a table before you came?

In each sentence, **hade + supine** marks the earlier event; the other past event or point in time provides the reference. The earlier event can also be clear from the surrounding context.

## Common confusion: which form follows *hade*?

After **hade**, use the **supine**, not the preterite (simple past) or the infinitive:

- **hade gick** — incorrect; **gick** is the preterite.
- **hade gå** — incorrect; **gå** is the infinitive.
- **hade gått** — correct; **gått** is the supine.

Compare **har gått** (“has/have gone”), the perfect with a present reference point, and **hade gått** (“had gone”), the pluperfect with a past reference point. The preterite **gick** simply says that someone went; it does not by itself mark the action as earlier than another past event.

## Useful Expressions

1. **När vi kom, hade de börjat.** — When we arrived, they had started.
2. **Jag hade redan ätit.** — I had already eaten.
3. **Hon hade gått hem.** — She had gone home.
4. **Vi hade sett filmen förut.** — We had seen the film before.
5. **De hade glömt nyckeln.** — They had forgotten the key.
6. **Han hade ringt tidigare.** — He had called earlier.
7. **Bussen hade redan åkt.** — The bus had already left.
8. **Barnen hade somnat.** — The children had fallen asleep.
9. **Hade du varit där förut?** — Had you been there before?
10. **Jag hade inte hört nyheten.** — I had not heard the news.
