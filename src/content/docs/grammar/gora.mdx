**Göra** is a common Swedish verb that can mean **do** or **make**. Its English translation depends on the words around it. The verb is irregular, but the forms are used with every subject in the same way.

| Form | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **göra** | to do, to make |
| Present | **gör** | do/does, make/makes; am/is/are doing or making |
| Past | **gjorde** | did, made |
| Supine | **gjort** | done, made (used with **har**) |

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag gör kaffe.** | I am making coffee. |
| **I går gjorde jag kaffe.** | Yesterday I made coffee. |
| **Jag har gjort kaffe.** | I have made coffee. |

## Common expressions with göra

A **collocation** is a combination of words that often appear together. Learn **göra** as part of common phrases, because English *do* or *make* does not always translate as **göra**:

| Swedish expression | English |
|---|---|
| **göra läxan** | do one's homework |
| **göra en smörgås** | make a sandwich |
| **göra kaffe** | make coffee |
| **göra ett misstag** | make a mistake |
| **göra en övning** | do an exercise |

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag gör läxan efter skolan.** | I do my homework after school. |
| **Hon gör en smörgås.** | She is making a sandwich. |
| **Han gjorde ett misstag.** | He made a mistake. |
| **Vi gör en övning tillsammans.** | We are doing an exercise together. |

English *do* and *make* do not always use **göra** in Swedish. For example, Swedish commonly uses **fatta ett beslut** (make a decision) and **träna** (exercise): **Vi måste fatta ett beslut.** (We have to make a decision.) **Hon tränar varje dag.** (She exercises every day.)

## Asking questions with göra

In a yes-or-no question, put the finite form of the verb before the subject. Swedish does not add English-style *do* or *does*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Gör du kaffe på morgonen?** | Do you make coffee in the morning? |
| **Gjorde ni övningen i går?** | Did you do the exercise yesterday? |
| **Har du gjort läxan?** | Have you done your homework? |

With a question word, put the question word first, then the verb and the subject: **Vad gör du i dag?** (What are you doing today?) **Vad gjorde du i går?** (What did you do yesterday?)

The present form **gör** can describe an action happening now or something someone does regularly. Context and time words make the meaning clear: **Vad gör du nu?** (What are you doing now?) **Vad gör du på lördagar?** (What do you do on Saturdays?)

Remember the common forms **göra, gör, gjorde, gjort** and learn the whole expression. Swedish **göra** often means *do* or *make*, but some English expressions use a different Swedish verb.
