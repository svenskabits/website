Swedish **ja** means **yes**, **nej** means **no**, and **jo** is used to contradict a negative question or statement. In answers to negative questions, the full response helps make the meaning clear.

## Answering a positive question

For an ordinary positive yes-or-no question, use **ja** to answer positively and **nej** to answer negatively:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Kan du simma? – Ja, det kan jag.** | Can you swim? – Yes, I can. |
| **Kan du simma? – Nej, det kan jag inte.** | Can you swim? – No, I can't. |

The short phrases **ja** (yes) and **nej** (no) can also stand alone when the context is clear.

## Use jo to contradict a negative

Use **jo** when you want to say that a negative question or statement is wrong. In English, it often corresponds to *yes, actually* or *yes, I do*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Kan du inte simma? – Jo, det kan jag.** | Can't you swim? – Yes, I can. |
| **Du kan inte simma. – Jo, det kan jag.** | You can't swim. – Yes, I can. |

Here **jo** contradicts the negative **inte**. The response after it states the positive fact: **det kan jag** (I can).

## Use nej to agree with a negative

If the negative question or statement is correct, answer **nej** and keep the negative meaning in the response:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Kan du inte simma? – Nej, det kan jag inte.** | Can't you swim? – No, I can't. |
| **Du har inte tid. – Nej, jag har inte tid.** | You don't have time. – No, I don't have time. |

For a direct comparison:

| Question | Positive answer: contradict the negative | Negative answer: agree with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Kommer du inte?** (Aren't you coming?) | **Jo, jag kommer.** (Yes, I am.) | **Nej, jag kommer inte.** (No, I'm not.) |
| **Är du inte trött?** (Aren't you tired?) | **Jo, det är jag.** (Yes, I am.) | **Nej, det är jag inte.** (No, I'm not.) |

When answering a negative question, **jo** clearly rejects the negative idea. **Nej** confirms it. English *yes* and *no* can be confusing in this situation, so it is useful to include the rest of the Swedish answer.

## Jo also contradicts other negative words

**Jo** can contradict a statement with a negative word such as **ingen** (no/not any): **Du har ingen cykel. – Jo, jag har en cykel.** (You don't have a bicycle. – Yes, I do have a bicycle.)

Remember: use **ja** for a positive answer to a positive question, **nej** for a negative answer, and **jo** to correct or contradict a negative question or statement.
