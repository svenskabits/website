In a Swedish **main clause** (a clause that can stand as a sentence), the finite verb—the verb that carries the tense—usually comes in the **second position**. This is called the **V2 rule**. “Second” means the second sentence element, not necessarily the second word.

If the subject comes first, the usual order is subject + verb. If a time phrase, place phrase, or other element comes first, the finite verb still comes second, so the subject normally follows it:

| First element | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | **Jag åker till jobbet i dag.** | I am going to work today. |
| Time | **I dag åker jag till jobbet.** | Today I am going to work. |
| Place | **På bordet ligger en bok.** | There is a book on the table. |
| Frequency adverb | **Ofta går vi på bio.** | We often go to the cinema. |
| Object | **Den här boken läser jag på kvällen.** | I read this book in the evening. |

The subject and verb have changed places in the examples where something other than the subject comes first. This is often called **inversion**.

## Negatives and the V2 order

In a neutral main clause, **inte** (not) usually comes after the finite verb and subject. If a time or place phrase comes first, the verb remains in second position, the subject follows it, and **inte** comes after the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag arbetar inte hemma i dag.** | I am not working at home today. |
| **I dag arbetar jag inte hemma.** | Today I am not working at home. |
| **På måndagar tränar hon inte.** | She does not exercise on Mondays. |

So a fronted time or place phrase does not move **inte** in front of the finite verb. The neutral pattern is **I dag arbetar jag inte**, not *I dag jag arbetar inte*.

## Do not confuse statements with yes-or-no questions

Both a statement with a fronted element and a yes-or-no question can have the verb before the subject, but the patterns are different:

| Type | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Statement with a time phrase first | **I morgon kommer hon.** | She is coming tomorrow. |
| Yes-or-no question | **Kommer hon i morgon?** | Is she coming tomorrow? |

In the statement, **i morgon** (tomorrow) is the first element, so the finite verb comes second. In the question, the finite verb comes first. Swedish does not add English-style *do* or *does*.

Wh-questions have their own pattern: **question word + finite verb + subject**. For example, **När kommer tåget?** (When is the train coming?)

## Subordinate clauses are different

A **subordinate clause** is part of a larger sentence and often begins with a word such as **att** (that), **när** (when), or **eftersom** (because). Inside it, the subject normally comes before the finite verb; it does not follow V2 order:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag vet att hon arbetar hemma.** | I know that she works at home. |
| **Jag går hem när tåget kommer.** | I go home when the train comes. |
| **Jag vet att hon inte arbetar hemma.** | I know that she is not working at home. |

Notice the different position of **inte** (not): in the main clause **Hon arbetar inte hemma.** (She is not working at home.), **inte** follows the finite verb. In the subordinate clause **att hon inte arbetar hemma** (that she is not working at home), it comes before the finite verb.

If a subordinate clause comes first, the main clause that follows it still obeys V2. The whole subordinate clause counts as the first element, so the main clause's finite verb comes before its subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **När tåget kommer, går jag hem.** | When the train comes, I go home. |
| **Eftersom jag är trött, går jag och lägger mig.** | Because I am tired, I am going to bed. |

Inside **när tåget kommer** (when the train comes), the subject **tåget** comes before the verb **kommer**. In the following main clause, **går jag** follows V2 because **när tåget kommer** occupies the first position. A comma is commonly used after an opening subordinate clause; it is not normally needed before a subordinate clause that follows its main clause.

Remember: main clauses keep the finite verb in second position. If something other than the subject is first, the subject usually follows the verb. Subordinate clauses use subject-before-verb order, while yes-or-no questions put the finite verb first.
