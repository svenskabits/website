Swedish often uses a passive verb when the action is more important than the person doing it, or when the person is unknown or does not need to be mentioned. One common passive is formed with **-s**. It is frequent in instructions, public information, news, and formal writing.

## How the -s passive is formed

The passive form is made from the verb's active form by adding **s**. In the present tense, the active ending **-r** is normally replaced by **-s**. In the past and supine, add **s** to the active form. The passive infinitive also ends in **-s**.

| Form | Active: *öppna* (open) | Passive: *öppnas* (be opened / is opened) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive | öppna | öppnas |
| Present | öppnar | öppnas |
| Past (preterite) | öppnade | öppnades |
| Supine | öppnat | öppnats |

> Butiken **öppnas** klockan nio.
> *The shop opens / is opened at nine.*
>
> Butiken **öppnades** förra året.
> *The shop was opened last year.*
>
> Butiken har **öppnats** tidigare än vanligt.
> *The shop has been opened earlier than usual.*

The verb form does not change for different subjects. The same passive form works with singular and plural subjects:

> Brevet **skickas** i dag.
> *The letter is being sent today.*
>
> Breven **skickas** i dag.
> *The letters are being sent today.*

## Changing an active sentence into a passive one

In an active sentence, the subject does the action. In the passive, the thing or person affected by the action becomes the subject. The person doing the action can be left out.

> Personalen **kontrollerar** biljetterna.
> *The staff check the tickets.*
>
> Biljetterna **kontrolleras**.
> *The tickets are checked.*

Here, *biljetterna* was the object of *kontrollerar*. It becomes the subject of *kontrolleras*. The passive sentence does not say who checks them.

> Någon **reparerade** bron.
> *Someone repaired the bridge.*
>
> Bron **reparerades** i somras.
> *The bridge was repaired last summer.*

If it is useful to name the person or thing doing the action, use **av** (“by”):

> Tavlan **målades av en lokal konstnär**.
> *The painting was painted by a local artist.*

The agent phrase with *av* is often omitted because it is already known, unimportant, or unclear.

## Using the passive in different tenses

The passive form follows the tense pattern of the active verb. With *skriva* (“write”), the forms are:

| Tense or form | Passive form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive | skrivas | Dokumentet måste **skrivas** under. — *The document must be signed.* |
| Present | skrivs | Namnet **skrivs** här. — *The name is written here.* |
| Past (preterite) | skrevs | Avtalet **skrevs** i går. — *The agreement was written yesterday.* |
| Perfect | har skrivits | Rapporten **har skrivits** på svenska. — *The report has been written in Swedish.* |
| Pluperfect | hade skrivits | Brevet **hade skrivits** innan mötet. — *The letter had been written before the meeting.* |
| Future | kommer att skrivas | Beslutet **kommer att skrivas** in i protokollet. — *The decision will be entered in the minutes.* |

After a modal verb, use the passive infinitive:

> Ansökan **kan skickas** digitalt.
> *The application can be sent digitally.*
>
> Blanketten **måste fyllas i** innan du lämnar in den.
> *The form must be filled in before you hand it in.*

In the perfect and pluperfect, use *har* or *hade* followed by the passive supine. In a future expression with *kommer att*, use the passive infinitive.

## General statements and notices

The -s passive is useful for rules and general processes, especially when no particular person is meant:

> Man **använder** BankID för att logga in. → BankID **används** för att logga in.
> *People use BankID to log in. → BankID is used to log in.*
>
> Man **säljer** färska grönsaker på torget. → Färska grönsaker **säljs** på torget.
> *They sell fresh vegetables in the square. → Fresh vegetables are sold in the square.*
>
> Man **öppnar** dörrarna klockan åtta. → Dörrarna **öppnas** klockan åtta.
> *They open the doors at eight. → The doors open / are opened at eight.*

Swedish often uses *det* in an impersonal passive when the sentence introduces an activity or event, particularly if there is no clear subject:

> Det **byggs** många nya bostäder i området.
> *Many new homes are being built in the area.*
>
> Det **diskuterades** länge på mötet.
> *It was discussed for a long time at the meeting.*

## Word order and negation

The passive verb occupies the same place in the sentence as an ordinary finite verb. In a main clause, it usually comes second. *Inte* follows it in a main clause, but comes before it in a subordinate clause:

> Paketet **levereras** inte på söndagar.
> *The parcel is not delivered on Sundays.*
>
> De sa att paketet **inte levereras** på söndagar.
> *They said that the parcel is not delivered on Sundays.*

Questions use the ordinary question word order:

> **Säljs** biljetter på bussen?
> *Are tickets sold on the bus?*

## Do not confuse passive -s with active s-verbs

Not every Swedish verb ending in **-s** is passive. Some verbs have **s** as part of their normal active form, such as *hoppas* (“hope”), *trivas* (“feel at home”), *finnas* (“exist”), and *lyckas* (“succeed”). These are not made passive by adding another *s*.

> Jag **hoppas** att tåget kommer i tid.
> *I hope the train arrives on time.*
>
> Hon **trivs** på sitt nya jobb.
> *She feels at home in her new job.*
>
> Det **finns** ett apotek nära stationen.
> *There is a pharmacy near the station.*

Compare an active s-verb with a true passive:

> De **hoppas** på bättre väder.
> *They hope for better weather.*
>
> Beslutet **fattas** i morgon.
> *The decision will be made tomorrow.*

In *fattas*, the decision receives the action: it is made. In *hoppas*, the subject is doing the hoping; the verb is simply an s-verb.

## -s passive and *bli* passive

Swedish also forms passives with *bli* (“become/get”) and a past participle, as in *Dörren blev stängd* (“The door was closed”). The -s passive is especially common for general facts, routines, official information, and concise written statements. The *bli* passive often presents an event or change more directly. Both forms can describe the same event, but they may place different emphasis on it.

> Nya regler **införs** nästa månad.
> *New rules are being introduced next month.*
>
> Nya regler **blir införda** nästa månad.
> *New rules will be introduced next month.*

Choose the -s passive when the focus is on what happens or what is done, and leave out the agent unless the reader needs to know who does it.
