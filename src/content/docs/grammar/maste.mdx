**Måste** expresses a strong need or obligation. It is a modal verb, and its form is the same with every subject. Put another verb in the infinitive directly after **måste**, without **att** (to):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag måste gå nu.** | I have to go now. |
| **Du måste läsa den här texten.** | You must read this text. |
| **Vi måste köpa mat.** | We have to buy food. |

The obligation can come from a rule, a situation, or the speaker's judgement:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Du måste ha en biljett när du åker buss.** | You have to have a ticket when you travel by bus. |
| **Jag måste ringa läkaren i dag.** | I have to call the doctor today. |

Sometimes **måste** expresses a strong conclusion rather than an obligation: **Hon måste vara hemma nu.** (She must be at home now.)

## Negatives: “not have to” or “not be allowed to”

Put **inte** after **måste** and before the infinitive. Be careful: **måste inte** usually means **do not have to**—something is not necessary. It does not normally mean that something is forbidden.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Du måste inte komma i morgon.** | You do not have to come tomorrow. |
| **Vi måste inte köpa biljetter i förväg.** | We do not have to buy tickets in advance. |

To say that something is forbidden or not allowed, use **får inte**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Du får inte parkera här.** | You must not park here. / You are not allowed to park here. |

## Questions

For a yes-or-no question, put **måste** before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Måste jag betala nu?** | Do I have to pay now? |
| **Måste ni gå redan?** | Do you have to leave already? |

With a question word, put the question word first, then **måste**, then the subject: **Varför måste hon vänta?** (Why does she have to wait?)

## Talking about past necessity

For a clear past obligation, a common construction is **var tvungen att** in the singular, **var tvunget att** with a neuter singular subject, or **var tvungna att** with a plural subject. Follow this expression with an infinitive. Here **att** is part of the construction:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag var tvungen att gå hem tidigt i går.** | I had to go home early yesterday. |
| **Barnet var tvunget att stanna hemma.** | The child had to stay at home. |
| **Vi var tvungna att boka bord.** | We had to book a table. |

Remember: use **måste + infinitive** without **att** for necessity. **Måste inte** means that something is not necessary; use **får inte** for a prohibition. For past necessity, a common clear construction is **var tvungen/tvunget/tvungna att**.
