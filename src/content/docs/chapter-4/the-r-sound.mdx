Swedish **r** has more than one pronunciation. In much of Sweden, speakers make it with the tongue tip near the ridge just behind the upper front teeth. The tip may vibrate for a trill, or make a shorter tap. In many parts of southern Sweden, **r** is instead made farther back in the mouth, near the uvula; it may sound like a soft friction or a small trill.

These are normal regional pronunciations. There is no single R sound that every Swedish speaker uses. An English **r**, as in *red*, is not a close model for the tongue-tip Swedish sound: it is usually made without a tap or trill.

## Tongue-tip R

For a tongue-tip R, place the tongue tip lightly near the ridge behind your upper teeth and let the air make it tap or vibrate. Do not press the tongue tightly against the ridge. Some speakers use a clear trill, while others use a brief tap, especially between vowels. Both are common ways of making a tongue-tip R.

Listen for the R in these words:

- *röd* — red
- *resa* — to travel; a trip
- *öra* — ear
- *vara* — to be
- *hör* — hears; listen!

If you cannot make the tongue vibrate, a light tap is a useful start. Keep the sound short and relaxed rather than adding a vowel after it.

## R with another consonant

With a tongue-tip R, **r** often changes the sound of a following **t, d, n, l,** or **s**. In much of central and northern Sweden, the tongue tip curls or moves back for the pair, making a single retroflex sound. “Retroflex” means the tongue tip is turned or drawn back from its usual position near the teeth. The spelling stays the same.

| Spelling | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **rt** | *kort* | short; a card |
| **rd** | *bord* | table |
| **rn** | *barn* | child; children |
| **rl** | *pärla* | pearl |
| **rs** | *först* | first |

In these examples, the sounds written **r** plus the next consonant often blend together in tongue-tip-R speech. You do not need to insert a pause between them. In southern Swedish varieties that use a back-of-the-mouth R, the consonants are usually pronounced without the same tongue-tip retroflexion. The exact realization varies by region and speaker.

The same pattern can be heard across a word boundary for some speakers, as in *var tog du vägen?* (where did you go?). How much sounds blend in connected speech depends on speaking style and region; pronounce each word clearly while you are learning.

## R and spelling

The letter **r** is pronounced in many ordinary words, but its exact sound depends on the speaker and its position. A doubled **rr** does not mean that you must make a different kind of R. It often follows a short vowel and marks a longer consonant, as in *kärr* (marsh), compared with *kär* (dear). The tongue-tip or back-of-the-mouth pronunciation can be used in either spelling.

## Practice

Try these short, natural phrases. First say each word clearly; then say the phrase smoothly:

- *Rita ritar en röd ros.* — Rita is drawing a red rose.
- *Barnet är först.* — The child is first.
- *Var är min pärla?* — Where is my pearl?

Notice how your R is made, and listen for other speakers’ versions. A tongue-tip trill, a tongue-tip tap, and a back-of-the-mouth R are all heard in Swedish. Aim for a relaxed, consistent sound rather than trying to copy a regional R that feels unnatural for you.
