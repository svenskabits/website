**Gilla** and **tycka om** both mean **like**. They can describe liking a person, thing, or activity. **Gilla** is especially common in conversation; **tycka om** is also a natural, widely used expression.

| Form | **gilla** | **tycka om** | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infinitive | **gilla** | **tycka om** | to like |
| Present | **gillar** | **tycker om** | like/likes |
| Past | **gillade** | **tyckte om** | liked |
| Supine | **gillat** | **tyckt om** | liked (used with **har**) |

The present form is the same for every subject: **Jag gillar kaffe.** (I like coffee.) **Hon gillar kaffe.** (She likes coffee.)

## Liking a thing or person

Put the thing or person directly after **gilla** or **tycka om**. Keep **om** as part of the expression **tycka om**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag gillar musik.** | I like music. |
| **Jag tycker om musik.** | I like music. |
| **De gillar sin lärare.** | They like their teacher. |
| **Vi tycker om vår nya granne.** | We like our new neighbour. |

The expressions are close in meaning, so you can often use either one. The best choice depends on the phrase and tone; **tycka om** is a safe, neutral way to say *like*.

## Liking an activity

When the object is an activity, use **att** before the infinitive with both expressions. In standard Swedish, do not normally leave **att** out here:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag gillar att läsa.** | I like reading. |
| **Hon tycker om att laga mat.** | She likes cooking. |
| **Gillar ni att dansa?** | Do you like dancing? |
| **Tycker du om att resa?** | Do you like travelling? |

English often uses an *-ing* form after *like*, but Swedish uses **att + infinitive**: **att läsa** (to read), **att laga mat** (to cook).

## Negatives and questions

In a negative statement, put **inte** after the finite verb. With **tycka om**, **inte** comes between **tycker** and **om**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag gillar inte kaffe.** | I do not like coffee. |
| **Jag tycker inte om kaffe.** | I do not like coffee. |
| **Han gillar inte att springa.** | He does not like running. |
| **De tycker inte om att vänta.** | They do not like waiting. |

For a yes-or-no question, put the finite verb before the subject. Swedish does not add English-style *do* or *does*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Gillar du filmen?** | Do you like the film? |
| **Tycker hon om te?** | Does she like tea? |

With a question word, put the question word first, followed by the finite verb and subject: **Vad gillar du?** (What do you like?) **Vad tycker ni om att göra på helgen?** (What do you like doing at the weekend?)

## Past and perfect forms

Use **gillade** or **tyckte om** for something someone liked in the past:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag gillade den här filmen.** | I liked this film. |
| **Vi tyckte om att bo i Malmö.** | We liked living in Malmö. |

With **har**, use the supine form: **Jag har alltid tyckt om musik.** (I have always liked music.) **Hon har gillat den här serien länge.** (She has liked this series for a long time.)

## Tycka om or tycka att?

**Tycka om** means *like*. Without **om**, **tycka** usually means *think* or *have an opinion*. Compare:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag tycker om filmen.** | I like the film. |
| **Jag tycker att filmen är bra.** | I think the film is good. |

In the second sentence, **att** means *that* and introduces a clause. It is not the infinitive marker used in **Jag tycker om att läsa.** (I like reading.)

Remember: use **gilla/tycka om + noun** for liking a thing or person, and **gilla/tycka om + att + infinitive** for liking an activity. Keep **om** in **tycka om**, and place **inte** between **tycker** and **om** when making it negative.
