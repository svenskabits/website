Swedish uses **hålla på att + infinitive** to present an activity as ongoing, developing, or about to happen. The phrase does not create a separate verb tense: **hålla** changes for tense and the main verb stays in the infinitive after **att**.

## An activity in progress

Use the construction when you want to emphasize that an activity is underway or taking time. The subject is the person or thing doing the activity:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag håller på att skriva ett mejl.** | I am in the middle of writing an email. |
| **De håller på att bygga en ny skola.** | They are building a new school. |
| **Vad håller du på att läsa?** | What are you reading at the moment? |

It is often possible to use the ordinary present tense instead. The longer construction puts more focus on the activity being in progress:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hon lagar middag.** | She is making dinner. / She makes dinner. |
| **Hon håller på att laga middag.** | She is in the middle of making dinner. |

## Forms across time

The verb **hålla** carries the tense. The verb naming the activity remains in the infinitive after **att**:

| Time | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| Present | **Vi håller på att renovera lägenheten.** | We are renovating the apartment. |
| Past | **Vi höll på att renovera lägenheten när vattenläckan upptäcktes.** | We were renovating the apartment when the water leak was discovered. |
| Perfect | **Vi har hållit på att renovera lägenheten i flera månader.** | We have been renovating the apartment for several months. |
| Pluperfect | **Vi hade hållit på att renovera lägenheten i flera månader när vi äntligen blev klara.** | We had been renovating the apartment for several months when we finally finished. |

With a modal verb or a future expression, **hålla** is in the infinitive:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi ska hålla på att renovera hela sommaren.** | We will be renovating all summer. |
| **De kan fortfarande hålla på att diskutera frågan.** | They may still be discussing the issue. |

## Change or an event that is about to happen

The construction can also describe a change that is underway or an event that seems imminent. The exact meaning depends on the verb and context:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Dagarna håller på att bli längre.** | The days are getting longer. |
| **Det håller på att börja regna.** | It is about to start raining. |
| **Tåget håller på att lämna stationen.** | The train is about to leave the station. |

With **höll på att** in the past, the sentence can mean that an event was close to happening but did not happen. This is often translated as *almost* or *nearly*:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag höll på att glömma nycklarna.** | I almost forgot the keys. |
| **Hon höll på att ramla, men tog tag i räcket.** | She nearly fell, but grabbed the railing. |

Context helps distinguish an activity that was actually underway from an event that nearly happened:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag höll på att laga mat när du ringde.** | I was in the middle of cooking when you called. |
| **Jag höll på att bränna maten.** | I nearly burned the food. |

## Negatives and questions

In a main clause, put **inte** after the finite form of **hålla** and before **på**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag håller inte på att laga mat just nu.** | I am not cooking right now. |
| **Håller ni på att flytta?** | Are you moving? |
| **Höll de på att lämna när du kom?** | Were they about to leave when you arrived? |

In a subordinate clause, **inte** normally comes before the finite verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag tror att hon inte håller på att flytta längre.** | I think she is no longer in the middle of moving. |

## Hålla på att or hålla på med?

Use **hålla på att** before a verb in the infinitive. Use **hålla på med** before a noun or noun phrase to say that someone is occupied with something:

| Pattern | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| **hålla på att + infinitive** | **Jag håller på att skriva en rapport.** | I am in the middle of writing a report. |
| **hålla på med + noun** | **Jag håller på med en rapport.** | I am working on a report. |

The question **Vad håller du på med?** means *What are you doing?* It asks generally about someone's activity; the more specific **Vad håller du på att göra?** asks what action they are in the middle of doing.
