## The usual order

When a Swedish statement gives both **when** something happens and **where** it happens, a useful neutral order is:

> **Subject + verb + (object) + time + place**

The time phrase says when the action happens. The place phrase says where. For example:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi träffade våra vänner i lördags på ett café.** | We met our friends on Saturday at a café. |
| **Jag arbetar varje dag på kontoret.** | I work at the office every day. |
| **De åt middag klockan sju hemma.** | They ate dinner at seven at home. |

This order is especially useful when both phrases come after the verb and any object. It is a good starting point for a neutral statement.

## Starting with the time

You can also put the time phrase first to make it the starting point of the sentence. The finite verb—the verb that carries the tense—still comes in the second position. The subject then follows the verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **I lördags träffade vi våra vänner på ett café.** | On Saturday, we met our friends at a café. |
| **Klockan sju åt de middag hemma.** | At seven, they ate dinner at home. |

The time phrase is one sentence part, even if it has several words. In **I lördags träffade vi ...**, that phrase is first and **träffade** is second.

## Word order can change for emphasis

Time-before-place is a helpful neutral pattern, not an unbreakable rule. Speakers may choose another order when they want to emphasize or contrast a detail, or when the sentence has a particular rhythm. For example, both of these can be used:

- **Vi träffades i går på stationen.** — We met at the station yesterday.
- **Vi träffades på stationen i går.** — We met at the station yesterday.

The second sentence puts the place before the time. The context or the speaker's emphasis helps explain why that order was chosen. When you are not sure, put the time before the place in the neutral sentence, or put the time first if you want to set the time as the topic.

## Keep the phrases together

Treat each time or place expression as a unit. A time expression can be a single word or a longer phrase, and the same is true of a place expression:

- **Vi ses imorgon efter jobbet i stan.** — We'll meet tomorrow after work in town.
- **Hon studerade svenska förra året i Uppsala.** — She studied Swedish last year in Uppsala.
- **På kvällen läste jag en bok hemma.** — In the evening, I read a book at home.

In the last example, the time phrase is first, so the verb comes next, before the subject: **På kvällen + läste + jag**.

## Quick guide

- In a neutral statement with both details after the verb, use **time before place**: **Vi åt lunch i dag på jobbet.** — We ate lunch at work today.
- You can begin with time; keep the finite verb in second position: **I dag åt vi lunch på jobbet.** — Today we ate lunch at work.
- Place can come before time when the context or emphasis calls for it: **Vi åt lunch på jobbet i dag.** — We ate lunch at work today.
