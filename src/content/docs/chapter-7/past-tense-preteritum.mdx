The **preterite** (Swedish *preteritum*) is the verb form used to talk about situations in the past. It does not change according to who did the action: the same form is used with **jag** (I), **du** (you), **hon** (she), **vi** (we), and other subjects.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag jobbade hemma i går.** | I worked at home yesterday. |
| **Hon jobbade hemma i går.** | She worked at home yesterday. |
| **De jobbade hemma i går.** | They worked at home yesterday. |

## When to use the preterite

Use the preterite for a past event or situation, often with a time expression that places it in a finished past period:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi reste till Malmö förra veckan.** | We travelled to Malmö last week. |
| **Han ringde mig klockan sju.** | He called me at seven. |
| **De bodde i Uppsala 2020.** | They lived in Uppsala in 2020. |

It can describe a one-time event, a repeated action, or a state that lasted for a time:

- **Jag träffade Sara på jobbet i går.** (I met Sara at work yesterday.)
- **När jag var barn spelade jag fotboll varje dag.** (When I was a child, I played football every day.)
- **Hon var trött hela kvällen.** (She was tired all evening.)

Swedish does not have a separate verb ending for an action that was “in progress” in the past. The preterite can describe an ongoing background action, especially with another event or a time expression:

- **Jag läste när du ringde.** (I was reading when you called.)
- **Klockan åtta lagade vi middag.** (At eight, we were making dinner.)

To emphasize that someone was in the middle of doing something, Swedish can also use **hålla på att** in the preterite, **höll på att**:

- **Jag höll på att laga middag när du ringde.** (I was in the middle of making dinner when you called.)

## How to form it

Many verbs follow regular patterns. The infinitive and present form can help you recognize the pattern, but there are exceptions. Learn the preterite when you learn a new verb.

| Pattern | Infinitive | Present | Preterite | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Group 1: **-ade** | **arbeta** (work) | **arbetar** | **arbetade** | **Jag arbetade hemma i går.** (I worked at home yesterday.) |
| Group 2a: usually **-de** | **ringa** (call) | **ringer** | **ringde** | **Hon ringde sin vän.** (She called her friend.) |
| Group 2b: usually **-te** | **köpa** (buy) | **köper** | **köpte** | **Vi köpte kaffe.** (We bought coffee.) |
| Group 3: **-dde** | **bo** (live) | **bor** | **bodde** | **De bodde i Lund.** (They lived in Lund.) |

Group 2a often has a stem ending in a voiced sound, while group 2b often has a stem ending in a voiceless sound such as **p**, **t**, **k**, or **s**. This is a guide, not a rule that predicts every verb. Some verbs have a spelling change as well: **läsa, läser, läste** (read, read(s), read) and **röka, röker, rökte** (smoke, smoke(s), smoked).

Many common verbs are irregular. Some change their vowel, and some have a form that cannot be predicted from the regular patterns. Learn these forms as a set:

| Infinitive | Present | Preterite | Meaning of the preterite |
|---|---|---|---|
| **vara** | **är** | **var** | was/were |
| **ha** | **har** | **hade** | had |
| **göra** | **gör** | **gjorde** | did/made |
| **gå** | **går** | **gick** | went/walked |
| **komma** | **kommer** | **kom** | came |
| **se** | **ser** | **såg** | saw |
| **säga** | **säger** | **sa** (also **sade**) | said |
| **få** | **får** | **fick** | got/received; was allowed to |
| **ta** | **tar** | **tog** | took |
| **ge** | **ger** | **gav** | gave |
| **bli** | **blir** | **blev** | became |
| **äta** | **äter** | **åt** | ate |
| **dricka** | **dricker** | **drack** | drank |
| **skriva** | **skriver** | **skrev** | wrote |
| **sova** | **sover** | **sov** | slept |
| **veta** | **vet** | **visste** | knew |

## Put the verb in a sentence

In an ordinary statement, the preterite verb follows the subject:

- **Jag såg en bra film i går.** (I saw a good film yesterday.)
- **Barnen åt frukost före skolan.** (The children ate breakfast before school.)

If another word or phrase comes first, the verb still comes second. The subject then follows the verb:

- **I går såg jag en bra film.** (Yesterday I saw a good film.)
- **Efter skolan gick barnen hem.** (After school, the children went home.)

For a yes/no question, put the verb before the subject. To make a negative statement, put **inte** after the verb:

- **Ringde du henne i går?** (Did you call her yesterday?)
- **Jag ringde inte henne i går.** (I did not call her yesterday.)

With a question word, the question word comes first, followed by the verb and then the subject:

- **När kom tåget?** (When did the train arrive?)
- **Varför gick ni hem?** (Why did you go home?)

In a subordinate clause, **inte** usually comes before the verb:

- **Jag stannade hemma eftersom jag inte mådde bra.** (I stayed home because I did not feel well.)

## Preterite or present perfect?

The preterite presents something as belonging to a past time. The **present perfect** uses **har** plus a special verb form called the **supine**; it often connects a past event to the present or leaves its exact time unstated. The choice does not always match the choice between English *simple past* and *present perfect* word for word.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag åt lunch klockan tolv.** | I ate lunch at twelve. |
| **Jag har redan ätit lunch.** | I have already eaten lunch. |
| **Hon bodde i Sverige förra året.** | She lived in Sweden last year. |
| **Hon har bott i Sverige i fem år.** | She has lived in Sweden for five years. |

Use the preterite with a finished past time such as **i går** (yesterday), **förra året** (last year), or **klockan tolv** (at twelve). The present perfect is common when the time period continues to the present, as in **i dag** (today), or when the result matters now. These are useful patterns rather than absolute rules; focus on whether you are placing the event in a finished past time or connecting it to the present.

## Past forms of modal verbs

Some modal verbs also have preterite forms. The verb after a modal is usually an infinitive without **att**:

| Present | Preterite | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **kan** (can) | **kunde** (could) | **Jag kunde inte komma i går.** (I could not come yesterday.) |
| **vill** (want to) | **ville** (wanted to) | **Hon ville stanna hemma.** (She wanted to stay home.) |
| **ska** (will/be going to) | **skulle** (would/was going to) | **Vi skulle ta tåget.** (We were going to take the train.) |

For past obligation, Swedish commonly uses **var tvungen att** (had to), with **tvungen** changing for the subject: **tvungen** with *jag/du/han/hon*, **tvunget** with *ett*-words, and **tvungna** with plural subjects.

- **Jag var tvungen att gå tidigt.** (I had to leave early.)
- **De var tvungna att vänta.** (They had to wait.)
