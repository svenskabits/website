The verbs **vara** (be), **ha** (have), and **göra** (do, make) are very common. Their preterite forms are irregular, but they stay the same with every subject.

| Infinitive | Present | Preterite | Supine |
|---|---|---|---|
| **vara** | **är** | **var** | **varit** |
| **ha** | **har** | **hade** | **haft** |
| **göra** | **gör** | **gjorde** | **gjort** |

The **preterite** tells about a past situation or event, often in a finished time such as **i går** (yesterday) or **förra året** (last year). The **supine** is the form used after **har** or **hade** to make other past tenses.

## Vara: was and were

Use **var** to describe where someone or something was, what someone was like, or how a past situation was. English uses both *was* and *were*, but Swedish uses **var** for every subject.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag var hemma i går.** | I was at home yesterday. |
| **Vi var trötta efter resan.** | We were tired after the trip. |
| **Mötet var kort.** | The meeting was short. |
| **När hon var barn bodde hon i Umeå.** | When she was a child, she lived in Umeå. |

## Ha: had

Use **hade** for possession, relationships, time, and many conditions in the past. It does not change with the subject.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Han hade en gammal cykel.** | He had an old bicycle. |
| **Vi hade gäster i helgen.** | We had guests at the weekend. |
| **Hade du tid efter jobbet?** | Did you have time after work? |
| **De hade ont i ryggen.** | They had back pain. |

## Göra: did and made

Use **gjorde** for past actions with **göra**, including doing an activity or task and making something. The form is the same with every subject.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vad gjorde du i går?** | What did you do yesterday? |
| **Jag gjorde läxan efter middagen.** | I did the homework after dinner. |
| **Hon gjorde en smörgås till lunch.** | She made a sandwich for lunch. |
| **Vi gjorde mycket på jobbet i går.** | We did a lot at work yesterday. |

In everyday speech, **Vad gjorde du?** is a common way to ask what someone did. Swedish does not add a separate word for *did* in this question: **gjorde** already carries the past meaning.

## Questions and negative sentences

In a normal statement, put the subject before the verb. In a yes-or-no question, put **var**, **hade**, or **gjorde** before the subject. In a main-clause negative sentence, put **inte** after the verb.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hon var inte på jobbet i går.** | She was not at work yesterday. |
| **Hade ni en hund när ni bodde där?** | Did you have a dog when you lived there? |
| **Jag gjorde inte maten.** | I did not make the food. |
| **Varför gjorde han det?** | Why did he do that? |

If a time phrase or another phrase comes first, the verb still comes second, before the subject:

- **I går var jag hemma hela dagen.** (Yesterday I was at home all day.)
- **Efter skolan gjorde barnen sina läxor.** (After school, the children did their homework.)

In a subordinate clause, such as a clause beginning with **eftersom** (because), **inte** usually comes before the verb:

- **Jag stannade hemma eftersom jag inte var frisk.** (I stayed home because I was not well.)

## Do not confuse preterite and supine

Use the preterite when you place the situation or action in a finished past time. Use **har** with the supine when the past connects to the present or the time is not presented as finished. For example, **var** means *was/were*, while **varit** is used after **har**.

| Preterite | Present perfect | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Jag var i Göteborg i går.** | **Jag har varit i Göteborg.** | I was in Gothenburg yesterday. / I have been to Gothenburg. |
| **Hon hade en katt förut.** | **Hon har haft en katt.** | She had a cat before. / She has had a cat. |
| **Vi gjorde maten klockan sex.** | **Vi har gjort maten.** | We made the food at six. / We have made the food. |

Do not put the preterite after **har**: say **har varit**, **har haft**, and **har gjort**, not *har var*, *har hade*, or *har gjorde*.

## Hade + supine: something had happened earlier

**Hade** can also be an auxiliary verb followed by a supine. This is the past perfect: it shows that something happened before another past event. In this pattern, **hade** does not mean possession.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag hade varit där en gång tidigare.** | I had been there once before. |
| **Hon hade haft bilen i flera år när hon sålde den.** | She had had the car for several years when she sold it. |
| **Vi hade redan gjort maten när gästerna kom.** | We had already made the food when the guests arrived. |

Compare **Jag hade en bil** (I had a car), where **hade** is the main verb, with **Jag hade köpt en bil** (I had bought a car), where **hade** is followed by the supine **köpt**.
