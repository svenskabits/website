**Så** and **för** can connect two main clauses, but they show different relationships. **Så** introduces a result; **för** introduces a reason.

## **Så** introduces a result

Use **så** to show what happens as a result of the first clause. It often means **so** in English:

> Det blev sent, så vi tog en taxi hem.  
> It got late, so we took a taxi home.

> Jag är trött, så jag går och lägger mig.  
> I am tired, so I am going to bed.

The second clause can also describe what will happen if someone does something:

> Ta med en varm tröja, så slipper du frysa.  
> Bring a warm sweater, and then you won't have to be cold.

In a sequence of events, **och så** often means **and then**:

> Vi åt middag och så såg vi en film.  
> We had dinner and then watched a film.

## **För** introduces a reason

Use **för** to explain why something is true or happens. As a conjunction, it often means **because** or **for**:

> Jag tar bussen, för det regnar.  
> I am taking the bus, because it is raining.

> Vi måste gå nu, för tåget går om fem minuter.  
> We have to leave now, for the train leaves in five minutes.

The clause after conjunction **för** has ordinary main-clause word order. It gives the reason for the statement before it.

## Compare the direction of the explanation

The same two ideas can be joined with either word. The order of the clauses changes which one is presented as the reason and which as the result:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag är trött, så jag går hem. | I am tired, so I am going home. |
| Jag går hem, för jag är trött. | I am going home, because I am tired. |

With **så**, the result comes after the conjunction. With **för**, the reason comes after it.

## Word order and punctuation

Both words can join two main clauses. The clause after **så** or **för** keeps main-clause word order: normally the subject comes before the verb, unless another part of the sentence comes first. A comma is commonly used before the conjunction when it joins two full clauses:

> Det började regna, så efter en stund gick vi in.  
> It started raining, so after a while we went inside.

> Jag ringer henne, för i morgon reser hon bort.  
> I am calling her, because tomorrow she is going away.

## Other uses of **så** and **för**

The meanings above are specific to **så** and **för** as conjunctions. In **så att** (“so that”), the words work together to introduce an intended result or purpose:

> Vi går nu så att vi inte missar tåget.  
> We are leaving now so that we don't miss the train.

**För** can also be a preposition, meaning **for** before a noun or pronoun. That is a different use from the conjunction:

> Den här presenten är för dig.  
> This present is for you.
