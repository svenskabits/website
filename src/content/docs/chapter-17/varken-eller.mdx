## Meaning and scope

**Varken … eller** means **neither … nor**. It joins two alternatives and says that neither one applies. Swedish *varken* already makes the coordination negative; it is not an ordinary positive *either* that needs a separate *inte*.

- *Jag dricker varken kaffe eller te.* — I drink neither coffee nor tea.
- *Varken förslaget eller ändringen löste problemet.* — Neither the proposal nor the change solved the problem.

The pair can join two or more matching elements. Put *varken* before the first element and *eller* before the next one. The elements should be parallel—that is, they should play the same role and have a similar grammatical form.

## Words, phrases, and actions

The coordinated elements can be nouns, adjectives, prepositional phrases, infinitives, or other phrases:

- *Hon talar varken svenska eller norska.* — She speaks neither Swedish nor Norwegian.
- *Beslutet var varken enkelt eller billigt.* — The decision was neither simple nor inexpensive.
- *De vill varken flytta till Stockholm eller bo kvar här.* — They want neither to move to Stockholm nor to stay here.
- *Vi har varken tid för en lång diskussion eller möjlighet att skjuta upp beslutet.* — We have neither time for a long discussion nor the possibility of postponing the decision.

The pair can also connect whole clauses. Each clause keeps its own word order:

- *Varken kunde kommunen finansiera projektet eller ville staten ta över ansvaret.* — Neither could the municipality fund the project nor did the state want to take over responsibility.
- *Hon varken ringde när hon kom fram eller skickade ett meddelande.* — She neither called when she arrived nor sent a message.

For long or complex alternatives, repeat small words when they make the parallel structure clear. A shared subject can apply to both coordinated verb phrases:

- *Hon varken ringde när hon kom fram eller skickade ett meddelande under resan.* — She neither called when she arrived nor sent a message during the journey.

## Position in main and subordinate clauses

Place the pair as close as practical to the alternatives it covers. Its position can change what is being denied:

- *Hon varken läser eller skriver på svenska.* — She neither reads nor writes in Swedish.
- *Hon läser varken svenska eller danska.* — She reads neither Swedish nor Danish.

In a main clause, a finite verb (the verb marked for tense) normally comes before *varken* when the pair covers a later part of the predicate. This follows ordinary Swedish main-clause word order:

- *De har varken svarat på mejlet eller förklarat förseningen.* — They have neither replied to the email nor explained the delay.
- *Han ville varken be om hjälp eller erkänna att han hade gjort fel.* — He neither wanted to ask for help nor admit that he had made a mistake.

In a subordinate clause, the subject and a sentence adverb such as *varken* normally come before the finite verb. This is the usual subordinate-clause placement of negation:

- *Jag tror att de varken har svarat på mejlet eller förklarat förseningen.* — I think they have neither replied to the email nor explained the delay.
- *Hon sa att han varken ville be om hjälp eller erkänna sitt misstag.* — She said that he neither wanted to ask for help nor admit his mistake.

When *varken* begins a main clause and applies to the predicate, the finite verb normally follows it, as required by the V2 pattern (the finite verb is in second position):

- *Varken kom hon i tid eller bad hon om ursäkt.* — She neither arrived on time nor apologized.
- *Varken hade företaget informerat kunderna eller ändrat sina rutiner.* — The company had neither informed the customers nor changed its procedures.

Here the subject follows the finite verb in each clause. By contrast, when the coordinated pair itself is the subject, use ordinary subject–verb order:

- *Varken chefen eller hennes kollegor kunde svara.* — Neither the manager nor her colleagues could answer.

## Agreement and reference

Swedish finite verbs do not change form to agree with a singular or plural subject. So a subject joined by the pair does not require a special verb form:

- *Varken läraren eller eleverna förstod instruktionen.* — Neither the teacher nor the students understood the instruction.
- *Varken jag eller mina vänner har fått besked.* — Neither my friends nor I have received an answer.

The form of a later pronoun or adjective depends on what it refers to, not on a special agreement rule for *varken … eller*. If the alternatives have different genders or numbers and a later reference could be unclear, repeat the noun or rephrase:

- *Varken rapporten eller bilagorna var färdiga, så vi skickade inte in dokumenten.* — Neither the report nor the appendices were ready, so we did not submit the documents.

The plural *dokumenten* clearly refers to both alternatives together.

## Avoiding a second negation

Unlike English, Swedish does not normally add another *inte* to a phrase already made negative by *varken*. Use a positive or neutral expression for each alternative:

- *Jag har varken bil eller cykel.* — I have neither a car nor a bicycle.
- Not normally: *Jag har inte varken bil eller cykel.*

Adding *inte* to the same negative scope is nonstandard for the intended meaning and can make the sentence sound contradictory or suggest a different scope. Similarly, do not normally combine the pair with another negative word such as *ingen* (“no one/no”) or *aldrig* (“never”) to express the same single negation. Use *varken* with the things or actions being excluded:

- *Varken Sara eller någon annan kunde hjälpa till.* — Neither Sara nor anyone else could help.
- *Hon har varken ringt eller skrivit på flera veckor.* — She has neither called nor written for several weeks.

In the first example, *någon annan* (“anyone else”) is not itself negative; the negative force comes from the whole paired subject. A negative word can still occur in a separate clause or with a genuinely separate meaning, but do not stack negatives mechanically.

## Choosing between *varken … eller* and *inte … heller*

Use the paired form when you want to name both rejected alternatives explicitly. Swedish also uses *inte* with *eller* in ordinary negated clauses, and *heller* commonly means “either” after a negative statement:

- *Jag dricker inte kaffe eller te.* — I do not drink coffee or tea. (The negation covers the coordinated items; the paired form is more explicit.)
- *Jag dricker inte kaffe. Jag dricker inte te heller.* — I do not drink coffee. I do not drink tea either.
- *Jag dricker varken kaffe eller te.* — I drink neither coffee nor tea.

These forms overlap in meaning, but the paired form makes the two-part coordination especially clear. Do not translate English **neither … nor** word for word as Swedish *inte … eller* when you want to avoid ambiguity.
