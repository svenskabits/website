Swedish has a subjunctive mood, but it is not a productive part of everyday speech in the way it is in some languages. Most subjunctive forms survive in a small number of established wishes, blessings, and formal or literary expressions. Learn these as whole expressions rather than trying to form a subjunctive for every verb.

## *Leve*: wishes and celebrations

**Leve** is a surviving present subjunctive of *leva* (to live). It expresses a wish that someone or something should live, prosper, or endure. It usually comes before the subject, especially in a short celebratory formula:

- *Leve Sverige!* — Long live Sweden!
- *Leve brudparet!* — Long live the newlyweds!

This use is emphatic and ceremonial. In ordinary conversation, speakers would normally express a wish in another way, for example with *Jag hoppas att Sverige fortsätter att utvecklas väl* (I hope Sweden continues to develop well).

## *Vare*: blessings and formal formulas

**Vare** is a surviving subjunctive of *vara* (to be). It occurs in blessings and solemn or literary formulas:

- *Gud vare med dig.* — God be with you.
- *Frid vare med de döda.* — Peace be with the dead.

These expressions sound formal, religious, or old-fashioned; *vare* is not the normal form of *vara* in everyday sentences. For example, an ordinary statement uses *är*: *Gud är med dig* (God is with you).

The sequence **vare sig** also occurs in contemporary Swedish, especially in the conjunction *vare sig … eller* (whether … or; regardless of whether). Treat it as a fixed conjunction, not as a freely chosen subjunctive form:

- *Vare sig du håller med eller inte, måste vi fatta ett beslut.* — Whether you agree or not, we have to make a decision.

In less formal wording, *oavsett om* can often replace *vare sig*: *Oavsett om du håller med eller inte …* (Regardless of whether you agree or not …).

## *Ske*: accepting what happens

**Ske** is a surviving subjunctive of *ske* (to happen, occur). It appears in the fixed expression *ske det som ske vill*, which conveys acceptance of whatever happens:

- *Ske det som ske vill; vi måste ändå gå vidare.* — Come what may, we still have to move on.

The expression is literary or elevated. In everyday Swedish, *vad som än händer* (whatever happens) is a more usual alternative. In ordinary statements, the present indicative is *sker*: *Vad sker nu?* (What is happening now?).

## *Må*: wishes and blessings

**Må** can introduce a wish or hope, often with the sense “may …”. It is common in slogans, toasts, blessings, and other deliberately elevated or playful formulas:

- *Må bästa lag vinna!* — May the best team win!
- *Må er framtid tillsammans bli lycklig.* — May your future together be happy.

This optative use sounds more formal or ceremonial than an everyday wish. Depending on the situation, speakers can instead say *Jag hoppas att det bästa laget vinner* (I hope the best team wins) or *Jag hoppas att ni får ett lyckligt liv tillsammans* (I hope you have a happy life together).

Do not confuse this wish particle with the ordinary verb *må* “feel/be” (often used with *bra* or *dåligt*). That verb changes form in the present:

- *Jag mår bra.* — I feel well.
- *Hon mår bättre nu.* — She feels better now.

The *må* in *Må bästa lag vinna!* does not take the present-tense ending *-r* and is not that verb's ordinary present-tense use.

## *Vore*: hypothetical situations

**Vore** is the subjunctive form of *vara* used for unreal or imagined situations. Unlike many of the older formulas above, it is still widely used in modern Swedish hypothetical clauses and polite suggestions:

- *Om jag vore du skulle jag tacka ja.* — If I were you, I would accept.
- *Det vore bra om vi kunde börja tidigare.* — It would be good if we could start earlier.
- *Det vore trevligt att träffas igen.* — It would be nice to meet again.

In these examples, *vore* presents the situation as imagined, not as a past fact. The form *var* is the ordinary past indicative of *vara*, as in *Jag var trött i går* (I was tired yesterday). In hypothetical clauses, *om jag var du* is also widely used, particularly in speech; *om jag vore du* makes the hypothetical meaning explicit and can sound more careful or formal. Do not assume that every past-tense form after *om* is a subjunctive: *var* remains the same form used for past statements.

## Recognizing the limits

The examples above are established forms, not a template for making new subjunctives. In most present-day contexts, Swedish uses ordinary present forms, modal verbs, or phrases such as *jag hoppas att* instead of a special subjunctive form. For instance, *Hon kommer i morgon* (She is coming tomorrow) uses the ordinary present; it is not a subjunctive simply because English might use a different mood in a related sentence.

Also distinguish these surviving forms from ordinary past-tense forms that may look unfamiliar or express a hypothetical idea. For example, *gick* in *Om tåget gick oftare skulle fler åka kollektivt* (If the train ran more often, more people would use public transport) is the past form of *gå*, not a subjunctive. The form *vore* is a genuine surviving subjunctive, but *var* and forms such as *gick* do not become subjunctive just because a sentence is hypothetical.
