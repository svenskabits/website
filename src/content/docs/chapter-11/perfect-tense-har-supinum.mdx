Swedish forms the **present perfect** with the present-tense auxiliary **har** (have/has) and a verb form called the **supine**:

> **subject + har + supine**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har bokat ett rum.** | I have booked a room. |
| **Hon har rest till Sverige.** | She has travelled to Sweden. |
| **Vi har sett den filmen.** | We have seen that film. |

The auxiliary is always **har**, whatever the subject. The supine does not change to agree with the subject or with a noun:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har packat väskan.** | I have packed the bag. |
| **De har packat väskorna.** | They have packed the bags. |

## When to use it

Use the perfect to talk about a past event without placing it at a specific, finished time, or when the past event connects to the present. Common uses include:

- **Experience:** **Har du varit i Stockholm?** (Have you been to Stockholm?)
- **A result that matters now:** **Jag har tappat min nyckel.** (I have lost my key.)
- **Something that has happened during a period that continues now:** **Vi har haft fint väder i veckan.** (We have had nice weather this week.)
- **Something that started in the past and is still true:** **Hon har bott här i fem år.** (She has lived here for five years.)

For an ongoing situation, **sedan** means “since” and **i** often means “for” with a length of time:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har arbetat här sedan 2022.** | I have worked here since 2022. |
| **Vi har varit här i två dagar.** | We have been here for two days. |

The perfect does not always match English *have/has* word for word. Swedish often uses it where English uses the simple past, especially when talking about an experience or an event whose exact time is not important.

## How to form the supine

The supine is a verb form used after **har** (and also after **hade** in the past perfect). Regular verbs have useful patterns, but many common verbs are irregular. Learn the supine along with the infinitive and past-tense form.

| Verb pattern | Infinitive | Present | Preterite | Supine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Group 1: usually **-at** | **prata** (talk) | **pratar** | **pratade** | **pratat** |
| Group 2: usually **-t** | **köpa** (buy) | **köper** | **köpte** | **köpt** |
| Group 2: spelling change | **läsa** (read) | **läser** | **läste** | **läst** |
| Group 3: usually **-tt** | **bo** (live) | **bor** | **bodde** | **bott** |

Examples with common irregular verbs:

| Infinitive | Present | Preterite | Supine | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **vara** (be) | **är** | **var** | **varit** | **Det har varit varmt.** (It has been warm.) |
| **ha** (have) | **har** | **hade** | **haft** | **Vi har haft tur.** (We have been lucky.) |
| **göra** (do, make) | **gör** | **gjorde** | **gjort** | **Vad har du gjort?** (What have you done?) |
| **gå** (go, walk) | **går** | **gick** | **gått** | **De har gått hem.** (They have gone home.) |
| **komma** (come) | **kommer** | **kom** | **kommit** | **Tåget har kommit.** (The train has arrived.) |
| **se** (see) | **ser** | **såg** | **sett** | **Jag har sett den filmen.** (I have seen that film.) |
| **säga** (say) | **säger** | **sa** | **sagt** | **Hon har sagt det.** (She has said that.) |
| **få** (get, receive) | **får** | **fick** | **fått** | **Vi har fått ett mejl.** (We have received an email.) |
| **ta** (take) | **tar** | **tog** | **tagit** | **Han har tagit bussen.** (He has taken the bus.) |
| **ge** (give) | **ger** | **gav** | **gett** | **Jag har gett henne nyckeln.** (I have given her the key.) |
| **bli** (become) | **blir** | **blev** | **blivit** | **Det har blivit kallare.** (It has become colder.) |
| **äta** (eat) | **äter** | **åt** | **ätit** | **Barnen har ätit frukost.** (The children have eaten breakfast.) |
| **dricka** (drink) | **dricker** | **drack** | **druckit** | **Jag har druckit kaffe.** (I have drunk coffee.) |
| **skriva** (write) | **skriver** | **skrev** | **skrivit** | **Hon har skrivit ett meddelande.** (She has written a message.) |

## Word order, negatives, and questions

In an ordinary statement, **har** follows the subject and **inte** comes after **har**, before the supine:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har redan packat.** | I have already packed. |
| **Vi har inte bokat hotellet än.** | We have not booked the hotel yet. |

If another phrase comes first, the finite verb **har** still comes in the second position, before the subject:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **I år har det regnat mycket.** | This year it has rained a lot. |
| **Efter resan har hon vilat hemma.** | After the trip, she has rested at home. |

For a yes-or-no question, put **har** before the subject. A question word comes before **har**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Har du varit i Malmö?** | Have you been to Malmö? |
| **Har de kommit än?** | Have they arrived yet? |
| **Vad har ni sett?** | What have you seen? |

In a subordinate clause, such as a clause beginning with **att** (that), **om** (if/whether), or **eftersom** (because), **inte** usually comes before **har**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag vet att hon inte har kommit än.** | I know that she has not arrived yet. |
| **Vi stannar inne eftersom det har börjat regna.** | We are staying inside because it has started to rain. |

## Perfect or preterite?

The **preterite** presents an event as belonging to a finished time in the past. The **perfect** connects a past event to the present or leaves its exact past time unspecified.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag såg den filmen i går.** | I saw that film yesterday. |
| **Jag har sett den filmen.** | I have seen that film. |
| **Vi bodde i Göteborg förra året.** | We lived in Gothenburg last year. |
| **Vi har bott i Göteborg i tre år.** | We have lived in Gothenburg for three years. |

Use the preterite when you say when something happened in a finished period, for example **i går** (yesterday), **förra veckan** (last week), or **2020**. The perfect is common with an unfinished period such as **i dag** (today) or **i år** (this year), and when the exact time is not the point. The choice can depend on how the speaker views the time period and its connection to now, so these are useful patterns rather than absolute rules.
