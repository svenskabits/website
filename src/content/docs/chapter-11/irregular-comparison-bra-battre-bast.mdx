The adjective **bra** (good) has irregular comparative and superlative forms. Learn all three forms together:

| Basic form | Comparative | Superlative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bra** | **bättre** | **bäst** | good, better, best |

Do not add the regular endings to **bra** or use **mer/mest** with it. The standard forms are **bättre** and **bäst**.

## Comparing two things: bättre

Use **bättre än** to say that one person or thing is better than another. **Än** means “than.”

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Den här restaurangen är bättre än den andra.** | This restaurant is better than the other one. |
| **Vädret är bättre i dag.** | The weather is better today. |
| **Jag mår bättre nu.** | I feel better now. |

The form **bättre** stays the same with en-words, ett-words, and plural nouns:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vi behöver en bättre karta.** | We need a better map. |
| **Det finns ett bättre hotell nära stationen.** | There is a better hotel near the station. |
| **De här skorna är bättre för långa promenader.** | These shoes are better for long walks. |

You can leave out **än** when the comparison is clear from the conversation: **Den här är bättre.** (This one is better.)

## Saying that something is the best: bäst

Use **bäst** when the quality describes a person or thing and there is no noun immediately after it. It can be followed by a phrase that says where or among whom it is best:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det här hotellet är bäst.** | This hotel is the best. |
| **Hon är bäst i laget.** | She is the best on the team. |
| **Vilken dag passar bäst?** | Which day works best? |

When the superlative comes before a definite noun, use **bästa**. The noun is definite too, and normally has **den**, **det**, or **de** before it:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det här är den bästa restaurangen i stan.** | This is the best restaurant in town. |
| **Vi bodde på det bästa hotellet.** | We stayed at the best hotel. |
| **De bästa skorna är redan sålda.** | The best shoes have already been sold. |

With a possessive such as **min** (my) or **vår** (our), use **bästa** without **den/det/de**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det här är min bästa vän.** | This is my best friend. |
| **Det var vår bästa semester.** | It was our best holiday. |

If the noun is understood, use **bästa** in a definite phrase: **Det här är det bästa.** (This is the best one.)

## Bra can also describe an action

**Bra** can describe how something is done, like “well” in English. Its comparison forms are still **bättre** and **bäst**:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Du spelar bra.** | You play well. |
| **Du spelar bättre än jag.** | You play better than I do. |
| **Hon spelar bäst av alla.** | She plays the best of all. |

Useful combinations include **bli bättre på** (get better at) and **göra sitt bästa** (do one’s best):

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag vill bli bättre på svenska.** | I want to get better at Swedish. |
| **Vi gjorde vårt bästa.** | We did our best. |

To add emphasis, put **mycket** or **ännu** before the comparative, and **allra** before the superlative: **mycket bättre** (much better), **ännu bättre** (even better), **allra bäst** (the very best).
