# En- and ett-words
*En- och ett-ord*

## Goal

After this page you can use **en** or **ett** with a common Swedish noun.

---

## Overview

| Swedish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **en** | a, an (en-word) | Jag har **en** katt. *(I have a cat.)* |
| **ett** | a, an (ett-word) | Jag har **ett** äpple. *(I have an apple.)* |

---

## How it works

1. **You cannot always guess.** There is no simple rule that tells you whether a noun takes *en* or *ett*. Learn each noun with its article.
   *Det är **en** stol.* (It is a chair.) · *Det är **ett** bord.* (It is a table.)
2. **Put the article before a singular noun.** Use *en* or *ett* when you mean one thing.
   *Ali har **en** hund.* (Ali has a dog.) · *Sara har **ett** barn.* (Sara has a child.)
3. **Do not use en or ett with plural nouns.** The noun has a plural form instead.
   *Här är **en** katt. Här är två katter.* (Here is a cat. Here are two cats.)

---

## Good to know

- **The same word group affects “the.”** *En bil* becomes *bilen*; *ett hus* becomes *huset*.
- **The word group also affects “it.”** Use *den* for an en-word and *det* for an ett-word: *en bil* → *den*, *ett hus* → *det*.

---

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Jag har **ett** bil. | Jag har **en** bil. | *Bil* is an en-word. |
| Det är **en** hus. | Det är **ett** hus. | *Hus* is an ett-word. |
| Jag ser **en** äpple. | Jag ser **ett** äpple. | *Äpple* is an ett-word. |

---

## Recap

- Swedish nouns are **en-words** or **ett-words**.
- Learn the article with the noun: **en bil**, **ett hus**.
- Use **en** or **ett** with a singular noun, not with a plural noun.
