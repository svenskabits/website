Swedish nouns belong to one of two grammatical groups: **en-words** and **ett-words**. The group is part of the noun, so learn the article together with each new word: **en stol** (a chair), **ett bord** (a table).

## The indefinite article

Use **en** or **ett** before a singular noun when you mean one, not a particular one. English uses *a* or *an* for both.

| Noun group | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| En-word | **en** katt | a cat |
| Ett-word | **ett** äpple | an apple |

The article does not change for the first letter or sound of the next word. Swedish uses **en** or **ett** according to the noun's group, not according to whether the following sound is a vowel or consonant.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Hon har **en** idé. | She has an idea. |
| Han har **ett** jobb. | He has a job. |

The words **en** and **ett** can also mean the number *one*. The context makes the meaning clear. When you emphasize the number, you can stress it: *Jag vill ha en, inte två.* (I want one, not two.)

## Noun group is part of the word

There is no dependable rule that lets you work out the group of every noun. Most Swedish nouns are en-words, but many common nouns are ett-words. Learn a noun with its article, for example in a vocabulary list or phrase: **en nyckel** (a key), **ett fönster** (a window).

Some word endings give useful clues, but they are tendencies, not guarantees:

| Often an en-word | Often an ett-word |
|---|---|
| Nouns ending in **-ning**: **en tidning** (a newspaper), **en övning** (an exercise) | Nouns ending in **-ande** or **-ende** that name an action or result: **ett meddelande** (a message), **ett leende** (a smile) |
| Nouns ending in **-het**: **en möjlighet** (a possibility), **en frihet** (a freedom) | Many nouns ending in **-eri**: **ett bageri** (a bakery), **ett mejeri** (a dairy) |
| Nouns ending in **-tion** or **-sion**: **en station** (a station), **en diskussion** (a discussion) | Many nouns ending in **-um**: **ett centrum** (a centre), **ett museum** (a museum) |
| Nouns ending in **-else**: **en händelse** (an event), **en upplevelse** (an experience) | |

These clues do not replace learning the article. Some endings occur in both groups, and borrowed or short words may not follow a pattern. Nouns ending in **-ande** or **-ende** can refer to a person as well as an action or result: **en sökande** (an applicant), but **ett erbjudande** (an offer). A reliable way to learn is to note **ett meddelande**, but **en lärare** (a teacher).

The noun's grammatical group does not always match whether a person or animal is male or female. Most words for people and animals are en-words, but there are exceptions, such as **ett barn** (a child) and **ett lejon** (a lion). Grammatical group belongs to the word, not to the real-world object's sex.

## Singular, plural and definite forms

The en/ett distinction is visible in several forms of a noun. In the singular, the indefinite article comes before the noun. In the definite singular, the noun usually takes an ending instead. There is no **en** or **ett** before an ordinary plural noun.

| Form | En-word: **bil** (car) | Ett-word: **hus** (house) |
|---|---|---|
| Indefinite singular | **en bil** — a car | **ett hus** — a house |
| Definite singular | **bilen** — the car | **huset** — the house |
| Indefinite plural | **bilar** — cars | **hus** — houses |
| Definite plural | **bilarna** — the cars | **husen** — the houses |

Plural endings vary from noun to noun. The table shows common forms, not a rule that every en-word adds **-ar** or every ett-word stays unchanged. Learn the plural with the noun when it is not predictable.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| Jag köper **en stol**. | I am buying a chair. |
| **Stolen** är dyr. | The chair is expensive. |
| Vi köper **stolar**. | We are buying chairs. |
| **Stolarna** är dyra. | The chairs are expensive. |
| De bor i **ett hus**. | They live in a house. |
| **Huset** är gammalt. | The house is old. |
| Det finns **hus** här. | There are houses here. |
| **Husen** är gamla. | The houses are old. |

## Agreement with adjectives

An adjective directly describing an indefinite singular noun usually has an **-t** ending with an ett-word, but not with an en-word:

| Noun group | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| En-word | **en stor bil** | a big car |
| Ett-word | **ett stort hus** | a big house |

The same difference appears after **är** (is) when the adjective describes the noun: **Bilen är stor.** (The car is big.) **Huset är stort.** (The house is big.)

With a definite noun and an adjective in front, Swedish normally uses **den** with an en-word and **det** with an ett-word. The adjective takes the definite form, often ending in **-a**:

| Noun group | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| En-word | **den stora bilen** | the big car |
| Ett-word | **det stora huset** | the big house |

In the plural, adjectives normally take **-a** for both groups: **stora bilar** (big cars), **stora hus** (big houses), **de stora bilarna** (the big cars), **de stora husen** (the big houses).

## Pronouns and other words that show the group

When **den** or **det** stands for an inanimate noun, choose it to match the noun's group. English usually uses *it* for either one.

| Noun | Swedish | English |
|---|---|---|
| **en bil** (a car) | **Bilen är röd. Den är ny.** | The car is red. It is new. |
| **ett hus** (a house) | **Huset är rött. Det är nytt.** | The house is red. It is new. |

The same forms appear with **här** and **där**: **den här bilen** (this car), **det här huset** (this house), **den där stolen** (that chair), **det där bordet** (that table).

Possessive words also show the group in the singular: **min** with an en-word, **mitt** with an ett-word. In the plural, use **mina** with either group.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **min bok** | my book |
| **mitt rum** | my room |
| **mina böcker** | my books |
| **mina rum** | my rooms |

Other possessive words also change in this way: **din jacka** (your jacket), **ditt namn** (your name), **hans bil** (his car), **hans hus** (his house). The form **hans** does not change because it refers to the owner's gender, not the possessed noun. Some possessives, such as **hans** and **deras**, stay the same with all nouns.

## Compounds

A compound is a word made from two or more joined words. Its en/ett group usually comes from its final part, which names the main thing:

| Main noun | Compound | English |
|---|---|---|
| **en nyckel** (a key) | **en bilnyckel** | a car key |
| **ett kort** (a card) | **ett bankkort** | a bank card |
| **ett rum** (a room) | **ett sovrum** | a bedroom |
| **en bok** (a book) | **en kokbok** | a cookbook |

The first part does not determine the group: **bil** is an en-word, but **ett bilsäte** is an ett-word because **säte** (seat) is the main noun. Compounds are also individual vocabulary items, so check the whole word if you are unsure.

## Using nouns without an article

Do not put **en** or **ett** before an ordinary plural noun: **Jag har två katter.** (I have two cats.) Indefinite plural nouns also appear without an article when you mean things in general or an unspecified number: **Katter tycker om värme.** (Cats like warmth.) **Vi behöver stolar.** (We need chairs.)

Swedish also often uses a noun without **en** or **ett** when it refers generally to a substance, an idea, or an activity, rather than one countable item: **Kaffe är varmt.** (Coffee is hot.) **Hon tycker om musik.** (She likes music.) Whether a noun is countable and how it is used can affect the sentence; do not add an article just because English has one.

## Common mistakes

| Avoid | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *ett bil* | **en bil** | **Bil** is an en-word. |
| *en hus* | **ett hus** | **Hus** is an ett-word. |
| *en äpple* | **ett äpple** | **Äpple** is an ett-word. |
| *två en katt* | **två katter** | Do not use the singular indefinite article with a plural noun. |
| *ett stort bil* | **en stor bil** | The article and adjective both agree with the en-word. |

## Quick reference

- Learn the noun together with **en** or **ett**; the group is usually not possible to predict with certainty.
- Use **en/ett** for an indefinite singular countable noun. Do not use them before an ordinary plural noun.
- The noun group also affects the definite singular (**bilen**, **huset**), some adjective forms (**stor bil**, **stort hus**), and words such as **den/det** and **min/mitt**.
- Compounds usually follow the group of their final, main noun.
