The question word **var** means **where** and asks about a location. Use it to ask where a person, place, or thing is, lives, works, or can be found.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Var är Anna?** | Where is Anna? |
| **Var bor du?** | Where do you live? |
| **Var ligger stationen?** | Where is the station? |
| **Var arbetar din syster?** | Where does your sister work? |

## Word order in a direct question

In a direct question, **var** comes first, followed by the finite verb and then the subject:

**var + verb + subject + the rest**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Var bor Ali?** | Where does Ali live? |
| **Var är toaletten?** | Where is the toilet? |
| **Var kan jag köpa kaffe?** | Where can I buy coffee? |

With a modal verb such as **kan** (can), the modal verb comes before the subject, and the next verb stays in the infinitive: **Var kan vi äta?** (Where can we eat?)

You can answer with a short place expression or a complete sentence. The preposition in the answer depends on the place:

| Question and answer | English |
|---|---|
| **Var bor du? – I Malmö.** | Where do you live? – In Malmö. |
| **Var är hon? – På jobbet.** | Where is she? – At work. |
| **Var är katten? – Den är under bordet.** | Where is the cat? – It is under the table. |

The form **var** stays the same whether you ask one person or several: **Var bor du?** (Where do you live?) **Var bor ni?** (Where do you live? [plural or polite])

## Var in an indirect question

An indirect question is part of a larger sentence. After **var** in the indirect question, put the subject before the verb:

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Var bor Anna?** | **Jag vet var Anna bor.** | Where does Anna live? / I know where Anna lives. |
| **Var är stationen?** | **Kan du säga var stationen är?** | Where is the station? / Can you tell me where the station is? |
| **Var stannar bussen?** | **Jag undrar var bussen stannar.** | Where does the bus stop? / I wonder where the bus stops. |

In **Kan du säga** (Can you tell me), the whole sentence is a direct question, so its verb comes before its subject. Inside it, **var stationen är** (where the station is) is an indirect question, so **stationen** comes before **är**. An indirect question in a statement usually ends with a period; the whole sentence takes a question mark if it is itself a direct question.

## Var asks for a location, not a destination

In standard Swedish, **var** asks where someone or something is. Use **vart** to ask where someone or something is going:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Var är du?** | Where are you? |
| **Vart går du?** | Where are you going? |

In some everyday speech, **var** is also used for a destination, but the distinction between **var** (location) and **vart** (destination) is a useful standard pattern.

Remember: use **var** to ask for a place, put the verb before the subject in a direct question, and put the subject before the verb after **var** in an indirect question.
