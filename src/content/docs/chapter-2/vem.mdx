The question word **vem** means **who**. It asks about a person. Swedish uses the same form whether the person is doing the action (the **subject**) or receiving it (the **object**). In natural English, *who* is common for both roles; *whom* is a more formal object form.

## Vem as the subject

When **vem** is the subject—the person doing the action—it comes before the verb. There is no other subject after the verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vem kommer i dag?** | Who is coming today? |
| **Vem ringde dig?** | Who called you? |
| **Vem känner Sara?** | Who knows Sara? |

In **Vem ringde dig?** (Who called you?), **vem** is the person who called, and **dig** (you) is the person they called.

## Vem as the object

When **vem** asks about the person receiving the action, the verb comes before the subject, just as in other direct questions:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vem ringde du?** | Who did you call? |
| **Vem träffar Anna?** | Who is Anna meeting? |
| **Vem väntar ni på?** | Who are you waiting for? |

In **Vem ringde du?** (Who did you call?), **du** (you) is the subject and **vem** asks about the person you called. Compare it with **Vem ringde dig?** (Who called you?): the form **vem** is the same, but its role in the question is different.

The direct-question pattern for an object question is:

**vem + verb + subject + the rest**

Swedish does not add *do* or *does* in the English-style question. For example, **Vem ser du?** means *Who do you see?* (or, more formally, *Whom do you see?*).

## Vem in indirect questions

In an indirect question, the subject normally comes before the verb when **vem** is the object:

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Vem träffade du?** | **Jag vet vem du träffade.** | Who did you meet? / I know who you met. |
| **Vem ser Anna?** | **Kan du säga vem Anna ser?** | Who does Anna see? / Can you tell me who Anna sees? |

When **vem** is the subject in an indirect question, Swedish commonly puts **som** (who/that) after **vem**, before the verb:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag vet vem som kommer.** | I know who is coming. |
| **Vet du vem som ringde?** | Do you know who called? |

In **Vet du** (Do you know), the outer sentence is a direct question, so the verb comes before its subject. Inside it, **vem som ringde** (who called) is an indirect question.

## Do not confuse vem with vad or var

Use **vem** for a person, **vad** for a thing or information, and **var** for a location:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vem är läraren?** | Who is the teacher? |
| **Vad heter läraren?** | What is the teacher's name? |
| **Var arbetar läraren?** | Where does the teacher work? |

To ask for someone's name, say **Vad heter du?** (What is your name?), not *Vem heter du?*

Remember: **vem** keeps the same form as subject and object. In a direct object question, put the verb before the subject; in an indirect question, put the subject before the verb. For an indirect question where **vem** is the subject, use **vem som** before the verb.
