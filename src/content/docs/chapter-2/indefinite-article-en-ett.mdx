Swedish has two indefinite articles, **en** and **ett**. They usually correspond to English *a* or *an* and come before a singular noun when you introduce one unspecified thing.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har en bil.** | I have a car. |
| **Hon köper ett äpple.** | She is buying an apple. |

The choice between **en** and **ett** belongs to the noun. Learn the article together with each noun. It does not depend on whether the next word starts with a vowel or a consonant:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en idé** | an idea |
| **ett jobb** | a job |

## When to use the article

Use **en** or **ett** when a singular countable noun means one, non-specific item—often when you mention it for the first time:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Det står en kopp på bordet.** | There is a cup on the table. |
| **Jag behöver ett paraply.** | I need an umbrella. |

The article stays before the noun if an adjective comes between them:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **en gammal bil** | an old car |
| **ett stort fönster** | a big window |

Do not use **en** or **ett** with an ordinary plural noun. The plural is used by itself for an unspecified number or for things in general:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag köper äpplen.** | I am buying apples. |
| **Hundar behöver motion.** | Dogs need exercise. |

Some nouns that refer to substances or ideas are also commonly used without an article when they are meant generally: **Vi dricker kaffe.** (We drink coffee.) **Hon tycker om musik.** (She likes music.)

## When another word takes its place

Do not put **en** or **ett** together with a possessive such as **min** (my) or **din** (your). The possessive already comes before the noun:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **min bok** | my book |
| **ditt namn** | your name |

Likewise, a negative word such as **ingen** or **inget** is used instead of the article: **Jag har ingen bil.** (I don't have a car.) **Vi har inget hus.** (We don't have a house.)

## Professions and roles

After **vara** (to be), a profession or role is often used without **en** or **ett** when there is no adjective:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Han är lärare.** | He is a teacher. |
| **Hon är läkare.** | She is a doctor. |

If you describe the profession with an adjective, the article is normally used:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Han är en erfaren lärare.** | He is an experienced teacher. |
| **Hon är en skicklig läkare.** | She is a skilled doctor. |

## The meaning of en as “one”

**En** can be the article *a* or the number *one*. Usually the context makes the meaning clear. Stress **en** when you want to emphasize the number:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Jag har en bil.** | I have a car. |
| **Jag har en bil, inte två.** | I have one car, not two. |

Use the article with one unspecified singular item; do not add it before a plural: **en katt** (a cat), **två katter** (two cats).
