# Indefinite article (en/ett)
*Obestämd artikel (en/ett)*

## Goal

After this page you can use **en** or **ett** to introduce a singular noun.

---

## Overview

| Swedish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **en** | a, an (with en-words) | **En** bil står här. *(A car is here.)* |
| **ett** | a, an (with ett-words) | **Ett** hus står här. *(A house is here.)* |

---

## How it works

1. **Use en or ett for one, non-specific thing.** It introduces a thing when the listener does not know exactly which one you mean.
   *Ali har **en** hund.* (Ali has a dog.) · *Sara har **ett** barn.* (Sara has a child.)
2. **Learn the article with the noun.** Every Swedish noun is an en-word or an ett-word, and there is no simple way to guess which one.
   *Det är **en** stol.* (It is a chair.) · *Det är **ett** bord.* (It is a table.)
3. **Use en or ett with a singular noun, not a plural noun.** The plural noun does not take this article.
   *Här är **en** katt. Här är två katter.* (Here is a cat. Here are two cats.)

---

## Good to know

- **English uses a or an by sound.** Swedish uses *en* or *ett* based on the noun, not the sound that follows.
- **En can also mean “one.”** In *Jag har en bil*, it usually means “I have a car”; context can make the number important: *Jag har en bil, inte två.* (I have one car, not two.)

---

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Jag har **ett** bil. | Jag har **en** bil. | *Bil* is an en-word. |
| Det är **en** hus. | Det är **ett** hus. | *Hus* is an ett-word. |
| Jag ser **en** äpple. | Jag ser **ett** äpple. | *Äpple* is an ett-word. |

---

## Recap

- Use **en** or **ett** before a singular noun to introduce one non-specific thing.
- Learn the article with each noun: **en bil**, **ett hus**.
- Do not use **en** or **ett** with plural nouns.
