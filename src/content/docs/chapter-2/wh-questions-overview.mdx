Swedish question words ask for information such as a person, thing, place, time, reason, or manner. In a **direct question**, the question word usually comes first, followed by the verb and then the subject. Swedish does not add *do* or *does* to make a question.

| Question word | Meaning | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **vad** | what | **Vad heter du?** | What is your name? |
| **vem** | who/whom | **Vem träffar du?** | Who are you meeting? |
| **var** | where (location) | **Var bor du?** | Where do you live? |
| **vart** | where to (destination) | **Vart går bussen?** | Where is the bus going? |
| **varifrån** | where from | **Varifrån kommer du?** | Where are you from? |
| **när** | when | **När börjar filmen?** | When does the film start? |
| **varför** | why | **Varför är du trött?** | Why are you tired? |
| **hur** | how | **Hur mår du?** | How are you? |

## Word order in direct questions

With a question word as the object or another part of the sentence, use this order:

**question word + verb + subject + the rest**

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vad läser Sara?** | What is Sara reading? |
| **Var arbetar din bror?** | Where does your brother work? |
| **När kommer tåget?** | When is the train coming? |
| **Vem väntar du på?** | Who are you waiting for? |

The verb comes before the subject even when English uses *do* or *does*: **Var bor Ali?** (Where does Ali live?)

When the question word itself is the subject, it comes before the verb and there is no separate subject after the verb: **Vem kommer i dag?** (Who is coming today?) **Vad hände?** (What happened?)

Compare these questions:

| Swedish | English | What is being asked? |
|---|---|---|
| **Vem ringde dig?** | Who called you? | **Vem** is the subject: the person who called. |
| **Vem ringde du?** | Who did you call? | **Du** is the subject; **vem** asks about the person called. |

## More question words and common phrases

**Vilken**, **vilket**, and **vilka** mean *which* or sometimes *what*. Their form changes to match the noun: use **vilken** with an en-word, **vilket** with an ett-word, and **vilka** with a plural noun.

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Vilken bok vill du läsa?** | Which book do you want to read? |
| **Vilket tåg tar vi?** | Which train are we taking? |
| **Vilka språk talar du?** | Which languages do you speak? |

Use **hur mycket** for an amount or price, and **hur många** for a number of countable things:

| Swedish | English |
|---|---|
| **Hur mycket kostar det?** | How much does it cost? |
| **Hur många syskon har du?** | How many siblings do you have? |

Other useful combinations include **hur gammal** (how old) and **hur ofta** (how often): **Hur gammal är din son?** (How old is your son?) **Hur ofta tränar du?** (How often do you exercise?)

In standard Swedish, **var** asks about a location and **vart** about a destination. In everyday speech, people may use them differently, but keeping this distinction is a useful guide. **Var är du?** (Where are you?) asks for a location; **Vart ska du?** (Where are you going?) asks for a destination.

## Indirect questions

An indirect question is part of a larger statement or question. After its question word, the subject normally comes before the verb, unlike in a direct question:

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|---|---|---|
| **Var bor hon?** | **Jag vet var hon bor.** | Where does she live? / I know where she lives. |
| **Vad heter han?** | **Kan du säga vad han heter?** | What is his name? / Can you tell me what his name is? |
| **När börjar filmen?** | **Jag undrar när filmen börjar.** | When does the film start? / I wonder when the film starts. |

Notice that **Kan du säga** (Can you tell me) is itself a direct question, so its verb comes before its subject. Inside that question, **vad han heter** (what his name is) is an indirect question, so its subject **han** comes before its verb **heter**. An indirect question usually ends with a period unless the whole sentence is a direct question.

For an indirect yes-or-no question, use **om** (if/whether): **Jag undrar om hon kommer.** (I wonder if she is coming.)

## Common mistakes

| Avoid | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Var heter du?* | **Vad heter du?** (What is your name?) | Use **vad** to ask for a name. |
| *Vad du läser?* | **Vad läser du?** (What are you reading?) | In a direct question, put the verb before the subject. |
| *Jag vet var bor hon.* | **Jag vet var hon bor.** (I know where she lives.) | In an indirect question, put the subject before the verb. |

Choose the question word for the information you need, then use direct-question word order in the direct question. In an indirect question, keep the subject before the verb after the question word.
