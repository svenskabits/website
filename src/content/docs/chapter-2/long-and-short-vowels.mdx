# Long and short vowels
*Långa och korta vokaler*

## Goal

After this page you can use Swedish spelling to choose a long or short vowel when you read a common word.

---

## Overview

| What you see | What you say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Stressed vowel + **one** consonant | **Long** vowel | **bil** *(car)* |
| Stressed vowel + **two** consonants | **Short** vowel | **buss** *(bus)* |
| Stressed vowel at the **end** of a word | **Long** vowel | **ny** *(new)* |

---

## How it works

1. **A stressed syllable has one long sound.** Make either the vowel long or the consonant after it long. In *bil*, hold the vowel; in *buss*, hold the final consonant.
   *bil* (car) · *buss* (bus)
2. **One consonant after the vowel usually means a long vowel.** This is common in the first, stressed part of a word.
   *fin* (nice) · *sova* (to sleep) · *ny* (new)
3. **Two consonants after the vowel usually mean a short vowel.** The consonants can be the same or different.
   *katt* (cat) · *sommar* (summer) · *fisk* (fish)
4. **A stressed vowel at the end of a word is long.** Keep it steady; do not add a consonant sound after it.
   *bo* (to live) · *vi* (we) · *nu* (now)

## Words to practice

Say each pair slowly. Notice whether the vowel or the consonant is long.

| Long vowel | Short vowel |
|---|---|
| **vit** *(white, common form)* | **vitt** *(white, neuter form)* |
| **glas** *(drinking glass)* | **glass** *(ice cream)* |
| **väg** *(road)* | **vägg** *(wall)* |
| **vin** *(wine)* | **vinn** *(win!)* |
| **tal** *(number)* | **tall** *(pine)* |

---

## Good to know

- **Length can change meaning.** A long and a short vowel can distinguish words, so listen for the length as well as the vowel's quality.
- **The spelling is a useful guide, not a complete rule for every word.** Learn less common exceptions as whole words.
- **Vowel quality varies slightly by region.** The long-versus-short contrast remains useful across standard Swedish pronunciation.

---

## Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **buss** said with a long u and short s | **buss** said with a short u and long s | Two consonant letters follow the vowel |
| **fin** said with a short i | **fin** said with a long i | One consonant follows the vowel |
| **katt** said with a long a | **katt** said with a short a and long t | The double t marks a short vowel |
| **ny** said with a short y | **ny** said with a long y | The stressed vowel ends the word |

---

## Recap

- A stressed syllable usually has **one long sound**: a long vowel or a long consonant.
- **One consonant** after a stressed vowel usually signals a **long vowel**; **two** usually signal a **short vowel**.
- A stressed vowel at the **end of a word** is long.
